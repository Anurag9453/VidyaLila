## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Euro VI / BS-VI Standards** | Stringent vehicular emission and fuel purity standards mandating ultra-low sulfur content in petrol and diesel | Maximum sulfur content capped at roughly 10 ppm |
| **Catalytic converter** | An exhaust-pipe emissions control device that utilizes precious metal catalysts to convert toxic exhaust gases into benign atmospheric compounds | Modern passenger car exhaust units |
| **Catalyst** | A substance that accelerates the rate of a chemical reaction without being consumed or chemically altered itself ([chemical-changes-and-reactions-part4.md](../chemical-changes-and-reactions/chemical-changes-and-reactions-part4.md)) | Platinum ($\text{Pt}$), palladium ($\text{Pd}$), rhodium ($\text{Rh}$) |
| **Scrubber** | An industrial gas-cleaning system that sprays alkaline chemical slurries (lime or limestone) to capture acidic $\text{SO}_2$ gas from chimney flue streams | Flue-gas desulfurization (FGD) systems in thermal plants |
| **Electrostatic precipitator (ESP)** | An industrial filtration device that uses high-voltage electrostatic charges to attract and remove fine solid ash and dust particles from flue gases | Particulate fly ash collectors in power station smokestacks |
| **Renewable energy** | Naturally replenished, non-combustive energy sources that generate electricity without emitting atmospheric pollutants | Solar photovoltaic, wind turbines, hydroelectric power |
| **Green hydrogen** | High-purity hydrogen gas produced by the electrolytic splitting of water ($2\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow 2\text{H}_2 + \text{O}_2$) powered entirely by renewable electricity | Zero-carbon fuel for heavy transport and steel manufacturing |
| **Green belt** | A deliberate perimeter buffer zone of dense tree and shrub plantings surrounding industrial parks or highways | Roadside vegetative sound and dust barriers |
| **Waste segregation** | The systematic separation of municipal refuse into distinct organic (biodegradable), dry recyclable, and hazardous waste streams | Multi-bin domestic waste collection |
| **Carbon footprint** | The total quantity of greenhouse gas emissions (expressed in equivalent mass of $\text{CO}_2$) generated directly and indirectly by an individual, household, or organization | Daily commuting and electricity tracking |

## Part A: Stopping Pollution at the Source: Clean Fuels and Low-Emission Vehicles

The most effective environmental engineering strategy is **source prevention**: preventing pollutants from ever forming in the first place.

**1. Ultra-Low-Sulfur Fuels (Euro VI / BS-VI Framework)**
In Topic 2, we discovered that sulfur impurities in fossil fuels combust directly into sulfur dioxide:
$$\text{S} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{SO}_2$$
Modern vehicular fuel regulations (such as Euro VI in Europe and Bharat Stage VI across India) mandate that sulfur content in commercial petrol and diesel must not exceed **$10\text{ ppm}$ by mass** — down from $50\text{ ppm}$ in previous standards and $350\text{ ppm}$ in historical diesel fuels.

**Example 1: Calculating sulfur and $\text{SO}_2$ reductions.**
Consider a delivery truck fleet consuming $1{,}000\text{ kg}$ of diesel fuel:
- Under older $50\text{ ppm}$ diesel:
  $$\text{Sulfur mass} = 1{,}000\text{ kg} \times \frac{50}{1{,}000{,}000} = 0.05\text{ kg} = \mathbf{50\text{ g of sulfur}}$$
  Burning $50\text{ g}$ of sulfur generates:
  $$\text{SO}_2\text{ mass} = 50\text{ g} \times \frac{64\text{ g/mol}}{32\text{ g/mol}} = \mathbf{100\text{ g of }\text{SO}_2}$$
- Under modern $10\text{ ppm}$ ultra-low-sulfur diesel:
  $$\text{Sulfur mass} = 1{,}000\text{ kg} \times \frac{10}{1{,}000{,}000} = 0.01\text{ kg} = \mathbf{10\text{ g of sulfur}}$$
  $$\text{SO}_2\text{ mass} = 10\text{ g} \times 2 = \mathbf{20\text{ g of }\text{SO}_2}$$
- Net reduction:
  $$100\text{ g} - 20\text{ g} = \mathbf{80\text{ g of }\text{SO}_2\text{ prevented}}\ (80\%\text{ reduction!})$$

> **Verification:**
> Percentage drop: $\frac{50 - 10}{50} \times 100 = 80\%$ ✅.

**2. Gaseous Alternative Fuels: CNG and LPG**
**Compressed Natural Gas (CNG)** consists predominantly of methane ($\text{CH}_4$), while **Liquefied Petroleum Gas (LPG)** consists of propane ($\text{C}_3\text{H}_8$) and butane ($\text{C}_4\text{H}_{10}$). These gaseous fuels burn much more cleanly than liquid diesel or petrol, yielding virtually zero soot particles, negligible sulfur emissions, and fewer carbon monoxide molecules.

**Example 2: Comparing $\text{CO}_2$ emissions per kilogram of fuel.**
- Burning $1\text{ kg}$ of CNG (methane, $16\text{ g/mol}$):
  $$\text{CH}_4 + 2\text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{CO}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
  $$\text{Mass of }\text{CO}_2 = 1\text{ kg} \times \frac{44}{16} = \mathbf{2.75\text{ kg of }\text{CO}_2}$$
- Burning $1\text{ kg}$ of petrol (modeled as octane, $114\text{ g/mol}$):
  $$2\text{C}_8\text{H}_{18} + 25\text{O}_2 \rightarrow 16\text{CO}_2 + 18\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
  $$\text{Mass of }\text{CO}_2 = 1\text{ kg} \times \frac{16 \times 44}{2 \times 114} = 1\text{ kg} \times \frac{704}{228} = \mathbf{3.09\text{ kg of }\text{CO}_2}$$
Per kilogram of fuel consumed, methane releases approximately **11% less carbon dioxide** than petrol. Furthermore, because methane possesses a higher energy density per unit mass, the net carbon advantage per megajoule of delivered engine energy is even greater!

**3. Electric Vehicles (EVs)**
Battery electric vehicles generate zero tailpipe emissions, immediately improving local roadside air quality by eliminating street-level $\text{NO}_x$, $\text{CO}$, and exhaust soot. To maximize their planetary benefit, however, the electricity charging their batteries must originate from **clean renewable sources** (solar, wind, hydro) rather than coal-fired power stations.

**4. Advanced Domestic Cookstoves**
Transitioning developing households from raw firewood and cow dung to LPG, biogas, or electric induction cooktops directly eliminates indoor particulate soot and deadly carbon monoxide exposure for millions of families.

## Part B: Catalytic Converters: The Chemical Cleaning Unit in Automobiles

Internal combustion engines emit three dangerous primary pollutants:
1. Lethal, unburnt **carbon monoxide ($\text{CO}$)**.
2. Acid-forming, smog-producing **nitrogen oxides ($\text{NO}$ and $\text{NO}_2$)**.
3. **Unburnt volatile hydrocarbons ($\text{C}_n\text{H}_m$)** from incomplete fuel combustion.

The **catalytic converter** is installed directly into the exhaust pipe upstream of the silencer. Inside its stainless steel canister lies an extruded ceramic **honeycomb matrix** featuring thousands of micro-channels. The interior walls of these channels are wash-coated with microscopic nanoparticles of precious transition metals: **platinum ($\text{Pt}$)**, **palladium ($\text{Pd}$)**, and **rhodium ($\text{Rh}$)**.

```
   Raw Engine Exhaust Gases
   (Hot CO, NO, NO₂, unburnt hydrocarbons)
              |
              v
   +------------------------------------+
   |  # # # # # # # # # # # # # # # #   |  Ceramic honeycomb substrate
   |  # # # # # # # # # # # # # # # #   |  coated with precious metal catalysts:
   |  # # # # # # # # # # # # # # # #   |  Platinum (Pt), Palladium (Pd), Rhodium (Rh)
   +------------------------------------+
              |
              v
   Purified Tailpipe Emissions
   (Harmless atmospheric N₂, H₂O vapour, and recycled CO₂)
              |
              v
        Out to Ambient Air
```

> **Figure 6.1** – Schematic architecture of an automotive catalytic converter: hot exhaust gases stream across catalytic honeycomb channels where oxidation and reduction reactions simultaneously convert toxic $\text{CO}$, $\text{NO}_x$, and unburnt fuel into harmless $\text{N}_2$, water vapour, and $\text{CO}_2$.

**Chemical Reactions Inside the Converter:**

| Target Pollutant | Governing Chemical Reaction | Environmental Transformation |
|---|---|---|
| **Carbon Monoxide ($\text{CO}$)** | $2\text{CO} + \text{O}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Pt/Pd}} 2\text{CO}_2$ | Toxic $\text{CO}$ is oxidized into stable $\text{CO}_2$. |
| **Nitric Oxide ($\text{NO}$)** | $2\text{NO} + 2\text{CO} \xrightarrow{\text{Rh}} \text{N}_2 + 2\text{CO}_2$ | $\text{NO}$ is reduced to harmless atmospheric nitrogen gas ($\text{N}_2$), simultaneously neutralizing $\text{CO}$. |
| **Nitrogen Dioxide ($\text{NO}_2$)** | $2\text{NO}_2 + 4\text{CO} \xrightarrow{\text{Rh}} \text{N}_2 + 4\text{CO}_2$ | Corrosive brown $\text{NO}_2$ is converted into inert $\text{N}_2$. |
| **Unburnt Hydrocarbons** | $2\text{C}_8\text{H}_{18} + 25\text{O}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Pt}} 16\text{CO}_2 + 18\text{H}_2\text{O}$ | Smog-forming fuel vapours are fully oxidized into $\text{CO}_2$ and steam. |

Notice the elegant chemical symmetry: **reducing hazardous nitrogen oxides into inert $\text{N}_2$** (which already makes up 78% of our atmosphere) simultaneously provides the oxygen needed to **oxidize toxic carbon monoxide into $\text{CO}_2$**!

**Example 3: Stoichiometric oxidation of carbon monoxide.**
Suppose a vehicle engine releases $2.8\text{ g}$ of $\text{CO}$ during a cold start:
$$\text{Moles of CO} = \frac{2.8\text{ g}}{28\text{ g/mol}} = 0.1\text{ mol}$$
From $2\text{CO} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{CO}_2$, exactly $0.1\text{ mol of }\text{CO}_2$ is produced:
$$\text{Mass of }\text{CO}_2 = 0.1\text{ mol} \times 44\text{ g/mol} = \mathbf{4.4\text{ g of }\text{CO}_2}$$
$$\text{Mass of }\text{O}_2\text{ consumed} = 0.05\text{ mol} \times 32\text{ g/mol} = \mathbf{1.6\text{ g of }\text{O}_2}$$
> **Mass Conservation Check:**
> $2.8\text{ g } (\text{CO}) + 1.6\text{ g } (\text{O}_2) = \mathbf{4.4\text{ g}}$ ✅.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> A critical truth: **catalytic converters eliminate toxic chemical poisons, but they do NOT eliminate carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$).** In fact, they produce $\text{CO}_2$ by oxidizing $\text{CO}$ and unburnt hydrocarbons. Thus, combating global warming requires consuming less fuel overall and adopting zero-emission technologies.

**Example 4: Reduction of nitric oxide ($\text{NO}$) into molecular nitrogen ($\text{N}_2$).**
An engine produces $60\text{ g}$ of $\text{NO}$ ($\text{molar mass } 30\text{ g/mol}$):
$$\text{Moles of NO} = \frac{60\text{ g}}{30\text{ g/mol}} = 2.0\text{ mol}$$
From $2\text{NO} + 2\text{CO} \rightarrow \text{N}_2 + 2\text{CO}_2$:
- Molecular nitrogen formed: $1.0\text{ mol} \times 28\text{ g/mol} = \mathbf{28\text{ g of }\text{N}_2}$
- Carbon monoxide consumed: $2.0\text{ mol} \times 28\text{ g/mol} = \mathbf{56\text{ g of CO}}$
- Carbon dioxide formed: $2.0\text{ mol} \times 44\text{ g/mol} = \mathbf{88\text{ g of }\text{CO}_2}$
> **Mass Balance Verification:**
> Reactants: $60\text{ g } (\text{NO}) + 56\text{ g } (\text{CO}) = \mathbf{116\text{ g}}$.
> Products: $28\text{ g } (\text{N}_2) + 88\text{ g } (\text{CO}_2) = \mathbf{116\text{ g}}$ ✅.

**Operational Requirements and Precautions:**
1. **Light-Off Temperature:** Catalytic converters require a high operating temperature (roughly $300^\circ\text{C}$ to $400^\circ\text{C}$) to become fully active; emissions during the first three minutes of a trip are highest before the matrix warms up.
2. **Strict Use of Unleaded Fuel:** Lead additives (historically added to petrol to boost octane) deposit permanently onto precious metal active sites, physically blinding them and destroying catalytic activity ("catalyst poisoning"). Consequently, leaded petrol was banned worldwide.

## Part C: Industrial Chimney Emissions Control: Scrubbers and Precipitators

Thermal power plants, metal smelters, and cement factories emit colossal plumes of gas and particulate matter. Industrial engineers deploy two complementary technologies: **flue-gas scrubbers** for gaseous acids, and **electrostatic precipitators** for solid ash particles.

---

### Industrial Flue-Gas Scrubbers: Capturing $\text{SO}_2$ with Limestone

Sulfur dioxide is an **acidic anhydride**. To neutralize and trap it before it escapes into the clouds to form acid rain, power station smokestacks install wet scrubbers that spray an alkaline slurry of pulverized **limestone ($\text{CaCO}_3$)** or slaked lime directly into the ascending flue gas stream.

Governing chemical reactions:
$$\text{CaCO}_3 + \text{SO}_2 \rightarrow \text{CaSO}_3 + \text{CO}_2\uparrow$$
In the presence of forced air aeration, the calcium sulfite oxidizes into **calcium sulfate dihydrate (synthetic gypsum, $\text{CaSO}_4\cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$)**:
$$2\text{CaSO}_3 + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{CaSO}_4$$
Net overall reaction:
$$2\text{CaCO}_3 + 2\text{SO}_2 + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{CaSO}_4 + 2\text{CO}_2\uparrow$$

This process mirrors the corrosion of marble studied in Topic 2, but here environmental engineers conduct the reaction deliberately inside the chimney to capture sulfur as solid, commercial-grade gypsum used to manufacture drywall, plaster, and construction cement!

**Example 5: Industrial scrubber mass balance.**
A thermal power plant combusts $1{,}000\text{ tonnes}$ of coal containing 1.0% sulfur daily:
- Total sulfur: $1{,}000\text{ tonnes} \times 0.01 = 10\text{ tonnes of S}$.
- Total $\text{SO}_2$ produced ($\text{S}=32,\ \text{SO}_2=64$):
  $$10\text{ tonnes} \times \frac{64}{32} = \mathbf{20\text{ tonnes of }\text{SO}_2}$$
- Limestone required ($1\text{ mol }\text{CaCO}_3 [100\text{ g}] \text{ per mol }\text{SO}_2 [64\text{ g}]$):
  $$\text{Mass of }\text{CaCO}_3 = 20\text{ tonnes} \times \frac{100}{64} = \mathbf{31.25\text{ tonnes of limestone}}$$
- Synthetic gypsum produced ($1\text{ mol }\text{CaSO}_4 [136\text{ g}] \text{ per mol }\text{SO}_2 [64\text{ g}]$):
  $$\text{Mass of }\text{CaSO}_4 = 20\text{ tonnes} \times \frac{136}{64} = \mathbf{42.5\text{ tonnes of solid gypsum}}$$
> **Verification:**
> $20\text{ tonnes of }\text{SO}_2 \div 64 = 0.3125\text{ mega-moles}$.
> $\text{Limestone} = 0.3125 \times 100 = 31.25\text{ tonnes}$ ✅; $\text{Gypsum} = 0.3125 \times 136 = 42.5\text{ tonnes}$ ✅.

---

### Electrostatic Precipitators (ESP): Trapping Particulate Fly Ash with Static Electricity

While scrubbers neutralize gases, solid particulate fly ash and soot bypass liquid sprays. Power stations deploy **Electrostatic Precipitators (ESPs)**:
1. Flue gases pass between high-voltage discharge electrode wires charged negatively to roughly $-50{,}000\text{ volts}$.
2. The intense electric field ionizes surrounding gas molecules, transferring strong negative electrostatic charges onto floating soot and fly ash particles.
3. The negatively charged particles are drawn across the stream by strong electrostatic forces toward grounded, positively charged metal collector plates (applying the core physics rule: *opposite charges attract; like charges repel*).
4. Mechanical rapper hammers strike the collector plates periodically, dropping accumulated cakes of dry fly ash down into hoppers for safe disposal and incorporation into eco-friendly concrete.

**Example 6: ESP collection efficiency.**
A modern industrial ESP operates at a **99.0% collection efficiency**. If a power station smokestack carries $100\text{ kg}$ of raw fly ash per hour:
- Ash captured on collector plates: $100\text{ kg} \times 0.99 = \mathbf{99.0\text{ kg/hr}}$
- Ash escaping into ambient air: $100\text{ kg} \times (1 - 0.99) = \mathbf{1.0\text{ kg/hr}}$

```
  Summary of Chimney Controls:
  - Scrubber captures ACIDIC GASES (SO₂) using alkaline chemistry.
  - Electrostatic Precipitator captures SOLID PARTICLES (fly ash, soot) using static charges.
```

## Part D: Renewable Energy, Green Hydrogen, and Urban Green Belts

**1. The Clean Energy Transition**
Solar photovoltaic panels, wind turbines, and hydroelectric stations generate electrical power without combustion. Because nothing burns, they generate **zero $\text{SO}_2$, zero $\text{NO}_x$, zero PM2.5, and zero direct operational $\text{CO}_2$**. Transitioning national power grids to renewables tackles acid rain, urban smog, and global climate change simultaneously at their common root.

**2. Green Hydrogen**
As introduced in our study of hydrogen ([hydrogen.md](../hydrogen/hydrogen.md)), hydrogen is an exceptionally clean fuel that produces only pure water when combusted:
$$2\text{H}_2 + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
When hydrogen is synthesized through the electrolysis of water powered entirely by surplus wind or solar electricity, it is designated **Green Hydrogen**. It offers a transformative zero-carbon fuel for long-haul shipping, freight trains, and industrial green steel manufacturing.

**3. Urban Green Belts and Forest Buffers**
Trees absorb carbon dioxide via photosynthesis, cast cooling urban shade, and filter airborne dust on their textured foliage. However, planting trees is a **complementary support measure**, not a total substitute for emissions reduction. A single vehicle burns hundreds of litres of fuel annually, requiring dozens of mature trees to sequester its emissions; thus, reducing combustion at the source remains the indispensable primary imperative.

**4. Waste Segregation and Eliminating Open Burning**
Open incineration of municipal solid waste and agricultural crop residue releases dense clouds of toxic PM2.5 and carcinogenic dioxins. Adopting three-bin **waste segregation** (wet biodegradable organics, dry clean recyclables, and domestic hazardous items) enables widespread composting and materials recycling, removing the incentive for open trash fires. Agricultural crop straw can be mechanically blended back into soil as mulch, compressed into bio-fuel pellets, or digested into biomethane.

**Calculating Impact from Diary Data:**

$$\text{Petrol consumed} = \frac{12\text{ km}}{15\text{ km/L}} = 0.8\text{ L}$$

- Daily electrical energy saved:
  $$0.05\text{ kW} \times 5\text{ hours} = \mathbf{0.25\text{ kWh per day}}$$

  $$0.25\text{ kWh/day} \times 365\text{ days} = \mathbf{91.25\text{ kWh per year}}$$

Replacing just one single light bulb prevents over $73\text{ kg}$ of greenhouse gas emissions every year!

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1. **Mistake 1: Believing catalytic converters eliminate carbon dioxide emissions**
   Catalytic converters oxidize toxic carbon monoxide and hydrocarbons into carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$). They do not eliminate greenhouse gases; cutting $\text{CO}_2$ requires consuming less fuel.

2. **Mistake 2: Assuming electric vehicles have zero environmental connection to air pollution**
   While electric vehicles have zero tailpipe emissions, if the electrical grid relies on burning coal, emissions are shifted from highways to power plant smokestacks. True environmental sustainability requires powering EVs with renewable energy.

3. **Mistake 3: Believing individual personal habits have negligible impact**
   As demonstrated in Example 7, switching a single household light bulb prevents $73\text{ kg}$ of $\text{CO}_2$ emissions annually. When adopted across millions of homes, small conscious habits drive massive aggregate reductions.

4. **Mistake 4: Assuming tree planting alone can offset all fossil fuel emissions**
   Afforestation is vital, but trees cannot absorb the billions of tonnes of fossil carbon combusted globally each year. Source emissions reduction must always come first.

5. **Mistake 5: Confusing flue-gas scrubbers with electrostatic precipitators**
   Scrubbers capture **acidic gases** ($\text{SO}_2$) using alkaline chemical solutions; electrostatic precipitators capture **solid particulate matter** (fly ash, soot) using electrostatic charges.

6. **Mistake 6: Believing burning municipal waste is an acceptable disposal method**
   Open incineration generates toxic PM2.5, carbon monoxide, and hazardous chemical carcinogens. Organic composting, material recycling, and sanitary landfilling are vastly superior.

## Quick Recap

- **Source Prevention:** The primary environmental imperative is stopping pollutants before combustion (ultra-low-sulfur fuels $\le 10\text{ ppm}$, clean alternative fuels like CNG and LPG, and electric mobility).
- **Automotive Catalytic Converters:** Honeycomb channels wash-coated with platinum, palladium, and rhodium catalyze $2\text{CO} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{CO}_2$, $2\text{NO} + 2\text{CO} \rightarrow \text{N}_2 + 2\text{CO}_2$, and hydrocarbon oxidation. Requires unleaded fuel to avoid catalyst poisoning.
- **Industrial Smokestack Controls:** Wet limestone scrubbers capture acidic $\text{SO}_2$, converting it into commercial gypsum ($\text{CaSO}_4$); Electrostatic Precipitators (ESPs) charge and capture over 99% of solid particulate fly ash on grounded plates.
- **Renewable Power & Green Hydrogen:** Solar, wind, and hydro eliminate combustion entirely. Green hydrogen generated via renewable water electrolysis produces only water when utilized.
- **Individual Action:** Energy conservation, LED retrofitting, public transportation, waste segregation, and personal carbon auditing ("My Pollution Diary").

## Practice Questions

1. Inside an automotive catalytic converter, $5.6\text{ g}$ of carbon monoxide gas undergoes catalytic oxidation according to $2\text{CO} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{CO}_2$. Calculate the mass of carbon dioxide formed and the mass of oxygen consumed in grams. ($\text{Molar masses: CO}=28\text{ g/mol},\ \text{CO}_2=44\text{ g/mol},\ \text{O}_2=32\text{ g/mol}$.)

2. An internal combustion engine emits $150\text{ g}$ of nitric oxide ($\text{NO}$). Using the converter reaction $2\text{NO} + 2\text{CO} \rightarrow \text{N}_2 + 2\text{CO}_2$:
   (a) Calculate the mass of molecular nitrogen ($\text{N}_2$) generated.
   (b) Calculate the mass of carbon monoxide ($\text{CO}$) consumed.
   (c) Calculate the mass of carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$) produced.
   (d) Demonstrate that mass is conserved across reactants and products.

3. In a catalytic converter, nitrogen dioxide is reduced by carbon monoxide:
   $$2\text{NO}_2 + 4\text{CO} \rightarrow \text{N}_2 + 4\text{CO}_2$$
   If $9.2\text{ g}$ of $\text{NO}_2$ ($46\text{ g/mol}$) reacts, determine the mass of $\text{N}_2$ and $\text{CO}_2$ formed, and the mass of $\text{CO}$ consumed.

4. A thermal power plant burns $600\text{ tonnes}$ of coal per day containing 2.5% sulfur by mass.
   (a) How many tonnes of $\text{SO}_2$ are released into the flue stream daily?
   (b) How many tonnes of pure limestone ($\text{CaCO}_3$) are required to neutralize this gas according to $\text{CaCO}_3 + \text{SO}_2 \rightarrow \text{CaSO}_3 + \text{CO}_2$?
   (c) If the captured sulfur is completely oxidized into calcium sulfate ($\text{CaSO}_4$), what total mass of gypsum in tonnes is produced?

5. Older diesel fuel contained $350\text{ ppm}$ sulfur by mass, whereas modern ultra-low-sulfur diesel contains $10\text{ ppm}$. For a fuel tanker carrying $500\text{ kg}$ of diesel:
   (a) Find the mass of elemental sulfur in grams in each grade.
   (b) Find the mass of $\text{SO}_2$ produced upon complete combustion in each case.
   (c) Calculate the percentage reduction in sulfur emissions achieved by modern fuel standards.

6. An industrial Electrostatic Precipitator operates at a $99.5\%$ particulate collection efficiency. If raw smokestack flue gas introduces $240\text{ kg}$ of particulate fly ash per hour into the unit:
   (a) What mass of fly ash escapes into the atmosphere each hour?
   (b) What mass of fly ash is captured on collector plates each hour?
   (c) Which core principle of electrostatic physics governs this operation?

7. Commercial liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) consists primarily of propane ($\text{C}_3\text{H}_8$, molar mass $44\text{ g/mol}$). Burning $1\text{ kg}$ of propane according to $\text{C}_3\text{H}_8 + 5\text{O}_2 \rightarrow 3\text{CO}_2 + 4\text{H}_2\text{O}$ yields $3.0\text{ kg of }\text{CO}_2$.
   (a) Compare this emission value with methane ($2.75\text{ kg/kg}$) and octane ($3.09\text{ kg/kg}$).
   (b) Does a lower $\text{CO}_2$-per-kilogram figure automatically mean a fuel burns cleaner in all respects? Explain the broader chemical criteria.

8. A green hydrogen fuel-cell passenger bus consumes $10\text{ g}$ of pure hydrogen gas according to $2\text{H}_2 + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$.
   (a) Calculate the mass of atmospheric oxygen consumed and the mass of water vapour produced.
   (b) Explain why hydrogen is described as "clean" at the point of use, and specify what criteria must be met for it to be classified as "green hydrogen".

9. A household replaces a standard $75\text{ W}$ incandescent light bulb with a $15\text{ W}$ LED bulb operated for 4 hours daily over a 30-day billing cycle.
   (a) Calculate the total electrical energy saved in kilowatt-hours ($\text{kWh}$).
   (b) Assuming grid electricity generation emits $0.8\text{ kg of }\text{CO}_2\text{ per kWh}$, calculate the total mass of $\text{CO}_2$ emissions avoided.

10. A group of four students travels $20\text{ km}$ to an inter-school athletic tournament. If they traveled in two separate private cars (each achieving $16\text{ km/L}$), calculate the petrol consumed. If they carpool in a single car of the same efficiency, calculate the litres of petrol and kilograms of $\text{CO}_2$ saved (using $2.3\text{ kg of }\text{CO}_2\text{ per litre}$).

11. An automotive advertisement claims: *"Our advanced catalytic converters have made modern petrol cars completely harmless to the environment."* Provide two scientific reasons why this claim is misleading. Additionally, explain why running leaded petrol ruins a converter permanently.

12. A mature tree absorbs approximately $22\text{ kg of }\text{CO}_2$ annually.
    (a) Using the figure of $2.3\text{ kg of }\text{CO}_2$ per litre of petrol, determine how many litres of petrol combustion match the annual sequestration of one tree.
    (b) State two additional ecological benefits provided by urban trees, and explain why afforestation alone cannot substitute for emissions reduction.

13. Match each air pollution problem with its most effective engineering solution, giving a concise one-line scientific justification:
    - (a) $\text{SO}_2$ emissions from a coal power station.
    - (b) Fine particulate dust in industrial chimney smoke.
    - (c) Toxic $\text{CO}$ and $\text{NO}_x$ in automobile exhaust.
    - (d) High winter PM2.5 from agricultural crop stubble burning.
    - (e) $\text{CO}_2$ emissions from national electrical grids.
    *Solutions:* Renewable solar/wind power; Electrostatic Precipitator; Wet limestone scrubber; Three-way catalytic converter; Agricultural bio-mulching and biogas digestion.

14. State whether each statement is True or False, providing a clear scientific reason:
    (a) A wet scrubber and an electrostatic precipitator perform the exact same industrial function.
    (b) Driving an electric vehicle guarantees that zero environmental pollution occurred to power your journey.
    (c) Domestic waste segregation reduces open trash burning and facilitates organic composting.
    (d) The chemical catalyst in a vehicle catalytic converter is consumed during driving and must be refilled weekly.

### Answers

1. Moles of $\text{CO}$: $\frac{5.6\text{ g}}{28\text{ g/mol}} = 0.2\text{ mol}$.
   From $2\text{CO} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{CO}_2$:
   - $\text{Moles of }\text{CO}_2 = 0.2\text{ mol} \rightarrow \text{Mass} = 0.2 \times 44 = \mathbf{8.8\text{ g of }\text{CO}_2}$.
   - $\text{Moles of }\text{O}_2 = 0.1\text{ mol} \rightarrow \text{Mass} = 0.1 \times 32 = \mathbf{3.2\text{ g of }\text{O}_2}$.
   *Verification:* $5.6 + 3.2 = 8.8\text{ g}$ ✅.

2. Molar mass of $\text{NO} = 14 + 16 = 30\text{ g/mol}$.
   $$\text{Moles of NO} = \frac{150\text{ g}}{30\text{ g/mol}} = 5.0\text{ mol}$$
   Reaction: $2\text{NO} + 2\text{CO} \rightarrow \text{N}_2 + 2\text{CO}_2$ ($1 : 1 : 0.5 : 1$ mole ratios):
   (a) Moles of $\text{N}_2 = 2.5\text{ mol} \rightarrow \text{Mass} = 2.5 \times 28 = \mathbf{70\text{ g of }\text{N}_2}$.
   (b) Moles of $\text{CO} = 5.0\text{ mol} \rightarrow \text{Mass} = 5.0 \times 28 = \mathbf{140\text{ g of CO}}$.
   (c) Moles of $\text{CO}_2 = 5.0\text{ mol} \rightarrow \text{Mass} = 5.0 \times 44 = \mathbf{220\text{ g of }\text{CO}_2}$.
   (d) Total reactant mass: $150\text{ g } (\text{NO}) + 140\text{ g } (\text{CO}) = \mathbf{290\text{ g}}$.
   Total product mass: $70\text{ g } (\text{N}_2) + 220\text{ g } (\text{CO}_2) = \mathbf{290\text{ g}}$ ✅.

3. Moles of $\text{NO}_2 = \frac{9.2\text{ g}}{46\text{ g/mol}} = 0.2\text{ mol}$.
   Reaction: $2\text{NO}_2 + 4\text{CO} \rightarrow \text{N}_2 + 4\text{CO}_2$ (ratio $2 : 4 : 1 : 4$):
   - Moles of $\text{CO}$ consumed: $0.4\text{ mol} \rightarrow 0.4 \times 28 = \mathbf{11.2\text{ g of CO}}$.
   - Moles of $\text{N}_2$ formed: $0.1\text{ mol} \rightarrow 0.1 \times 28 = \mathbf{2.8\text{ g of }\text{N}_2}$.
   - Moles of $\text{CO}_2$ formed: $0.4\text{ mol} \rightarrow 0.4 \times 44 = \mathbf{17.6\text{ g of }\text{CO}_2}$.
   *Verification:* Reactants: $9.2 + 11.2 = 20.4\text{ g}$; Products: $2.8 + 17.6 = 20.4\text{ g}$ ✅.

4. (a) Daily sulfur: $600\text{ tonnes} \times 0.025 = 15\text{ tonnes of S}$.
   $$\text{Mass of }\text{SO}_2 = 15\text{ tonnes} \times \frac{64}{32} = \mathbf{30\text{ tonnes of }\text{SO}_2}$$
   (b) Limestone required ($\text{CaCO}_3 = 100\text{ g/mol}$, $\text{SO}_2 = 64\text{ g/mol}$):
   $$\text{Mass of }\text{CaCO}_3 = 30\text{ tonnes} \times \frac{100}{64} = \mathbf{46.875\text{ tonnes of limestone}}\ (\approx 46.9\text{ tonnes})$$
   (c) Gypsum produced ($\text{CaSO}_4 = 136\text{ g/mol}$):
   $$\text{Mass of }\text{CaSO}_4 = 30\text{ tonnes} \times \frac{136}{64} = \mathbf{63.75\text{ tonnes of gypsum}}$$
   *Verification:* $30 \div 64 = 0.46875\text{ M-mol}$; $0.46875 \times 100 = 46.875$ ✅; $0.46875 \times 136 = 63.75$ ✅.

5. (a) For $500\text{ kg}$ of diesel:
   - Older diesel ($350\text{ ppm}$): $500\text{ kg} \times \frac{350}{1{,}000{,}000} = 0.175\text{ kg} = \mathbf{175\text{ g of sulfur}}$.
   - Ultra-low-sulfur diesel ($10\text{ ppm}$): $500\text{ kg} \times \frac{10}{1{,}000{,}000} = 0.005\text{ kg} = \mathbf{5\text{ g of sulfur}}$.
   (b) $\text{SO}_2$ produced ($\times 2$ mass factor):
   - Older diesel: $175\text{ g} \times 2 = \mathbf{350\text{ g of }\text{SO}_2}$.
   - Ultra-low-sulfur diesel: $5\text{ g} \times 2 = \mathbf{10\text{ g of }\text{SO}_2}$.
   (c) Percentage reduction:
   $$\frac{350 - 10}{350} \times 100 = \frac{340}{350} \times 100 \approx \mathbf{97.1\%}\ (\approx 97\%)$$

6. (a) Escaping ash ($0.5\%$):
   $$240\text{ kg/hr} \times (1 - 0.995) = 240 \times 0.005 = \mathbf{1.2\text{ kg/hr}}$$
   (b) Captured ash ($99.5\%$):
   $$240\text{ kg/hr} \times 0.995 = \mathbf{238.8\text{ kg/hr}}$$
   (c) Electrostatic physics: Opposite electrostatic charges attract (negatively ionized dust particles migrate across the stream to grounded positive collection plates).

7. (a) Per kilogram of fuel, methane releases $2.75\text{ kg}$, propane releases $3.0\text{ kg}$, and octane releases $3.09\text{ kg of }\text{CO}_2$.
   (b) No. A lower $\text{CO}_2$-per-kg rating does not alone define "clean" combustion. One must also consider emissions of particulate soot (PM2.5), sulfur dioxide ($\text{SO}_2$), carbon monoxide ($\text{CO}$), and nitrogen oxides ($\text{NO}_x$). For instance, while CNG and LPG produce comparable $\text{CO}_2$, they burn with virtually zero particulate soot compared to diesel, making them dramatically cleaner overall.

8. (a) Moles of $\text{H}_2 = \frac{10\text{ g}}{2\text{ g/mol}} = 5.0\text{ mol}$.
   From $2\text{H}_2 + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$:
   - Moles of $\text{O}_2 = 2.5\text{ mol} \rightarrow 2.5 \times 32 = \mathbf{80\text{ g of }\text{O}_2\text{ consumed}}$.
   - Moles of $\text{H}_2\text{O} = 5.0\text{ mol} \rightarrow 5.0 \times 18 = \mathbf{90\text{ g of water produced}}$.
   *Verification:* $10 + 80 = 90\text{ g}$ ✅.
   (b) It is "clean" at the point of use because its only exhaust product is pure water vapour — no $\text{CO}_2$, $\text{CO}$, $\text{SO}_2$, or soot. To be "green", the hydrogen must be produced through the electrolysis of water powered entirely by zero-emission renewable energy (solar, wind).

9. (a) Power reduction: $75\text{ W} - 15\text{ W} = 60\text{ W} = 0.06\text{ kW}$.
   $$\text{Total operating hours} = 4\text{ hr/day} \times 30\text{ days} = 120\text{ hours}$$
   $$\text{Energy saved} = 0.06\text{ kW} \times 120\text{ hours} = \mathbf{7.2\text{ kWh}}$$
   (b) Avoided emissions:
   $$7.2\text{ kWh} \times 0.8\text{ kg/kWh} = \mathbf{5.76\text{ kg of }\text{CO}_2}$$

10. Total round-trip distance = $20\text{ km}$.
    - Driving two separate cars: $2 \times \left(\frac{20\text{ km}}{16\text{ km/L}}\right) = 2 \times 1.25\text{ L} = 2.5\text{ L}$.
    - Carpooling in a single car: $1 \times 1.25\text{ L} = 1.25\text{ L}$.
    - Petrol saved: $2.5\text{ L} - 1.25\text{ L} = \mathbf{1.25\text{ litres of petrol}}$.
    - $\text{CO}_2$ saved: $1.25\text{ L} \times 2.3\text{ kg/L} = \mathbf{2.875\text{ kg of }\text{CO}_2}\ (\approx 2.9\text{ kg})$.

11. (i) Catalytic converters convert toxic $\text{CO}$ into $\text{CO}_2$; they do not reduce greenhouse gas emissions driving climate change. (ii) They do not eliminate fine brake-wear and tyre-wear dust (PM10), nor do they operate during the first few cold minutes before reaching light-off temperature ($300^\circ\text{C}$).
    Leaded petrol contains tetraethyl lead; lead atoms coat the porous active sites of the platinum/palladium catalyst, permanently "poisoning" and deactivating the unit.

12. (a) Fuel equivalent: $\frac{22\text{ kg of }\text{CO}_2}{2.3\text{ kg/L}} \approx \mathbf{9.57\text{ litres of petrol}}\ (\approx 9.6\text{ L})$.
    (b) Trees provide microclimate cooling shade, filter coarse airborne dust on leaf cuticles, stabilize watershed soil against erosion, and support avian biodiversity. They are not a total substitute for emissions cuts because an average passenger car burning $800\text{ L}$ of fuel annually produces $\approx 1{,}840\text{ kg of }\text{CO}_2$, which would require roughly $84$ mature trees to sequester. Source reduction is mandatory.

13. Matching solutions:
    - (a) $\text{SO}_2$ from power plant $\rightarrow$ **Wet limestone scrubber** (alkaline $\text{CaCO}_3$ reacts with acidic $\text{SO}_2$ to form gypsum).
    - (b) Fine dust in chimney smoke $\rightarrow$ **Electrostatic Precipitator** (high-voltage charge attracts dust particles to collector plates).
    - (c) $\text{CO}$ and $\text{NO}_x$ in car exhaust $\rightarrow$ **Three-way catalytic converter** (precious metals catalyze conversion into $\text{N}_2$ and $\text{CO}_2$).
    - (d) High winter PM2.5 from stubble burning $\rightarrow$ **Agricultural bio-mulching and biogas digestion** (converts agricultural residue into organic compost or bio-energy without open fire).
    - (e) $\text{CO}_2$ from electrical grids $\rightarrow$ **Renewable solar/wind power** (generates electricity directly without combustion reactions).

14. (a) **False** ❌. A scrubber captures gaseous chemical pollutants ($\text{SO}_2$) through acid-base chemical reactions; an ESP captures solid particulates (fly ash) using electrostatic charges.
    (b) **False** ❌. An EV eliminates direct tailpipe emissions, but if the local electrical grid generates charging electricity by burning coal, upstream emissions still occur.
    (c) **True** ✅. Separating organic waste from dry recyclables prevents mass dumping, stops uncontrolled landfill combustion, and enables composting.
    (d) **False** ❌. Catalysts accelerate chemical reactions without being consumed; a catalytic converter lasts for over $150{,}000\text{ km}$ provided it is not poisoned by leaded fuel.

## Chapter Summary: Atmospheric Pollution in Review

| Topic | Core Scientific Themes | Summary Takeaway |
|---|---|---|
| **Topic 1** | Clean Air Composition, Pollutants, and the AQI | Clean dry air consists of ~78% $\text{N}_2$, 21% $\text{O}_2$, 0.93% $\text{Ar}$, and ~0.04% $\text{CO}_2$. Pollutants are classified as primary or secondary. The Air Quality Index (0–500) quantifies health risk. Fine PM2.5 particles are ~28 times thinner than a human hair. |
| **Topic 2** | Acid Rain | Industrial $\text{SO}_2$ and vehicular $\text{NO}_x$ oxidize into sulfuric ($\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$) and nitric ($\text{HNO}_3$) acids, lowering rain pH below 5.6 and corroding marble monuments ($\text{CaCO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \rightarrow \text{CaSO}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2$), while acidifying aquatic ecosystems. |
| **Topic 3** | Greenhouse Effect and Global Warming | Natural greenhouse gases ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $\text{CO}_2$, $\text{CH}_4$) trap terrestrial infrared radiation, keeping Earth habitable (+15°C instead of -18°C). Anthropogenic emissions have surged $\text{CO}_2$ to ~420 ppm, driving an enhanced greenhouse effect and sea-level rise. Burning 1 L of petrol generates ~2.28 kg of $\text{CO}_2$. |
| **Topic 4** | The Ozone Layer and Its Depletion | Stratospheric ozone ($\text{O}_3$, 15–35 km) filters lethal solar UV-B radiation. Synthetic CFCs release chlorine radicals that catalytically destroy ~100,000 ozone molecules each. The universal 1987 Montreal Protocol successfully phased out CFCs, enabling ongoing planetary shield recovery. |
| **Topic 5** | Smog, Particulate Matter, and Health | Winter temperature inversions trap pollutants beneath a warm "thermal ceiling", creating sulfurous smog. Photochemical smog generates ground-level ozone. PM2.5 penetrates deep into lung alveoli. Carbon monoxide binds blood haemoglobin with 200× the affinity of oxygen. N95 respirators block $\ge 95\%$ of fine particles. |
| **Topic 6** | Clean Air Technologies and Retrospective | Catalytic converters ($\text{Pt/Pd/Rh}$), flue-gas limestone scrubbers, electrostatic precipitators (ESPs), ultra-low-sulfur fuels ($\le 10\text{ ppm}$), renewable electricity, and green hydrogen work alongside individual conservation to restore clean skies. |
