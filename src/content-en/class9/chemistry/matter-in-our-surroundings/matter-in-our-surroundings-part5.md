# Topic 5: Plasma, Bose–Einstein Condensate, and Chapter Summary

In Topic 4 we saw how fast-moving particles escape from a liquid's surface during evaporation and leave the remaining liquid cooler. Up to now, our entire story has focused on the **three everyday states of matter**: solid, liquid, and gas. Are those the only states that exist? In this final topic of the chapter, we will meet **two extraordinary additional states of matter** that appear at extreme temperatures, clear up common misconceptions (such as "What state of matter is fire?"), test our understanding across the whole chapter, and bring every topic together in a single master summary table.

## Neon Signs on the Street and Lightning in the Sky

One evening, Mr. Diaz and his daughter Sophia were walking home down a busy street as dusk settled. Outside a diner, a bright red-orange **neon sign** buzzed and glowed with the word "OPEN." Inside the diner window, overhead **fluorescent tubes** shone with crisp white light. Off in the distance, a summer storm was rolling in, and a jagged bolt of **lightning** flashed across the dark clouds.

Sophia looked from the glowing neon glass tube up to the sky. "Dad, what is actually glowing inside that neon sign? We learned that ordinary gases are invisible. Why does the gas inside that tube shine so brightly? And does it have anything in common with that bolt of lightning—or with the stars?"

Mr. Diaz grinned. "What a great connection! Inside that glass tube is neon gas, but when electricity runs through it, some of its particles become electrically charged and glow. And yes—that neon sign, the lightning bolt, and the Sun and stars are all examples of the **fourth state of matter**. Let's meet both the fourth and fifth states tonight."

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Plasma** | A superheated, electrically charged gas-like state of matter (**ionised gas**) | The Sun and stars, lightning bolts, neon signs |
| **Ionised** | Having particles that have gained or lost electrons so they carry an electric charge | Ionised gas inside a glowing plasma tube |
| **Charged particle** | A particle carrying a positive (+) or negative (−) electric charge | Positive ions and free negative electrons in plasma |
| **Bose–Einstein Condensate (BEC)** | A state of matter formed when an extremely low-density gas is cooled to within a tiny fraction of a kelvin above absolute zero | Created only in specialized physics laboratories |
| **Absolute zero** | The lowest possible temperature in nature: **0 K** (about **−273 °C**) | The starting point of the Kelvin temperature scale |
| **Density** | How much mass is packed into a given volume | A BEC is made from a gas at roughly 1/100,000 the density of normal air |

## Part A: Plasma: The Glowing, Electrically Charged Fourth State

In Topic 1 we learned that all matter is made of tiny **particles** (atoms or molecules). In an ordinary gas—like the air around you—those particles are **electrically neutral**: they carry no overall positive or negative charge.

Now imagine heating a gas to **extremely high temperatures**—or sending a strong **electric current** through it. The gas particles collide so violently that outer negatively charged pieces (**electrons**) get knocked right off the atoms! What remains is a swirling soup of **positively charged ions (+)** and **free negatively charged electrons (−)**. This super-energetic, electrically charged gas-like state is called **plasma** (or an **ionised gas**).

```
      ORDINARY GAS                      PLASMA

     o         o                  +    -        +
          o                    -       +    -
     o              o               +      -   +
         o      o                -      +      -
```

> **Figure 5.1** – In an ordinary gas, particles (o) are electrically neutral. In a **plasma**, particles are broken into fast-moving positively charged ions (+) and negatively charged electrons (−).

Key properties of plasma:

- Its particles have **even higher kinetic energy** than particles in an ordinary gas.
- Because it contains free positive and negative charges, plasma **conducts electricity** and **glows brightly** (emits light).
- Like a gas, plasma has no fixed shape or fixed volume unless confined.

### Where Do We Find Plasma?

- **The Sun and all stars:** The interiors and atmospheres of stars are so intensely hot that their hydrogen and helium exist as glowing plasma.
- **Lightning bolts:** When lightning strikes through the air, the electrical discharge superheats a narrow channel of air to nearly 30,000 °C for a split second, turning it into a brilliant ribbon of glowing plasma.
- **Neon signs:** A sealed glass tube is filled with neon gas (or another noble gas) at low pressure. When high-voltage electricity passes through the tube, the gas becomes partially ionised into plasma and glows (pure neon glows reddish-orange; other gases produce blue, green, or pink glows).
- **Fluorescent light tubes (tubelights):** Inside the tube is low-pressure gas with a tiny trace of mercury vapor. Electricity ionises the gas into plasma, which emits ultraviolet light that makes the white phosphor coating on the glass glow brightly.

**A mind-blowing fact:** Even though solids, liquids, and ordinary gases dominate our everyday life on Earth, **more than 99% of all visible matter in the universe is in the plasma state**, because stars and glowing interstellar clouds are made of plasma!

## Part B: Bose–Einstein Condensate (BEC): The Ultracold Fifth State

Now let us travel to the exact opposite extreme of the temperature scale: from the blistering heat of stars down to the deepest imaginable **cold**.

In Topic 3 we learned that the lowest possible temperature in nature is **0 K (about −273.15 °C)**, called **absolute zero**. What happens if you take a gas of **extremely low density** (about one hundred-thousandth the density of ordinary air) and cool it down to within **a few billionths of a kelvin above 0 K**?

At such unimaginably cold temperatures, the particles lose almost all their kinetic energy. They practically stop zipping around as separate individuals and instead **coalesce into a single quantum state**, behaving in unison like one giant, coordinated "super-particle." This fifth state of matter is called a **Bose–Einstein Condensate (BEC)**.

```
   Ordinary gas                  BEC (just above 0 K, absolute zero)

    o      o                      (  o o o  )
        o        o                (  o o o  )
     o      o                     (  o o o  )
   separate, fast-moving        barely moving, acting together as one
```

> **Figure 5.2** – In an ordinary gas, particles move rapidly and independently. In a **Bose–Einstein Condensate (BEC)** just above 0 K, ultracold particles overlap and act in unison like a single "super-particle."

Three fascinating facts about BEC:

1. **Why is it named Bose–Einstein?** In **1924**, the brilliant Indian physicist **Satyendra Nath Bose** worked out new statistical calculations for subatomic particles and sent his paper to **Albert Einstein**. Recognizing its importance, Einstein extended Bose's mathematics to atoms and predicted that at ultracold temperatures, matter would condense into this brand-new fifth state.
2. **Created in a lab 70 years later:** Because reaching temperatures a fraction of a millionth of a kelvin above absolute zero is extraordinarily difficult, no one could actually make a BEC for seven decades! Finally, in **1995**, physicists **Eric Cornell, Carl Wieman**, and **Wolfgang Ketterle** succeeded in creating the first gaseous Bose–Einstein Condensate in a laboratory (earning the 2001 Nobel Prize in Physics).
3. **Only in specialized labs:** Unlike plasma, which lights up the sky and our street signs, a BEC does not occur naturally in our everyday environment—it exists only inside advanced cryogenic physics laboratories.

## Part C: Comparing All Five States of Matter

Let us line up all five states of matter in a single table. Keep in mind that **particle energy** refers to the average kinetic energy (motion) of the particles:

| State of Matter | Particle Energy | Particle Arrangement | Everyday / Scientific Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **BEC** | **Lowest** (almost zero; just above 0 K) | Extremely low density; particles act in unison as one wave/super-particle | Created only in ultracold physics labs |
| **Solid** | Low (vibrate in fixed positions) | Tightly packed in a regular, rigid pattern | Ice, wood, iron, rock |
| **Liquid** | Moderate (slide past one another) | Closely packed, random arrangement | Water, milk, cooking oil, mercury |
| **Gas** | High (fly rapidly in all directions) | Very far apart, random, neutral particles | Air, steam, helium, oxygen |
| **Plasma** | **Highest** (extremely fast, ionised) | Far apart, mixture of positive ions (+) and free electrons (−) | The Sun and stars, lightning, neon signs |

Ranked in order of **increasing particle energy** (and temperature):

> **BEC < Solid < Liquid < Gas < Plasma**

## Part D: Clearing Up Three Classic Questions

### 1. "Is fire a state of matter?"

No, **fire (a flame) is not a separate state of matter**. When wood, candle wax, or cooking gas burns, a **chemical reaction** (combustion) takes place with oxygen in the air, releasing heat and light. The visible flame you see is primarily a **rising stream of very hot, glowing gases** mixed with tiny incandescent soot particles—and in the hottest part of a flame, a tiny fraction of the gas particles become ionised (**partial plasma**). So fire is a *process* (a mixture of hot glowing gases and a trace of plasma), not its own distinct state of matter.

### 2. "Can every substance exist as a solid, a liquid, and a gas?"

Many pure substances—like water, iron, oxygen, and gold—can exist in all three states if you heat or cool them enough. However, **not every substance can!** If you heat a piece of **wood** or **paper**, it never melts into "liquid wood" or "liquid paper"—instead, before its particles can slide apart into a liquid, the heat causes its complex molecules to **break down and burn (a chemical change)** into ash, smoke, and gases. Even table sugar tends to caramelize and char if heated too strongly. A true change of state happens only when the **same substance** changes its particle arrangement without decomposing chemically.

### 3. "Can we see plasma and BEC at home?"

You can see **plasma** in action whenever you look at a fluorescent light tube, a neon sign, a plasma globe toy, a lightning storm, or the Sun (safely!). You **cannot** see a **BEC** at home—it requires laser-cooling and magnetic-trapping equipment inside a specialized research laboratory.

**Q4 (Sublimation – Topic 3):** Mothballs (naphthalene) placed in a storage closet gradually shrink and disappear over several weeks without leaving any wet spots on the clothes. Name this change of state and explain why no liquid appears.

**Q5 (Evaporative Cooling – Topic 4):** Why do you feel much cooler sitting under a spinning ceiling fan when your skin is sweaty after a workout?

**Q6 (Particle Energy – Topic 5):** Arrange **solid, gas, plasma, and BEC** in order of increasing particle energy.

**Q7 (Plasma & BEC – Topic 5):** Which state of matter glows inside a neon sign, and near what temperature must a low-density gas be cooled to form a BEC?

### Answers

**A1.** In the **warm room (30 °C)**. At higher temperatures, particles have more kinetic energy and move faster, so the fragrance particles diffuse through the air more rapidly. ✅

**A2.** The **air-filled syringe** compresses easily. Gas particles have huge empty spaces between them that shrink when pushed together, whereas liquid water particles are already closely packed with almost no empty space left. ✅

**A3.** Using **K = °C + 273**:
(a) 27 + 273 = **300 K**
(b) 250 − 273 = **−23 °C**
Check: 300 − 273 = 27 °C ✅ and −23 + 273 = 250 K ✅

**A4.** The change is **sublimation**: the solid naphthalene turns directly into a gas without passing through the liquid state, which is why the clothes stay completely dry. ✅

**A5.** The moving air from the fan sweeps humid air away from your skin and **speeds up the evaporation of your sweat**. As the fastest water particles evaporate, they absorb latent heat directly from your skin, cooling you down. ✅

**A6.** **BEC < Solid < Gas < Plasma** (and if liquid is included: **BEC < Solid < Liquid < Gas < Plasma**). ✅

**A7.** **Plasma** (ionised gas) glows inside a neon sign. A BEC forms when an ultralow-density gas is cooled to **just above absolute zero (0 K, or −273 °C)**. ✅

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

**Mistake 1: Confusing "plasma" in physics/chemistry with "blood plasma" in biology.**

Same word, completely different meanings! In biology, *blood plasma* is the pale-yellow liquid part of blood. In chemistry and physics, **plasma** is the fourth state of matter—a superheated, electrically charged (ionised) gas.

**Mistake 2: "Fire is the fourth state of matter."**

No. **Plasma** is the fourth state of matter. Fire (a flame) is a chemical combustion reaction consisting mostly of hot, glowing gases (with at most a tiny trace of ionised plasma in the hottest flames).

**Mistake 3: Mixing up which extreme state is hot and which is cold.**

Remember: **Plasma = super-hot** (stars, lightning). **BEC = super-cold** (just above 0 K, absolute zero).

**Mistake 4: "Ordinary gas and plasma are the exact same thing."**

Both expand to fill space, but ordinary gas consists of **neutral** particles that do not conduct electricity well, whereas **plasma** consists of **charged particles** (positive ions and free electrons) that conduct electricity and glow.

**Mistake 5: "Every solid melts into a liquid if you heat it enough."**

Many complex solids—like wood or paper—chemically decompose or burn before they can melt.

## Quick Recap

- **Plasma** is the fourth state of matter: a high-energy, **ionised gas** containing positively charged ions and free negatively charged electrons. It conducts electricity and glows.
- Where plasma is found: the **Sun and stars** (over 99% of visible matter in the universe!), **lightning bolts**, **neon signs**, and **fluorescent tubes**.
- **Bose–Einstein Condensate (BEC)** is the fifth state of matter, formed by cooling an extremely low-density gas to within a tiny fraction of a kelvin above **absolute zero (0 K, or −273 °C)** so its particles act together like a single super-particle.
- BEC was predicted in 1924 by **Satyendra Nath Bose** and **Albert Einstein**, and first created in a laboratory in **1995**.
- Order of particle energy across all five states: **BEC < Solid < Liquid < Gas < Plasma**.
- **Fire** is not a state of matter (it is a mixture of hot glowing gases from combustion), and substances like wood burn chemically rather than melting.
