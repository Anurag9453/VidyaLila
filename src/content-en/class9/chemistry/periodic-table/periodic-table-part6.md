# Topic 6: Predicting from the Table: Practice and Chapter Review

In our previous topics, we saw how elements in the same vertical group form close-knit chemical families sharing identical valence counts and matching chemical behaviours. Now comes the most satisfying and practical skill in chemistry: **reading the periodic table as a predictive roadmap**.

Given only an element's **period and group coordinates**, you can instantly deduce its electron configuration, calculate its valency, classify it as a metal or non-metal, determine what kind of bond it forms, and predict the exact chemical formulas of its compounds! This is the predictive power that Dmitri Mendeleev unlocked, and today we will master it through an engaging mystery game, followed by a complete synthesis of the entire chapter.

## A Rainy Afternoon and "Guess Who?"

Outside the window, a heavy autumn rain pounded against the glass. Maya and Leo sat by the fireplace, looking for an entertaining indoor activity. Leo pulled an old boxed deduction game from the game shelf: **"Guess Who?"**

"In this game," Leo explained, "each player secretly chooses a character card from a roster of diverse faces. We take turns asking targeted yes-or-no questions: 'Does your character wear glasses?' Maya smiled as they played a few rounds. Suddenly, she paused and looked across the room at the periodic table hanging on the wall. "Uncle Leo, this game is identical to how the periodic table works! Leo nodded enthusiastically. "You have hit on the exact beauty of chemistry! Let us turn this into our own scientific puzzle: the **Clue Card Game**. On each card, we will write two or three distinct physical or chemical clues. Your mission is to deduce the exact element. Maya grabbed her notebook. "Let's begin! First, let me assemble my deductive toolkit."

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Coordinates (Position)** | An element's precise address defined by its horizontal period (row) and vertical group (column) | Sodium ($\text{Na}$): Period 3, Group 1 |
| **Prediction** | Deducing an element's physical and chemical properties solely from its position on the table | Group 17 element $\rightarrow$ valency 1 halogen |
| **Clue card** | A puzzle card listing 2–3 properties used to identify a single unique chemical element | "Period 3 element with configuration $2, 8, 1$" |

| **Periodic pattern** | The regular, systematic recurrence of elemental properties across the table grid | Identical valencies down each group |

## Part A: Reading Everything from Coordinates (Position)

We can synthesize all the principles of this chapter into a single, foolproof **6-step master recipe**. For any main-group element among the first 20 elements:

1. **Write the electron configuration:** Distribute electrons across shells ($K, L, M, N$) following Bohr-Bury capacities ($2, 8, 8, 2$).
2. **Find the Period (Row):** $\mathbf{\text{Period Number}} = \text{Total number of occupied shells}$.
3. **Find the Group (Column):**
   - For 1 or 2 valence electrons $\rightarrow$ **Group 1 or Group 2**.
   - For 3 to 8 valence electrons $\rightarrow \mathbf{\text{Group}} = \text{Valence electrons} + 10$ (Groups 13 to 18).
   - *Helium exception:* 2 electrons in its complete $K$-shell $\rightarrow$ **Group 18**.
4. **Determine the Valency:**
   - For 1 to 4 valence electrons: $\text{Valency} = \text{Valence electrons}$.
   - For 5 to 7 valence electrons: $\text{Valency} = 8 - \text{Valence electrons}$.
   - For noble gases (8 valence electrons, or 2 for He): $\text{Valency} = \mathbf{0}$.
5. **Classify the Elemental Type:**
   - Left side of the table: **Metals** (Group 1, Group 2, Aluminium in Group 13).
   - Right side of the table: **Non-metals** (Groups 14 to 18, plus Hydrogen).
   - Diagonal staircase boundary: **Metalloids** (**Boron [$\text{B}$]** and **Silicon [$\text{Si}$]**).
6. **Predict the Chemical Bond Type:**
   - $\text{Metal} + \text{Non-metal} \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{Ionic Bond}}$ (electron transfer).
   - $\text{Non-metal} + \text{Non-metal} \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{Covalent Bond}}$ (electron sharing).

Let us test this master recipe on two contrasting elements:

### Case 1: Sulphur ($\text{S}$, Atomic Number $Z = 16$)
- Configuration: $K = 2, L = 8, M = 6 \rightarrow \mathbf{2, 8, 6}$.
- Shell count: 3 occupied shells $\rightarrow$ **Period 3**.
- Group: 6 valence electrons $\rightarrow 6 + 10 = \mathbf{\text{Group 16}}$.
- Valency: $8 - 6 = \mathbf{2}$.
- Location: Right-hand side of staircase $\rightarrow$ **Non-metal**.
- Compound with Hydrogen: Non-metal + Non-metal $\rightarrow$ **Covalent bond**. Applying criss-cross ($\text{H}$ valency 1, $\text{S}$ valency 2) yields **$\text{H}_2\text{S}$ (Hydrogen Sulfide)**.

### Case 2: Calcium ($\text{Ca}$, Atomic Number $Z = 20$)
- Configuration: $K = 2, L = 8, M = 8, N = 2 \rightarrow \mathbf{2, 8, 8, 2}$.
- Shell count: 4 occupied shells $\rightarrow$ **Period 4**.
- Group: 2 valence electrons $\rightarrow$ **Group 2**.
- Valency: $\mathbf{2}$.
- Location: Left-hand side $\rightarrow$ **Alkaline earth metal**.
- Compound with Chlorine: Metal + Non-metal $\rightarrow$ **Ionic bond**. Applying criss-cross ($\text{Ca}^{2+}$ and $\text{Cl}^-$) yields **$\text{CaCl}_2$ (Calcium Chloride)**.

```
   Group:    1    2      13   14   15   16   17   18
            +---------------------------------------+
   P1        H                                  He
   P2        Li   Be     B    C    N    O    F    Ne
   P3        Na   Mg     Al   Si   P    S    Cl   Ar
   P4        K    Ca
            +---------------------------------------+
   Valency:  1    2      3    4    3    2    1    0
   Type:     METALS (Left) ... METALLOIDS ... NON-METALS (Right)
             (Boron and Silicon on the staircase; Al is metal; H is non-metal)

   Reading Coordinates:
     Row (Period)    = Number of occupied shells
     Column (Group)  = Governed by valence electrons
     Valency Cycle   = 1, 2, 3, 4, 3, 2, 1, 0
```

> **Figure 6.1** – The complete predictive master grid for the first 20 elements. Period coordinates indicate energy shells, while group coordinates dictate valence electrons, valency, elemental classification, and bonding behaviour.

Remember: **you never need to memorize 118 random valency facts**. Once you know an element's position on the periodic grid, its entire chemical profile unfolds automatically.

## Part B: The Clue Card Game

You can play this tabletop deduction game at home using small index cards, paper, and a pencil: **completely safe, educational, and chemical-free!**

```
   +---------------------------------------+
   |   CLUE CARD  No. 1                    |
   |                                       |
   |   Clue 1: I reside in Period 3.       |
   |   Clue 2: I reside in Group 17.       |
   |                                       |
   |   Who am I?                           |
   +---------------------------------------+
                      |
                      v
   Period 3 Candidates:   Na  Mg  Al  Si  P  S  Cl  Ar
   Group 17 Candidates:   F   Cl  Br  I
   Intersection Match:    Chlorine (Cl)  [The ONLY element fitting both!]
```

> **Figure 6.2** – Mechanism of a Clue Card deduction. Clue 1 isolates the horizontal row (Period 3); Clue 2 isolates the vertical column (Group 17). The intersection pinpoints a single, unambiguous element: Chlorine ($\text{Cl}$).

Here are 15 mystery puzzles solved step by step. Each puzzle provides clues that eliminate all distractors to leave **exactly one correct answer**:

- **Puzzle 1:** *"I am in Period 3 and Group 17."*
  - Period 3 candidates: $\text{Na}$ to $\text{Ar}$. Group 17 candidates: $\text{F}, \text{Cl}$. 
  - Intersection: **Chlorine ($\text{Cl}$)**, a halogen non-metal.

- **Puzzle 2:** *"My electron configuration is $2, 8, 1$."*
  - 3 shells (Period 3) + 1 valence electron (Group 1): **Sodium ($\text{Na}$)**, an alkali metal.

- **Puzzle 3:** *"I am an alkaline earth metal with exactly 3 electron shells."*
  - Alkaline earth metals (Group 2): $\text{Be}$ (2 shells), $\text{Mg}$ (3 shells), $\text{Ca}$ (4 shells). 
  - Exactly 3 shells isolates: **Magnesium ($\text{Mg}$)**.

- **Puzzle 4:** *"I am a noble gas with exactly 2 electron shells."*
  - Noble gases (Group 18): $\text{He}$ (1 shell), $\text{Ne}$ (2 shells), $\text{Ar}$ (3 shells). 
  - Exactly 2 shells isolates: **Neon ($\text{Ne}$)**, famous for reddish-orange advertising signs.

- **Puzzle 5:** *"I am a metalloid with a valency of 4 residing in Period 3."*
  - The metalloids among the first 20 elements are Boron ($\text{B}$, Period 2, valency 3) and Silicon ($\text{Si}$, Period 3, valency 4). 
  - Solution: **Silicon ($\text{Si}$)**.

- **Puzzle 6:** *"I am a non-metal with a valency of 2 and possess 2 electron shells."*
  - Period 2 non-metals: $\text{C}$ (valency 4), $\text{N}$ (3), $\text{O}$ (2), $\text{F}$ (1), $\text{Ne}$ (0). 
  - Valency 2 isolates: **Oxygen ($\text{O}$)**.

- **Puzzle 7:** *"I am an alkali metal with 4 electron shells."*
  - Alkali metals (Group 1): $\text{Li}$ (2 shells), $\text{Na}$ (3 shells), $\text{K}$ (4 shells). 
  - Solution: **Potassium ($\text{K}$)**.

- **Puzzle 8:** *"I am a metal belonging to Group 13."*
  - Group 13 contains Boron (a metalloid) and Aluminium (a metal). 
  - Solution: **Aluminium ($\text{Al}$)**.

- **Puzzle 9:** *"I have only 1 electron shell, 1 valence electron, and I am a non-metal."*
  - Period 1 has only $\text{H}$ and $\text{He}$. Helium has 2 electrons; Hydrogen has 1. 
  - Solution: **Hydrogen ($\text{H}$)**.

- **Puzzle 10:** *"I am a non-metal in Period 3 with a chemical valency of 3."*
  - In Period 3, elements with valency 3 are Aluminium (a metal) and Phosphorus (a non-metal: $8 - 5 = 3$). 
  - Non-metal isolates: **Phosphorus ($\text{P}$)**.

- **Puzzle 11:** *"I am an element in Period 4 with a chemical valency of 2."*
  - Period 4 elements among the first 20: Potassium (Group 1, valency 1) and Calcium (Group 2, valency 2). 
  - Solution: **Calcium ($\text{Ca}$)**.

- **Puzzle 12:** *"I am a non-metal with 2 shells and a valency of 3."*
  - Period 2 non-metals: $\text{N}$ has configuration $2, 5$, giving valency $8 - 5 = 3$. 
  - Solution: **Nitrogen ($\text{N}$)**.

- **Puzzle 13:** *"I reside in Period 2 and form four covalent bonds with hydrogen."*
  - Period 2 element with valency 4 is Carbon ($Z = 6$, configuration $2, 4$). 
  - Solution: **Carbon ($\text{C}$)** (forming $\text{CH}_4$).

- **Puzzle 14:** *"I am an unreactive noble gas with 3 electron shells."*
  - 3 shells in Group 18 indicates configuration $2, 8, 8$ ($Z = 18$). 
  - Solution: **Argon ($\text{Ar}$)**, used inside light bulbs.

- **Puzzle 15 (Formula Clue):** *"I form an ionic chloride of the formula $\text{XCl}_2$ and reside in Period 4."*
  - Formula $\text{XCl}_2$ requires $\text{X}$ to have a valency of 2 ($\text{X}^{2+}$). In Period 4, the divalent metal is Calcium. 
  - Solution: **Calcium ($\text{Ca}$)**, forming $\text{CaCl}_2$ ✅.

## Part C: Deducing Formulas and Bond Types from Position

When two elements combine, their table positions immediately reveal:
1. **Bond Type:** Metal + Non-metal forms an **ionic bond**; Non-metal + Non-metal forms a **covalent bond**.
2. **Formula Subscripts:** Determined by cross-multiplying their periodic valencies and simplifying to the lowest whole-number ratio.

| Combining Pair | Elemental Valencies | Resulting Formula | Bond Type | Underlying Reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **$\text{Na}$ and $\text{Cl}$** | $\text{Na} = 1, \text{Cl} = 1$ | **$\text{NaCl}$** | Ionic | Metal ($\text{Na}$) + Non-metal ($\text{Cl}$) |
| **$\text{Mg}$ and $\text{Cl}$** | $\text{Mg} = 2, \text{Cl} = 1$ | **$\text{MgCl}_2$** | Ionic | Metal ($\text{Mg}$) + Non-metal ($\text{Cl}$) |
| **$\text{Ca}$ and $\text{Cl}$** | $\text{Ca} = 2, \text{Cl} = 1$ | **$\text{CaCl}_2$** | Ionic | Metal ($\text{Ca}$) + Non-metal ($\text{Cl}$) |
| **$\text{Li}$ and $\text{F}$** | $\text{Li} = 1, \text{F} = 1$ | **$\text{LiF}$** | Ionic | Metal ($\text{Li}$) + Non-metal ($\text{F}$) |
| **$\text{Mg}$ and $\text{O}$** | $\text{Mg} = 2, \text{O} = 2$ | **$\text{MgO}$** | Ionic | Metal ($\text{Mg}$) + Non-metal ($\text{O}$) [Ratio $2:2 \rightarrow 1:1$] |
| **$\text{Al}$ and $\text{O}$** | $\text{Al} = 3, \text{O} = 2$ | **$\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$** | Ionic | Metal ($\text{Al}$) + Non-metal ($\text{O}$) |
| **$\text{K}$ and $\text{O}$** | $\text{K} = 1, \text{O} = 2$ | **$\text{K}_2\text{O}$** | Ionic | Metal ($\text{K}$) + Non-metal ($\text{O}$) |
| **$\text{H}$ and $\text{Cl}$** | $\text{H} = 1, \text{Cl} = 1$ | **$\text{HCl}$** | Covalent | Non-metal ($\text{H}$) + Non-metal ($\text{Cl}$) |
| **$\text{H}$ and $\text{O}$** | $\text{H} = 1, \text{O} = 2$ | **$\text{H}_2\text{O}$** | Covalent | Non-metal ($\text{H}$) + Non-metal ($\text{O}$) |
| **$\text{N}$ and $\text{H}$** | $\text{N} = 3, \text{H} = 1$ | **$\text{NH}_3$** | Covalent | Non-metal ($\text{N}$) + Non-metal ($\text{H}$) |
| **$\text{C}$ and $\text{H}$** | $\text{C} = 4, \text{H} = 1$ | **$\text{CH}_4$** | Covalent | Non-metal ($\text{C}$) + Non-metal ($\text{H}$) |
| **$\text{H}$ and $\text{S}$** | $\text{H} = 1, \text{S} = 2$ | **$\text{H}_2\text{S}$** | Covalent | Non-metal ($\text{H}$) + Non-metal ($\text{S}$) |
| **$\text{C}$ and $\text{Cl}$** | $\text{C} = 4, \text{Cl} = 1$ | **$\text{CCl}_4$** | Covalent | Non-metal ($\text{C}$) + Non-metal ($\text{Cl}$) |

Notice the remarkable family harmony:
- Magnesium chloride is $\text{MgCl}_2$, and Calcium chloride is $\text{CaCl}_2$ (both Group 2 metals require two halogens).
- Sodium chloride is $\text{NaCl}$, and Potassium chloride is $\text{KCl}$ (both Group 1 metals require one halogen).
- Water is $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, and Hydrogen sulfide is $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ (both Group 16 non-metals require two hydrogens).

### Formula Mass Calculations
Using standard atomic masses ($\text{Mg} = 24, \text{Cl} = 35.5, \text{Ca} = 40, \text{Al} = 27, \text{O} = 16, \text{C} = 12, \text{H} = 1, \text{K} = 39$):
- **$\text{MgCl}_2$:** $24 + 2(35.5) = 24 + 71 = \mathbf{95\text{ u}}$
- **$\text{CaCl}_2$:** $40 + 2(35.5) = 40 + 71 = \mathbf{111\text{ u}}$
- **$\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$:** $2(27) + 3(16) = 54 + 48 = \mathbf{102\text{ u}}$

### Electronic Mechanism of Ionic Bond Formation in $\text{MgCl}_2$
Magnesium ($2, 8, 2$) possesses 2 valence electrons in its outer $M$-shell. To attain the stable electron configuration of Neon ($2, 8$), it donates both electrons:
$$\text{Mg} \longrightarrow \text{Mg}^{2+} + 2e^-$$
Each of two Chlorine atoms ($2, 8, 7$) requires 1 electron to complete its outer octet ($2, 8, 8$, resembling Argon):
$$2\text{Cl} + 2e^- \longrightarrow 2\text{Cl}^-$$
The resulting $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ cation and two $\text{Cl}^-$ anions are held firmly together by omnidirectional electrostatic attraction into a crystalline lattice of **$\text{MgCl}_2$** ✅.

- With Hydrogen (valency 1): Forms covalent **$\text{H}_2\text{S}$ (Hydrogen Sulfide)**.

### Example 3: Reaction Between Lithium and Fluorine
Predict the compound formed between Lithium and Fluorine.
- Lithium ($Z = 3$, $2, 1$): Period 2, Group 1 metal (valency 1).
- Fluorine ($Z = 9$, $2, 7$): Period 2, Group 17 non-metal (valency 1).
- Electron transfer forms an ionic bond: **$\text{LiF}$ (Lithium Fluoride)**.
- Formula mass: $7 + 19 = \mathbf{26\text{ u}}$ ✅.

### Example 4: Compound of Aluminium and Oxygen
Explain the formula of the oxide formed by Aluminium.
- Aluminium ($Z = 13$, $2, 8, 3$) has valency 3. Oxygen ($Z = 8$, $2, 6$) has valency 2.
- Applying criss-cross: $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$.
- Two $\text{Al}^{3+}$ ions surrender 6 electrons, which are accepted by three $\text{O}^{2-}$ ions.
- The bond is **ionic**, and the formula is **$\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$** ✅.

### Example 5: Finding the Odd One Out
Identify the odd one out among $\text{Ne}, \text{Ar}, \text{Na}$, and $\text{He}$. Justify your choice.
- $\text{Ne}, \text{Ar}$, and $\text{He}$ are chemically inert noble gases belonging to Group 18 with valency 0.
- $\text{Na}$ is a fiercely reactive alkali metal belonging to Group 1 with valency 1.
- The odd one out is **Sodium ($\text{Na}$)** ✅.

### Example 6: Comparing Reactivity: $\text{Na}$ vs. $\text{K}$
Which element is more reactive: Sodium or Potassium?
- Both belong to Group 1. Potassium has 4 shells while Sodium has 3.
- Potassium's valence electron is farther from the nucleus and shielded by more inner shells, making it easier to lose.
- Therefore, **Potassium is more reactive than Sodium ($\text{K} > \text{Na}$)** ✅.

### Example 7: Halogen Reactivity: $\text{F}$ vs. $\text{Cl}$
Which halogen is more reactive: Fluorine or Chlorine?
- In Group 17, reactivity depends on attracting an incoming electron.
- Fluorine has only 2 shells; its nucleus is closer to the valence shell, pulling incoming electrons with much greater intensity.
- Therefore, **Fluorine is more reactive than Chlorine ($\text{F} > \text{Cl}$)** ✅.

### Example 8: Predicting Properties of Undiscovered Element Rubidium ($\text{Rb}$)
Rubidium sits directly below Potassium in Group 1 with 5 electron shells. Predict its properties without prior study.
- Valency: **1**.
- Physical nature: Soft, silvery metal with very low density.
- Reactivity: Reacts even more violently with water than Potassium.
- Chloride formula: **$\text{RbCl}$**.
- This is the exact predictive methodology used by Mendeleev ✅.

### Example 9: Deducing Identity of Element $X$
An unknown element $X$ has a valency of 3, 3 occupied shells, and is a metal.
- 3 occupied shells $\rightarrow$ Period 3. Valency 3 metal $\rightarrow$ Group 13.
- The element is **Aluminium ($\text{Al}$)**. Its oxide is $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ ✅.

### Example 10: Nature of Bonding in Water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$)
What type of chemical bonding exists in water?
- Both Hydrogen and Oxygen are non-metals.
- Non-metals share electrons rather than transferring them, forming **covalent bonds**.
- Two Hydrogen atoms share electrons with one Oxygen atom: $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ ✅.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1. **Mistake 1: Misidentifying Period 3, Group 1 as Lithium instead of Sodium.**
   Lithium sits in Period 2 (2 shells). Period 3, Group 1 is **Sodium ($\text{Na}$)** (3 shells). Always count shell rows carefully.

2. **Mistake 2: Stating Group 17 elements have a valency of 7.**
   Group 17 elements have 7 valence electrons, but their combining capacity (valency) is $8 - 7 = \mathbf{1}$.

3. **Mistake 3: Assuming valency always equals valence electrons.**
   Valency equals valence electrons only up to 4 (Groups 1, 2, 13, 14). For Groups 15, 16, and 17, valency equals $8 - \text{valence electrons}$ (3, 2, and 1 respectively).

4. **Mistake 4: Believing a single clue is always sufficient to isolate an element.**
   A single clue like "I am an alkali metal" leaves $\text{Li}, \text{Na}$, and $\text{K}$ as possibilities. You must cross-reference a second coordinate (such as shell count or period) to isolate a single element.

5. **Mistake 5: Classifying metal-nonmetal bonds as covalent.**
   Metals transfer electrons to non-metals, forming **ionic bonds**. Covalent bonds form exclusively between non-metals through electron sharing.

6. **Mistake 6: Classifying Hydrogen as an alkali metal.**
   Hydrogen sits at the top of Group 1 for valence electron consistency, but it is a diatomic non-metal gas.

## Quick Recap

- **Reading coordinates:**
  - $\text{Period number} = \text{number of occupied electron shells}$.
  - $\text{Group number} = \text{dictated by valence electrons}$ (Groups 1, 2, and 13–18).
  - Main-group valency progression: $1, 2, 3, 4, 3, 2, 1, 0$.
- **Geography of the table:** Metals dominate the left; non-metals dominate the right; Boron and Silicon are metalloids lining the staircase.
- **Predicting bonds:**
  - Metal + Non-metal $\rightarrow$ **Ionic bond** (transfer of electrons).
  - Non-metal + Non-metal $\rightarrow$ **Covalent bond** (sharing of electrons).
- **Formulas:** Determined via the criss-cross valency method ($\text{MgCl}_2, \text{Al}_2\text{O}_3, \text{CH}_4$). Elements within the same group produce identical compound formulas ($\text{NaCl}$ and $\text{KCl}$; $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ and $\text{H}_2\text{S}$).
- **The Clue Card Method:** Cross-referencing two independent structural coordinates uniquely isolates any element on the periodic table.

10. Calcium ($2, 8, 8, 2$) surrenders its 2 valence electrons to achieve an Argon configuration ($2, 8, 8$), forming $\text{Ca}^{2+}$. Two Chlorine atoms ($2, 8, 7$) each accept 1 electron to complete their octet ($2, 8, 8$), forming two $\text{Cl}^-$ anions. Strong electrostatic attraction binds them into ionic **$\text{CaCl}_2$**.

11. Strontium belongs to Group 2, so its **valency is 2**. Because it sits lower in Group 2 with 5 shells, its valence electrons are farther from the nucleus and lost more easily, making **Strontium more reactive than Calcium**. Its chloride salt has the formula **$\text{SrCl}_2$** (identical to $\text{CaCl}_2$).

12. **Calcium is more reactive than Magnesium**, and Calcium possesses **4 shells** (Magnesium has 3). Moving down the group adds a shell, placing outer electrons farther from the nucleus and weakening the nuclear hold, allowing them to be lost with less energy.

    (b) **True** ✅. Nitrogen and Hydrogen are both non-metals that share electron pairs.
    (c) **False** ❌. Sodium (metal) and Chlorine (non-metal) form an **ionic** compound ($\text{NaCl}$) via electron transfer.

## Chapter Summary: The Periodic Table

Let us take a bird's-eye view of our entire journey across this foundational chapter:

| Topic | Core Subject Explored | Key Conceptual Breakthrough |
|---|---|---|
| **Topic 1** | Early Classification: Dobereiner and Newlands | Dobereiner's triads ($\text{Li}, \text{Na}, \text{K}$) and Newlands' Law of Octaves revealed the first signs of order and periodicity, but failed beyond light elements. A great classification must possess **predictive capability**. |
| **Topic 2** | Mendeleev's Periodic Table | Mendeleev organized elements by atomic mass into periods and groups, boldly left blank spaces, and accurately predicted the properties of Gallium, Scandium, and Germanium. Limitations: Hydrogen, isotopes, and inverted pairs ($\text{Ar}-\text{K}$). |
| **Topic 3** | The Modern Periodic Table | Henry Moseley proved that **atomic number ($Z$)** is the fundamental organizing parameter, eliminating all inverted pairs. The modern table comprises **18 vertical groups** and **7 horizontal periods**, where periods match electron shells and groups match valence electrons. |
| **Topic 4** | Periodic Trends | Across a period: shells remain constant, nuclear charge rises, valency cycles $1 \rightarrow 4 \rightarrow 0$, atomic size decreases, and metallic character decreases. Down a group: shells increase, atomic size increases, metallic character increases, and valency remains constant. |
| **Topic 5** | Families of Elements | Vertical groups form chemical families: Group 1 (Alkali Metals), Group 2 (Alkaline Earth Metals), Group 17 (Halogens), and Group 18 (Noble Gases). Shared valence electrons govern matching chemical personalities and reactivity trends. |
| **Topic 6** | Predicting from Coordinates | An element's position on the periodic grid allows instantaneous deduction of its electron configuration, valency, metallic nature, bonding mechanism, and compound formulas. |
