# Topic 2: Some Angle Relations: Adjacent Angles and Linear Pair

In Topic 1, we studied individual angles: their arms and vertex, how to measure them in degrees, and whether they are acute, right, obtuse, straight, reflex, or complete. Using Maya's wall clock, we saw $90^\circ$ at $3{:}00$ and $180^\circ$ at $6{:}00$. Now we take the next step and examine **two or more angles together**: when their sum is $90^\circ$, when their sum is $180^\circ$, and when they sit side by side as "neighbours."

---

## The Pizza Story

**Sophia** orders a large round pizza, and the chef slices it cleanly into **$8$ equal slices**.

Back in Topic 1, we saw that a full circle is a **complete angle ($360^\circ$)**, so each slice of an $8$-slice pizza measures $360^\circ \div 8 = \mathbf{45^\circ}$. Now watch what happens when Sophia and her family take **several slices together**:

- Sophia grabs **$2$ slices** sitting side by side. Their combined angle is $45^\circ + 45^\circ = \mathbf{90^\circ}$ — a crisp **right angle** (like the corner of a square tray).
- Her brother **Leo** takes **$4$ slices** in a row. Their combined angle is $4 \times 45^\circ = \mathbf{180^\circ}$ — **half the pizza**, with a **straight line** running right across the pan from rim to rim!
- The remaining **$4$ slices** left in the pan also total **$180^\circ$**.

Looking down at the pizza box, Sophia noticed three big geometric ideas:
- Two angles can add up to **$90^\circ$**.
- Two angles can add up to **$180^\circ$**.
- Neighbouring slices share a **common edge (cut line)** between them.

Those three observations are the three pillars of this topic: **complementary angles**, **supplementary angles**, and **adjacent angles / linear pairs**.

---

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Complementary angles** | Two angles whose sum is **$90^\circ$** | $30^\circ$ and $60^\circ$ |
| **Supplementary angles** | Two angles whose sum is **$180^\circ$** | $110^\circ$ and $70^\circ$ |
| **Complement** | The angle needed to bring a given angle up to $90^\circ$ | The complement of $40^\circ$ is $50^\circ$ |
| **Supplement** | The angle needed to bring a given angle up to $180^\circ$ | The supplement of $40^\circ$ is $140^\circ$ |
| **Adjacent angles** | "Neighbour" angles sharing a common vertex and common arm, lying on opposite sides of that arm | Two neighbouring pizza slices |
| **Linear pair** | Two adjacent angles whose outer (non-common) arms form a **straight line** | Two halves making up half a pizza |
| **Common arm** | The shared ray separating two adjacent angles | The cut line between two neighbouring slices |

## Part A: Complementary Angles (Sum $= 90^\circ$)

**Complementary angles** are two angles whose **sum is $90^\circ$**.

Memory trick: **C**omplementary $\implies$ **C**orner (a $90^\circ$ square corner).

### Examples

| Angle 1 | Angle 2 | Sum | Complementary? |
|---|---|---|---|
| $30^\circ$ | $60^\circ$ | $90^\circ$ | Yes |
| $45^\circ$ | $45^\circ$ | $90^\circ$ | Yes |
| $20^\circ$ | $70^\circ$ | $90^\circ$ | Yes |
| $50^\circ$ | $50^\circ$ | $100^\circ$ | No |

### How to Find a Complement

The complement of any angle $x$ is **$90^\circ - x$**.

- Complement of $40^\circ = 90^\circ - 40^\circ = \mathbf{50^\circ}$
- Complement of $63^\circ = 90^\circ - 63^\circ = \mathbf{27^\circ}$
- Complement of $89^\circ = \mathbf{1^\circ}$

**Note:** Only an **acute angle** (less than $90^\circ$) has a complement. Trying to find the complement of $100^\circ$ gives $90^\circ - 100^\circ = -10^\circ$, which is impossible here, so an obtuse angle has no complement.

### Two Complementary Angles Side by Side (Splitting a Corner)

Imagine a $90^\circ$ corner of a drafting board split by a diagonal ray $\overrightarrow{OC}$:

```
  A
  |
  |     C
  |    /
  |   /
  |  /
  | /
  |/________ B
  O
```

Here $\angle AOB = 90^\circ$, and ray $\overrightarrow{OC}$ divides it into $\angle AOC$ and $\angle COB$.
Therefore, **$\angle AOC + \angle COB = 90^\circ$**.

**Example:** Suppose $\angle AOC = (2x + 6)^\circ$ and $\angle COB = (x + 12)^\circ$. Find $x$.

$$(2x + 6) + (x + 12) = 90$$
$$3x + 18 = 90$$
$$3x = 72 \implies \mathbf{x = 24}$$

$\angle AOC = 2(24) + 6 = \mathbf{54^\circ}$, and $\angle COB = 24 + 12 = \mathbf{36^\circ}$.

Check: $54^\circ + 36^\circ = 90^\circ$ ✅

## Part B: Supplementary Angles (Sum $= 180^\circ$)

**Supplementary angles** are two angles whose **sum is $180^\circ$**.

Memory trick: **S**upplementary $\implies$ **S**traight line ($180^\circ$).

### Examples

| Angle 1 | Angle 2 | Sum | Supplementary? |
|---|---|---|---|
| $110^\circ$ | $70^\circ$ | $180^\circ$ | Yes |
| $90^\circ$ | $90^\circ$ | $180^\circ$ | Yes |
| $150^\circ$ | $30^\circ$ | $180^\circ$ | Yes |
| $100^\circ$ | $100^\circ$ | $200^\circ$ | No |

### How to Find a Supplement

The supplement of any angle $x$ is **$180^\circ - x$**.

- Supplement of $40^\circ = 180^\circ - 40^\circ = \mathbf{140^\circ}$
- Supplement of $75^\circ = \mathbf{105^\circ}$
- Supplement of $128^\circ = \mathbf{52^\circ}$
- Supplement of $90^\circ = \mathbf{90^\circ}$ (a right angle is its own supplement!)

### A Quick Logical Check

| If one angle is... | Then its supplement must be... |
|---|---|
| Acute ($< 90^\circ$) | Obtuse ($> 90^\circ$) |
| Right ($90^\circ$) | Right ($90^\circ$) |
| Obtuse ($> 90^\circ$) | Acute ($< 90^\circ$) |

This immediately shows why **two acute angles can never be supplementary** (their sum stays below $180^\circ$), and **two obtuse angles can never be supplementary** (their sum exceeds $180^\circ$).

## Part C: Word Problems on Complements and Supplements

Here is a reliable strategy: **call the unknown angle $x$**, write its complement as $(90 - x)$ and its supplement as $(180 - x)$, and translate the sentence directly into an equation.

**Example 1:** An angle is $20^\circ$ more than its complement. Find the angle.

Let the angle $= x$, so its complement $= 90 - x$.
*"Angle is $20^\circ$ more than its complement"*:
$$x = (90 - x) + 20$$
$$x = 110 - x \implies 2x = 110 \implies \mathbf{x = 55^\circ}$$

Complement $= 90^\circ - 55^\circ = 35^\circ$.
Check: $55 - 35 = 20$ ✅, and $55 + 35 = 90$ ✅

**Example 2:** Two supplementary angles are in the ratio **$2 : 3$**. Find both angles.

Let the angles be $2k$ and $3k$.
$$2k + 3k = 180 \implies 5k = 180 \implies k = 36$$
Angles $= 2(36) = \mathbf{72^\circ}$ and $3(36) = \mathbf{108^\circ}$.

Check: $72 + 108 = 180$ ✅, and $72 : 108 = 2 : 3$ ✅

**Example 3:** An angle is $4$ times its complement. Find the angle.

$$x = 4(90 - x) \implies x = 360 - 4x \implies 5x = 360 \implies \mathbf{x = 72^\circ}$$

Complement $= 18^\circ$. Check: $4 \times 18 = 72$ ✅

**Example 4:** Find the angle that is equal to its supplement.

$$x = 180 - x \implies 2x = 180 \implies \mathbf{x = 90^\circ}$$

**Example 5:** The supplement of an angle is $3$ times its complement. Find the angle.

$$180 - x = 3(90 - x)$$
$$180 - x = 270 - 3x$$
$$3x - x = 270 - 180 \implies 2x = 90 \implies \mathbf{x = 45^\circ}$$

Check: supplement $= 135^\circ$, complement $= 45^\circ$, and $3 \times 45 = 135$ ✅

## Part D: Adjacent Angles (Neighbour Angles)

Remember two neighbouring slices in Sophia's pizza: they share the **exact same centre**, share **one cut edge**, and sit on **opposite sides** of that cut edge. In geometry, angles like these are called **adjacent angles** (*"adjacent"* means right next to each other).

### The 3 Conditions for Adjacent Angles

Two angles are adjacent **only if all three** conditions hold:

1. **Common vertex:** Both angles share the exact same vertex.
2. **Common arm:** Both angles share one ray (arm) between them.
3. **Non-common arms lie on opposite sides of the common arm:** The two angles sit side by side and do **not overlap**.

```
         A
        /
       /     C
      /    /
     /   /
    /  /
   / /
  O/___________ B
```

Look at $\angle AOC$ and $\angle COB$:
- Common vertex: **$O$** ✅
- Common arm: **$\overrightarrow{OC}$** ✅
- Non-common arms $\overrightarrow{OA}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB}$ lie on opposite sides of $\overrightarrow{OC}$ ✅

Therefore, **$\angle AOC$ and $\angle COB$ are adjacent angles**.

Notice also: when two angles are adjacent, their measures add up to the full angle formed by the outer arms:
$$\angle AOC + \angle COB = \angle AOB$$

### Non-Examples (Not Adjacent)

**Non-example 1: Different vertices**

```
     A               D
    /               /
   /               /
  O------ B       P------ E
```

$\angle AOB$ and $\angle DPE$ have different vertices ($O$ and $P$), so they are **not adjacent**.

**Non-example 2: Same vertex, no common arm**

```
   A         D
    \       /
     \     /
      \   /
       \ /
        O
       / \
      /   \
     B     E
```

$\angle AOB$ and $\angle DOE$ share vertex $O$, yet they do not share an arm, so they are **not adjacent**.

**Non-example 3: One angle inside another (overlap)**

Look back at the first diagram and compare $\angle AOB$ and $\angle AOC$. They share vertex $O$ and arm $\overrightarrow{OA}$. **However**, ray $\overrightarrow{OC}$ lies *inside* $\angle AOB$, meaning the non-common arms $\overrightarrow{OB}$ and $\overrightarrow{OC}$ lie on the **same side** of $\overrightarrow{OA}$. Because $\angle AOC$ overlaps inside $\angle AOB$, they are **not adjacent**.

### Quick Check Table

| Condition | $\angle AOC$ & $\angle COB$ | $\angle AOB$ & $\angle AOC$ |
|---|---|---|
| Common vertex? | Yes ($O$) | Yes ($O$) |
| Common arm? | Yes ($\overrightarrow{OC}$) | Yes ($\overrightarrow{OA}$) |
| Non-common arms on opposite sides? | Yes | **No** (they overlap) |
| **Adjacent?** | **Yes** | **No** |

## Part E: Linear Pair

Now picture **half of Sophia's pizza** — $4$ slices forming a straight diameter. If you cut that half-pizza into just **two pieces**, those two angles:
- are adjacent (common vertex, common arm), and
- have outer arms that form a **single straight line**.

Such a pair is called a **linear pair**.

> **Linear pair:** Two adjacent angles whose **non-common arms form a straight line**.

```
                C
               /
              /
             /
            /
  A -------O------- B
```

Ray $\overrightarrow{OC}$ stands on straight line $AB$.
- $\angle AOC$ and $\angle COB$ are adjacent (common vertex $O$, common arm $\overrightarrow{OC}$).
- Non-common arms $\overrightarrow{OA}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB}$ form the straight line $AB$.

Hence **$\angle AOC$ and $\angle COB$ form a linear pair**, and
$$\angle AOC + \angle COB = 180^\circ$$
(because a straight angle measures $180^\circ$).

### Linear Pair Axiom

> **If a ray stands on a line, then the sum of the two adjacent angles so formed is $180^\circ$.**

We accept this statement as an **axiom** without needing proof (as seen in *Euclid's Geometry*, an axiom is an accepted starting rule).

**Example:** Ray $\overrightarrow{OC}$ stands on line $AB$, and $\angle AOC = 65^\circ$. Find $\angle BOC$.

By the Linear Pair Axiom: $\angle AOC + \angle BOC = 180^\circ$
$$65^\circ + \angle BOC = 180^\circ \implies \mathbf{\angle BOC = 115^\circ}$$

### Converse of the Linear Pair Axiom

> **If the sum of two adjacent angles is $180^\circ$, then the non-common arms of the angles form a straight line.**

In other words, if two neighbouring angles add up to $180^\circ$, you know for certain that their outer edges form one continuous straight line!

**Example:** $\angle AOC = 110^\circ$ and $\angle COB = 70^\circ$ are adjacent angles. Are points $A, O, B$ collinear (on a straight line)?

$110^\circ + 70^\circ = 180^\circ$. By the converse of the Linear Pair Axiom, $\overrightarrow{OA}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB}$ form a straight line. Therefore, **yes, $A, O, B$ lie on a straight line (are collinear)**.

If $\angle COB$ had been $60^\circ$ instead, the sum would be $170^\circ$, and $A, O, B$ would **not** form a straight line.

### Linear Pair Axiom and Its Converse Side by Side

| | What Is Given | What Follows |
|---|---|---|
| **Linear Pair Axiom** | A ray stands on a **straight line** | The two adjacent angles sum to **$180^\circ$** |
| **Converse of Linear Pair Axiom** | Two adjacent angles sum to **$180^\circ$** | Their non-common arms form a **straight line** |

Each is the exact reverse of the other — which is why both together are often referred to simply as the **Linear Pair Axiom**.

## Part F: Difference Between a Linear Pair and Supplementary Angles

This is one of the most common points of confusion in geometry, so compare them carefully:

- Every **linear pair** adds up to $180^\circ$, so **every linear pair is supplementary.**
- However, **not every pair of supplementary angles is a linear pair.** Supplementary angles only need a sum of $180^\circ$ — they do **not** have to be adjacent! They can sit in completely different corners of a diagram.

```
  Supplementary, yet NOT a linear pair:

        D                         R
       /                         /
      / 120°                    / 60°
     P--------- E              Q--------- S
```

Here $\angle DPE = 120^\circ$ and $\angle RQS = 60^\circ$. Their sum is $180^\circ$, so they are **supplementary**. Yet they have separate vertices ($P$ and $Q$), so they are **not adjacent** and **not a linear pair**.

| | Must Sum to $180^\circ$? | Must Be Adjacent? | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Supplementary** | Yes | **No** | $120^\circ$ and $60^\circ$ in separate locations |
| **Linear pair** | Yes | **Yes**, with outer arms forming a straight line | Two angles formed by a ray standing on a line |

In one sentence: **A linear pair is a special kind of supplementary pair that also sits side by side along a straight line.**

Similarly, **complementary** angles can be adjacent (like the split $90^\circ$ corner in Part A) or separated anywhere on the page.

## Part G: Multiple Rays on a Line, and Angles Around a Point

### Multiple Rays on One Side of a Straight Line (Sum $= 180^\circ$)

In a linear pair, one ray splits a straight line into two angles. If **several rays** fan out from a point on the **same side** of a straight line, all of those angles still add up to **$180^\circ$** — just like $4$ slices still made half a pizza!

```
            D     C
             \   /
        E     \ /
          \    /
           \  /
  A ---------O--------- B
```

$$\angle AOE + \angle EOD + \angle DOC + \angle COB = 180^\circ$$

**Example 1:** Three angles on one side of a straight line at a point are $x, 2x,$ and $3x$. Find $x$ and each angle.

$$x + 2x + 3x = 180^\circ \implies 6x = 180^\circ \implies \mathbf{x = 30^\circ}$$

Angles: **$30^\circ, 60^\circ, 90^\circ$**. Check: $30 + 60 + 90 = 180$ ✅

**Example 2:** Three angles along one side of a straight line are $40^\circ, x,$ and $65^\circ$. Find $x$.

$$40 + x + 65 = 180 \implies \mathbf{x = 75^\circ}$$

### Angles Around a Single Point (Sum $= 360^\circ$)

Think of the whole pizza: all slices around the centre complete one **full turn**, or **$360^\circ$** (a complete angle from Topic 1).

> **The sum of all angles around a point is $360^\circ$.**

```
           B
           |
     C     |     A
       \   |   /
         \ | /
  D -------O
         / | \
       /   |   \
     E     |     H
           G
```

*(All angles meeting around point $O$ add up to $360^\circ$.)*

**Example 3:** Four angles around a point are $x, 2x, 3x,$ and $4x$. Find each angle.

$$x + 2x + 3x + 4x = 360^\circ \implies 10x = 360^\circ \implies x = 36^\circ$$
Angles: **$36^\circ, 72^\circ, 108^\circ, 144^\circ$**.

Check: $36 + 72 + 108 + 144 = 360$ ✅

**Example 4:** Four angles around a point are $100^\circ, 80^\circ, x,$ and $2x$. Find $x$.

$$100 + 80 + x + 2x = 360 \implies 180 + 3x = 360 \implies 3x = 180 \implies \mathbf{x = 60^\circ}$$
So $2x = 120^\circ$. Check: $100 + 80 + 60 + 120 = 360$ ✅

## Part H: "Find $x$" Problems Using Linear Pairs

**Example 1:** Two angles $(3x + 10)^\circ$ and $(2x - 5)^\circ$ form a linear pair. Find $x$ and both angles.

The sum of a linear pair is $180^\circ$:
$$(3x + 10) + (2x - 5) = 180$$
$$5x + 5 = 180 \implies 5x = 175 \implies \mathbf{x = 35}$$

Angles: $3(35) + 10 = \mathbf{115^\circ}$ and $2(35) - 5 = \mathbf{65^\circ}$.

Check: $115 + 65 = 180$ ✅

**Example 2:** Ray $\overrightarrow{OC}$ stands on line $AB$. $\angle AOC = (2y + 15)^\circ$ and $\angle BOC = (3y - 10)^\circ$. Find $y$.

$$(2y + 15) + (3y - 10) = 180 \implies 5y + 5 = 180 \implies 5y = 175 \implies \mathbf{y = 35}$$

$\angle AOC = 85^\circ$, $\angle BOC = 95^\circ$. Check: $85 + 95 = 180$ ✅

**Example 3:** Two angles forming a linear pair are in the ratio $4 : 5$. Find both angles.

$$4k + 5k = 180 \implies 9k = 180 \implies k = 20$$
Angles: **$80^\circ$** and **$100^\circ$**.

**Example 4:** Ray $\overrightarrow{OC}$ stands on line $AB$ with $\angle AOC = 70^\circ$. Ray $\overrightarrow{OD}$ bisects $\angle BOC$ (splits it into two equal angles, as in Topic 1). Find $\angle COD$.

First, $\angle BOC = 180^\circ - 70^\circ = 110^\circ$ (linear pair).
Since $\overrightarrow{OD}$ bisects $\angle BOC$, $\angle COD = 110^\circ \div 2 = \mathbf{55^\circ}$.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1. **Mistake 1: Swapping complementary and supplementary.**
   Complementary $= 90^\circ$ (**C**orner); Supplementary $= 180^\circ$ (**S**traight). Remember the first letters!

2. **Mistake 2: Assuming every supplementary pair is a linear pair.**
   $120^\circ$ and $60^\circ$ are supplementary, yet if they sit at separate vertices they are **not** a linear pair. A linear pair must also be **adjacent**.

3. **Mistake 3: Calling overlapping angles adjacent.**
   When $C$ lies inside $\angle AOB$, $\angle AOB$ and $\angle AOC$ share a vertex and an arm, yet they are **not** adjacent because one overlaps inside the other. Never skip the third condition!

4. **Mistake 4: Trying to find the complement of an obtuse angle.**
   $120^\circ$ has no complement ($90^\circ - 120^\circ = -30^\circ$ is not a positive angle). Only acute angles have complements.

5. **Mistake 5: Using $360^\circ$ for angles on one side of a straight line.**
   Angles on **one side of a straight line** sum to **$180^\circ$**. Only angles going **all the way around a point** sum to **$360^\circ$**.

6. **Mistake 6: Stopping right after finding $x$.**
   If a question asks *"Find $x$ and both angles,"* substitute $x$ back in and state the actual angle measures. Writing only $x = 35$ leaves the answer half-finished.

7. **Mistake 7: Setting up complement word problems backwards.**
   *"An angle is $20^\circ$ more than its complement"* translates to $x = (90 - x) + 20$, **not** $x + 20 = 90 - x$. Always ask which quantity is larger before placing $+20$.

## Quick Recap

- **Complementary angles:** sum $= \mathbf{90^\circ}$. Complement of $x = \mathbf{90^\circ - x}$ (acute angles only).
- **Supplementary angles:** sum $= \mathbf{180^\circ}$. Supplement of $x = \mathbf{180^\circ - x}$.
- **Adjacent angles:** $3$ conditions — **common vertex**, **common arm**, and non-common arms on **opposite sides** of the common arm (no overlap).
- **Linear pair:** two adjacent angles whose outer arms form a **straight line**. Sum $= \mathbf{180^\circ}$.
- **Linear Pair Axiom:** ray standing on a line $\implies$ adjacent angles sum to $180^\circ$.
- **Converse:** two adjacent angles summing to $180^\circ$ $\implies$ outer arms form a straight line.
- **Every linear pair is supplementary, yet not every supplementary pair is a linear pair.**
- Angles on one side of a straight line sum to **$180^\circ$**; angles around a point sum to **$360^\circ$**.
- For *"Find $x$"* problems, pick the right target total ($90^\circ, 180^\circ,$ or $360^\circ$), solve, and **check** your answer.

## Practice Questions

1. Find:
   (a) the complement of $40^\circ$
   (b) the complement of $63^\circ$
   (c) the supplement of $75^\circ$
   (d) the supplement of $128^\circ$

2. Find the angle which is equal to its complement.

3. Two supplementary angles differ by $40^\circ$. Find the angles.

4. An angle is four times its supplement. Find the angle.

5. The angles $(5x - 20)^\circ$ and $(3x + 40)^\circ$ form a linear pair. Find $x$ and both angles.

6. Three angles $2x, (3x + 10)^\circ,$ and $(4x - 10)^\circ$ lie on one side of a straight line at the same point. Find each angle.

7. Four angles around a point are $x, (x + 20)^\circ, (x + 40)^\circ,$ and $(x + 60)^\circ$. Find each angle.

8. Ray $\overrightarrow{OC}$ stands on line $AB$ such that $\angle AOC = 65^\circ$. Find $\angle BOC$. If ray $\overrightarrow{OD}$ bisects $\angle BOC$, find $\angle COD$.

9. State whether each statement is true or false. Give a reason.
   (a) Two acute angles can be supplementary.
   (b) Every linear pair is supplementary.
   (c) Two supplementary angles always form a linear pair.
   (d) Two right angles are supplementary.
   (e) Two obtuse angles can be complementary.

### Answers

1. (a) $90^\circ - 40^\circ = \mathbf{50^\circ}$
   (b) $90^\circ - 63^\circ = \mathbf{27^\circ}$
   (c) $180^\circ - 75^\circ = \mathbf{105^\circ}$
   (d) $180^\circ - 128^\circ = \mathbf{52^\circ}$

2. $x = 90 - x \implies 2x = 90 \implies \mathbf{x = 45^\circ}$

3. Let the angles be $x$ and $x + 40$.
   $x + (x + 40) = 180 \implies 2x = 140 \implies x = 70$.
   Angles: **$70^\circ$ and $110^\circ$**. Check: $70 + 110 = 180$ ✅, $110 - 70 = 40$ ✅

4. $x = 4(180 - x) \implies x = 720 - 4x \implies 5x = 720 \implies \mathbf{x = 144^\circ}$
   Supplement $= 36^\circ$. Check: $4 \times 36 = 144$ ✅

5. $(5x - 20) + (3x + 40) = 180 \implies 8x + 20 = 180 \implies 8x = 160 \implies \mathbf{x = 20}$
   Angles: $5(20) - 20 = \mathbf{80^\circ}$ and $3(20) + 40 = \mathbf{100^\circ}$. Check: $80 + 100 = 180$ ✅

6. $2x + (3x + 10) + (4x - 10) = 180 \implies 9x = 180 \implies x = 20$
   Angles: **$40^\circ, 70^\circ, 70^\circ$**. Check: $40 + 70 + 70 = 180$ ✅

7. $x + (x + 20) + (x + 40) + (x + 60) = 360 \implies 4x + 120 = 360 \implies 4x = 240 \implies x = 60$
   Angles: **$60^\circ, 80^\circ, 100^\circ, 120^\circ$**. Check: $60 + 80 + 100 + 120 = 360$ ✅

8. By the Linear Pair Axiom: $\angle BOC = 180^\circ - 65^\circ = \mathbf{115^\circ}$.
   Since $\overrightarrow{OD}$ bisects $\angle BOC$, $\angle COD = 115^\circ \div 2 = \mathbf{57.5^\circ}$.

9. (a) **False ❌.** Both acute angles are under $90^\circ$, so their sum is always strictly less than $180^\circ$.
   (b) **True ✅.** The angles in a linear pair always add up to $180^\circ$.
   (c) **False ❌.** Supplementary angles can sit in separate locations; a linear pair must also be adjacent along a line.
   (d) **True ✅.** $90^\circ + 90^\circ = 180^\circ$.
   (e) **False ❌.** Each obtuse angle is already over $90^\circ$, so two of them can never add up to $90^\circ$.
