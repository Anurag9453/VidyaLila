# Topic 6: Word Problems I: Numbers, Ages, Fractions, and Digits

By now you have every solving tool in your toolkit: substitution, elimination, cross-multiplication, graphing, and even the variable-renaming trick from Topic 5 for equations like $\frac{2}{x} + \frac{3}{y} = 13$. Up to this point, though, the equations were always handed to you **ready-made**. In real life, nobody walks up and hands you "$x + y = 50$." Problems arrive **in words**: *"Two numbers add up to 50..."*

In this topic, we practice the single most valuable skill in applied algebra: **translating words into equations**. You already know how to solve the system; the new skill is reading a story carefully and extracting its two equations.

---

## The Town Fair Riddle Booth

At the community summer fair, a bright banner hung over a wooden booth: **"Solve the Riddle, Win a Kite!"** Leo and his younger sister Sophia walked up to test their luck.

The booth host read from a card: *"I am thinking of two numbers. Their **sum is $50$**, and their **difference is $12$**. Name both numbers!"*

Leo started guessing right away: *"$25$ and $25$?"* Sum is $50$, difference is $0$. *"$30$ and $20$?"* Sum $50$, difference $10$. *"$32$ and $18$?"* Sum $50$, difference $14$. Sophia laughed: *"Leo, you're just shooting in the dark!"*

Sophia flipped over the fair flyer and jotted down three lines with a pencil:
- Larger number $= x$, smaller number $= y$
- $x + y = 50$
- $x - y = 12$

*"We did this back in Topic 2! Add both equations: $2x = 62$, so $x = 31$. Then $y = 19$."* The host checked $31 + 19 = 50$ and $31 - 19 = 12$, smiled, and handed Sophia the winning kite.

Sophia did not invent any new mathematics. She simply did **two deliberate things**: first she defined what $x$ and $y$ stood for, and then she turned each sentence into an equation. That is the habit we will build throughout this topic.

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Variable** | A letter representing an unknown quantity | $x =$ the larger number |
| **Word problem** | A math problem stated in sentences rather than equations | *"The sum of two numbers is $50$"* |
| **Translate** | Turning English phrases into algebraic expressions or equations | *"$5$ more than $x$"* $\to x + 5$ |
| **Present age** | Someone's age right now | Leo is $14$ years old today |
| **Digit** | A single numeral from $0$ to $9$ inside a number | In $47$, the digits are $4$ and $7$ |
| **Place value** | The value a digit holds because of its position | In $47$, the digit $4$ has value $40$ |
| **Reversed digits** | Swapping the tens and units digits of a two-digit number | Reversing $47$ gives $74$ |
| **Ratio** | A comparison between two quantities by division | $3 : 4$ |

## Part A: The 4-Step Method for Word Problems

Every word problem—no matter how long—follows the exact same four steps:

> **Step 1: Define your variables.** State clearly in words what $x$ and $y$ stand for (*"Let $x = \dots$"*).
> **Step 2: Turn each sentence into an equation.** Two unknowns require two separate pieces of information, giving two equations.
> **Step 3: Solve the system.** Use substitution, elimination, or cross-multiplication—whichever is quickest.
> **Step 4: Check your answer against the ORIGINAL WORDS.** Do not only check inside your equations—test your numbers directly against every sentence of the problem!

Step 4 is the one students skip most often, yet it is the only check that catches a misread sentence. If you set up an equation incorrectly, plugging your answer back into that flawed equation will still look "correct," whereas checking against the original English sentences immediately exposes the error.

### Words to Algebra: Translation Table

| In words | In algebra | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Sum of $x$ and $y$ | $x + y$ | |
| Difference of $x$ and $y$ | $x - y$ | Larger $-$ smaller (put the larger one first) |
| Twice $x$ (two times $x$) | $2x$ | |
| Three times $x$ | $3x$ | |
| $5$ more than $x$ | $x + 5$ | |
| $5$ less than $x$ | $x - 5$ | |
| $x$ is $5$ more than $y$ | $x = y + 5$ | $x$ is the larger quantity |
| $3$ more than twice $y$ | $2y + 3$ | Multiply by $2$ first, then add $3$ |
| Ratio of $x$ to $y$ is $3 : 4$ | $\dfrac{x}{y} = \dfrac{3}{4} \implies 4x = 3y$ | Cross-multiplying gives a clean linear equation |
| Age $x$ years ago | $\text{present age} - x$ | Looking $x$ years back in time |
| Age $y$ years from now | $\text{present age} + y$ | Looking $y$ years ahead |
| Sum of digits | $x + y$ | For a two-digit number with digits $x$ and $y$ |

**Watch carefully:** *"$x$ is $5$ more than $y$"* means $x = y + 5$, **not** $y = x + 5$. Ask yourself: which quantity is larger? Add $+5$ to the *smaller* side to balance them!

## Part B: Number Problems

### Example 1: Sum and Difference

The sum of two numbers is $50$ and their difference is $12$. Find the numbers. *(Sophia's fair riddle!)*

**Step 1:** Let the larger number be $x$ and the smaller number be $y$.
**Step 2:** $x + y = 50$ ...(1) and $x - y = 12$ ...(2).
**Step 3:** Add (1) and (2): $2x = 62 \implies x = 31$. Substitute into (1): $31 + y = 50 \implies y = 19$.
**Step 4 (Check in words):** Sum: $31 + 19 = 50$ ✅. Difference: $31 - 19 = 12$ ✅.

The two numbers are **$31$ and $19$**.

### Example 2: "3 More Than Twice Another Number"

One number is $3$ more than twice another number. The sum of the two numbers is $54$. Find both numbers.

**Step 1:** Let the second number be $y$ and the first number be $x$.
**Step 2:** *"First is $3$ more than twice the second"*: $x = 2y + 3$ ...(1). *"Sum is $54$"*: $x + y = 54$ ...(2).
**Step 3:** Substitute (1) into (2): $(2y + 3) + y = 54 \implies 3y + 3 = 54 \implies 3y = 51 \implies y = 17$. Then $x = 2(17) + 3 = 37$.
**Step 4 (Check in words):** Twice $17$ is $34$, and $3$ more is $34 + 3 = 37$ ✅. Sum: $37 + 17 = 54$ ✅.

The numbers are **$37$ and $17$**.

### Example 3: Two Weighted Sums

Three times the first number plus the second number equals $40$. The first number plus three times the second number equals $24$. Find the numbers.

**Step 1:** Let the first number be $x$ and the second number be $y$.
**Step 2:** $3x + y = 40$ ...(1) and $x + 3y = 24$ ...(2).
**Step 3:** From (1), $y = 40 - 3x$. Substitute into (2): $x + 3(40 - 3x) = 24 \implies x + 120 - 9x = 24 \implies -8x = -96 \implies x = 12$. Then $y = 40 - 3(12) = 4$.
**Step 4 (Check in words):** $3(12) + 4 = 40$ ✅; $12 + 3(4) = 24$ ✅.

The numbers are **$12$ and $4$**.

## Part C: Age Problems

Age problems introduce a new twist: **time moves forward or backward**. One sentence talks about ages *today*, while the other talks about *"5 years ago"* or *"10 years from now."* The safest way to keep everything straight is a small table:

| | Present age | $x$ years ago / $y$ years from now |
|---|---|---|
| First person | ... | ... |
| Second person | ... | ... |

**Golden rule:** A shift like *"5 years ago"* or *"6 years from now"* applies to **both** people—everyone ages at the same rate!

### Example 4: Present Age and One Future Condition

Today, Leo's father is $3$ times as old as Leo. In $10$ years, his father will be twice as old as Leo. Find their present ages.

**Step 1:** Let the father's present age be $x$ years and Leo's present age be $y$ years.
**Step 2:**
- Today: $x = 3y$ ...(1)
- In $10$ years: father is $x + 10$ and Leo is $y + 10$. Condition: $x + 10 = 2(y + 10)$ ...(2)

**Step 3:** Substitute (1) into (2): $3y + 10 = 2y + 20 \implies y = 10$. Then $x = 3(10) = 30$.
**Step 4 (Check in words):** Today $30$ and $10$: $30 = 3 \times 10$ ✅. In $10$ years, $40$ and $20$: $40 = 2 \times 20$ ✅.

Leo's father is **$30$ years old** and Leo is **$10$ years old**.

### Example 5: Both Past and Future

Five years ago, a father was $7$ times as old as his son. Five years from now, the father will be $3$ times as old as his son. Find their present ages.

**Step 1:** Let the father's present age be $x$ and the son's present age be $y$.
**Step 2:**

| | Present | $5$ years ago | $5$ years from now |
|---|---|---|---|
| Father | $x$ | $x - 5$ | $x + 5$ |
| Son | $y$ | $y - 5$ | $y + 5$ |

- $5$ years ago: $x - 5 = 7(y - 5)$ ...(1)
- $5$ years from now: $x + 5 = 3(y + 5)$ ...(2)

**Step 3:** Expand (1): $x - 5 = 7y - 35 \implies x = 7y - 30$. Expand (2): $x + 5 = 3y + 15 \implies x = 3y + 10$.
Equate the two expressions for $x$: $7y - 30 = 3y + 10 \implies 4y = 40 \implies y = 10$. Then $x = 3(10) + 10 = 40$.
**Step 4 (Check in words):** Present ages: father $40$, son $10$.
- $5$ years ago: father $35$, son $5$. Indeed $35 = 7 \times 5$ ✅.
- $5$ years from now: father $45$, son $15$. Indeed $45 = 3 \times 15$ ✅.

The father is **$40$ years old** and the son is **$10$ years old**.

### Example 6: Ratio and Sum

The present ages of two sisters are in the ratio $3 : 4$. Six years from now, the sum of their ages will be $40$ years. Find their present ages.

**Step 1:** Let the younger sister be $x$ years old and the older sister be $y$ years old.
**Step 2:**
- Ratio $3 : 4$: $\dfrac{x}{y} = \dfrac{3}{4} \implies 4x = 3y$ ...(1)
- In $6$ years: $(x + 6) + (y + 6) = 40 \implies x + y = 28$ ...(2)

**Step 3:** From (2), $x = 28 - y$. Substitute into (1): $4(28 - y) = 3y \implies 112 - 4y = 3y \implies 112 = 7y \implies y = 16$. Then $x = 28 - 16 = 12$.
**Step 4 (Check in words):** Today $12$ and $16$: ratio $12 : 16 = 3 : 4$ ✅. In $6$ years, $18$ and $22$: sum $18 + 22 = 40$ ✅.

The younger sister is **$12$ years old** and the older sister is **$16$ years old**.

## Part D: Fraction Problems

A fraction is written as $\text{numerator} \div \text{denominator}$, so we write an unknown fraction as:

$$\mathbf{\text{Fraction} = \frac{x}{y}}$$

where $x$ is the numerator and $y$ is the denominator ($y \neq 0$).

When a problem says *"add $1$ to the numerator,"* the new fraction is $\dfrac{x + 1}{y}$. When it says *"add $1$ to both numerator and denominator,"* the new fraction is $\dfrac{x + 1}{y + 1}$.

To turn any fraction equation into a clean linear equation, simply **cross-multiply**.

### Example 7: Adding to Both, Subtracting from Both

A fraction becomes $\frac{1}{2}$ when $1$ is added to both its numerator and denominator, and becomes $\frac{1}{3}$ when $1$ is subtracted from both. Find the fraction.

**Step 1:** Let the fraction be $\dfrac{x}{y}$.
**Step 2:**
- $\dfrac{x + 1}{y + 1} = \dfrac{1}{2}$. Cross-multiply: $2(x + 1) = y + 1 \implies 2x + 2 = y + 1 \implies \mathbf{y = 2x + 1}$ ...(1)
- $\dfrac{x - 1}{y - 1} = \dfrac{1}{3}$. Cross-multiply: $3(x - 1) = y - 1 \implies 3x - 3 = y - 1 \implies \mathbf{y = 3x - 2}$ ...(2)

**Step 3:** Equate both expressions for $y$: $2x + 1 = 3x - 2 \implies x = 3$. Then $y = 2(3) + 1 = 7$.
**Step 4 (Check in words):** Fraction $\dfrac{3}{7}$.
- Add $1$ to both: $\dfrac{4}{8} = \dfrac{1}{2}$ ✅
- Subtract $1$ from both: $\dfrac{2}{6} = \dfrac{1}{3}$ ✅

The fraction is **$\dfrac{3}{7}$**.

### Example 8: Sum and One Condition

The sum of the numerator and denominator of a fraction is $12$. If $3$ is added to the denominator, the fraction becomes $\frac{1}{2}$. Find the fraction.

**Step 1:** Let the fraction be $\dfrac{x}{y}$.
**Step 2:**
- Sum is $12$: $x + y = 12$ ...(1)
- Add $3$ to denominator: $\dfrac{x}{y + 3} = \dfrac{1}{2} \implies 2x = y + 3$ ...(2)

**Step 3:** From (2), $y = 2x - 3$. Substitute into (1): $x + 2x - 3 = 12 \implies 3x = 15 \implies x = 5$. Then $y = 12 - 5 = 7$.
**Step 4 (Check in words):** Fraction $\dfrac{5}{7}$. Sum: $5 + 7 = 12$ ✅. Add $3$ to denominator: $\dfrac{5}{10} = \dfrac{1}{2}$ ✅.

The fraction is **$\dfrac{5}{7}$**.

## Part E: Two-Digit Number Problems

The central idea here is **place value**. The number $47$ does not mean "$4$ stuck next to $7$." It means $4$ tens and $7$ ones: $4 \times 10 + 7 = 47$.

> For any two-digit number with **tens digit $x$** and **units digit $y$**:
> **Original number $= 10x + y$**
> **Number with digits reversed $= 10y + x$**

Quick check with $47$ ($x = 4, y = 7$): $10(4) + 7 = 47$ ✅. Reversed: $10(7) + 4 = 74$ ✅.

**Handy shortcut:** The difference between $(10x + y)$ and $(10y + x)$ is always a multiple of $9$:

$$(10y + x) - (10x + y) = 9y - 9x = 9(y - x)$$

So a clue like *"reversing the digits increases the number by $27$"* simplifies instantly to **$y - x = 3$**!

### Example 9: Digit Sum and Reversed Number

The sum of the digits of a two-digit number is $9$. When the digits are reversed, the new number is $27$ more than the original number. Find the number.

**Step 1:** Let the tens digit be $x$ and the units digit be $y$. Original number $= 10x + y$.
**Step 2:**
- Sum of digits: $x + y = 9$ ...(1)
- Reversed number is $27$ more: $(10y + x) = (10x + y) + 27 \implies 9y - 9x = 27$. Divide by $9$: **$y - x = 3$** ...(2)

**Step 3:** Add (1) and (2): $2y = 12 \implies y = 6$. Then $x = 9 - 6 = 3$.
**Step 4 (Check in words):** The number is $10(3) + 6 = \mathbf{36}$.
- Digit sum: $3 + 6 = 9$ ✅
- Reversed: $63$, and $63 - 36 = 27$ ✅
- Both digits lie between $0$ and $9$ ($x \neq 0$) ✅

### Example 10: A Number Equal to $7$ Times Its Digit Sum

A two-digit number is $7$ times the sum of its digits. If the digits are reversed, the new number is $36$ less than the original number. Find the number.

**Step 1:** Let the tens digit be $x$ and the units digit be $y$. Original number $= 10x + y$.
**Step 2:**
- Number $= 7 \times (\text{digit sum})$: $10x + y = 7(x + y) \implies 3x = 6y \implies \mathbf{x = 2y}$ ...(1)
- Reversed is $36$ less: $(10y + x) = (10x + y) - 36 \implies 9x - 9y = 36 \implies \mathbf{x - y = 4}$ ...(2)

**Step 3:** Substitute (1) into (2): $2y - y = 4 \implies y = 4$. Then $x = 2(4) = 8$.
**Step 4 (Check in words):** The number is **$84$**.
- Digit sum: $8 + 4 = 12$, and $7 \times 12 = 84$ ✅
- Reversed: $48$, and $84 - 48 = 36$ ✅
- Both digits $8$ and $4$ are valid digits ($0$ to $9$) ✅

### Example 11: Another Sum-and-Reverse Pair

The sum of the digits of a two-digit number is $12$. Reversing the digits increases the number by $36$. Find the number.

**Step 1:** Let the tens digit be $x$ and the units digit be $y$.
**Step 2:** $x + y = 12$ ...(1). Reversed is $36$ more: $9(y - x) = 36 \implies y - x = 4$ ...(2).
**Step 3:** Add (1) and (2): $2y = 16 \implies y = 8$, $x = 4$.
**Step 4 (Check in words):** Number **$48$**. Digit sum $4 + 8 = 12$ ✅. Reversed $84$, and $84 - 48 = 36$ ✅.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1. **Mistake 1: Writing a two-digit number as "$xy$" instead of $10x + y$.**
   In algebra, "$xy$" means **$x \times y$**, not two digits placed side by side. For $36$ ($x = 3, y = 6$):
   - Wrong: $xy = 3 \times 6 = 18 \neq 36$.
   - Right: $10x + y = 10(3) + 6 = \mathbf{36}$ ✅.
   Always write **$10x + y$** for the number and **$10y + x$** for its reverse.

2. **Mistake 2: Applying "years ago" or "years from now" to only one person.**
   In Example 5, *"5 years ago, the father was $7$ times as old as his son"* must adjust **both** ages:
   - Wrong: $x - 5 = 7y$ *(only the father went back $5$ years, not the son!)*
   If you solve that flawed equation with $x + 5 = 3(y + 5)$, you get $y = 1.25$ and $x = 13.75$—a $13\text{-year-old}$ father! Using $x - 5 = 7(y - 5)$ shifts both people back $5$ years and gives $40$ and $10$. Building the age table prevents this error completely.

3. **Mistake 3: Forgetting to check whether the answer makes real-world sense.**
   Just because algebra spits out numbers does not mean the problem is valid or your equations were right. Check three common-sense rules:
   - **Ages** must be positive ($> 0$).
   - **Digits** of a two-digit number must be integers from $0$ to $9$ (with tens digit $x \neq 0$).
   - **Counts of people or items** must be non-negative whole numbers.
   Look at two quick warning signs:
   - *"Maya is $5$ years older than Leo, and the sum of their ages is $4$."* Solving $x = y + 5$ and $x + y = 4$ gives $y = -0.5$. A negative age means the scenario is **impossible**!
   - *"A two-digit number's digits add to $5$, and reversing increases it by $40$."* Solving $x + y = 5$ and $9(y - x) = 40$ gives $y - x = \frac{40}{9}$, which is not an integer. No such two-digit number exists!
   Step 4 catches both misread equations and impossible problems.

4. **Mistake 4: Reversing the sides in a "more than" sentence.**
   Writing *"$x$ is $5$ more than $y$"* as $y = x + 5$ puts $+5$ on the wrong side. Since $x$ is larger, **$x = y + 5$**. Whenever unsure, test with numbers: if $y = 10$, then $x$ must be $15$, which matches $x = y + 5$ ✅.

## Quick Recap

- **4-step word-problem method:** (1) define $x$ and $y$ clearly, (2) translate each sentence into an equation, (3) solve the system, (4) verify in the **original words** and check reasonableness.
- **Translation basics:** *"sum"* $\to +$; *"difference"* $\to -$; *"twice"* $\to 2x$; *"$5$ more than $x$"* $\to x + 5$; *"ratio $3 : 4$"* $\to 4x = 3y$; *"$x$ years ago"* $\to \text{age} - x$; *"$y$ years from now"* $\to \text{age} + y$.
- **Age problems:** Build an age table and shift **both** people by the same number of years.
- **Fraction problems:** Write the fraction as $\frac{x}{y}$, apply changes to numerator/denominator, and cross-multiply.
- **Two-digit numbers:** Write **$10x + y$** (tens digit $x$, units digit $y$), reversed **$10y + x$**, and remember $(10y + x) - (10x + y) = 9(y - x)$.
- Always check real-world validity (positive ages, whole digits $0$–$9$).

## Practice Questions

1. The sum of two numbers is $41$ and their difference is $9$. Find the numbers.

2. One number is $5$ less than three times another number. The sum of the two numbers is $63$. Find the numbers.

3. A mother is $3$ times as old as her daughter. After $12$ years, the mother will be twice as old as her daughter. Find their present ages.

4. Four years ago, an uncle was $5$ times as old as his nephew. Four years from now, the uncle will be $3$ times as old as the nephew. Find their present ages.

5. A fraction becomes $\dfrac{9}{11}$ when $2$ is added to both its numerator and denominator, and becomes $\dfrac{5}{6}$ when $3$ is added to both. Find the fraction.

6. The sum of the digits of a two-digit number is $11$. If the digits are reversed, the new number is $27$ more than the original number. Find the original number.

7. A two-digit number is $4$ times the sum of its digits. If the digits are reversed, the new number is $18$ more than the original number. Find the number.

8. (a) A student writes a two-digit number with tens digit $x$ and units digit $y$ as "$xy$." Explain what is wrong and write the correct expression.
   (b) The sum of the present ages of two friends is $30$, and one is $40$ years older than the other. Set up the equations, solve them, and explain why the answer shows the problem cannot be valid.

### Answers

1. Larger $= x$, smaller $= y$. $x + y = 41$ and $x - y = 9$. Add: $2x = 50 \implies x = 25, y = 16$.
   Check: $25 + 16 = 41$ ✅, $25 - 16 = 9$ ✅. Numbers: **$25$ and $16$**.

2. Second number $= y$, first $= x$. $x = 3y - 5$ and $x + y = 63$. Substitute: $3y - 5 + y = 63 \implies 4y = 68 \implies y = 17$, $x = 3(17) - 5 = 46$.
   Check: $3(17) = 51$, $51 - 5 = 46$ ✅; $46 + 17 = 63$ ✅. Numbers: **$46$ and $17$**.

3. Mother $= x$, daughter $= y$. $x = 3y$. In $12$ years: $x + 12 = 2(y + 12)$. Substitute: $3y + 12 = 2y + 24 \implies y = 12, x = 36$.
   Check: $36 = 3 \times 12$ ✅; in $12$ years, $48$ and $24$, and $48 = 2 \times 24$ ✅. Mother is **$36$ years old**, daughter is **$12$ years old**.

4. Uncle $= x$, nephew $= y$. Four years ago: $x - 4 = 5(y - 4) \implies x = 5y - 16$. Four years from now: $x + 4 = 3(y + 4) \implies x = 3y + 8$. Equate: $5y - 16 = 3y + 8 \implies 2y = 24 \implies y = 12$, $x = 3(12) + 8 = 44$.
   Check: $4$ years ago, $40$ and $8$ ($40 = 5 \times 8$) ✅; $4$ years from now, $48$ and $16$ ($48 = 3 \times 16$) ✅. Uncle is **$44$ years old**, nephew is **$12$ years old**.

5. Fraction $\frac{x}{y}$. From $\frac{x + 2}{y + 2} = \frac{9}{11}$: $11x + 22 = 9y + 18 \implies 11x - 9y = -4$ ...(1). From $\frac{x + 3}{y + 3} = \frac{5}{6}$: $6x + 18 = 5y + 15 \implies 6x - 5y = -3$ ...(2).
   Multiply (1) by $5$ and (2) by $9$: $55x - 45y = -20$ and $54x - 45y = -27$. Subtract: $x = 7$. From (2): $42 - 5y = -3 \implies 5y = 45 \implies y = 9$.
   Check: $\frac{7 + 2}{9 + 2} = \frac{9}{11}$ ✅; $\frac{7 + 3}{9 + 3} = \frac{10}{12} = \frac{5}{6}$ ✅. Fraction: **$\dfrac{7}{9}$**.

6. Tens digit $x$, units digit $y$. $x + y = 11$. Reversed is $27$ more: $9(y - x) = 27 \implies y - x = 3$. Add: $2y = 14 \implies y = 7, x = 4$.
   Check: number $47$. Digit sum $4 + 7 = 11$ ✅. Reversed $74$, and $74 - 47 = 27$ ✅. Original number: **$47$**.

7. Tens digit $x$, units digit $y$. $10x + y = 4(x + y) \implies 6x = 3y \implies y = 2x$. Reversed is $18$ more: $9(y - x) = 18 \implies y - x = 2$. Substitute: $2x - x = 2 \implies x = 2, y = 4$.
   Check: number $24$. Digit sum $6$, and $4 \times 6 = 24$ ✅. Reversed $42$, and $42 - 24 = 18$ ✅. Number: **$24$**.

8. (a) In algebra, "$xy$" means $x \times y$, not a two-digit number. For $x = 3, y = 6$, $xy = 18$, whereas the number is $36$. Correct expression: **$10x + y$**.
   (b) Let the friends' ages be $x$ and $y$ ($x > y$). Then $x + y = 30$ and $x - y = 40$. Adding gives $2x = 70 \implies x = 35, y = -5$. An age cannot be negative ($-5$), which proves the problem statement is impossible: when two positive ages add up to $30$, their difference cannot exceed $30$.
