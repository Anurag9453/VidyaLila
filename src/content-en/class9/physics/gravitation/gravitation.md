# Topic 1: What is Gravity?

In the previous chapter, Newton’s laws taught us a foundational truth: **an object’s state of motion changes only when an unbalanced force acts upon it**. A resting object begins moving only when nudged or pulled, and a moving object turns or halts only when a force opposes or redirects it. But an everyday mystery remained: what is that unseen force that pulls a falling apple, raindrops, or a dropped ball steadily towards the ground? The opening topic of this chapter is dedicated to this **extraordinary universal force**: **gravity**.

---

## Leo's Question in the Orchard

It was a quiet, warm summer afternoon. Dr. Newton was relaxing under the shade of an apple tree in the garden, reading a notebook, while his curious young student Leo sat nearby watching the calm branches. There was virtually no breeze; not a single leaf stirred.

Suddenly, a crisp, ripe red apple detached from its stem and fell straight to the lawn below: *thump!*

Leo walked over, picked up the apple, examined it thoughtfully, and asked: "Dr. Newton, why does an apple always drop straight down? The air is completely still. It didn't drift sideways, nor did it float upward into the sky. It behaves as though an invisible hand reached up from the earth and pulled it downward. **What is pulling it?**"

Dr. Newton smiled warmly. "A brilliant observation, Leo! You spotted the core truth immediately: something *is* pulling it. It cannot be seen with our eyes, yet it pulls on everything around us. Let us explore and uncover this remarkable force today."

---

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Gravitational force (gravity)** | The mutual force of attraction that pulls any two objects together because of their masses | The Earth pulling an apple down |
| **Attractive force** | A force that tends to draw objects closer together | Gravity |
| **Repulsive force** | A force that pushes objects away from each other | Two identical magnetic poles repelling |
| **Non-contact force** | A force that acts across space without physical contact | Gravity, magnetic attraction |
| **Mass** | The quantity of matter contained within an object, measured in kilograms (kg) | An apple with a mass of 0.4 kg |
| **Centre of the Earth** | The central point of the Earth | The gravitational pull is directed toward it |

---

## Part A: Why Objects Fall Downward

Dr. Newton suggested to Leo: "Let us try a quick experiment. Hold this apple in your outstretched hand, and then simply let go."

Leo opened his hand. The apple plummeted straight down. Next, Leo tossed a small pebble straight up into the air: it rose for a moment, slowed down, paused briefly at its highest point, and then plunged right back down into his palm.

Dr. Newton asked, "Have you ever observed an unassisted object suddenly shoot sideways or drift upwards on its own without anything touching or propelling it?" 

Leo shook his head, "Never. Without exception, it falls downward."

This brings us to a clear conclusion: **the ground—or more accurately, the Earth—is continuously pulling every object towards itself.** This invisible pull is a force, and we call it the **gravitational force**, or simply **gravity**.

> **Gravity = the attractive pull that acts between any two objects possessing mass.**

When we use the word "gravity" in daily life without specifying another celestial body, we almost always mean the **gravitational pull of the Earth** (also referred to as the **Earth's gravity**). An apple drops because the Earth exerts a downward gravitational force upon it.

---

## Part B: What "Down" Really Means: Towards the Centre

Leo pondered for a moment and posed another clever question: "Dr. Newton, my pen pal Liam lives in Australia, on the opposite side of our spherical planet. If Liam drops an apple there, it falls towards the ground at his feet as well—not out into space. If he is 'upside down' relative to us, how does that work?"

Dr. Newton picked up a stick, traced a large circle in the garden soil, and explained: "That is the wonderful elegance of spherical symmetry. The Earth is roughly spherical, and it pulls every object **inward towards its centre**. For anyone standing on the surface, 'down' is not an absolute direction in deep space; 'down' simply means **toward the centre of the Earth**. That is why Liam in Australia experiences 'down' as directed straight into the ground beneath his feet, just as we do!"

```
               Observer in the Northern Hemisphere
                               |
                     apple     v
                       v   .---------.   v
                      ->  /           \  <-
                         |    centre   |
                     ->  |      *      |  <-
                         |             |
                      ->  \           /  <-
                       ^   '---------'   ^
                               ^
                               |
               Observer in the Southern Hemisphere (Australia)
               (for them, the arrow also points towards the centre)
```

> **Figure 1.1** – Gravitational force vectors point inward towards the Earth's centre from every location on the surface. Consequently, an observer anywhere on the globe defines "down" as the direction pointing toward Earth's centre.

In short: **"down" is not a fixed, absolute direction in the universe.** The gravitational force always points radially inward towards the centre of the Earth. From our home it points beneath our shoes, in Australia it points beneath theirs, and at the equator it points inward towards the exact same core.

## Part C: Gravity Exists Between All Masses, Not Just Earth

Leo wondered: "Does that mean only massive planets like Earth possess gravity?"

"Not at all," replied Dr. Newton. "Gravitational attraction exists between **any two objects that possess mass**. That means:

- The Earth pulls the apple.
- **The apple also pulls the Earth!**
- The **Moon** pulls the Earth, and the Earth pulls the Moon.
- The **Sun** pulls the Earth, and the Earth pulls the Sun.
- In fact, Leo, you and I are exerting a gravitational pull on each other right this moment!"

Leo laughed, "Then why aren't we sliding across the lawn and bumping into one another?"

### Why a Pen and a Book Do Not Pull Each Other Noticeably

Dr. Newton explained: "The reality is that a **pen and a notebook resting on a desk genuinely attract each other via gravity**. However, the magnitude of gravitational force between everyday objects is **exceedingly minuscule**. It becomes significant and easily noticeable only when at least one of the interacting bodies has an **enormous mass**, such as the Earth. The gravitational force between a pen and a book is so microscopic that our senses cannot detect it, and it cannot overcome even the tiny static friction between the objects and the desk surface."

| Objects | Nature of Pull | Why We Do or Do Not Notice It |
|---|---|---|
| Earth and apple | Very strong effect | Earth's immense mass produces a noticeable acceleration; the apple drops instantly |
| Moon and Earth | Enormous | Keeps the Moon locked in orbit around Earth and causes ocean tides (Topic 6) |
| Sun and Earth | Immense | Both bodies have colossal masses, keeping Earth in its stable annual orbit |
| Pen and notebook | Extremely tiny | Both masses are tiny; force is too faint to overcome table friction |
| Two people standing nearby | Extremely tiny | Human masses are negligible compared to planetary scales |

**Why don't we notice the apple pulling the Earth?** Earth pulls the apple, and by **Newton's Third Law of Motion (action and reaction are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction)**, the **apple pulls Earth with an identical force**. However, Earth’s mass is so incomprehensibly massive ($6 \times 10^{24}\text{ kg}$) that this tiny force produces an acceleration so close to zero that no human instrument could possibly observe Earth moving toward the apple! We will calculate these exact numbers in Topic 2.

## Part D: Key Characteristics of Gravity

### 1. Gravity is Always Attractive

Gravity only **pulls**; it never pushes. Scientists have never discovered any form of ordinary matter that repels another through gravity. Therefore, gravitational force is **strictly attractive**, never repulsive.

### 2. Gravity is a Non-Contact Force

When the apple separated from the branch, it hung freely in mid-air. Nothing physical was touching it, yet the Earth pulled it down. Hence, gravity is classified as a **non-contact force** (or action-at-a-distance force). In [Topic 1](../force-and-laws-of-motion/force-and-laws-of-motion.md) (Part F) of our Force chapter, we categorised non-contact forces into three major types: gravity, magnetic force, and electrostatic force. Gravity stands right at the top of that list.

### 3. Gravity Acts Everywhere and Always

Earth's gravity does not stop at the surface. It acts at sea level, on high mountain peaks, at airplane cruising altitudes, and out in **deep space**. As distance between objects increases, the force decreases, but theoretically it approaches zero only at infinite distance (we will explore this inverse-square relationship in Topic 2).

### 4. Gravity Acts on All Objects, Light or Heavy

Earth’s gravity attracts everything possessing mass. A dry leaf, a dense stone, an apple, a heavy truck: gravity acts on every single one. Even though their masses differ, each experiences Earth's gravitational pull.

## Part E: The Bond Between the Earth, Moon, and Sun

Leo gazed upward into the afternoon sky, where the pale crescent of the Moon was visible. "Dr. Newton, if the Earth pulls on the Moon, why doesn't the Moon crash straight into us like the apple did?"

"That is a profound question," Dr. Newton answered with a twinkle in his eye, "and we will unpack its full beauty in Topic 6. For now, understand that the **Earth's gravitational pull bends the Moon's path into an orbit**, preventing it from flying away in a straight line into deep space. Similarly, the **Sun's massive gravitational pull** keeps the Earth orbiting faithfully year after year."

Thus, gravity is not merely a local force that causes fruit to fall—it is the **cosmic glue that holds the Solar System and entire galaxies together**.

## Part F: Gravity and Newton's Laws of Motion

Our understanding from the previous chapter connects seamlessly here:

- While attached to the tree branch, the apple was **at rest**. The branch pulled upward with a stem force, while gravity pulled downward: the two forces were **balanced**.
- When the stem broke, the upward supporting force vanished. Only downward gravity remained: an **unbalanced net force**.
- **Newton’s Second Law** states that an unbalanced force causes an object to **accelerate** ($F = ma$). The apple accelerated downward from $0\text{ m/s}$ to greater speeds.
- **Newton’s First Law** explains that without an unbalanced force, an object maintains its velocity. Hence, the apple remained resting on the branch until the forces became unbalanced.

For instance, if a hypothetical net unbalanced force of $2\text{ N}$ acts on a $0.5\text{ kg}$ object, its acceleration is:
$$a = \frac{F}{m} = \frac{2\text{ N}}{0.5\text{ kg}} = 4\text{ m/s}^2$$

We will see the exact acceleration caused by Earth's gravity in Topic 3. For now, remember: gravity is a genuine mechanical force obeying all the laws of motion.

**Check:** $4\text{ m/s}^2 \times 0.5\text{ kg} = 2\text{ N}$ ✅

### A Hanging Object: When Nothing Falls

Consider an electric ceiling lamp hanging from a cord. The lamp remains **at rest** and does not fall. Has gravity vanished? Not at all!

```
        ===== Ceiling =====
                 |
                 |   <- Tension in the cord (upward)
                 |
               ( O )  Lamp
                 |
                 v   <- Gravitational force / Weight (downward)
```

> **Figure 1.2** – A hanging lamp experiences two forces: gravity acting downward and cord tension pulling upward. Because they are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction, the net force is zero and the lamp stays in equilibrium.

Gravity pulls the lamp downward, while the tension in the cord pulls it upward with an equal force. These forces are **balanced**, yielding a net force of zero. (This downward gravitational force is what we call the **weight** of the object, which we will formally study in Topic 5.)

## Part G: What If Gravity Ceased to Exist? (A Thought Experiment)

Dr. Newton smiled and said, "Imagine if gravity were suddenly switched off for ten seconds."

- A detached apple would not fall; it would **float motionless or drift away in whatever direction it was nudged**.
- Raindrops would cease falling to the ground.
- Humans could not walk on Earth’s surface; a gentle hop would launch you floating uncontrollably into the air.
- Earth’s atmosphere of life-giving gases would scatter into outer space.

A sneak peek: **Astronauts aboard a space station** appear to float weightlessly. Does that mean there is zero gravity where they orbit? That is a widespread misconception! In Topics 5 and 6, we will discover the true physics of **free-fall weightlessness**.

## Part H: The Legend of Newton and the Apple

**Legend has it** that Isaac Newton was sitting beneath an apple tree when an apple fell to the ground, prompting him to wonder: "Why did that apple fall straight down? Could the very force pulling the apple extend all the way up to the Moon?"

Whether the apple actually struck his head is a matter of **folklore and historical legend**. However, the scientific breakthrough was very real: Newton realised that **the force bringing an apple to the ground is the exact same fundamental force keeping the Moon in orbit around the Earth**. Earthly physics and celestial physics are governed by the same universal law: the **Universal Law of Gravitation**, which we will study in Topic 2.

## Part I: Comparing Gravity and Magnetic Force

Both operate across empty space, yet they display essential differences:

| Property | Gravity | Magnetic Force |
|---|---|---|
| Acts without physical contact? | Yes, non-contact | Yes, non-contact |
| Can attract? | Yes, always | Yes (between opposite magnetic poles) |
| Can repel? | Never | Yes (between like magnetic poles) |
| Acts on which objects? | **Every object with mass** | Only magnetic materials (e.g., iron, nickel) and magnets |
| When is it noticeable? | When at least one mass is enormous (e.g., Earth) | Readily noticeable even with everyday small magnets |

---

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1. **Mistake 1: Believing gravity exists only near the ground.**
   *Correction:* Gravitational force reaches vast distances across space. Earth’s gravity binds the Moon over $384,000\text{ km}$ away. It weakens with distance, but never abruptly cuts off.

2. **Mistake 2: Thinking "down" is a universal absolute direction in space.**
   *Correction:* "Down" always means **toward the centre of the Earth**. An observer in Australia considers their local ground to be down because the Earth’s centre is beneath their feet.

3. **Mistake 3: Believing that outer space has zero gravity.**
   *Correction:* Gravity is present throughout space; it is what holds moons, planets, and artificial satellites in orbit. The apparent floating of astronauts is due to continuous free fall, not zero gravity.

4. **Mistake 4: Assuming gravity acts only on heavy objects.**
   *Correction:* Gravity acts on every object with mass, light or heavy. A dry feather experiences Earth's gravity just as a heavy boulder does; feathers simply fall slower because of air resistance.

5. **Mistake 5: Thinking the apple does not pull the Earth.**
   *Correction:* By Newton's Third Law, the apple pulls the Earth with the exact same magnitude of force. The Earth's enormous mass simply makes its resulting acceleration negligibly small.

6. **Mistake 6: Believing atmospheric air pressure holds things down on the ground.**
   *Correction:* Gravitational attraction pulls objects to Earth, not air pressure. Air pressure exerts force equally in all directions, as we will study in Fluid Mechanics.

## Quick Recap

- Objects fall toward the ground because the Earth exerts a downward pull on them called **gravitational force (gravity)**.
- Gravitational force exists between any two objects that possess mass: the Earth, the Moon, the Sun, a pen, a book, and human beings.
- The Earth pulls every object radially towards its **centre**, which is why people anywhere around the globe define "down" as towards the Earth's centre.
- Gravity is strictly **attractive**, never repulsive, and acts as a **non-contact force**.
- The gravitational force between small everyday items like a pen and a book is imperceptibly tiny; gravity becomes noticeable only when huge masses like planets are involved.
- Gravity is active in outer space and keeps celestial bodies in orbit.
- Newton’s laws apply fully to gravity: an unsupported falling object experiences an unbalanced force and accelerates; a suspended hanging lamp remains at rest because upward tension balances downward gravity.
- The story of Newton and the falling apple illustrates the profound unification of terrestrial and celestial gravity under one universal principle.

(b) False ❌. Gravity is strictly an attractive force.

**A2.**  
(a) Downwards, towards the Earth's centre (gravity acts continuously at all points).  
(b) Downwards, towards the Earth's centre. The ball travels upward because of its initial velocity, but gravity pulls downward.  
(c) Towards the Earth's centre. For the person in Australia, this corresponds to local "down".  
(d) Towards the Earth's centre (the Earth pulls the Moon toward itself).  
(e) Towards the Earth's centre.  
(f) Downwards, towards the Earth's centre.  

**A3.** Downward gravitational force (weight) acts on the lamp, and upward tension acts through the chain. These two forces are **balanced** (equal in magnitude and opposite in direction), so the net force is zero and the lamp remains in static equilibrium.  

**A4.** Gravitational attraction does exist between the pen and the book, but because their masses are relatively tiny, the force is exceedingly weak. **A5.** An apple falls from a tree branch to the ground without any physical contact pulling it: Earth's gravity acts over a distance across empty space. Another non-contact force is magnetic force (or electrostatic force).  

**A6.** The Earth pulls everything radially inward towards its centre. For inhabitants anywhere on the globe, "down" means toward the centre of the Earth, keeping them firmly grounded without falling off into space.  

**A7.** While attached to the tree, the upward supporting force of the stem balances the downward gravitational force (Newton's First Law), keeping the fruit at rest. Once detached, the upward force vanishes, leaving gravity as an unbalanced net force. By Newton's Second Law ($F = ma$), this unbalanced force accelerates the fruit downward.  

**A8.**  
(i) Gravitational force is strictly attractive, whereas magnetic force can be either attractive (unlike poles) or repulsive (like poles).  
(ii) Gravitational force acts on all objects with mass, whereas magnetic force acts only on magnetic materials and magnetic poles.
