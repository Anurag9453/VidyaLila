# Topic 5: Salts Ke Parivaar, Types aur Unhe Banane Ke Tareeke

[Part 3](acids-bases-and-salts-part3.md) mein humne dekha ki jab ek acid aur ek base aapas mein milte hain, to neutralisation hokar **salt** aur **paani** banta hai. Aur [Part 4](acids-bases-and-salts-part4.md) mein humne 0 se 14 tak ka **pH scale** samjha. Par ab ek bada aur dilchasp sawaal uthta hai: **kya har salt ka paani mein ghol bilkul neutral (pH = 7) hota hai?** Aur lab mein kabhi koi salt paani mein poora ghul jaata hai (NaCl), to kabhi safed dhodha bankar neeche baith jaata hai (BaSO₄) — aakhir salts ke kitne parivaar aur types hote hain, aur unhein lab mein kaise banaya jaata hai? Aaj hum salts ki poori duniya kholenge.

## School lab ki 'Salt Almirah' aur teen rangon ka chamatkar

Shaniwar ki subah school ki chemistry lab mein **Chinmay** apne teacher **Mr. Dandekar** ki madad kar raha tha. Almirah ke andar darjanon kaanch ki botalon mein safed aur neele crystals rakhe the — kisi par Sodium chloride, kisi par Sodium sulphate, kisi par Ammonium chloride, aur kisi par Sodium carbonate likha tha.

Chinmay ne botalon ko jamaate hue kaha, "Sir, ye saare ke saare **salts** hain na? Aur salt to acid aur base ke neutralisation se banta hai — iska matlab in sabka ghol **neutral (pH = 7)** hona chahiye!"

Mr. Dandekar muskuraye: "Achha? Chalo khud jaanch kar dekh lete hain!" Unhone teen saaf test tubes mein barabar paani liya. Pehli tube mein **Sodium chloride (NaCl)**, doosri mein **Ammonium chloride (NH₄Cl)**, aur teesri mein **Sodium carbonate (Na₂CO₃)** ghola — teeno ghol dekhne mein bilkul paani jaise saaf aur rangheen the. Phir unhone Chinmay se kaha ki teeno mein **Universal Indicator** ki 2-2 boondein daale.

Jaise hi boondein girin, Chinmay ki aankhein khuli ki khuli reh gayin:
- NaCl wali tube **hari (green, pH = 7)** ho gayi,
- NH₄Cl wali tube **narangi-laal (orange-red, pH ≈ 5, acidic)** ho gayi, aur
- Na₂CO₃ wali tube **gehri neeli (blue, pH ≈ 10.5, basic)** ho gayi!

"Sir, ye kaise hua?!" Chinmay ne hairani se poocha. "Teeno salts hain, phir ek neutral, ek acidic aur ek basic kyun nikla?"

Mr. Dandekar bole, "Chinmay, jaise bachon mein apne maa-baap ke gunn aate hain, waise hi har salt ke andar uske **parent acid** aur **parent base** ki taakat chhupi hoti hai! Jo parent strong hota hai, paani mein usi ka raaj chalta hai." Chalo, Chinmay ke saath salts ke parivaar aur unke mizaj ko step-by-step samajhte hain.

## Naye shabd

| Shabd | Matlab (simple bhasha mein) | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Salt (lavan)** | Acid ke replaceable H⁺ ion ko kisi metal ion ya NH₄⁺ se hatane par banne wala ionic compound | NaCl, KNO₃, CuSO₄, NH₄Cl |
| **Basic radical (cation)** | Salt ka dhan-aveshit (positive) hissa jo **parent base** se aata hai | Na⁺, K⁺, Mg²⁺, Ca²⁺, NH₄⁺ |
| **Acidic radical (anion)** | Salt ka rin-aveshit (negative) hissa jo **parent acid** se aata hai | Cl⁻, SO₄²⁻, NO₃⁻, CO₃²⁻, CH₃COO⁻ |
| **Family of salts** | Jin salts mein ek hi positive ion ya ek hi negative ion common ho | Sodium salts (NaCl, Na₂SO₄), Chloride salts (NaCl, KCl) |

## Part A: Salts Kya Hain aur Unke Parivaar (Families of Salts)

Jab kisi acid ke ionisable hydrogen (H⁺) ko koi dhatu ion (metal cation jaise Na⁺, K⁺, Ca²⁺, Zn²⁺) ya ammonium ion (NH₄⁺) replace kar deta hai, to banne wale ionic compound ko **Salt** kehte hain.

Har salt ke do hisse hote hain (Class 9 Ch 3 ki yaad taaza karo, [language-of-chemistry-part4.md](../../../class9/chemistry/language-of-chemistry/language-of-chemistry-part4.md)):
1. **Basic Radical (Cation — Positive ion):** Ye **parent base** (ya metal) se aata hai — jaise NaOH se Na⁺, KOH se K⁺, Cu(OH)₂ se Cu²⁺, NH₄OH se NH₄⁺.
2. **Acidic Radical (Anion — Negative ion):** Ye **parent acid** se aata hai — jaise HCl se Cl⁻ (chloride), H₂SO₄ se SO₄²⁻ (sulphate), HNO₃ se NO₃⁻ (nitrate), H₂CO₃ se CO₃²⁻ (carbonate), aur CH₃COOH se CH₃COO⁻ (acetate/ethanoate).

```
   SALT KE DO HISSE: PARENT BASE AUR PARENT ACID KI PEHCHAAN

      Parent Base: NaOH                   Parent Acid: H₂SO₄
                   │                                   │
                   ▼                                   ▼
                [ Na⁺ ]          +                 [ SO₄²⁻ ]
             (Basic Radical)                    (Acidic Radical)
                   │                                   │
                   └───────────────┬───────────────────┘
                                   ▼
                             Salt: Na₂SO₄
                          (Sodium sulphate)
```

> **Figure 5.1** – Har salt ka positive hissa (basic radical) uske parent base se aur negative hissa (acidic radical) uske parent acid se aata hai.

### Salts ke Parivaar (Families of Salts)

Jin salts mein **ek hi positive ion (cation)** ya **ek hi negative ion (anion)** common hota hai, unhein ek hi **Family (Parivaar)** ka sadasya kaha jaata hai:

| Family ka Naam | Common Ion | Is Parivaar ke Salts (Examples) |
|---|---|---|
| **Sodium salts family** | Na⁺ | NaCl, Na₂SO₄, NaNO₃, Na₂CO₃, CH₃COONa |
| **Potassium salts family** | K⁺ | KCl, K₂SO₄, KNO₃, K₂CO₃ |
| **Magnesium salts family** | Mg²⁺ | MgCl₂, MgSO₄, Mg(NO₃)₂ |
| **Chloride salts family** | Cl⁻ | NaCl, KCl, NH₄Cl, MgCl₂, CaCl₂, ZnCl₂ |
| **Sulphate salts family** | SO₄²⁻ | Na₂SO₄, K₂SO₄, MgSO₄, CuSO₄, CaSO₄, (NH₄)₂SO₄ |
| **Nitrate salts family** | NO₃⁻ | NaNO₃, KNO₃, Ca(NO₃)₂, Pb(NO₃)₂ |
| **Carbonate salts family** | CO₃²⁻ | Na₂CO₃, K₂CO₃, CaCO₃, ZnCO₃ |

> **Khas baat:** Ek hi salt do alag-alag parivaaron ka hissa ho sakta hai! Jaise **Na₂SO₄** mein Na⁺ ion hai isliye wo **Sodium salts family** mein aata hai, aur usmein SO₄²⁻ ion bhi hai isliye wo **Sulphate salts family** ka bhi sadasya hai.

## Part B: pH of Salt Solutions: Kya Har Salt Neutral Hota Hai?

Chinmay ke teeno test tubes ka raaz yahan khulta hai. Jab kisi salt ko paani mein ghola jaata hai, to uske ghol ka pH is baat par nirbhar karta hai ki uska **parent acid** aur **parent base** kitna strong ya weak tha ([Part 2](acids-bases-and-salts-part2.md) ki strong/weak list yaad karo):

- **Strong Acids:** HCl, H₂SO₄, HNO₃
- **Weak Acids:** H₂CO₃ (carbonic acid), CH₃COOH (acetic acid), H₂SO₃
- **Strong Bases (Alkalis):** NaOH, KOH
- **Weak Bases:** NH₄OH, Cu(OH)₂, Al(OH)₃, Zn(OH)₂, Fe(OH)₃

Iske aadhar par salt solutions ke **3 pakke niyam** bante hain:

1. **Strong Acid + Strong Base → Neutral Salt Solution (pH = 7):**
   Jab dono parents barabar taakatwar (strong) hon, to koi kisi par bhaari nahi padta aur paani mein ghol bilkul **neutral (pH = 7)** rehta hai. Universal indicator **hara (green)** rehta hai aur litmus par koi asar nahi hota.
   - **Udaharan:** NaCl (HCl + NaOH), KNO₃ (HNO₃ + KOH), K₂SO₄ (H₂SO₄ + KOH), Na₂SO₄ (H₂SO₄ + NaOH).
2. **Strong Acid + Weak Base → Acidic Salt Solution (pH < 7):**
   Jab parent acid **strong** ho aur parent base **weak** ho, to paani mein ghulne par strong acid ka prabhav jeet jaata hai (ise *salt hydrolysis* kehte hain). Ghol mein free H⁺(aq) ions badh jaate hain aur **pH < 7 (acidic)** ho jaata hai (neela litmus → laal).
   - **Udaharan:** NH₄Cl (HCl + NH₄OH), CuSO₄ (H₂SO₄ + Cu(OH)₂), AlCl₃ (HCl + Al(OH)₃), ZnSO₄ (H₂SO₄ + Zn(OH)₂).
3. **Weak Acid + Strong Base → Basic Salt Solution (pH > 7):**
   Jab parent acid **weak** ho aur parent base **strong** ho, to paani mein ghulne par strong base ka prabhav jeet jaata hai. Ghol mein free OH⁻(aq) ions badh jaate hain aur **pH > 7 (basic/alkaline)** ho jaata hai (laal litmus → neela).
   - **Udaharan:** Na₂CO₃ (H₂CO₃ + NaOH), NaHCO₃ (H₂CO₃ + NaOH), CH₃COONa (CH₃COOH + NaOH), K₂CO₃ (H₂CO₃ + KOH).

| Salt ka Naam aur Formula | Parent Acid (Strength) | Parent Base (Strength) | Aqueous Ghol ka Nature | pH Value Range | Litmus / Universal Indicator Par Asar |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sodium chloride (NaCl)** | HCl (Strong acid) | NaOH (Strong base) | **Neutral** | pH = 7 | Koi badlav nahi / Green |
| **Potassium nitrate (KNO₃)** | HNO₃ (Strong acid) | KOH (Strong base) | **Neutral** | pH = 7 | Koi badlav nahi / Green |
| **Sodium sulphate (Na₂SO₄)** | H₂SO₄ (Strong acid) | NaOH (Strong base) | **Neutral** | pH = 7 | Koi badlav nahi / Green |
| **Ammonium chloride (NH₄Cl)** | HCl (Strong acid) | NH₄OH (Weak base) | **Acidic** | pH < 7 | Blue litmus → Red / Orange-red |
| **Copper(II) sulphate (CuSO₄)** | H₂SO₄ (Strong acid) | Cu(OH)₂ (Weak base) | **Acidic** | pH < 7 | Blue litmus → Red / Orange |
| **Aluminium chloride (AlCl₃)** | HCl (Strong acid) | Al(OH)₃ (Weak base) | **Acidic** | pH < 7 | Blue litmus → Red / Orange-red |
| **Sodium carbonate (Na₂CO₃)** | H₂CO₃ (Weak acid) | NaOH (Strong base) | **Basic** | pH > 7 | Red litmus → Blue / Blue |
| **Sodium hydrogencarbonate (NaHCO₃)** | H₂CO₃ (Weak acid) | NaOH (Strong base) | **Mildly Basic** | pH > 7 (~8.3) | Red litmus → Blue / Bluish-green |
| **Sodium acetate (CH₃COONa)** | CH₃COOH (Weak acid) | NaOH (Strong base) | **Basic** | pH > 7 | Red litmus → Blue / Blue |

> **Activity 5.1 (school lab mein teacher ke saath).** Chhah test tubes mein thoda-thoda distilled water lekar unmein alag-alag NaCl, KNO₃, AlCl₃, ZnSO₄, CuSO₄, aur CH₃COONa gholo. Sabhi mein pH paper dubo kar dekho: NaCl aur KNO₃ mein pH 7 (green) aayega; AlCl₃, ZnSO₄ aur CuSO₄ mein pH 7 se kam (acidic) aayega; jabki CH₃COONa mein pH 7 se zyada (basic) aayega!

## Part C: Types of Salts (Normal, Acid, Basic) aur Lab Mein Salts Banane Ke 6 Tareeke

### 1. Formula ke Hisaab se Salts ke 3 Types

Acid ke replaceable H⁺ ions ya base ke OH⁻ ions ki replacement kitni poori hui hai, iske aadhar par salts **3 tarah** ke hote hain:

1. **Normal Salts (Samanya Lavan):**
   Jab kisi acid ke **saare replaceable H⁺ ions** kisi metal ya NH₄⁺ ion se **poori tarah (completely)** hat jaate hain, to **Normal salt** banta hai. Iske formula mein koi replaceable H⁺ ya OH⁻ nahi bachta.
   - HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O
   - H₂SO₄ + 2NaOH → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O (Yahan H₂SO₄ ke dono H⁺ hat gaye)
   - **Udaharan:** NaCl, KNO₃, Na₂SO₄, Na₂CO₃, CaCO₃, Na₃PO₄, CH₃COONa.
   - *(Dhyan rahe: CH₃COONa mein jo 3 hydrogen hain we CH₃– group ke carbon se jude hain aur ionisable nahi hain, isliye CH₃COONa ek **Normal salt** hai!)*

2. **Acid Salts (Amliya Lavan):**
   Jab kisi polybasic acid (jaise H₂SO₄, H₂CO₃, H₃PO₄) ke replaceable H⁺ ions ka **sirf adhoora (partial) replacement** hota hai — aksar tab jab alkali kam matra (insufficient amount) mein ho — to salt ke formula mein abhi bhi ek ya do replaceable H⁺ bache rehte hain. Ise **Acid salt** kehte hain.
   - H₂SO₄ + NaOH (insufficient) → NaHSO₄ (Sodium hydrogensulphate) + H₂O
   - H₂CO₃ + NaOH → NaHCO₃ (Sodium hydrogencarbonate) + H₂O
   - H₃PO₄ + NaOH → NaH₂PO₄ (Sodium dihydrogenphosphate) + H₂O
   - Jab ek acid salt (jaise NaHSO₄) ko aur alkali (NaOH) milti hai, to bacha hua H⁺ bhi nikal kar wo normal salt (Na₂SO₄) ban jaata hai:
     NaHSO₄(aq) + NaOH(aq) → Na₂SO₄(aq) + H₂O(l)

3. **Basic Salts (Kshariya Lavan):**
   Jab kisi polyacidic base (jaise Pb(OH)₂, Cu(OH)₂, Mg(OH)₂) ke OH⁻ ions ka acid dwara **adhoora (partial) replacement** hota hai, to salt ke andar OH⁻ group bacha rehta hai. Ise **Basic salt** kehte hain.
   - Pb(OH)₂ + HCl (insufficient) → Pb(OH)Cl (Basic lead chloride) + H₂O
   - Cu(OH)₂ + HNO₃ (insufficient) → Cu(OH)NO₃ (Basic copper nitrate) + H₂O

### 2. Soluble vs Insoluble Salts aur Lab Preparation ke 6 Tareeke

Lab mein salt banane ka konsa rasta chuna jaayega, ye is par nirbhar karta hai ki wo salt **paani mein ghulansheel (soluble)** hai ya **aghulansheel (insoluble)**:

| Salt Category | Paani Mein Soluble (Ghulansheel) | Paani Mein Insoluble (Precipitate Banane Wale) |
|---|---|---|
| **Sodium (Na⁺), Potassium (K⁺), Ammonium (NH₄⁺) salts** | **Saare ke saare soluble hain** (bina kisi apvaad ke!) | Koi nahi |
| **Nitrates (NO₃⁻) aur Bicarbonates (HCO₃⁻)** | **Saare nitrates soluble hain** (KNO₃, Pb(NO₃)₂, AgNO₃) | Koi common nitrate insoluble nahi |
| **Chlorides (Cl⁻)** | Lagbhag saare soluble (NaCl, MgCl₂, ZnCl₂, CuCl₂) | **Exceptions:** AgCl (safed ppt) aur PbCl₂ (thande paani mein insoluble, garam paani mein soluble) |
| **Sulphates (SO₄²⁻)** | Zyadatar soluble (Na₂SO₄, MgSO₄, CuSO₄, ZnSO₄, FeSO₄) | **Exceptions:** BaSO₄ (safed ppt), PbSO₄ (safed ppt), CaSO₄ (sparingly soluble) |
| **Carbonates (CO₃²⁻), Sulphites (SO₃²⁻), Sulphides (S²⁻)** | **Sirf** Na⁺, K⁺, aur NH₄⁺ wale soluble hain (Na₂CO₃, K₂CO₃) | **Baaki saare insoluble hain** (CaCO₃, ZnCO₃, CuCO₃, CuS, PbS) |

```
   LAB MEIN SALT BANANE KA SAHI TAREEKA KAISE CHUNEIN?

                         [ Konsa Salt Banana Hai? ]
                                     │
                 ┌───────────────────┴───────────────────┐
                 ▼                                       ▼
        [ INSOLUBLE SALT ]                       [ SOLUBLE SALT ]
      (jaise BaSO₄, AgCl, PbSO₄)                         │
                 │                        ┌──────────────┴──────────────┐
                 ▼                        ▼                             ▼
        Method 6: Precipitation     Kya Na⁺, K⁺ ya NH₄⁺          Baaki Soluble Salts
        (Double Displacement:        ka salt hai?                (jaise ZnSO₄, CuSO₄)
         2 soluble salts milao       (jaise NaCl, KNO₃)                 │
         → ppt chhaan lo!)                │                             ▼
                                          ▼                    Methods 2, 3, 4:
                                 Method 5: Titration          Excess Active Metal /
                                 (Soluble Acid + Alkali       Insoluble Oxide / Base /
                                  indicator ke saath)         Carbonate + Dilute Acid
```

> **Figure 5.2** – Salt soluble hai ya insoluble, isi se tay hota hai ki lab mein konsa preparation method kaam aayega.

#### Salts Banane Ke 6 Pramukh Laboratory Methods:

1. **Method 1 – Direct Combination (Metal + Non-metal):**
   Dhatu aur adhatu ko seedha garam karke jodna:
   2Na(s) + Cl₂(g) → 2NaCl(s)
   2Fe(s) + 3Cl₂(g) (dry) → 2FeCl₃(s) (Iron(III) chloride)
   Fe(s) + S(s) --(Heat)--> FeS(s) (Iron(II) sulphide)
2. **Method 2 – Active Metal + Dilute Acid (Displacement — Soluble Salts ke liye):**
   Active metals (Mg, Zn, Fe) dilute acid se H₂ gas hata kar soluble salt banate hain:
   Zn(s) + H₂SO₄(aq) → ZnSO₄(aq) + H₂(g)↑
   Fe(s) + H₂SO₄(aq) → FeSO₄(aq) + H₂(g)↑
3. **Method 3 – Insoluble Metal Oxide / Hydroxide + Dilute Acid (Neutralisation):**
   Copper (Cu) dilute H₂SO₄ ke saath seedha react nahi karta (kyunki Cu hydrogen se kam reactive hai). Isliye CuSO₄ banane ke liye hum black **Copper(II) oxide (CuO)** ko garam dilute H₂SO₄ mein daalte hain:
   CuO(s) + H₂SO₄(aq) → CuSO₄(aq) + H₂O(l)
   PbO(s) + 2HNO₃(aq) → Pb(NO₃)₂(aq) + H₂O(l)
4. **Method 4 – Metal Carbonate / Hydrogencarbonate + Dilute Acid:**
   Carbonate mein dilute acid daalne par CO₂ ke bulbule nikalte hain aur soluble salt banta hai:
   ZnCO₃(s) + H₂SO₄(aq) → ZnSO₄(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g)↑
   CaCO₃(s) + 2HCl(aq) → CaCl₂(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g)↑
5. **Method 5 – Titration (Soluble Alkali + Dilute Acid — Na⁺, K⁺, NH₄⁺ salts ke liye):**
   Kyunki NaOH, KOH aur NH₄OH paani mein poori tarah ghulansheel hain, unke excess ko filter karke alag nahi kiya ja sakta. Isliye indicator ki madad se exact neutralisation point tak **titration** karte hain:
   NaOH(aq) + HCl(aq) → NaCl(aq) + H₂O(l)
   2KOH(aq) + H₂SO₄(aq) → K₂SO₄(aq) + 2H₂O(l)
6. **Method 6 – Precipitation / Double Decomposition (Insoluble Salts Banane Ke Liye):**
   Jab koi **insoluble salt** (BaSO₄, AgCl, PbSO₄, CaCO₃) banana ho, to **do soluble salts ke ghol** aapas mein milaye jaate hain. Ions ki adla-badli ([chemical-changes-and-reactions-part3.md](../../../class9/chemistry/chemical-changes-and-reactions/chemical-changes-and-reactions-part3.md)) hoti hai aur insoluble salt turant **precipitate (↓)** bankar neeche baith jaata hai:
   BaCl₂(aq) + Na₂SO₄(aq) → BaSO₄(s)↓ (White ppt) + 2NaCl(aq)
   AgNO₃(aq) + NaCl(aq) → AgCl(s)↓ (Curdy white ppt) + NaNO₃(aq)
   Pb(NO₃)₂(aq) + Na₂SO₄(aq) → PbSO₄(s)↓ (White ppt) + 2NaNO₃(aq)

## Part D: Solved Examples

### Example 1. Parent Acid, Parent Base, Family aur Aqueous pH Pehchanna

**Sawaal:** Neeche diye gaye chaar salts ke liye unka (a) Parent Acid, (b) Parent Base, (c) Common Family, aur (d) Aqueous solution ka nature (pH = 7, < 7 ya > 7) batao:
1. Potassium sulphate (K₂SO₄)
2. Ammonium sulphate ((NH₄)₂SO₄)
3. Copper(II) sulphate (CuSO₄)
4. Sodium carbonate (Na₂CO₃)

**Hal:**
1. **K₂SO₄:** Cation K⁺ → Parent Base = **KOH (Strong base)**; Anion SO₄²⁻ → Parent Acid = **H₂SO₄ (Strong acid)**. Strong acid + Strong base → **Neutral ghol (pH = 7)**.
2. **(NH₄)₂SO₄:** Cation NH₄⁺ → Parent Base = **NH₄OH (Weak base)**; Anion SO₄²⁻ → Parent Acid = **H₂SO₄ (Strong acid)**. Strong acid + Weak base → **Acidic ghol (pH < 7)**.
3. **CuSO₄:** Cation Cu²⁺ → Parent Base = **Cu(OH)₂ (Weak base)**; Anion SO₄²⁻ → Parent Acid = **H₂SO₄ (Strong acid)**. Strong acid + Weak base → **Acidic ghol (pH < 7)**.
4. **Na₂CO₃:** Cation Na⁺ → Parent Base = **NaOH (Strong base)**; Anion CO₃²⁻ → Parent Acid = **H₂CO₃ (Weak acid)**. Weak acid + Strong base → **Basic ghol (pH > 7)**.
- **Common Family:** Pehle teeno salts (K₂SO₄, (NH₄)₂SO₄, CuSO₄) mein SO₄²⁻ ion common hai, isliye ye **Sulphate salts family** ke sadasya hain.

**Jaanch:** Har formula mein kul charge zero hai (K₂SO₄: 2(+1)+(-2)=0; CuSO₄: (+2)+(-2)=0), aur strong parent hi pH tay karta hai ✅.

### Example 2. Precipitation Reaction se Insoluble Salt ki Matra

**Sawaal:** Mr. Dandekar ne ek teacher demo mein (barium ke compounds zeherile hote hain, isliye student inhe nahi chhoote) **4.16 g Barium chloride (BaCl₂)** ko paani mein ghol kar usmein excess Sodium sulphate (Na₂SO₄) solution milaya.
1. Is reaction ki balanced chemical equation likho aur batao ye kis prakar ka salt preparation method hai.
2. Kitne gram safed precipitate (BaSO₄) banega, aur ghol mein kitne gram Sodium chloride (NaCl) banega?
*(Atomic masses: Ba = 137 u, Cl = 35.5 u, Na = 23 u, S = 32 u, O = 16 u)*

**Hal:**
- **Step 1 (Balanced Chemical Equation aur Method):**
  BaCl₂(aq) + Na₂SO₄(aq) → BaSO₄(s)↓ + 2NaCl(aq)
  Ye **Precipitation (Double Decomposition)** method hai kyunki do soluble salts milkar ek insoluble salt (BaSO₄) ka safed precipitate bana rahe hain.
- **Step 2 (Formula Unit Masses):**
  - BaCl₂ = 137 + 2(35.5) = 137 + 71 = 208 u
  - Na₂SO₄ = 2(23) + 32 + 4(16) = 46 + 32 + 64 = 142 u
  - BaSO₄ = 137 + 32 + 4(16) = 137 + 32 + 64 = 233 u
  - 2NaCl = 2 × (23 + 35.5) = 2 × 58.5 = 117 u
- **Step 3 (Mass Calculation):**
  - 208 g BaCl₂ ke moles: 4.16 g ÷ 208 g/mol = 0.02 mol.
  - Banne wala BaSO₄ = 0.02 × 233 = **4.66 g**.
  - Banne wala NaCl = 0.02 × 117 = **2.34 g**.

**Jaanch:** Reacted Na₂SO₄ = 0.02 × 142 = 2.84 g. Kul reactants = 4.16 g + 2.84 g = 7.00 g; Kul products = 4.66 g + 2.34 g = 7.00 g (Conservation of mass satya hai) ✅.

### Example 3. Insoluble Metal Oxide se Soluble Salt Banana

**Sawaal:** Lab mein pure Copper(II) sulphate (CuSO₄) banane ke liye **3.975 g black Copper(II) oxide (CuO)** ko exact matra wale dilute Sulphuric acid (H₂SO₄) ke saath garam kiya gaya.
1. Balanced equation likho aur batao Cu metal ko seedha dilute H₂SO₄ mein daal kar ye salt kyun nahi banaya gaya?
2. Kitne gram anhydrous CuSO₄ aur kitne gram paani (H₂O) banega?
*(Atomic masses: Cu = 63.5 u, O = 16 u, H = 1 u, S = 32 u)*

**Hal:**
- **Step 1 (Equation aur Kaaran):**
  CuO(s) + H₂SO₄(aq) → CuSO₄(aq) + H₂O(l)
  Copper (Cu) reactivity series mein hydrogen se neeche aata hai, isliye wo dilute H₂SO₄ se H₂ gas displace nahi kar sakta. Isliye hum insoluble oxide (CuO) + dilute acid wala method lagate hain.
- **Step 2 (Formula Unit Masses):**
  - CuO = 63.5 + 16 = 79.5 u
  - H₂SO₄ = 2(1) + 32 + 4(16) = 98 u
  - CuSO₄ = 63.5 + 32 + 4(16) = 159.5 u
  - H₂O = 2(1) + 16 = 18 u
- **Step 3 (Mass Calculation):**
  - CuO ke moles = 3.975 ÷ 79.5 = 0.05 mol.
  - Banne wala CuSO₄ = 0.05 × 159.5 = **7.975 g**.
  - Banne wala H₂O = 0.05 × 18 = **0.90 g**.

**Jaanch:** Reacted H₂SO₄ = 0.05 × 98 = 4.90 g. Reactants ka kul mass = 3.975 + 4.90 = 8.875 g; Products ka kul mass = 7.975 + 0.90 = 8.875 g ✅.

## Aam galtiyan (bachke rehna)

1. **Galti 1: "Har salt neutral (pH = 7) hota hai" maan lena.**
   Sirf **Strong Acid + Strong Base** ke salts (NaCl, KNO₃, Na₂SO₄) neutral hote hain! NH₄Cl aur CuSO₄ ka ghol **acidic (pH < 7)** hota hai, jabki Na₂CO₃, NaHCO₃ aur CH₃COONa ka ghol **basic (pH > 7)** hota hai.
2. **Galti 2: "Acid salt" (NaHCO₃) aur "Salt giving acidic solution" (NH₄Cl) ko ek hi cheez samajh lena.**
   - **NaHCO₃** apne formula ke hisaab se ek **Acid salt** hai (kyunki usmein H₂CO₃ ka ek H bacha hai), par paani mein uska ghol **mildly basic (pH ≈ 8.3)** hota hai!
   - **NH₄Cl** aur **CuSO₄** apne formula ke hisaab se **Normal salts** hain (koi replaceable H⁺ nahi), par paani mein unka ghol **acidic (pH < 7)** hota hai!
3. **Galti 3: CH₃COONa mein 3 hydrogen dekh kar use Acid salt likh dena.**
   CH₃COOH mein sirf -COOH wala 1 hydrogen replaceable tha jo Na⁺ se hat chuka hai; CH₃– ke 3 hydrogen ionisable nahi hote. Isliye CH₃COONa ek **Normal salt** hai!
4. **Galti 4: Copper (Cu) + dilute H₂SO₄ se CuSO₄ + H₂ bana dena.**
   Copper dilute HCl ya dilute H₂SO₄ se H₂ displace nahi karta! CuSO₄ banane ke liye **CuO + dilute H₂SO₄** ya **CuCO₃ + dilute H₂SO₄** ka upyog karte hain.
5. **Galti 5: Insoluble PbCO₃ ya PbO par seedha dilute H₂SO₄ daal kar PbSO₄ banana.**
   Aisa karne par PbCO₃ ke upar insoluble PbSO₄ ki sakht parat (coating) jam jaati hai jo aage abhikriya rok deti hai! Sahi tareeka 2-step hai: pehle dilute HNO₃ daal kar soluble Pb(NO₃)₂ banao, phir usmein Na₂SO₄ mila kar PbSO₄↓ precipitate karo.

## Quick Recap

- **Salt** mein **Basic radical (Cation)** parent base se aur **Acidic radical (Anion)** parent acid se aata hai.
- **Family of Salts:** Common positive ya negative ion wale salts ek hi parivaar ke hote hain (jaise Sodium salts, Sulphate salts, Chloride salts).
- **pH of Salt Solutions:**
  - Strong Acid + Strong Base → Neutral (pH = 7) (NaCl, KNO₃, Na₂SO₄).
  - Strong Acid + Weak Base → Acidic (pH < 7) (NH₄Cl, CuSO₄, AlCl₃, ZnSO₄).
  - Weak Acid + Strong Base → Basic (pH > 7) (Na₂CO₃, NaHCO₃, CH₃COONa).
- **Types of Salts:** **Normal salt** (complete replacement of H⁺, e.g., NaCl, Na₂SO₄), **Acid salt** (partial replacement of H⁺, e.g., NaHSO₄, NaHCO₃), aur **Basic salt** (partial replacement of OH⁻, e.g., Pb(OH)Cl).
- **Preparation of Salts:** Soluble salts bante hain Active Metal / Insoluble Oxide / Carbonate + Dilute Acid se ya Alkali + Acid **Titration** se; jabki Insoluble salts (BaSO₄, AgCl, PbSO₄) hamesha do soluble salt solutions ke **Precipitation (Double Displacement)** se banaye jaate hain.

## Practice Questions

1. Define a "Family of Salts". Group the following six salts into two distinct families based on their common ions: NaCl, Na₂SO₄, K₂SO₄, NaNO₃, CaSO₄, MgSO₄.
2. Predict whether the aqueous solutions of the following salts will be acidic (pH < 7), basic (pH > 7), or neutral (pH = 7), and write the formula of the parent acid and parent base for each:
   (a) Ammonium nitrate (NH₄NO₃)
   (b) Potassium carbonate (K₂CO₃)
   (c) Sodium nitrate (NaNO₃)
3. Distinguish between a Normal Salt and an Acid Salt with one balanced chemical equation showing the formation of each when Sulphuric acid (H₂SO₄) reacts with Sodium hydroxide (NaOH).
4. A student claims: *"Every acid salt gives an acidic solution in water (pH < 7), and every normal salt gives a neutral solution (pH = 7)."* Disprove both parts of this statement using suitable chemical examples.
5. Why is Sodium acetate (CH₃COONa) classified as a Normal salt even though its formula contains three hydrogen atoms? What will be the effect of its aqueous solution on red and blue litmus papers?
6. Suggest the best laboratory method and write the balanced chemical equation to prepare each of the following salts:
   (a) Zinc sulphate (ZnSO₄) from Zinc metal
   (b) Silver chloride (AgCl) from Silver nitrate (AgNO₃)
   (c) Potassium sulphate (K₂SO₄) from Potassium hydroxide (KOH)
7. How will you prepare insoluble Lead(II) sulphate (PbSO₄) starting from insoluble Lead(II) oxide (PbO)? Explain why PbO cannot be reacted directly with dilute H₂SO₄ to obtain a pure sample of PbSO₄, and write the balanced equations for the correct two-step process.
8. When a solution containing **3.40 g of Silver nitrate (AgNO₃)** is mixed with an excess of Sodium chloride (NaCl) solution, a curdy white precipitate of Silver chloride (AgCl) is formed along with Sodium nitrate (NaNO₃) in solution. Calculate the mass of AgCl precipitated and the mass of NaNO₃ formed. (Ag = 108 u, N = 14 u, O = 16 u, Na = 23 u, Cl = 35.5 u)
9. Calculate the mass of Zinc carbonate (ZnCO₃) required to react completely with dilute Sulphuric acid (H₂SO₄) to produce **6.44 g of anhydrous Zinc sulphate (ZnSO₄)**. Also calculate the mass of CO₂ gas evolved in this reaction. (Zn = 65 u, C = 12 u, O = 16 u, S = 32 u, H = 1 u)
10. Three unlabelled test tubes X, Y, and Z contain aqueous solutions of NaCl, ZnSO₄, and Na₂CO₃ (all colourless; not necessarily in that order). When a few drops of Universal Indicator are added to each:
    - Tube X turns blue (pH ≈ 10.5).
    - Tube Y turns green (pH = 7.0).
    - Tube Z turns orange (pH ≈ 4.5).
    Identify the salt present in X, Y, and Z, and write the chemical equation for what happens when dilute HCl is added to the solution in Tube X.

### Answers

1. Jin salts mein ek hi positive radical (cation) ya ek hi negative radical (anion) common hota hai, unhein ek **Family of Salts** kaha jaata hai.
   - **Sodium salts family** (Na⁺ ion common): NaCl, Na₂SO₄, NaNO₃.
   - **Sulphate salts family** (SO₄²⁻ ion common): Na₂SO₄, K₂SO₄, CaSO₄, MgSO₄. *(Dhyan dein: Na₂SO₄ dono parivaaron ka sadasya hai!)*

2. (a) **NH₄NO₃:** Parent Acid = HNO₃ (Strong acid), Parent Base = NH₄OH (Weak base). Strong acid + Weak base hone ke kaaran iska aqueous solution **Acidic (pH < 7)** hoga.
   (b) **K₂CO₃:** Parent Acid = H₂CO₃ (Weak acid), Parent Base = KOH (Strong base). Weak acid + Strong base hone ke kaaran iska aqueous solution **Basic (pH > 7)** hoga.
   (c) **NaNO₃:** Parent Acid = HNO₃ (Strong acid), Parent Base = NaOH (Strong base). Dono strong hone ke kaaran iska aqueous solution **Neutral (pH = 7)** hoga.

3. - **Acid Salt:** Jab kisi polybasic acid (jaise H₂SO₄) ke replaceable H⁺ ions ka **partial (adhoora) replacement** hota hai, to **Acid salt** banta hai:
     H₂SO₄(aq) + NaOH(aq) (insufficient) → NaHSO₄(aq) (Sodium hydrogensulphate — Acid salt) + H₂O(l)
   - **Normal Salt:** Jab acid ke **saare replaceable H⁺ ions** poori tarah replace ho jaate hain, to **Normal salt** banta hai:
     H₂SO₄(aq) + 2NaOH(aq) → Na₂SO₄(aq) (Sodium sulphate — Normal salt) + 2H₂O(l)

4. Vidyarthi ke dono daave galat hain:
   - **Har acid salt ka ghol acidic nahi hota:** Sodium hydrogencarbonate (NaHCO₃) formula ke hisaab se ek **Acid salt** hai (kyunki H₂CO₃ ka ek H⁺ bacha hai), par paani mein iska ghol **mildly basic (pH ≈ 8.3)** hota hai kyunki iska parent base (NaOH) strong hai aur parent acid (H₂CO₃) weak hai.
   - **Har normal salt ka ghol neutral nahi hota:** Ammonium chloride (NH₄Cl) aur Copper(II) sulphate (CuSO₄) **Normal salts** hain, par paani mein inka ghol **acidic (pH < 7)** hota hai. Isi tarah Na₂CO₃ ek **Normal salt** hai par uska ghol **basic (pH > 7)** hota hai.

5. Acetic acid (CH₃COOH) ek monobasic acid hai jisme **sirf ek replaceable H⁺ ion** (–COOH group wala) hota hai. Baaki teen hydrogen atoms (CH₃– group wale) carbon se covalently jude hote hain aur paani mein ionise nahi hote. Jab wo akela replaceable H⁺ ion Na⁺ se hat jaata hai, to complete replacement hone ke kaaran CH₃COONa ek **Normal salt** kehlata hai.
   Kyunki ye weak acid (CH₃COOH) aur strong base (NaOH) se bana hai, iska aqueous ghol **basic (pH > 7)** hota hai — ye **laal litmus ko neela** kar dega aur neele litmus par koi asar nahi daalega.

6. (a) **ZnSO₄ (Soluble salt):** **Active Metal + Dilute Acid (Displacement)** method:
   Zn(s) + H₂SO₄(aq) → ZnSO₄(aq) + H₂(g)↑
   (b) **AgCl (Insoluble salt):** **Precipitation (Double Displacement)** method (do soluble ghol mila kar):
   AgNO₃(aq) + NaCl(aq) → AgCl(s)↓ (White ppt) + NaNO₃(aq)
   (c) **K₂SO₄ (Soluble alkali salt):** **Acid-Base Titration (Neutralisation)** method:
   2KOH(aq) + H₂SO₄(aq) → K₂SO₄(aq) + 2H₂O(l)

7. Agar hum insoluble PbO par seedha dilute H₂SO₄ daalenge, to PbO ke tukdon ke upar insoluble PbSO₄ ki ek sakht parat (coating) ban jaayegi jo andar bache PbO ko acid se milne hi nahi degi, aur abhikriya adhoori ruk jaayegi.
   **Sahi 2-Step Tareeka:**
   - **Step 1:** Pehle insoluble PbO ko dilute Nitric acid (HNO₃) mein ghol kar **soluble Lead(II) nitrate (Pb(NO₃)₂)** banao:
     PbO(s) + 2HNO₃(aq) → Pb(NO₃)₂(aq) + H₂O(l)
   - **Step 2:** Ab is soluble Pb(NO₃)₂ ghol mein Sodium sulphate (Na₂SO₄) ya dilute H₂SO₄ mila kar safed PbSO₄ precipitate kar lo:
     Pb(NO₃)₂(aq) + Na₂SO₄(aq) → PbSO₄(s)↓ (White ppt) + 2NaNO₃(aq)

8. Balanced Equation: AgNO₃(aq) + NaCl(aq) → AgCl(s)↓ + NaNO₃(aq)
   - Formula Unit Masses: AgNO₃ = 108 + 14 + 48 = 170 u; NaCl = 23 + 35.5 = 58.5 u; AgCl = 108 + 35.5 = 143.5 u; NaNO₃ = 23 + 14 + 48 = 85 u.
   - Moles of AgNO₃ = 3.40 ÷ 170 = 0.02 mol.
   - Mass of AgCl precipitated = 0.02 × 143.5 = **2.87 g**.
   - Mass of NaNO₃ formed = 0.02 × 85 = **1.70 g** ✅ (Jaanch: Reacted NaCl = 0.02 × 58.5 = 1.17 g; Reactants 3.40 + 1.17 = 4.57 g = Products 2.87 + 1.70 = 4.57 g ✅).

9. Balanced Equation: ZnCO₃(s) + H₂SO₄(aq) → ZnSO₄(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g)↑
   - Formula Unit Masses: ZnCO₃ = 65 + 12 + 48 = 125 u; ZnSO₄ = 65 + 32 + 64 = 161 u; CO₂ = 12 + 32 = 44 u.
   - Moles of ZnSO₄ desired = 6.44 ÷ 161 = 0.04 mol.
   - Mass of ZnCO₃ required = 0.04 × 125 = **5.00 g**.
   - Mass of CO₂ gas evolved = 0.04 × 44 = **1.76 g** ✅ (Jaanch: Reacted H₂SO₄ = 0.04 × 98 = 3.92 g, H₂O = 0.04 × 18 = 0.72 g; Reactants 5.00 + 3.92 = 8.92 g = Products 6.44 + 0.72 + 1.76 = 8.92 g ✅).

10. - **Tube X (pH ≈ 10.5, Blue — Basic):** Ismein **Sodium carbonate (Na₂CO₃)** hai, jo weak acid (H₂CO₃) aur strong base (NaOH) ka salt hai.
    - **Tube Y (pH = 7.0, Green — Neutral):** Ismein **Sodium chloride (NaCl)** hai, jo strong acid (HCl) aur strong base (NaOH) ka salt hai.
    - **Tube Z (pH ≈ 4.5, Orange — Acidic):** Ismein **Zinc sulphate (ZnSO₄)** hai, jo strong acid (H₂SO₄) aur weak base (Zn(OH)₂) ka salt hai.
    - **Tube X (Na₂CO₃) mein dilute HCl daalne par:** CO₂ gas ke tez bulbule (brisk effervescence) niklenge:
      Na₂CO₃(aq) + 2HCl(aq) → 2NaCl(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g)↑
