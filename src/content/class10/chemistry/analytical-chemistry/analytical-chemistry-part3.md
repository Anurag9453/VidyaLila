# Topic 3: Ammonium Hydroxide Aur Amphoteric Swabhav

Topic 2 mein humne ek hi reagent, NaOH, se saat ions ke rang dekhe, aur ye bhi ki Zn²⁺, Pb²⁺ aur Al³⁺ ke safed precipitate excess NaOH mein ghul jaate hain. Ab hum dusra alkali lete hain: **ammonium hydroxide, NH₄OH**. Wahi saat ions, wahi sawaal: boond-boond daalne par kya dikhta hai, aur zyada daalne par kya? Aaj ke jawab kuch alag honge, aur unhe milakar hum ek **"kaun sa ion?" wala nirnay-table** banayenge.

## Kile ka do-zubaan wala guide

Mr. Dhumal apne bhanje Ameya ko Sahyadri ke ek purane kile par ghumane le gaye. Gate par do tourist groups ek saath pahunche: ek Hindi bolne wala parivaar aur ek Marathi bolne wala school-group. Kile ka guide dono ke beech khada tha. Hindi walon se wo saaf Hindi mein baat karta, Marathi walon se utni hi saaf Marathi mein. Ek hi insaan, do taraf ke log, aur dono ko wo apna lagta tha.

Ameya ne dheere se poocha, "Mama, ye to dono se baat kar leta hai. Aisa kaise?" Mr. Dhumal muskuraye. "Kyunki iske paas **do zubaan** hain. Jab jis taraf ka aadmi aata hai, ye usi ki zubaan bol leta hai. Chemistry mein bhi kuch precipitate aise hote hain: **acid ke saath base ki tarah** behave karte hain aur **alkali ke saath acid ki tarah**. Bas, wo bhi do zubaan jaante hain."

"Aur Mama, saare precipitate aisa karte hain?" Mr. Dhumal ne sir hilaya. "Nahi. Koi sirf ek zubaan samajhta hai, aur kuch ko ek alkali ki zubaan aati hai doosre ki nahi. Aaj hum yahi dekhenge ki **NH₄OH ki zubaan kise aati hai aur kise nahi**."

```
   Hindi wale  --->  [ GUIDE ]  <---  Marathi wale
                      ek hi insaan,
                      do zubaan

   acid        --->  [ Zn(OH)₂, Al(OH)₃, Pb(OH)₂ ]  <---  alkali
                      ek hi precipitate,
                      do swabhav (amphoteric)
```

> **Figure 3.1** – Guide ek hi insaan hai par dono taraf ke logon se unki zubaan mein baat karta hai. Amphoteric hydroxide bhi ek hi padarth hai: acid se milne par base jaisa, alkali se milne par acid jaisa. Ye sirf ek analogy hai.

## Part A: NH₄OH aur precipitate: weak alkali bhi kaam kar jaata hai

**Safety pehle (strict).** Ye saare tests **school lab mein teacher ke saath** (goggles aur gloves ke saath, teacher boond-boond dalta hai) ya **sirf dekhne ke liye (teacher demo)** hain. NH₄OH se **chubhne wali gas** nikalti hai: use fume cupboard mein ya khuli hawa mein rakha jaata hai, kabhi mooh ke paas nahi sungha jaata (teacher haath se halka sa "waft" dikhata hai: sirf padhne ke liye). Copper ke namak nigalne par nuksaan karte hain. Koi bhi student ye ghar par ya akele nahi karta, kuch chakhta nahi, aur bacha hua ghol naali mein nahi daalta (heavy metal ka kachra teacher sambhalta hai).

Topic 1 (Figure 1.3) mein dekha tha ki NH₄OH ek **weak alkali** hai (Chapter 4 ka weak electrolyte), isliye uske ghol mein **OH⁻ kam** hote hain, phir bhi Cu²⁺ aur Fe³⁺ ka precipitate ban jaata hai. Aaj ye baat saaton ions par lagate hain.

Yaad karo kyun: ye metal hydroxide itne kam ghulte hain ki thode OH⁻ bhi Cu²⁺ ya Fe³⁺ ko pakad kar jama dene ke liye kaafi hain. Ek ion ek-ek OH⁻ se milta jaata hai aur precipitate aahista-aahista jam jaata hai. Sirf **Ca(OH)₂** thoda sa ghulta hai (Chapter 1 ka chuna-paani yaad karo), isliye Ca²⁺ ko jamane ke liye zyada OH⁻ chahiye, jo kamzor NH₄OH nahi de paata. Isliye Ca²⁺ ke saath precipitate **nahi banta ya bahut halka banta hai**.

Neeche ke equation mein dekho kya hota hai. Metal ke saath jo ion (Cl⁻, NO₃⁻) tha, wo nahi badalta; NH₄OH ka NH₄⁺ uska sathi ban jaata hai.

### Example 1. CuCl₂ + NH₄OH: pehla stage

(Copper ke namak harmful hain, sirf dekhne ke liye.) Teacher CuCl₂ ke neela-hara ghol mein NH₄OH ki kuch boond daalta hai. **Halka neela (pale blue) precipitate** neeche baith jaata hai.

Molecular equation: CuCl₂ + 2NH₄OH → Cu(OH)₂↓ + 2NH₄Cl
Ionic equation: Cu²⁺ + 2NH₄OH → Cu(OH)₂↓ + 2NH₄⁺

Atom ginti (ionic): Cu 1 = 1, N 2 = 2, H 10 = 2 + 8 = 10, O 2 = 2 ✅. Charge: +2 = 0 + 2 × (+1) = +2 ✅. Cl⁻ spectator ion hai (Topic 1): wo dono taraf wahi rehta hai.

**Jaanch:** molecular equation mein Cu 1 = 1, Cl 2 = 2, N 2 = 2, H 10 = 2 + 8 = 10, O 2 = 2 ✅.

### Example 2. Fe(NO₃)₃ + NH₄OH

Teacher Fe(NO₃)₃ ke peele-bhure ghol mein NH₄OH daalta hai. **Reddish-brown (laal-bhura) precipitate Fe(OH)₃** banta hai, aur **excess NH₄OH mein bhi nahi ghulta**.

Fe(NO₃)₃ + 3NH₄OH → Fe(OH)₃↓ + 3NH₄NO₃
Ionic: Fe³⁺ + 3NH₄OH → Fe(OH)₃↓ + 3NH₄⁺

**Jaanch:** Fe 1 = 1, N 3 + 3 = 6 = 6, O 9 + 3 = 12 = 3 + 9 = 12, H 15 = 3 + 12 = 15 ✅. Charge (ionic): +3 = 3 × (+1) ✅.

Fe²⁺ wale kisi bhi ghulansheel namak ke saath NH₄OH **dirty green (gande hare) Fe(OH)₂** deta hai, jo excess mein bhi nahi ghulta. Tabhi Topic 2 ki tarah **Fe²⁺ aur Fe³⁺ ko rang se alag** pehchana jaata hai.

### Example 3. CaCl₂ + NH₄OH: lagbhag kuch nahi

CaCl₂ ke ghol mein NH₄OH daalne par **precipitate nahi banta, ya sirf bahut halki dhundh** aati hai, aur excess mein koi badlav nahi. (NaOH ke saath yahi Ca²⁺ ek halka safed precipitate deta tha, Topic 2.) Equation: CaCl₂ + 2NH₄OH ⇌ Ca(OH)₂ + 2NH₄Cl, par ye bahut kam hota hai kyunki Ca(OH)₂ ghulansheel sa hai.

**Jaanch:** Ca 1 = 1, Cl 2 = 2, N 2 = 2, H 10 = 2 + 8 = 10, O 2 = 2 ✅. Isi wajah se NH₄OH Ca²⁺ ko baaki ions se alag karne mein kaam aata hai.

### Example 4. Kitna precipitate? (ek chhota hisaab)

13.45 g CuCl₂ ke ghol mein zaroorat jitna NH₄OH daala gaya. Kitna Cu(OH)₂ banega? (Cu 63.5, Cl 35.5, O 16, H 1, N 14.)

CuCl₂ = 63.5 + 71 = 134.5 g/mol. Moles = 13.45 ÷ 134.5 = **0.1 mol**. Equation CuCl₂ + 2NH₄OH → Cu(OH)₂ + 2NH₄Cl mein 1 : 1, to Cu(OH)₂ = 0.1 mol. Cu(OH)₂ = 63.5 + 34 = 97.5 g/mol. Mass = 0.1 × 97.5 = **9.75 g**.

**Jaanch:** 2NH₄OH = 0.2 mol × 35 = 7.0 g, NH₄Cl = 0.2 × 53.5 = 10.7 g. Reactants 13.45 + 7.0 = 20.45 g, products 9.75 + 10.7 = 20.45 g ✅.

## Part B: Do khaas natije: gehra neela aur rangheen

Ab wo hissa jahan NH₄OH ka swabhav NaOH se alag dikhta hai: **excess**.

### Cu²⁺: halka neela precipitate, phir inky (gehra) neela ghol

Pehle kuch boond daalo to Cu(OH)₂ ka halka neela precipitate. Ab NH₄OH daalte raho: precipitate **ghulne lagta hai** aur ghol **gehra (inky) neela** ho jaata hai. Ye rang ek naye ion ka hai: **[Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺**, ek **complex ion** (aage ki class mein). NaOH ke excess mein Cu(OH)₂ nahi ghulta tha (Topic 2): yahi NH₄OH ki apni pehchaan hai.

### Zn²⁺: safed precipitate, phir rangheen (colourless) ghol

ZnCl₂ ya Zn(NO₃)₂ ke ghol mein kuch boond NH₄OH daalne par **safed Zn(OH)₂** banta hai; excess mein wo ghul kar **rangheen ghol** deta hai. Ye ghol ek aur complex ion **[Zn(NH₃)₄]²⁺** ka hai (complex ion, aage ki class mein).

```
   Tube A              Tube B              Tube C
   :              :    :              :    :              :
   :  CuCl₂ ghol  :    : kuch boond   :    :  zyada NH₄OH :
   :  neela-hara  :    : NH₄OH, ghol  :    :  ghol: gehra :
   :              :    : dhundla      :    :  neela (inky):
   :              :    :              :    :              :
   :              :    :  ##########  :    :  (ppt nahi)  :
   '--------------'    '--------------'    '--------------'
   Cu²⁺ ghol           ppt bana           ppt ghul gaya
```

> **Figure 3.2** – Cu²⁺ ke saath NH₄OH: Tube A shuru ka ghol, Tube B mein kuch boond ke baad halka neela precipitate (# nishan) tube ke neeche, Tube C mein excess NH₄OH ke baad precipitate ghul gaya aur ghol gehra neela hai.

```
   Tube P              Tube Q              Tube R
   :              :    :              :    :              :
   :  ZnCl₂ ghol  :    : kuch boond   :    :  zyada NH₄OH :
   :  rangheen    :    : NH₄OH, ghol  :    :  ghol saaf,  :
   :              :    : safed dhundla:    :  rangheen    :
   :              :    :              :    :              :
   :              :    :  ##########  :    :  (ppt nahi)  :
   '--------------'    '--------------'    '--------------'
   Zn²⁺ ghol           safed ppt          ppt ghul gaya
```

> **Figure 3.3** – Zn²⁺ ke saath NH₄OH: Tube Q mein safed precipitate neeche (# nishan), Tube R mein excess ke baad precipitate ghul kar rangheen (colourless) saaf ghol.

### Example 5. Zn(NO₃)₂ ke saath poora tareeka

Teacher ek saaf test tube mein Zn(NO₃)₂ ka ghol leta hai, **dropper se kuch boond** NH₄OH daalta hai: safed precipitate. Phir **boond-boond** karke zyada daalta hai: precipitate ghul jaata hai.

Pehla stage: Zn(NO₃)₂ + 2NH₄OH → Zn(OH)₂↓ + 2NH₄NO₃. Ionic: Zn²⁺ + 2NH₄OH → Zn(OH)₂↓ + 2NH₄⁺.
Dusra stage: Zn(OH)₂ ghul kar [Zn(NH₃)₄]²⁺ banata hai (complex ion, aage ki class mein): ghol rangheen.

**Jaanch:** Zn 1 = 1, N 2 + 2 = 4 = 4, O 6 + 2 = 8 = 2 + 6 = 8, H 10 = 2 + 8 = 10 ✅; charge (ionic) +2 = 2 × (+1) ✅.

Dhyan do: Cu²⁺ aur Zn²⁺ ko ek-saath yaad rakho. **Dono excess NH₄OH mein ghulte hain**, ek gehra neela ghol deta hai aur doosra rangheen ghol. Isi se do ion alag pehchane jaate hain.

## Part C: Pb²⁺ aur Al³⁺: NH₄OH ke saath twins

Topic 2 mein dekha tha ki Pb²⁺ aur Al³⁺ dono NaOH ke saath safed precipitate dete hain jo excess NaOH mein ghul jaata hai. NH₄OH ke saath bhi dono **safed precipitate** dete hain, aur dono **excess NH₄OH mein nahi ghulte**. Yaani NH₄OH inhe bhi **alag nahi kar pata**. Twin jaisi pehchaan!

**Lead acetate: zehreela (sirf dekhne ke liye).** Is topic mein lead ka ghulansheel namak **lead acetate, (CH₃COO)₂Pb** liya hai. Lead ke saare namak **zehreele** hain: koi student unhe haath nahi lagata, chakhta nahi, aur kachra teacher sambhalta hai. Aluminium ke liye **AlCl₃** lete hain.

(CH₃COO)₂Pb + 2NH₄OH → Pb(OH)₂↓ + 2CH₃COONH₄
AlCl₃ + 3NH₄OH → Al(OH)₃↓ + 3NH₄Cl

**Jaanch:** Pb: Pb 1 = 1, C 4 = 4, H 6 + 10 = 16 = 2 + 14 = 16, O 4 + 2 = 6 = 2 + 4 = 6, N 2 = 2 ✅. Al: Al 1 = 1, Cl 3 = 3, N 3 = 3, H 15 = 3 + 12 = 15, O 3 = 3 ✅.

```
   Tube 1              Tube 2              Tube 3
   :              :    :              :    :              :
   :   Pb ghol    :    :   Al ghol    :    :   Zn ghol    :
   :   + NH₄OH    :    :   + NH₄OH    :    :   + NH₄OH    :
   :    zyada     :    :    zyada     :    :    zyada     :
   :              :    :              :    :              :
   :  ##########  :    :  ##########  :    :  (ppt nahi)  :
   '--------------'    '--------------'    '--------------'
```

> **Figure 3.4** – Excess NH₄OH ke baad: Pb²⁺ (Tube 1) aur Al³⁺ (Tube 2) dono mein safed precipitate neeche (# nishan) bacha hai, jabki Zn²⁺ (Tube 3) mein precipitate ghul gaya. Pb aur Al ko NH₄OH se alag nahi kiya ja sakta, Zn ko kiya ja sakta hai.

### Example 6. Pb²⁺ aur Al³⁺ ko alag kaise karein?

Dono ke tube mein ek jaisa safed precipitate hai, to ek **alag test** chahiye. Lead ke ghol ki ek aur pehchaan hai (sirf padhne ke liye, teacher demo):

- Lead acetate ke ghol mein **dilute HCl** ki kuch boond: **safed precipitate PbCl₂**. (CH₃COO)₂Pb + 2HCl → PbCl₂↓ + 2CH₃COOH. AlCl₃ ke ghol mein koi precipitate nahi banta (Al ka chloride ghulansheel hai).
- Lead acetate mein **potassium iodide (KI)** ki boond: **chamkeela peela precipitate PbI₂** (Class 9 ka "golden rain" wala teacher demo yaad hai? Wahi PbI₂). (CH₃COO)₂Pb + 2KI → PbI₂↓ + 2CH₃COOK. Al ke ghol mein peela precipitate nahi aata.

Lead ke ye saare precipitate bhi zehreele hain, to sirf teacher ke haath se hote hain.
**Jaanch:** (CH₃COO)₂Pb + 2HCl: Pb 1 = 1, C 4 = 4, H 6 + 2 = 8 = 8, O 4 = 4, Cl 2 = 2 ✅. (CH₃COO)₂Pb + 2KI: Pb 1 = 1, K 2 = 2, I 2 = 2, C 4 = 4, H 6 = 6, O 4 = 4 ✅.

## Part D: Amphoteric swabhav: do zubaan, phir se

Chapter 2 ke Part E mein amphoteric **oxide** (Al₂O₃, ZnO) dekhe the: acid aur alkali dono se reaction. Wahi baat **hydroxide** par: **Zn(OH)₂, Al(OH)₃ aur Pb(OH)₂ amphoteric hydroxide** hain.

- **Acid ke saath base jaisa:** Zn(OH)₂ + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + 2H₂O; Al(OH)₃ + 3HCl → AlCl₃ + 3H₂O; Pb(OH)₂ + 2CH₃COOH → (CH₃COO)₂Pb + 2H₂O.
- **Strong alkali ke saath acid jaisa:** Zn(OH)₂ + 2NaOH → Na₂ZnO₂ + 2H₂O; Al(OH)₃ + NaOH → NaAlO₂ + 2H₂O; Pb(OH)₂ + 2NaOH → Na₂PbO₂ + 2H₂O.

**Jaanch:** Zn(OH)₂ + 2HCl: Zn 1, O 2, H 4 = 4, Cl 2 ✅. Al(OH)₃ + 3HCl: Al 1, O 3, H 6 = 6, Cl 3 ✅. Pb(OH)₂ + 2CH₃COOH: C 4, H 10 = 6 + 4, O 6 ✅. Zn(OH)₂ + 2NaOH: Na 2, Zn 1, O 4, H 4 ✅. Al(OH)₃ + NaOH: O 4 = 2 + 2, H 4 = 4 ✅. Pb(OH)₂ + 2NaOH: Na 2, Pb 1, O 4, H 4 ✅.

```
          acid (HCl)                        alkali (NaOH)
   ZnCl₂ + H₂O  <----  Zn(OH)₂  ---->  Na₂ZnO₂ + H₂O
   (base jaisa behave)               (acid jaisa behave)
```

> **Figure 3.5** – Amphoteric Zn(OH)₂: acid ke saath ZnCl₂ aur paani, strong alkali ke saath Na₂ZnO₂ aur paani. Ek hi padarth, do tarah ka behave.

### Example 7. Amphoteric hone ka matlab kya NH₄OH mein ghulna hai?

**Nahi.** Al(OH)₃ amphoteric hai, par **excess NH₄OH mein nahi ghulta**, kyunki NH₄OH ke OH⁻ itne kam hain ki wo acid jaisi zubaan sunne layak nahi (strong alkali chahiye). Zn(OH)₂ NH₄OH mein ghulta hai ek **alag wajah** se: wo complex ion banata hai (aage ki class mein). Cu(OH)₂ amphoteric nahi hai (NaOH ke excess mein nahi ghulta), phir bhi NH₄OH mein ghulta hai, ussi complex ion wali wajah se.

**Jaanch:** Table check: Al³⁺ ke excess NH₄OH mein ghulna "insoluble" hai ✅, Zn²⁺ "dissolves" ✅, Cu²⁺ "dissolves, deep blue" ✅.

## Part E: "Kaun sa ion?" wala nirnay-table

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ca²⁺ | safed ppt (halka) | nahi ghulta | ppt nahi ya bahut halka | koi badlav nahi |
| Fe²⁺ | dirty green ppt | nahi ghulta | dirty green ppt | nahi ghulta |
| Fe³⁺ | reddish-brown ppt | nahi ghulta | reddish-brown ppt | nahi ghulta |
| Cu²⁺ | halka neela ppt | nahi ghulta | halka neela ppt | ghulta hai, gehra (inky) neela ghol |
| Zn²⁺ | safed ppt | ghulta hai, rangheen ghol | safed ppt | ghulta hai, rangheen ghol |

```

   -------------------------------------------------------------------
   Ca²⁺  :  ppt nahi ya bahut halka  :  koi badlav nahi
   Fe²⁺  :  dirty green ppt          :  ppt bana rehta hai
   Fe³⁺  :  reddish-brown ppt        :  ppt bana rehta hai
   Cu²⁺  :  halka neela ppt          :  ppt ghulta, inky neela ghol
   Zn²⁺  :  safed ppt                :  ppt ghulta, rangheen ghol
   Pb²⁺  :  safed ppt                :  ppt bana rehta hai

```

> **Figure 3.6** – Saat ions ka NH₄OH-card: kuch boond aur excess mein kya dikhta hai. Do ion (Cu²⁺, Zn²⁺) excess mein ghulte hain, baaki nahi.

Ab ek **flowchart**: pehle NaOH se precipitate ka rang dekho, phir ruk-ruk kar sawaal poochho.

```
   Ghol + NaOH ki kuch boond: precipitate ka rang?
      :
      +--> reddish-brown       : Fe³⁺
      +--> dirty green         : Fe²⁺
      +--> halka neela         : Cu²⁺ (excess NH₄OH: gehra neela, pakka)
      +--> safed
             :
             +--> excess NaOH mein nahi ghula : Ca²⁺ (NH₄OH se kuch nahi)
             +--> excess NaOH mein ghul gaya  : Zn²⁺, Pb²⁺ ya Al³⁺
                    :
                    +--> excess NH₄OH mein ghul gaya  : Zn²⁺
                    +--> excess NH₄OH mein nahi ghula : Pb²⁺ ya Al³⁺

```

> **Figure 3.7** – Saat ions ka nirnay-flowchart. Rang se shuru karo, phir excess NaOH, phir excess NH₄OH, aur aakhir mein lead ke alag tests. Har raaste ke ant mein ek hi ion bachta hai.

### Example 8. Anjaan ghol ki pehchaan

Ek saaf rangheen ghol P (CaCl₂, ZnCl₂ ya AlCl₃ mein se koi ek) ke saath ye natije mile: NaOH ki kuch boond se safed precipitate, excess NaOH mein ghul gaya; NH₄OH ki kuch boond se safed gelatinous precipitate, excess NH₄OH mein nahi ghula. Kaun sa namak?

- Excess NaOH mein ghul gaya, to Ca²⁺ nahi (wo nahi ghulta).
- Excess NH₄OH mein nahi ghula, to Zn²⁺ nahi (wo ghulta hai).
- Bacha Al³⁺, aur gelatinous safed precipitate bhi Al(OH)₃ se mel khata hai: **AlCl₃**.

**Jaanch:** table ki Al³⁺ wali row: NaOH drops safed gelatinous ✅, excess NaOH ghulta ✅, NH₄OH drops safed ✅, excess NH₄OH nahi ghulta ✅.

## Aam galtiyan (bachke rehna)

**Galti: "NH₄OH excess mein har precipitate ko ghula deta hai."**
Ye galti isliye hoti hai ki NaOH ke excess mein teen precipitate ghulte the aur lagta hai ki "excess daalo to sab ghul jaata hai". Demonstration teen tube se:

1. **Tube 1 (Cu²⁺):** kuch boond NH₄OH, halka neela precipitate. Excess NH₄OH mein ghul gaya, gehra neela ghol. Yahan "ghulta hai" sahi hai.
2. **Tube 2 (Fe³⁺):** reddish-brown precipitate, excess NH₄OH mein **bacha rehta hai**, ghol saaf nahi hota.
3. **Tube 3 (Al³⁺):** safed gelatinous precipitate, excess mein **bacha rehta hai**. Aur dhyan do: Al(OH)₃ amphoteric hai, phir bhi nahi ghulta, kyunki NH₄OH weak alkali hai.

**Niyam:** NH₄OH ke excess mein **sirf do** ion ke precipitate ghulte hain: **Zn²⁺ (rangheen ghol)** aur **Cu²⁺ (gehra neela ghol)**. Pb²⁺, Al³⁺, Fe²⁺, Fe³⁺ nahi ghulte, aur Ca²⁺ mein pehle hi precipitate (lagbhag) nahi banta. Ye table mein "ghulta hai" likhne se pehle kabhi bhoolna nahi.

## Quick Recap

- **NH₄OH** weak alkali hai (OH⁻ kam, Chapter 4), phir bhi Cu²⁺, Fe²⁺, Fe³⁺, Zn²⁺, Pb²⁺, Al³⁺ ke hydroxide precipitate banata hai. Ca²⁺ ke saath precipitate nahi ya bahut halka.
- Ionic equation ka dhaancha: M²⁺ + 2NH₄OH → M(OH)₂↓ + 2NH₄⁺. Spectator ion (Cl⁻, NO₃⁻) nahi badalte.
- **Excess NH₄OH mein sirf do ghulte hain:** Cu²⁺ (gehra neela ghol, [Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺) aur Zn²⁺ (rangheen ghol, [Zn(NH₃)₄]²⁺). Complex ion aage ki class mein.
- **Fe²⁺, Fe³⁺, Pb²⁺, Al³⁺** ke precipitate excess NH₄OH mein nahi ghulte; Ca²⁺ mein koi badlav nahi.
- **Pb²⁺ aur Al³⁺** NaOH aur NH₄OH dono mein ek jaise: alag karne ke liye lead ke tests (HCl se safed PbCl₂, KI se peela PbI₂: sirf padhne ke liye, zehreela).
- **Amphoteric hydroxide:** Zn(OH)₂, Al(OH)₃, Pb(OH)₂ acid aur strong alkali dono se ghulte hain. NH₄OH mein ghulna alag baat hai.
- **Nirnay-table + flowchart:** rang, excess NaOH, excess NH₄OH, phir lead ke tests.

## Practice Questions

(Sab sawaal paper par hal karne ke liye hain; koi test student khud nahi karta.)

Use these values where needed: H 1, C 12, N 14, O 16, Al 27, Cl 35.5, Zn 65.

1. A teacher adds a few drops of NH₄OH to AlCl₃ solution and then excess. Write what is seen at each stage, and the molecular and ionic equations for the first stage.
2. A student says, "Zinc nitrate gives a white precipitate with NH₄OH, so it must behave like lead acetate." Do both give the same result in excess NH₄OH? Use the table to answer.
3. 9.45 g of Zn(NO₃)₂ reacts exactly with NH₄OH to form Zn(OH)₂ and NH₄NO₃. Find the mass of Zn(OH)₂ formed and of NH₄NO₃, and check using total mass (N 14, O 16, H 1, Zn 65).
4. Four colourless solutions are labelled W, X, Y, Z (ZnCl₂, CaCl₂, AlCl₃ and lead acetate, in some order). Few drops of NH₄OH: W gives no precipitate, the other three give white precipitates. Excess NH₄OH: X's precipitate dissolves; Y's and Z's remain. Dilute HCl: Y gives a white precipitate, Z gives none. Identify W, X, Y and Z.
5. State True or False and correct the false ones: (a) NH₄OH contains many OH⁻ ions, more than NaOH of the same strength. (b) Excess NH₄OH dissolves the precipitate formed by Fe³⁺. (c) Zn(OH)₂ reacts with both an acid and a strong alkali. (d) Ca²⁺ gives a heavy white precipitate with NH₄OH.
6. Why does a weak alkali like NH₄OH still precipitate Fe³⁺ but gives almost nothing with Ca²⁺? Answer in two or three sentences, using the idea of how little each hydroxide dissolves.
7. Solution R gives a pale blue precipitate with a few drops of NH₄OH, which dissolves in excess to give an inky blue solution. Name the metal ion, give the formula of the complex ion formed, and write one possible chloride salt of R.
8. Write the equations that show Al(OH)₃ is amphoteric (one with HCl, one with NaOH). Verify the atom count of each.
9. Which one of NaOH or NH₄OH can be used in excess to tell Zn²⁺ from Al³⁺? Explain the result seen with each reagent for both ions.
10. A student writes "CuCl₂ + 2NH₄OH → Cu(OH)₂ + NH₄Cl". Find the error by counting atoms, correct it, and write the ionic equation.
11. Draw a test-tube figure with the labels "Fe³⁺ solution", "few drops NH₄OH" and "excess NH₄OH" and show where the precipitate lies. What is the colour of the precipitate?
12. In the reaction of Fe(NO₃)₃ with NH₄OH, name the spectator ion and explain why it does not appear in the ionic equation.
13. List all the ions from the seven that give a precipitate with NH₄OH that does NOT dissolve in excess, and group them by colour.
14. Lead acetate solution is tested with dilute HCl in a teacher demo. State what is seen, write the equation, and give two safety rules for handling lead compounds.

### Answers

1. Kuch boond: **safed gelatinous precipitate Al(OH)₃**. Excess NH₄OH: **precipitate bana rehta hai** (nahi ghulta). Molecular: AlCl₃ + 3NH₄OH → Al(OH)₃↓ + 3NH₄Cl. Ionic: Al³⁺ + 3NH₄OH → Al(OH)₃↓ + 3NH₄⁺. Jaanch: Al 1, Cl 3, N 3, H 15 = 3 + 12, O 3 ✅; ionic charge +3 = 3 × (+1) ✅.
2. **Nahi.** Table: Zn²⁺ excess NH₄OH mein ghulta hai (rangheen ghol), Pb²⁺ nahi ghulta. Pehle stage mein dono safed precipitate dete hain, par excess mein alag. Jaanch: Zn "dissolves", Pb "insoluble" ✅.
3. Zn(NO₃)₂ = 65 + 2 × 62 = 189 g/mol, moles = 9.45 ÷ 189 = **0.05 mol**. Equation 1 : 1 : 2, to Zn(OH)₂ = 0.05 mol × 99 = **4.95 g**, NH₄NO₃ = 0.1 mol × 80 = **8.0 g**, NH₄OH = 0.1 × 35 = 3.5 g. Jaanch: 9.45 + 3.5 = 12.95 g aur 4.95 + 8.0 = 12.95 g ✅.
4. **W = CaCl₂** (NH₄OH se precipitate nahi). **X = ZnCl₂** (excess mein ghul gaya). Y aur Z mein se jisne HCl se safed precipitate diya wo lead acetate: **Y = lead acetate** (PbCl₂), **Z = AlCl₃**. Jaanch: Y ka precipitate excess NH₄OH mein bacha ✅, Z ke saath HCl se kuch nahi ✅.
5. (a) **False** ❌: NH₄OH weak alkali hai, OH⁻ kam hain. (b) **False** ❌: Fe(OH)₃ excess NH₄OH mein nahi ghulta. (c) **True** ✅: amphoteric hai. (d) **False** ❌: Ca²⁺ ke saath precipitate nahi ya bahut halka.
6. Fe(OH)₃ itna kam ghulta hai ki thode OH⁻ se bhi Fe³⁺ jam jaata hai, isliye precipitate banta hai. Ca(OH)₂ kuch ghulta hai, to wo jamne ke liye zyada OH⁻ maangta hai, jo weak NH₄OH nahi de paata. Isliye Ca²⁺ ke saath precipitate nahi ya halka.
7. Inky neela ghol excess NH₄OH mein, to metal ion **Cu²⁺**, complex ion **[Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺** (aage ki class mein). Ek chloride salt: **CuCl₂**. Jaanch: table ki Cu²⁺ row ✅.
8. Al(OH)₃ + 3HCl → AlCl₃ + 3H₂O (Al 1, O 3, H 6, Cl 3 dono taraf ✅). Al(OH)₃ + NaOH → NaAlO₂ + 2H₂O (Al 1, Na 1, O 4, H 4 dono taraf ✅). Dono se ghulta hai, to amphoteric.
9. **NH₄OH.** Zn²⁺ ka precipitate excess NH₄OH mein ghulta hai, Al³⁺ ka nahi. (NaOH ke excess mein dono ghulte hain, to NaOH se alag nahi hote.) Jaanch: Zn NaOH excess ghulta, NH₄OH excess ghulta; Al NaOH excess ghulta, NH₄OH excess nahi ✅.
10. Galat equation mein Cl: left 2, right 1; N: left 2, right 1; H: left 10, right 6. Sahi: CuCl₂ + 2NH₄OH → Cu(OH)₂ + 2NH₄Cl (Cu 1, Cl 2, N 2, H 10, O 2 ✅). Ionic: Cu²⁺ + 2NH₄OH → Cu(OH)₂↓ + 2NH₄⁺.
11. Figure: teen tube; pehle Fe³⁺ ghol, doosre mein kuch boond ke baad neeche precipitate, teesre mein excess ke baad bhi wahi precipitate neeche. Rang **reddish-brown**. Jaanch: Table ki Fe³⁺ row ✅.
12. Spectator ion **NO₃⁻**. Wo shuru aur ant dono mein ghol mein ghoomta hai aur reaction mein hissa nahi leta, isliye ionic equation mein nahi aata. Jaanch: NO₃⁻ 3 = 3 dono taraf ✅.
13. Excess mein nahi ghulte: **Fe²⁺ (dirty green)**, **Fe³⁺ (reddish-brown)**, **Pb²⁺ (safed)**, **Al³⁺ (safed gelatinous)**. Ca²⁺ ke saath to precipitate hi nahi (ya bahut halka) banta, aur Cu²⁺, Zn²⁺ excess mein ghul jaate hain.
14. Safed precipitate **PbCl₂** dikhta hai: (CH₃COO)₂Pb + 2HCl → PbCl₂↓ + 2CH₃COOH (Pb 1, C 4, H 8, O 4, Cl 2 dono taraf ✅). Safety: lead zehreela hai, chhuna ya chakhna nahi; kachra teacher sambhalta hai, naali mein nahi daala jaata; goggles aur gloves, teacher demo.
