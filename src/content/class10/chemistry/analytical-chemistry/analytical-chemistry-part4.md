# Topic 4: Anion Ki Pehchaan: Cl⁻, SO₄²⁻, CO₃²⁻ Aur NH₄⁺

Pichhle topics mein hum namak ke **cation** (positive ion) ko NaOH aur NH₄OH se pehchanna seekh chuke hain. Par har namak ke do hisse hote hain (Chapter 1): ek cation aur ek **anion** (negative ion). Aadhi pehchaan abhi baaki hai. Aaj hum anion dhoondhenge: **chloride Cl⁻, sulphate SO₄²⁻, carbonate CO₃²⁻**, aur ek positive ion ko bhi, jo bahut khaas tareeke se pakda jaata hai: **ammonium NH₄⁺**.

---

## Society ka gate aur har mehmaan ka apna check

Mr. Phansalkar apni bhatiji Prajakta ko saath lekar society ke gate par khade the. Ruk-ruk kar log andar aa rahe the, aur guard Bhimrao ji har kisi ko rok kar kuch na kuch check kar rahe the. Prajakta ne dhyan diya ki **har kisi se ek hi sawaal nahi poocha ja raha**.

Ek delivery wala aaya, to guard ne uske phone par aaya hua OTP flat ke resident se milaya. Phir ek mehmaan aaye, to guard ne intercom se flat mein phone karke poochha, "Aap ke yahan koi aaya hai?" Phir ek resident aaye, to guard ne unki gaadi par laga society ka sticker dekha. Prajakta hairan thi. "Chacha, guard sabko ek hi tarah kyun nahi check karte? Sabse OTP maang lete."

Mr. Phansalkar muskuraye. "Socho, agar mehmaan se OTP maangein to wo kya dega? Uske paas to koi OTP hai hi nahi. Aur resident ke sticker ko dekhne se delivery wale ki pehchaan nahi hogi. **Har kisam ke visitor ka apna fixed check** hota hai, aur wahi check us visitor ke liye **pakka saboot** deta hai. Ab dhyan se dekho: agar kisi ka check kaam na kare, to guard ko doosre check par jaana padta hai, par wo **bina soche-samjhe** nahi karta."

"Chacha, kya namak ke andar ke ions ke saath bhi aisa hi hota hai?" Mr. Phansalkar ne sir hilaya. "Bilkul. Chloride ka apna check, sulphate ka apna, carbonate ka apna. Aur ek mazedaar baat: kabhi-kabhi **do alag visitor ek hi jaise dikhte hain**, aur tab guard ko pehle ek chhota sa pre-check karna padta hai, taaki dhokha na ho. Ye pre-check hi aaj ka sabse dilchasp hissa hai."

```
   Visitor            Fixed check                  Anion / ion         Fixed test
   ----------------   --------------------------   -----------------   ----------------------------
   Delivery wala  --> OTP milao                    Chloride Cl⁻    -->  dil. HNO₃, phir AgNO₃
   Mehmaan        --> intercom se poochho          Sulphate SO₄²⁻  -->  dil. HCl, phir BaCl₂
   Resident       --> gaadi ka sticker             Carbonate CO₃²⁻ -->  dilute acid, chune ka paani
   Courier packet --> scanner se scan karo         Ammonium NH₄⁺   -->  NaOH ke saath halka garam
```

> **Figure 4.1** – Gate-check ka analogy: har kisam ke visitor ka apna fixed check hota hai, aur har ion ka apna fixed test. Ye sirf ek analogy hai, asli tests aage ke parts mein.

## Part A: Chloride Cl⁻: AgNO₃ se curdy safed dhodha

Chapter 1 mein humne dekha tha ki silver nitrate aur NaCl milte hi safed precipitate banta hai: AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl↓ + NaNO₃ (Ye [acids-bases-and-salts-part5.md](../acids-bases-and-salts/acids-bases-and-salts-part5.md) mein hai). Wahan ye namak banane ka tareeka tha. Ab hum use ulta padhenge: **agar ghol mein chloride ion hai to silver nitrate daalne par wahi dhodha banega**, isliye ye dhodha **chloride ka saboot** hai.

Isme saara kaam sirf do ions ka hai. Na⁺ (ya doosra cation) aur NO₃⁻ darshak (spectator) hain, wo kuch nahi badalte:

Ag⁺ + Cl⁻ → AgCl↓ (safed, curdy)

Is AgCl ki ek pehchaan aur hai: ye **dilute NH₄OH mein ghul jaata hai** (ek complex ion banta hai, [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺: complex ion, aage ki class mein). Isse pata chalta hai ki dhodha sach mein silver chloride hi tha.

### Pehle acid kyun?

Test mein **pehle dilute nitric acid (HNO₃)** daalte hain, phir AgNO₃. Kyun? Kyunki **carbonate** (CO₃²⁻) bhi silver ke saath ek **safed-sa (halka peela) precipitate** deta hai: Ag₂CO₃. Agar ghol mein carbonate hua to hum use galti se chloride samajh lenge. Dilute HNO₃ carbonate ko tod deta hai (CO₂ gas nikalti hai) aur Ag₂CO₃ ka dhodha bhi ghula deta hai, jabki AgCl ko wo nahi ghulata:

Na₂CO₃ + 2AgNO₃ → Ag₂CO₃↓ + 2NaNO₃
Ag₂CO₃ + 2HNO₃ → 2AgNO₃ + H₂O + CO₂↑

Aur acid HNO₃ hi kyun, HCl kyun nahi? Kyunki HCl ke andar khud Cl⁻ hai! Wo daalte hi AgNO₃ se AgCl bana dega, chahe sample mein chloride tha ya nahi. Guard ke analogy mein ye aisa hai ki **check karne wala khud visitor ka naam bol de**.

```
   Test tube mein :               AgNO₃ daalne ke baad :

    :              :              :              :
    :   saaf ghol  :              :  ghol upar   :
    :   (namak +   :              :  saaf hai    :
    :    dil. HNO₃):              :              :
    :              :              :  ########    :   <-- AgCl: safed,
    :              :              :  ########    :       curdy, neeche baitha
    :______________:              :______________:
      pehle (saaf)                  baad mein
```

> **Figure 4.2** – Chloride ka test: namak ke ghol ko dilute HNO₃ se acidify karke AgNO₃ ki kuch boondein daalne par safed curdy AgCl neeche baith jaata hai. Upar ka ghol saaf rehta hai.

### Example 1. NaCl mein Cl⁻ ka test

**Sawaal:** Ek colourless ghol mein NaCl hai. Teacher ne dilute HNO₃ ki kuch boondein aur phir AgNO₃ daali. Kya dikhega? Equation likho.

**Kadam 1 (kya daala):** dil. HNO₃ (pre-check), phir AgNO₃.
**Kadam 2 (kya dikha):** safed curdy dhodha, jo NH₄OH daalne par ghul jaata hai.
**Kadam 3 (kya matlab):** chloride ion maujood hai.
**Kadam 4 (equation):**
Molecular: NaCl + AgNO₃ → AgCl↓ + NaNO₃
Ionic: Ag⁺ + Cl⁻ → AgCl↓ (Na⁺ aur NO₃⁻ darshak)

**Jaanch:** atoms: Na 1 = 1, Cl 1 = 1, Ag 1 = 1, N 1 = 1, O 3 = 3 ✅. Ionic equation mein charge: bayen taraf (+1) + (−1) = 0, dayen taraf AgCl neutral = 0 ✅.

### Example 2. MgCl₂ mein do chloride

**Sawaal:** MgCl₂ ke ek ghol ko acidify karke AgNO₃ milaya. Balanced equation likho aur batao ki Mg²⁺ ka kya hua.

MgCl₂ + 2AgNO₃ → 2AgCl↓ + Mg(NO₃)₂

MgCl₂ ke ek formula unit mein **2 Cl⁻** hain, isliye **2 Ag⁺** lagenge aur **2 AgCl** banenge. Mg²⁺ ghol mein hi rehta hai aur Mg(NO₃)₂ ka hissa ban jaata hai: wo darshak hai, isse test par koi asar nahi.

**Jaanch:** Mg 1 = 1, Cl 2 = 2, Ag 2 = 2, N 2 = 2, O 6 = 6 ✅. Ionic equation har chloride ke liye wahi: Ag⁺ + Cl⁻ → AgCl↓, to 2 chloride ke liye 2 baar ✅.

## Part B: Sulphate SO₄²⁻: BaCl₂ se safed dhodha

Sulphate ka fixed test **barium chloride (BaCl₂)** hai. Barium ke sulphate ka **BaSO₄** paani mein nahi ghulta (Chapter 1 ki solubility table mein sulphate ke exceptions mein BaSO₄ tha), isliye wo safed dhodha banakar neeche baith jaata hai:

Ba²⁺ + SO₄²⁻ → BaSO₄↓ (safed)

**BaSO₄ ki khaas pehchaan:** wo **acid mein bhi nahi ghulta** (dilute HCl ya dilute HNO₃ mein bhi nahi). Yahi pehchaan agle hisse mein bahut kaam aayegi.

### Pehle dilute HCl kyun?

Carbonate yahan bhi dhokha de sakta hai: **barium carbonate (BaCO₃)** bhi safed hota hai. Isliye pehle ghol mein dilute **HCl** daalte hain. Agar carbonate tha, to wo tut jaata hai (CO₂ ke bulbule). Fir BaCl₂ daalne par agar safed dhodha banta hai, to wo **BaSO₄** hi ho sakta hai:

Na₂CO₃ + BaCl₂ → BaCO₃↓ + 2NaCl
BaCO₃ + 2HCl → BaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂↑

Yahan acid HCl liya (HNO₃ nahi, koi dikkat nahi), par **sulphuric acid (H₂SO₄) kabhi nahi**, kyunki uske apne SO₄²⁻ se BaCl₂ turant safed BaSO₄ bana dega, chahe sample mein sulphate ho ya na ho.

```
   Sample + BaCl₂ :              Phir dilute HCl daalo :

    :              :              :              :
    :  ghol        :              :  ghol        :
    :              :              :              :
    :  ########    :   ------>    :  ########    <-- BaSO₄ : nahi ghula
    :______________:              :______________:        (sulphate hai)

    :              :              :              :
    :  ghol        :              :  ghol saaf   :
    :  ########    :   ------>    :  (bulbule    :
    :______________:              :   uthe)      :
                                  :______________:   <-- BaCO₃ : ghul gaya
                                                         (carbonate tha)
```

> **Figure 4.3** – Upar wali jodi: BaSO₄ ka dhodha dilute HCl daalne ke baad bhi baitha rehta hai. Neeche wali jodi: BaCO₃ ka dhodha dilute HCl se ghul jaata hai aur CO₂ ke bulbule nikalte hain. Isi se dono ko alag pehchante hain.

### Example 3. Na₂SO₄ mein SO₄²⁻

**Sawaal:** Na₂SO₄ ke ghol mein teacher ne dilute HCl ki kuch boondein aur phir BaCl₂ ghol daala. Kya dikhega? Equation aur spectator ions batao.

**Kadam 1:** dil. HCl (carbonate hata do), phir BaCl₂.
**Kadam 2:** safed dhodha, jo aur HCl daalne par bhi nahi ghulta.
**Kadam 3:** sulphate ion maujood hai.
**Kadam 4:** Na₂SO₄ + BaCl₂ → BaSO₄↓ + 2NaCl. Ionic: Ba²⁺ + SO₄²⁻ → BaSO₄↓. Darshak: Na⁺ aur Cl⁻.

**Jaanch:** Na 2 = 2, S 1 = 1, O 4 = 4, Ba 1 = 1, Cl 2 = 2 ✅. Ionic charge: (+2) + (−2) = 0 ✅. (Barium zehreela hai: sirf teacher demo.)

### Example 4. MgSO₄ aur (NH₄)₂SO₄: ek hi anion, do alag cation

**Sawaal:** MgSO₄ aur (NH₄)₂SO₄ dono par acidify karke BaCl₂ daala gaya. Kya dono mein ek jaisa dhodha banega? Equations likho.

Haan, dono mein safed BaSO₄, kyunki **dhodha anion ka hota hai, cation ka nahi**.
MgSO₄ + BaCl₂ → BaSO₄↓ + MgCl₂
(NH₄)₂SO₄ + BaCl₂ → BaSO₄↓ + 2NH₄Cl

**Jaanch:** pehli mein Mg 1, S 1, O 4, Ba 1, Cl 2 dono taraf ✅. Doosri mein N 2, H 8, S 1, O 4, Ba 1, Cl 2 dono taraf ✅. Dono mein ionic equation wahi: Ba²⁺ + SO₄²⁻ → BaSO₄↓.

## Part C: Carbonate CO₃²⁻: acid daalo, bulbule dekho

Carbonate ka test sabse seedha hai kyunki **dhodha nahi, gas** dikhti hai. Chapter 1 mein humne dekha tha ki acid kisi metal carbonate par girta hai to tez bulbulahat ke saath CO₂ nikalti hai. Wahi yahan **test** ban jaata hai: **dilute acid daalo, tez bulbulahat (brisk effervescence) dikhe, aur gas ko chune ke paani mein guzaro: wo dudhiya ho jaaye.**

Na₂CO₃ + 2HCl → 2NaCl + H₂O + CO₂↑
CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂↑
Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂ → CaCO₃↓ + H₂O (chune ka paani dudhiya, CaCO₃ ke kaaran)

Ionic roop mein dono carbonate ke liye ek hi baat: CO₃²⁻ + 2H⁺ → H₂O + CO₂↑

```
   Teacher demo : carbonate + dilute acid

                 delivery tube (CO₂ isse jaati hai)
        .--------------------------.
        :                          :
     [cork]                        :
     :    :                   :    :    :
     :~~~~:                   :~~~~:~~~~:   <-- chune ka saaf paani
     :o o :                   :    o    :       (CO₂ ke bulbule)
     :_o__:                   :_________:
   test tube                  boiling tube
   (carbonate +
    dilute acid)
```

> **Figure 4.4** – Carbonate ka teacher demo: test tube mein carbonate par dilute acid daalne se CO₂ ke bulbule nikalte hain. Test tube ke mooh par ek cork hai jisme se delivery tube nikalti hai; uska doosra sira chune ke saaf paani mein dooba hai, aur CO₂ use dudhiya kar deti hai. Is demo mein kuch garam nahi kiya jaata.

**Suraksha aur sahi tareeka:** demo mein teacher dhyan rakhta hai ki jab bulbule nikalna band ho jaayein ya pehle hi, **delivery tube chune ke paani mein se bahar nikaal liya jaata hai**, warna chune ka paani wapas test tube mein khinch (suck-back) sakta hai. Ye test aam taur par thande mein hota hai, garam nahi karna padta.

### Example 5. Na₂CO₃ ka poora test

**Sawaal:** Na₂CO₃ ke ghol mein dilute HCl daala gaya. Kya dikhega, kis gas ko kaise pehchanoge, aur kya equation hai?

**Kadam 1:** dilute HCl daalo.
**Kadam 2:** turant tez bulbulahat. Gas ko chune ke paani mein guzaaro: paani dudhiya.
**Kadam 3:** ye CO₂ hai, to ghol mein carbonate hai.
**Kadam 4:** Na₂CO₃ + 2HCl → 2NaCl + H₂O + CO₂↑ aur Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂ → CaCO₃↓ + H₂O.

**Jaanch:** Na 2 = 2, C 1 = 1, O 3 = 1 + 2 = 3 ✅ (H₂O mein 1, CO₂ mein 2), H 2 = 2, Cl 2 = 2 ✅. Chune ke paani wali equation: Ca 1, O 2 + 2 = 4 = 3 + 1 ✅, H 2 = 2, C 1 = 1 ✅.

### Example 6. Thos CaCO₃ (chuna-patthar)

**Sawaal:** Safed thos chuna-patthar paani mein nahi ghulta. Usme carbonate kaise pehchanoge?

Teacher demo: teacher thos ko test tube mein lekar dilute HCl daalta hai: bulbule uthte hain, CO₂ nikalegi aur chune ka paani dudhiya hoga. Equation: CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂↑. Yahan Ca²⁺ (cation) aur CO₃²⁻ (anion) dono ek hi thos mein hain: acid ke baad jo ghol bana, usme Ca²⁺ ghula hua hai.

**Jaanch:** Ca 1 = 1, C 1 = 1, O 3 = 1 + 2 = 3, H 2 = 2, Cl 2 = 2 ✅.

## Part D: Ammonium NH₄⁺: NaOH ke saath halka garam karo

Ab ek positive ion. NH₄⁺ ka test **cation wale tests se alag** hai: isme dhodha nahi banta, balki **gas** nikalti hai. Chapter 7 (chemical bonding II) mein humne dekha tha ki NH₄⁺ ek polyatomic ion hai (N aur 4 H). Jab ammonium namak ko **NaOH** ke saath halka garam karte hain, OH⁻ NH₄⁺ se ek H⁺ le leta hai aur **ammonia gas** (NH₃) bana deta hai:

NH₄Cl + NaOH → NaCl + NH₃↑ + H₂O
Ionic: NH₄⁺ + OH⁻ → NH₃↑ + H₂O

NH₃ ki do pehchaan: (1) **tez, chubhne wali gandh**; (2) ye ek alkaline gas hai, to **geela (moist) laal litmus kaagaz neela** kar deti hai. Gandh sungna student ka kaam nahi: teacher agar sunghata hai to hand se halki hawa test tube ke mooh se apni taraf lata hai ("waft"), seedha nahi sungta. Hum yahan ise **sirf padh rahe hain**.

```
   Teacher demo (fume cupboard mein) : ammonium namak + NaOH, halka garam

        moist red litmus (mooh ke paas):   laal --> neela
               ^
               :   NH₃ gas upar uthti hai
               :
      :                :
      :     ghol       :   <-- ammonium namak + NaOH
      :   (halka garam):
      :________________:
              ^
         thodi aanch (teacher)
```

> **Figure 4.5** – Ammonium ka teacher demo: ammonium namak aur NaOH ke ghol ko halke se garam karne par NH₃ gas uthti hai. Moist red litmus kaagaz test tube ke mooh ke paas rakhne par neela ho jaata hai. Test tube khula rehta hai, band nahi.

### Example 7. NH₄Cl mein NH₄⁺

**Sawaal:** NH₄Cl ke ghol mein NaOH daalkar halka garam kiya. Kya hoga? Balanced equation aur ionic equation likho.

**Kadam 1:** NaOH daalo aur halka garam karo (teacher).
**Kadam 2:** tez gandh wali gas, moist red litmus neela.
**Kadam 3:** NH₄⁺ maujood hai.
**Kadam 4:** NH₄Cl + NaOH → NaCl + NH₃↑ + H₂O. Ionic: NH₄⁺ + OH⁻ → NH₃↑ + H₂O. Darshak: Na⁺ aur Cl⁻.

**Jaanch:** N 1 = 1, H 4 + 1 = 5 = 3 + 2 ✅, Cl 1 = 1, Na 1 = 1, O 1 = 1 ✅. Ionic charge: (+1) + (−1) = 0 = 0 ✅ (dono taraf neutral).

### Example 8. (NH₄)₂SO₄: ek namak, do pehchaan

**Sawaal:** Ammonium sulphate (NH₄)₂SO₄ mein ek hi namak se kaun-kaun se ion alag-alag tests se milenge?

Is namak mein **cation NH₄⁺** aur **anion SO₄²⁻** dono hain, aur **dono ka test alag** hai:
- NH₄⁺ ke liye NaOH ke saath halka garam: (NH₄)₂SO₄ + 2NaOH → Na₂SO₄ + 2NH₃↑ + 2H₂O.
- SO₄²⁻ ke liye acidify karke BaCl₂: (NH₄)₂SO₄ + BaCl₂ → BaSO₄↓ + 2NH₄Cl.

**Jaanch:** pehli equation: N 2 = 2, H 8 + 2 = 10 = 6 + 4 ✅, S 1 = 1, O 4 + 2 = 6 = 4 + 2 ✅, Na 2 = 2 ✅. Doosri: N 2, H 8, S 1, O 4, Ba 1, Cl 2 dono taraf ✅. Ek test se ek hi ion milta hai: isliye dono ion ke liye **do alag test** zaroori hain.

## Part E: Precipitate ka mass: ek chhota hisaab

Kya dhodhe ka mass pehle se bataya ja sakta hai? Haan, Chapter 5 (mole concept) ki wahi tareeka: pehle moles, phir mass. (Mass: Mg 24, S 32, O 16, Cl 35.5, Ba 137.)

### Example 9. MgSO₄ se kitna BaSO₄?

**Sawaal:** Teacher ne 1.20 g MgSO₄ ke ghol mein BaCl₂ itna daala ki sulphate poora dhodha ban jaaye. Kitna BaSO₄ banega?

**Kadam 1 (equation):** MgSO₄ + BaCl₂ → BaSO₄↓ + MgCl₂. Ratio 1 : 1.
**Kadam 2 (molar mass):** MgSO₄ = 24 + 32 + 4 × 16 = 24 + 32 + 64 = **120** g/mol. BaSO₄ = 137 + 32 + 64 = **233** g/mol.
**Kadam 3 (moles):** MgSO₄ = 1.20 ÷ 120 = **0.01 mol**.
**Kadam 4 (ratio se):** BaSO₄ ke moles bhi 0.01 mol, to mass = 0.01 × 233 = **2.33 g**.

**Jaanch:** BaCl₂ chahiye: 0.01 × 208 = 2.08 g (BaCl₂ = 137 + 71 = 208). Mass sangrakshan: reactants 1.20 + 2.08 = 3.28 g; products BaSO₄ 2.33 g + MgCl₂ (0.01 × 95 = 0.95 g) = 3.28 g ✅.

## Part F: Chaaron tests ek nazar mein

```
   Namak ka ghol (colourless) :

   Chloride?   dil. HNO₃, phir AgNO₃    --> safed curdy dhodha, NH₄OH mein ghulta hai
   Sulphate?   dil. HCl, phir BaCl₂     --> safed dhodha, acid mein NAHI ghulta
   Carbonate?  dilute acid              --> tez bulbule, CO₂, chune ka paani dudhiya
   Ammonium?   NaOH + halka garam       --> tez gandh, moist red litmus neela

   Teeno mein se koi nahi?              --> ek aur anion ho sakta hai, jaise nitrate NO₃⁻
```

> **Figure 4.6** – Chaar tests ka saar: har ion ka reagent, kya dikhta hai, aur kya matlab nikalta hai. Nitrate (NO₃⁻) ke liye alag test hai, aage ki class mein.

| Ion | Reagent (pehle acid?) | Kya dikhta hai | Equation (ionic) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chloride Cl⁻ | pehle dil. HNO₃, phir AgNO₃ | safed curdy dhodha, NH₄OH mein ghulta hai | Ag⁺ + Cl⁻ → AgCl↓ |
| Sulphate SO₄²⁻ | pehle dil. HCl, phir BaCl₂ | safed dhodha, acid mein nahi ghulta | Ba²⁺ + SO₄²⁻ → BaSO₄↓ |
| Carbonate CO₃²⁻ | dilute acid | tez bulbule, CO₂, chune ka paani dudhiya | CO₃²⁻ + 2H⁺ → H₂O + CO₂↑ |
| Ammonium NH₄⁺ | NaOH, halka garam | NH₃: tez gandh, moist red litmus neela | NH₄⁺ + OH⁻ → NH₃↑ + H₂O |

Ek aur sachchi baat: agar kisi namak mein upar ke teeno anion nahi milte (na dhodha, na bulbule), to anion **nitrate (NO₃⁻)** ya koi aur ho sakta hai. Nitrate ka apna test hota hai, jo aage ki class mein aayega.

## Aam galtiyan (bachke rehna)

**Galti: "BaCl₂ daalne par safed dhodha bana, to sulphate pakka ho gaya."**
Ye sach lagta hai kyunki BaSO₄ safed hota hai. Par **BaCO₃ bhi safed** hota hai. Demo teen kadam mein (teacher demo, sirf dekhne ke liye: barium zehreela hai):

1. **Teacher ek ghol leta hai jisme Na₂CO₃ hai.** Usme BaCl₂ daala: Na₂CO₃ + BaCl₂ → BaCO₃↓ + 2NaCl. Safed dhodha ban gaya! Agar yahin ruk jaate to hum bolte "sulphate", jo galat hota (isme sulphate hai hi nahi).
2. **Ab us dhodhe par dilute HCl daalo.** BaCO₃ + 2HCl → BaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂↑. Dhodha ghul gaya aur bulbule uthe: ye carbonate ka saboot hai, sulphate ka nahi.
3. **Ab Na₂SO₄ wale ghol mein wahi karo.** BaCl₂ se dhodha, par dilute HCl daalne par **dhodha nahi ghulta**. Isse pata chalta hai ki wo BaSO₄ tha.

| Ghol | BaCl₂ daalne par | Dilute HCl daalne par | Matlab |
|---|---|---|---|
| Na₂CO₃ | safed dhodha | ghul gaya, bulbule | carbonate |
| Na₂SO₄ | safed dhodha | nahi ghula | sulphate |

**Niyam:** BaCl₂ ka safed dhodha **sirf shuruaat** hai. Pehle dilute HCl se carbonate hataao (ya dhodha ban jaane par use dilute HCl se test karo). Agar dhodha **acid mein bhi nahi ghulta**, tab hi sulphate. (Aur H₂SO₄ ka istemaal acid ke roop mein nahi: wo khud SO₄²⁻ de deta hai.)

## Quick Recap

- Har namak mein cation aur anion hote hain: Topic 2 aur 3 mein cation, ab anion aur ammonium.
- **Chloride Cl⁻:** dilute HNO₃, phir AgNO₃: safed curdy AgCl, jo NH₄OH mein ghulta hai. Ag⁺ + Cl⁻ → AgCl↓. HCl nahi, kyunki use khud Cl⁻ hai.
- **Sulphate SO₄²⁻:** dilute HCl, phir BaCl₂: safed BaSO₄, jo acid mein nahi ghulta. Ba²⁺ + SO₄²⁻ → BaSO₄↓. H₂SO₄ nahi, kyunki use khud SO₄²⁻ hai.
- **Carbonate CO₃²⁻:** dilute acid se tez bulbule (CO₂), jo chune ke paani ko dudhiya karti hai. CO₃²⁻ + 2H⁺ → H₂O + CO₂↑.
- **Ammonium NH₄⁺:** NaOH ke saath halka garam: NH₃ gas, tez gandh, moist red litmus neela. NH₄⁺ + OH⁻ → NH₃↑ + H₂O.
- Dhodha anion ka hota hai: MgSO₄ aur (NH₄)₂SO₄ dono BaCl₂ se BaSO₄ dete hain.
- Silver aur barium ke compounds zehreele hain, ammonia gas bhi: sab test teacher ke saath ya sirf padhne ke liye.
- Precipitate ka mass mole se: 1.20 g MgSO₄ ke liye 2.33 g BaSO₄.
- **Galti se bacho:** BaCl₂ ka safed dhodha carbonate ka bhi ho sakta hai, isliye acid se check zaroori.

## Practice Questions

(Sab sawaal paper par hal karne ke liye hain. Mass: H 1, C 12, N 14, O 16, Na 23, Mg 24, S 32, Cl 35.5, K 39, Ag 108, Ba 137.)

1. A teacher tests a colourless solution of potassium chloride, KCl, by adding dilute nitric acid and then silver nitrate solution. State what is seen, write the balanced equation and the ionic equation, and name the spectator ions.
2. Potassium sulphate, K₂SO₄, solution is acidified with dilute HCl and barium chloride solution is added. State what is seen, write the balanced equation and the ionic equation. What would be seen if dilute HCl is added to the precipitate?
3. Sodium carbonate solution is treated with dilute nitric acid. State what is seen, how the gas is tested, and write the balanced equation and the ionic equation. Which spectator ions are present?
4. Ammonium nitrate solution, NH₄NO₃, is warmed with NaOH solution in a teacher demo. Write the balanced equation and the ionic equation, and state what happens to a moist red litmus paper held near the mouth of the test tube. Why is the paper held near the mouth and not dipped in the liquid?
5. A teacher adds excess barium chloride to a solution containing 5.22 g of potassium sulphate, so that all the sulphate forms a precipitate. Find the moles of K₂SO₄ and the mass of BaSO₄ formed.
6. A solution contains 5.35 g of ammonium chloride, NH₄Cl. Excess acidified silver nitrate is added in a teacher demo. Find the mass of silver chloride formed and the mass of AgNO₃ used up. Which two ions of NH₄Cl are present, and which test would show the other one?
7. Three colourless solutions are labelled P, Q and R. They are MgCl₂, K₂SO₄ and Na₂CO₃ in some order. Dilute HCl produces brisk bubbles only in P. After the dilute HCl treatment, BaCl₂ produces a white precipitate only in Q. Identify P, Q and R, and name the test that confirms R.
8. State True or False and correct the false ones: (a) Dilute HCl is a good acid to add before the silver nitrate test for chloride. (b) BaSO₄ dissolves in dilute HCl. (c) The white precipitate AgCl dissolves in dilute NH₄OH. (d) NH₃ turns moist red litmus blue. (e) MgSO₄ gives no precipitate with BaCl₂.
9. Why is dilute H₂SO₄ not used to acidify a solution before the barium chloride test for sulphate? What would the test falsely show?
10. A student adds BaCl₂ to a colourless solution and gets a white precipitate. The precipitate dissolves completely in dilute HCl with bubbles. Which ion was present, which ion was NOT present, and what is the gas?
11. Write the ionic equation for each of the four tests of this topic, and for each say which common ions in the unreacted solution are spectators. Use the salts NaCl, MgSO₄, Na₂CO₃ and NH₄Cl.
12. Describe the safe way a teacher carries out the ammonia test and the carbonate test: which gas is dangerous, where is the test done, how is the gas smelt or tested, what precautions are taken with the delivery tube, and why can a student never do these at home?

### Answers

1. Dilute HNO₃ pehle (carbonate ho to hata de), phir AgNO₃: **safed curdy dhodha AgCl**, jo dilute NH₄OH mein ghulta hai. Equation: KCl + AgNO₃ → AgCl↓ + KNO₃. Ionic: Ag⁺ + Cl⁻ → AgCl↓. Darshak: **K⁺ aur NO₃⁻**. Jaanch: K 1, Cl 1, Ag 1, N 1, O 3 dono taraf ✅.
2. **Safed dhodha BaSO₄** banega. K₂SO₄ + BaCl₂ → BaSO₄↓ + 2KCl. Ionic: Ba²⁺ + SO₄²⁻ → BaSO₄↓. Dilute HCl daalne par dhodha **nahi ghulega** (BaSO₄ acid mein nahi ghulta). Jaanch: K 2, S 1, O 4, Ba 1, Cl 2 dono taraf ✅, ionic charge (+2) + (−2) = 0 ✅.
3. **Turant tez bulbule**; gas ko chune ke saaf paani mein guzaaro: **dudhiya** ho jaata hai, ye CO₂ hai. Equation: Na₂CO₃ + 2HNO₃ → 2NaNO₃ + H₂O + CO₂↑. Ionic: CO₃²⁻ + 2H⁺ → H₂O + CO₂↑. Darshak: **Na⁺ aur NO₃⁻**. Jaanch: Na 2, C 1, O 3 + 6 = 9 = 6 + 1 + 2, H 2, N 2 ✅.
4. NH₄NO₃ + NaOH → NaNO₃ + NH₃↑ + H₂O. Ionic: NH₄⁺ + OH⁻ → NH₃↑ + H₂O. Moist red litmus **neela** ho jaata hai. Mooh ke paas isliye rakhte hain kyunki gas upar uthti hai, aur litmus ko ghol mein dubo diya to NaOH (alkali) se hi neela ho jaata, jisse ammonia ka saboot nahi milta. Jaanch: N 2 = 1 + 1, H 4 + 1 = 5 = 3 + 2, O 3 + 1 = 4 = 3 + 1, Na 1 = 1 ✅.
5. K₂SO₄ ka molar mass = 2 × 39 + 32 + 64 = **174** g/mol. Moles = 5.22 ÷ 174 = **0.03 mol**. K₂SO₄ + BaCl₂ → BaSO₄↓ + 2KCl (ratio 1 : 1), to BaSO₄ = 0.03 mol. BaSO₄ = 233 g/mol, mass = 0.03 × 233 = **6.99 g**. Jaanch: BaCl₂ chahiye 0.03 × 208 = 6.24 g; reactants 5.22 + 6.24 = 11.46 g; products 6.99 + (0.06 × 74.5 = 4.47 g KCl) = 11.46 g ✅.
6. NH₄Cl = 14 + 4 + 35.5 = **53.5** g/mol. Moles = 5.35 ÷ 53.5 = **0.1 mol**. NH₄Cl + AgNO₃ → AgCl↓ + NH₄NO₃ (ratio 1 : 1 : 1). AgCl = 108 + 35.5 = 143.5, mass = 0.1 × 143.5 = **14.35 g**. AgNO₃ = 108 + 14 + 48 = 170, mass = 0.1 × 170 = **17 g**. Dono ion: **NH₄⁺ aur Cl⁻**. Doosra ion (NH₄⁺) NaOH ke saath halka garam karke NH₃ gas se (moist red litmus neela). Jaanch: Cl⁻ ka AgNO₃ test ✅ aur 14.35 + (0.1 × 80 = 8 g NH₄NO₃) = 22.35 g = 5.35 + 17 ✅.
7. Sirf **P** mein tez bulbule: **P = Na₂CO₃** (carbonate). HCl daalne ke baad BaCl₂ sirf **Q** mein safed dhodha deta hai, to **Q = K₂SO₄**. Bacha **R = MgCl₂**. R ka confirm test: dilute HNO₃ phir AgNO₃: safed curdy AgCl (jo NH₄OH mein ghulta hai). Jaanch: teeno anion alag alag, har ek ke liye ek hi match ✅.
8. (a) **False** ❌: HCl mein khud Cl⁻ hai, to AgNO₃ se AgCl bana dega; **dilute HNO₃** lena chahiye. (b) **False** ❌: BaSO₄ acid mein nahi ghulta. (c) **True** ✅. (d) **True** ✅. (e) **False** ❌: MgSO₄ mein SO₄²⁻ hai, to BaCl₂ se safed BaSO₄ banega.
9. H₂SO₄ mein khud SO₄²⁻ hai. Wo BaCl₂ se turant BaSO₄ bana dega, chahe original ghol mein sulphate ho ya na ho. To test **galat se sulphate dikha dega**. Isliye pehle dilute **HCl** daalte hain.
10. Ghula hua dhodha **BaCO₃** tha, to ghol mein **carbonate (CO₃²⁻)** tha. **Sulphate maujood nahi tha**, kyunki BaSO₄ dilute HCl mein nahi ghulta. Gas **CO₂** hai (chune ka paani dudhiya). Equation: BaCO₃ + 2HCl → BaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂↑ ✅ (Ba 1, C 1, O 3, H 2, Cl 2 dono taraf).
11. Chloride (NaCl): Ag⁺ + Cl⁻ → AgCl↓, darshak Na⁺ aur NO₃⁻. Sulphate (MgSO₄): Ba²⁺ + SO₄²⁻ → BaSO₄↓, darshak Mg²⁺ aur Cl⁻. Carbonate (Na₂CO₃): CO₃²⁻ + 2H⁺ → H₂O + CO₂↑, darshak Na⁺ aur acid ka anion (jaise Cl⁻). Ammonium (NH₄Cl): NH₄⁺ + OH⁻ → NH₃↑ + H₂O, darshak Cl⁻ aur Na⁺. Jaanch: har ionic equation ka charge dono taraf barabar: (+1) + (−1) = 0, (+2) + (−2) = 0, (−2) + 2(+1) = 0, (+1) + (−1) = 0 ✅.
12. **Dangerous gas:** ammonia (aankh, naak, gale ko jalati hai). Ammonia test **fume cupboard** mein hota hai, teacher moist red litmus test tube ke mooh ke paas rakhta hai aur sungne ke liye sirf "waft" (haath se halki hawa) karta hai, seedha nahi sungta. Carbonate test mein teacher test tube **khula** rakhta hai, aur gas ka kaam hote hi ya bulbule rukne se pehle **delivery tube chune ke paani se bahar nikaal leta hai**, taaki suck-back na ho. Student ghar par nahi kar sakta kyunki ghar par fume cupboard, goggles, gloves aur teacher nahi hote, aur NaOH corrosive hai.
