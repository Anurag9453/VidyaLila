# Topic 1: Jaasoos Chemistry: Test, Precipitate Aur Ions Ka Hisaab

Pichhle chapter mein humne dekha ki ek hi compound ke andar ionic aur covalent bond saath reh sakte hain, aur OH⁻ ya NH₄⁺ jaise jhund paani mein **poore ion ki tarah** ghoomte hain. Ab sawaal ulta hai: agar humein koi ghol mila aur uski shakal-soorat se kuch pata nahi chala, to hum kaise jaanein ki usmein **kaun se ion** hain? Is chapter mein hum yahi seekhenge: ek **jaasoos** ki tarah ek-ek clue (rang, precipitate, gas) padhna. Aaj uski neev: test kya hota hai, aur ions ka hisaab kaise lagta hai.

## Teen ek jaise bag

Mr. Nene apne bhatije Kedar ko school ki ek din ki trip se bus se wapas la rahe the. Bus se utarte hi platform par teen bilkul ek jaise **neele bag** ek line mein rakhe the: ek Kedar ka, ek uske dost ka, aur ek Mr. Nene ka. Teeno par koi naam nahi, teeno ki size aur chain tak ek jaisi.

"Chacha, ab kaun sa mera hai? Teeno ek jaise dikhte hain!" Kedar ghabra gaya. Mr. Nene hanse. "Bag ko kholna zaroori nahi. Pehle **clue dhoondho**. Mujhe yaad hai tumne apne bag mein ek keychain latkaya tha. Dost ke bag mein kuch aur hoga, mere bag mein shayad meri notebook."

Kedar ne dekha: ek bag ki zip par **cricket ball wala keychain** latka tha. "Ye mera!" Doosre bag ke side-pocket se **steel ka tiffin** (lunchbox) ka kona dikh raha tha, jo dost ka tha. Teesre bag ke upar **Mr. Nene ki notebook** ka kona nikla tha. Teeno bag **ek-ek clue** se alag pehchaan liye gaye, bina kisi ka saaman bigaade.

"Chacha, ye to ek khel jaisa tha!" "Haan," Mr. Nene ne kaha, "aur yahi **test** ka asli matlab hai: tum ek cheez karte ho (bag ke upar nazar daalte ho), **dekhte ho ki kya dikha** (keychain), aur phir soch kar kehte ho ki **iska matlab kya hua** (ye mera bag hai). Chemistry ka jaasoos bhi bilkul yahi karta hai, bas uske bag mein ghol hote hain aur clue hote hain rang, precipitate aur gas."

```
   Bag A            Bag B            Bag C
   +-------+        +-------+        +-------+
   [ neela ]        [ neela ]        [ neela ]   <- teeno ek jaise
   +-------+        +-------+        +-------+
   cricket ball     tiffin ka        notebook ka
   keychain         kona             kona
   (clue 1)         (clue 2)         (clue 3)
      v                v                v
   Kedar       dost             Mr. Nene       <- clue padhkar fasla
```

> **Figure 1.1** – Teen ek jaise bag: bahar se alag nahi dikhte, par har bag ka ek apna clue hai (keychain, tiffin, notebook). Clue padhne se pata chalta hai kaun sa bag kiska hai. Test ka yahi dhancha hai: clue dekho, matlab nikalo.

## Part A: Analysis kya hai: kya hai aur kitna hai

Chemistry mein kisi sample ke baare mein do alag sawaal pooche ja sakte hain:

- **"Isme kya hai?"** Ye **qualitative** analysis hai. Jawab ek naam hota hai: "isme copper ke ion hain."
- **"Isme kitna hai?"** Ye **quantitative** analysis hai. Jawab ek sankhya hoti hai: "0.01 mole" ya "1.6 gram." Ye hisaab Chapter 5 (mole concept) mein seekha tha.

Is poore chapter mein hum **pehle sawaal** ka jawab dhoondhenge: sirf ye pata karna ki ghol mein kaun se ion hain. Ek-do jagah "kitna precipitate banega" jaisa chhota sa hisaab bhi aayega, jisme wahi mole wala tareeka lagega.

### Example 1. Qualitative ya quantitative?

**Sawaal:** In kaamon ko alag karo. (a) "Ye namak ka ghol kisi copper salt ka hai ya nahi?" (b) "Is ghol ke 100 mL mein kitna NaOH ghula hai?" (c) "Ye safed powder kaun sa salt hai?"

- (a) **Qualitative**: sirf "hai ya nahi" poochha gaya.
- (b) **Quantitative**: "kitna" poochha gaya.
- (c) **Qualitative**: naam dhoondhna hai, matra nahi.

**Jaanch:** jahan jawab ek naam ya haan/nahi ho wahan qualitative; jahan jawab ek sankhya ho wahan quantitative ✅.

### Har test ke liye teen sawaal

Mr. Nene ne Kedar ko ek chhota niyam diya: **kisi bhi test ko samajhne ke liye hamesha teen sawaal poochho.**

| Sawaal | Bag wali kahani mein | Chemistry mein |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Kya daala gaya?** | bag ko dhyan se dekha | reagent, jaise NaOH |
| 2. **Kya dikha?** | cricket ball ka keychain | rang, precipitate ya gas |
| 3. **Iska matlab kya hua?** | ye mera bag hai | ghol mein ye ion hai |

```
   KYA DAALA?  ------>  KYA DIKHA?  ------>  KYA MATLAB?
   (reagent)           (observation)         (conclusion)
   NaOH ka ghol        pale blue ppt         Cu²⁺ maujood
```

> **Figure 1.2** – Test ka teen kadam wala dhancha. Pehla kadam hum karte hain, doosra aankh dekhti hai, teesra dimaag nikalta hai. Teesra kadam sirf dikhe hue clue se nikalna hai, andaaze se nahi.

## Part B: Ions ko kyun test karte hain

Chapter 1 mein humne dekha tha ki har **salt** ek positive ion (cation, jo base se aata hai) aur ek negative ion (anion, jo acid se aata hai) ka jod hota hai. Paani mein ghulne par ye ion alag-alag ho jaate hain (Chapter 4 ke electrolysis wale ions). Isliye ghol ko pehchaanne ka matlab hai: **uske ions ko pehchaanna.**

### Example 2. Salt ke ions likhna

**Sawaal:** CuSO₄, FeCl₃ aur Cu(NO₃)₂ paani mein kaun se ion dete hain? Charge balance bhi dekho.

- CuSO₄ → Cu²⁺ + SO₄²⁻. Charge: (+2) + (−2) = 0 ✅.
- FeCl₃ → Fe³⁺ + 3Cl⁻. Charge: (+3) + 3 × (−1) = 0 ✅.
- Cu(NO₃)₂ → Cu²⁺ + 2NO₃⁻. Charge: (+2) + 2 × (−1) = 0 ✅.

**Jaanch:** har formula mein ions ka kul charge shunya (0) nikla, aur atom ginti: Cu(NO₃)₂ mein N 2, O 6, jo 2NO₃⁻ ke barabar ✅.

**Ek aham baat:** ek hi **cation** alag-alag salts ke saath ghol mein milta hai. Cu²⁺ kabhi SO₄²⁻ ke saath, kabhi NO₃⁻ ke saath. Jaasoos ka kaam hai ye poochna ki **cation kaun sa hai**, aur **anion kaun sa hai**, alag-alag. Is chapter mein hum ek-ek karke dono ko pehchaanna seekhenge. Is topic mein hum do hi cation dekhenge, **Cu²⁺ aur Fe³⁺**, aur Na⁺, SO₄²⁻, Cl⁻, NO₃⁻ ko sirf **darshak (spectator)** ki tarah. Ca²⁺ ko ek line mein.

## Part C: Do reagent, ek hi ion OH⁻

Metal ions ko pehchaanne ke liye sabse upyogi reagent hain **sodium hydroxide (NaOH)** aur **ammonium hydroxide (NH₄OH)**. Dono **alkali** hain, aur dono ke ghol mein **OH⁻** ion hote hain. Ye wahi OH⁻ hai jo humne Chemical Bonding II ke Topic 4 mein dekha tha: O aur H ka ek jhund jisme −1 charge hai.

Dono mein farq ye hai:

- **NaOH** ek **strong alkali** hai. Chapter 4 mein dekha tha ki ye **strong electrolyte** hai: paani mein lagbhag poora Na⁺ aur OH⁻ mein toot jaata hai. To ghol mein **bahut saare OH⁻** hote hain.
- **NH₄OH** ek **weak alkali** hai (Chapter 4 ka weak electrolyte): bahut kam toota hua, ⇌ wale tir ke saath: NH₄OH ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻. To ghol mein **OH⁻ bahut kam** hote hain.

```
   NaOH ghol (strong)              NH₄OH ghol (weak)
   8 NaOH mein se 8 toote          8 NH₄OH mein se 1 toota

   Na⁺ OH⁻   Na⁺ OH⁻               NH₄OH  NH₄OH  NH₄OH  NH₄OH
   Na⁺ OH⁻   Na⁺ OH⁻               NH₄OH  NH₄OH  NH₄OH  NH₄⁺ OH⁻
   Na⁺ OH⁻   Na⁺ OH⁻
   Na⁺ OH⁻   Na⁺ OH⁻               (OH⁻ sirf ek)
   (OH⁻ aath)
```

> **Figure 1.3** – Sirf ek tasveer: strong alkali mein sab toot kar OH⁻ dete hain, weak alkali mein bahut kam. Ginti: NaOH wale mein 8 Na⁺ aur 8 OH⁻; NH₄OH wale mein 7 poore NH₄OH aur 1 joda NH₄⁺ OH⁻. Asli ghol mein ginti lakhon-karodon ions ki hoti hai, aur ye 8-8 sirf samjhane ke liye hain.

Par metal hydroxide ko banne ke liye bahut zyada OH⁻ nahi, bas **thode se OH⁻ jo ghol mein Cu²⁺ ya Fe³⁺ ke paas pahunch jaayein** kaafi hote hain. Isliye **dono reagent** Cu²⁺ aur Fe³⁺ ko precipitate kar dete hain. Kitni boondein aur kya hota hai jab zyada daalo, ye farq **Topic 2 (NaOH)** aur **Topic 3 (NH₄OH)** mein aayega. Aaj hum bas ye dekhenge ki OH⁻ ke milte hi **metal hydroxide** ka precipitate kyun banta hai.

## Part D: Precipitate ka hisaab: ionic equation

Acids, Bases aur Salts ke Topic 5 mein humne precipitation padha tha: do **soluble** salt ke ghol milao, aur ions ki adla-badli se ek **insoluble** salt neeche baith jaata hai (BaCl₂ + Na₂SO₄ aur AgNO₃ + NaCl ke examples wahin the). Ab wahi idea hum **metal hydroxide** par lagate hain. Zyadatar metal hydroxide paani mein **insoluble** hote hain (wahin weak bases ki list mein Cu(OH)₂ aur Fe(OH)₃ ka naam aaya tha).

### Example 3. CuSO₄ + NaOH: sab ions alag karke

**Teacher demo (school lab mein teacher ke saath):** ek saaf test-tube mein CuSO₄ ka dilute neela ghol (ek-do mL). Teacher dropper se NaOH ki kuch boondein daalta hai. Ghol mein **halka neela (pale blue) precipitate** banta hai aur neeche baith jaata hai.

**Kadam 1: Molecular equation.**
CuSO₄ + 2NaOH → Cu(OH)₂↓ + Na₂SO₄

Atom ginti: Cu: 1 = 1; S: 1 = 1; O: 4 + 2 = 6 aur 2 + 4 = 6 ✅; Na: 2 = 2; H: 2 = 2 ✅.

**Kadam 2: Har soluble cheez ko uske ions mein todo** (Cu(OH)₂ insoluble hai, use ek saath hi rakho):

Cu²⁺ + SO₄²⁻ + 2Na⁺ + 2OH⁻ → Cu(OH)₂↓ + 2Na⁺ + SO₄²⁻

**Kadam 3: Dono taraf jo ion same hai, use hata do.** Na⁺ (2 aur 2) aur SO₄²⁻ (1 aur 1) dono taraf barabar hain. Ye hi **spectator ions** hain.

**Kadam 4: Net ionic equation.**

**Cu²⁺ + 2OH⁻ → Cu(OH)₂↓**

**Jaanch:** atom: Cu 1 = 1, O 2 = 2, H 2 = 2 ✅. Charge: baayen taraf (+2) + 2 × (−1) = 0; daayen taraf Cu(OH)₂ neutral = 0 ✅.

Ye net equation ka matlab: precipitate ke liye **sirf Cu²⁺ aur OH⁻ ka milna** zaroori hai.

### Example 4. FeCl₃ + NaOH

**Teacher demo (school lab mein teacher ke saath):** FeCl₃ ka dilute peela-bhura ghol aur NaOH ki boondein. Is baar **lal-bhura (reddish-brown) precipitate** banta hai.

Molecular: FeCl₃ + 3NaOH → Fe(OH)₃↓ + 3NaCl

Atom ginti: Fe 1 = 1; Cl 3 = 3; Na 3 = 3; O 3 = 3; H 3 = 3 ✅.

Ions alag karke: Fe³⁺ + 3Cl⁻ + 3Na⁺ + 3OH⁻ → Fe(OH)₃↓ + 3Na⁺ + 3Cl⁻

Spectator: 3Na⁺ aur 3Cl⁻ hata do. **Net:** **Fe³⁺ + 3OH⁻ → Fe(OH)₃↓**

**Jaanch:** atom: Fe 1, O 3, H 3 dono taraf ✅. Charge: baayen (+3) + 3 × (−1) = 0, daayen 0 ✅. Dhyan do: Fe³⁺ ko **3** OH⁻ chahiye (kyunki charge +3 hai), jabki Cu²⁺ ko sirf **2**. Charge balance hi batata hai kitne OH⁻ lagenge.

### Example 5. Spectator badal do, precipitate wahi

**Sawaal:** CuSO₄ ki jagah Cu(NO₃)₂ ka ghol lo aur NaOH daalo. Kya dikhega aur net equation kya hogi?

**Teacher demo:** Cu²⁺ ke saath ab SO₄²⁻ ki jagah NO₃⁻ hai.

Molecular: Cu(NO₃)₂ + 2NaOH → Cu(OH)₂↓ + 2NaNO₃. Atom ginti: Cu 1, N 2, O 6 + 2 = 8 aur 2 + 6 = 8 ✅, Na 2, H 2 ✅.

Spectator ab **Na⁺ aur NO₃⁻** hain. Hatane ke baad net equation wahi: **Cu²⁺ + 2OH⁻ → Cu(OH)₂↓**. Isliye wahi **pale blue precipitate** dikhega.

**Jaanch:** ek hi cation, do alag spectator, ek hi net equation aur ek hi precipitate ✅. Yani **precipitate cation aur OH⁻ ka hai**, spectator ka nahi.

### Ek line mein Ca²⁺

Calcium ion bhi isi tarah OH⁻ se milta hai: **Ca²⁺ + 2OH⁻ → Ca(OH)₂↓**. Par ye precipitate bahut halka hota hai: **safed, thoda sa** (slight). (Ca(OH)₂ paani mein kuch hi ghulta hai: Chapter 1 ka chune ka paani.) Ca²⁺ ki poori kahani Topic 2 mein.

**Jaanch:** atom Ca 1, O 2, H 2 dono taraf; charge (+2) + 2 × (−1) = 0 ✅.

```
   Hisaab ki ek jhalak (CuSO₄ + NaOH)

   Ghol mein pehle:     Cu²⁺   SO₄²⁻   Na⁺   OH⁻
                         :                      :
                         +------- milte hain ---+
                                    :
                                    v
                              Cu(OH)₂ (ppt)
   Bina badle rahe:             SO₄²⁻   Na⁺      <- spectator
```

> **Figure 1.4** – Cu²⁺ aur OH⁻ milkar Cu(OH)₂ ka precipitate banate hain. SO₄²⁻ aur Na⁺ dono taraf bina badle ghol mein rehte hain (spectator). Picture mein hum ek Cu²⁺ aur ek OH⁻ ka joda dikha rahe hain; asli equation mein 2 OH⁻ lagte hain (Example 3).

## Part E: Precipitate neeche baithta hai: test-tube ka chitra

Precipitate ek thos hai jo ghol mein ghulta nahi, to wo dheere-dheere **neeche baith jaata hai** aur upar saaf ghol bacha rehta hai. Ye saaf ghol (jisme Na⁺ aur SO₄²⁻ jaise spectator hain) aur thos precipitate ko alag karne ke do tareeke hain: **decant** (upar ka ghol dheere se nikaal lena) ya **filter paper** se chhaan lena. Ghar ke aate ki chhalni ki tarah: chhalni mein moti cheez ruk jaati hai, baaki neeche nikal jaata hai.

```
   PEHLE                       BAAD MEIN (NaOH ki boondein)

   :              :            :              :
   :   CuSO₄ ka   :            :  saaf ghol   :   <- Na⁺, SO₄²⁻ (spectator)
   :   ghol       :            :              :
   :   (neela)    :            :              :
   :              :            :  ##########  :   <- Cu(OH)₂ ppt
   '--------------'            '--------------'      (pale blue, neeche)
```

> **Figure 1.5** – Test-tube ka pehle aur baad ka chitra. Baad wale tube mein precipitate neeche (bottom) par baitha hai, aur upar ka ghol ab Cu²⁺ nahi rakhta, sirf spectator ions rakhta hai (agar NaOH sahi matra mein daala gaya ho). Rang ki jaankari lagbhag hai, dilute ghol mein.

### Lab ka tareeka (school lab mein teacher ke saath)

1. **Ek saaf test-tube** lo (ek-do mL ghol). Saaf isliye ki purana koi ion mil kar galat clue na de.
2. **Dropper se** NaOH ki **kuch boondein** daalo. Teacher hi NaOH deta hai.
3. Jo dikhe, use **likh lo** (observation): rang, precipitate, ya kuch nahi.
4. Phir **zyada** NaOH daalkar dekho ki precipitate ka kya hota hai. Cu(OH)₂ aur Fe(OH)₃ ke precipitate zyada NaOH mein bhi **nahi ghulte**; kuch aur hydroxide ghul jaate hain, jo Topic 2 ki kahani hai.
5. Teen sawaal poochho: kya daala, kya dikha, matlab kya hua.

**Suraksha (strict):** Ye saari tests **school lab mein teacher ke saath** hain (goggles, gloves, dilute ghol, teacher bottle se dispense karta hai). NaOH ka ghol, dilute bhi, **corrosive** hai: aankh ya skin par lage to turant paani se dhokar teacher ko batao. Copper ke salt **nigalne par nuksaan** karte hain, isliye kabhi muh ke paas mat laao. Kuch bhi **chakhna nahi**, aur tube ke muh par naak laga kar sungna nahi (teacher haath se gas ko dheere se apni taraf hawa karke sungne ka "waft" tareeka dikhata hai: sirf padhne ke liye). **Ghol sink mein nahi phenkna**: heavy-metal waste teacher sambhalta hai. Aage ke topics mein lead, barium aur silver ke compounds aayenge: ye **zehreele** hote hain, student kabhi nahi chhoota.

### Example 6. Kitna precipitate banega: mass ka hisaab

**Sawaal (teacher demo, exact reacting matra):** 1.595 g CuSO₄ ko NaOH ki theek utni matra se milaya gaya ki dono poore kaam aayen. Kitna Cu(OH)₂ banega aur kitna NaOH lagega? (Cu 63.5, S 32, O 16, H 1, Na 23.)

- CuSO₄ ka molar mass = 63.5 + 32 + 4 × 16 = 63.5 + 32 + 64 = **159.5 g/mol**.
- Mole = 1.595 / 159.5 = **0.01 mol**.
- Equation CuSO₄ + 2NaOH → Cu(OH)₂↓ + Na₂SO₄: 1 mol CuSO₄ ke liye 2 mol NaOH, aur 1 mol Cu(OH)₂.
- NaOH = 2 × 0.01 = **0.02 mol** × 40 g/mol = **0.8 g**.
- Cu(OH)₂ ka molar mass = 63.5 + 2 × (16 + 1) = 63.5 + 34 = **97.5 g/mol**. Mass = 0.01 × 97.5 = **0.975 g**.

**Jaanch:** Na₂SO₄ = 0.01 × 142 = 1.42 g. Reaction se pehle: 1.595 + 0.8 = **2.395 g**; reaction ke baad: 0.975 + 1.42 = **2.395 g** ✅ (Mass conserve).

### Example 7. Observation se conclusion

**Sawaal:** Ek teacher demo mein ek ghol mein NaOH ki boondein daali gayin aur **reddish-brown precipitate** neeche baitha. Ghol mein Cu²⁺ ya Fe³⁺ mein se kaun sa hai? Net equation likho.

**Jaanch:** Cu(OH)₂ ka rang pale blue hai (Example 3), isliye reddish-brown Cu(OH)₂ ka nahi ho sakta ✅. Atom aur charge pehle jaise barabar ✅.

## Aam galtiyan (bachke rehna)

**Galti: "Precipitate akele metal se banta hai (OH⁻ ke bina), ya spectator ion bhi precipitate mein shaamil hote hain."**
Ye sach lagta hai kyunki ghol neela hota hai aur precipitate bhi neela dikhta hai, to lagta hai ki "copper ne apne aap thos ban kar baith gaya". Par ye galat hai. Teen chhote demo:

1. **Sirf Cu²⁺, bina OH⁻:** ek test-tube mein CuSO₄ ka ghol lo aur usme sirf **paani** daalo (teacher demo). Cu²⁺ ghol mein hai, par **koi precipitate nahi banta**. Ghol bas halka ho jaata hai. Ab ek doosre tube mein usi ghol mein NaOH daalo: precipitate bana. Farq sirf **OH⁻** ka tha. Isliye **precipitate ke liye OH⁻ zaroori hai.**
2. **Spectator ka kaam:** Example 3 ke tube ka upar ka saaf ghol alag karo (usme ab Na⁺ aur SO₄²⁻ hain, kyunki Cu²⁺ poora precipitate ban chuka). Usme aur NaOH daalne par **koi naya precipitate nahi** banta, kyunki ab Cu²⁺ bacha hi nahi. Na⁺ ne OH⁻ ke saath koi thos nahi banaya (NaOH ghulansheel hai), aur SO₄²⁻ ne bhi nahi. Net equation mein Na⁺ aur SO₄²⁻ **likhe hi nahi gaye**, yahi ishaara hai ki wo ghol mein hi rah gaye.
3. **Spectator badalne par bhi wahi:** CuSO₄ ki jagah Cu(NO₃)₂ lo (Example 5): precipitate ka rang aur net equation wahi rahe, jabki spectator SO₄²⁻ ki jagah NO₃⁻ ho gaya. Agar spectator precipitate ka hissa hote, to unke badalne se precipitate badal jaata.

**Niyam:** precipitate = **cation + OH⁻** (hydroxide). Spectator ions ghol mein hi rehte hain.

## Quick Recap

- **Analysis**: **qualitative** = "kya hai?", **quantitative** = "kitna hai?" (Chapter 5 ka mole hisaab). Is chapter mein mukhya kaam qualitative.
- Har **test** = reagent daalo, observation (rang, precipitate, gas) likho, conclusion nikalo. Teen sawaal: **kya daala, kya dikha, matlab kya hua**.
- Salt = cation + anion, aur ghol mein ions alag. Hum ions ko pehchaante hain.
- **NaOH strong alkali** (bahut OH⁻), **NH₄OH weak alkali** (kam OH⁻), dono metal hydroxide ka precipitate bana sakte hain.
- **Cu²⁺ + 2OH⁻ → Cu(OH)₂↓** (pale blue); **Fe³⁺ + 3OH⁻ → Fe(OH)₃↓** (reddish-brown); **Ca²⁺ + 2OH⁻ → Ca(OH)₂↓** (thoda safed).
- **Spectator ions** (Na⁺, SO₄²⁻, Cl⁻, NO₃⁻) dono taraf same rehte hain aur net equation mein nahi likhe jaate.
- Precipitate neeche baithta hai; **decant ya filter** se alag karte hain.
- Test: **school lab mein teacher ke saath**, dilute ghol, goggles, gloves, kabhi chakhna ya sungna nahi.

## Practice Questions

(Sab sawaal paper par hal karne ke liye hain. Atomic masses: H 1, N 14, O 16, Na 23, S 32, Cl 35.5, Fe 56, Cu 63.5.)

1. Classify each task as qualitative or quantitative: (a) deciding whether a solution contains Fe³⁺, (b) finding that a sample contains 0.03 mol of NaOH, (c) checking which of two bottles holds a copper salt.
2. Write the ions that iron(III) nitrate, Fe(NO₃)₃, gives in water and show the charge balance. Write the molecular equation for its reaction with NaOH, name the spectator ions and verify the atom count.
3. Write the equation for NH₄OH in water (use the ⇌ arrow), verify atoms and charge, and explain in two lines why this weak alkali can still precipitate Cu²⁺ even though it gives few OH⁻.
4. A student says: "In Cu²⁺ + 2OH⁻ → Cu(OH)₂, the sodium ion makes the precipitate because NaOH was added." Use the equation and the idea of spectator ions to show why this is wrong.
5. Here, 3.75 g of Cu(NO₃)₂ is mixed with exactly the amount of NaOH needed to react with all of it. Find the moles of Cu(NO₃)₂, the mass of NaOH needed, and the mass of Cu(OH)₂ formed. Check by adding masses (NaNO₃ is also formed).
6. Here, 1.07 g of Fe(OH)₃ is formed by the reaction of FeCl₃ and NaOH. Find the moles of Fe(OH)₃, the mass of FeCl₃ used, and the mass of NaOH used, assuming both reacted completely in the exact ratio.
7. In a model, there are 6 Cu²⁺ ions. How many OH⁻ ions are needed to precipitate all of them, and how many units of Cu(OH)₂ form? Do the same for 7 Fe³⁺ ions.
8. State True or False and correct the false ones: (a) NH₄OH cannot give a precipitate because it is a weak alkali. (b) The spectator ions are written in the net ionic equation. (c) Fe³⁺ needs 3 OH⁻ to form its hydroxide. (d) A precipitate settles at the bottom of the test tube. (e) Adding only water to CuSO₄ solution produces Cu(OH)₂.
9. A teacher adds NaOH drops to a solution and a pale blue precipitate forms. A student concludes "Fe³⁺". Find the error, state the correct conclusion, and write the net ionic equation.
10. Write the net ionic equation of Ca²⁺ with OH⁻ and describe the precipitate. Verify atoms and charge.
11. Using the three questions (what is added, what is seen, what does it tell), fill a small table for a test in which a few drops of NaOH are added to a solution that gives a reddish-brown precipitate.
12. Explain in 2 to 3 lines why the test tube must be clean and why the teacher, not the student, dispenses the NaOH solution. Also state what must be done with the left-over solution.
13. Make a paper game with no chemicals at home with an adult: three identical lunch boxes and three clues. Describe the clues and how a friend will identify each box using only the clues.
14. Describe how a precipitate is separated from the liquid above it, with a sketch using lines such as slashes (no line should start with a vertical bar).

### Answers

1. (a) **Qualitative**: "hai ya nahi." (b) **Quantitative**: "0.03 mol" ek sankhya hai. (c) **Qualitative**: kaun si bottle mein copper salt hai. Jaanch: jawab naam ya haan/nahi ho to qualitative, sankhya ho to quantitative ✅.
2. Fe(NO₃)₃ → Fe³⁺ + 3NO₃⁻. Charge: (+3) + 3 × (−1) = **0** ✅. Molecular: Fe(NO₃)₃ + 3NaOH → Fe(OH)₃↓ + 3NaNO₃ (reddish-brown precipitate). Spectator: **Na⁺ aur NO₃⁻**. Jaanch (atom ginti): Fe 1 = 1, N 3 = 3, O 9 + 3 = 12 aur 3 + 9 = 12, Na 3 = 3, H 3 = 3 ✅.
3. **NH₄OH ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻.** Atom: N 1, H 5, O 1 dono taraf; charge 0 = (+1) + (−1) ✅. Ghol mein OH⁻ kam hain, par precipitate banne ke liye bas utne OH⁻ chahiye jo Cu²⁺ ke paas pahunch kar Cu²⁺ + 2OH⁻ → Cu(OH)₂↓ poora kar dein; jaise-jaise OH⁻ kaam aate hain, aur NH₄OH toot kar naye OH⁻ deta jaata hai. (Ye detail Topic 3 mein.)
4. Net equation mein Na⁺ **hai hi nahi**: Na⁺ dono taraf ghol mein bina badle rehta hai (spectator). Precipitate Cu²⁺ aur OH⁻ ka hai. NaOH sirf **OH⁻ ka source** hai. Jaanch: Cu²⁺ + 2OH⁻ → Cu(OH)₂↓ mein atom aur charge barabar ✅, aur Na ka koi atom precipitate mein nahi.
5. Cu(NO₃)₂ ka molar mass = 63.5 + 2 × (14 + 48) = 63.5 + 124 = **187.5**. Mole = 3.75 / 187.5 = **0.02 mol**. NaOH = 2 × 0.02 = 0.04 mol × 40 = **1.6 g**. Cu(OH)₂ = 0.02 × 97.5 = **1.95 g**. NaNO₃ = 0.04 × 85 = 3.4 g. Jaanch: pehle 3.75 + 1.6 = **5.35 g**; baad mein 1.95 + 3.4 = **5.35 g** ✅.
6. Fe(OH)₃ ka molar mass = 56 + 3 × 17 = 56 + 51 = **107**. Mole = 1.07 / 107 = **0.01 mol**. FeCl₃ + 3NaOH → Fe(OH)₃ + 3NaCl: FeCl₃ = 0.01 mol × (56 + 106.5 = 162.5) = **1.625 g**. NaOH = 3 × 0.01 = 0.03 mol × 40 = **1.2 g**. NaCl = 0.03 × 58.5 = 1.755 g. Jaanch: 1.625 + 1.2 = **2.825 g**; 1.07 + 1.755 = **2.825 g** ✅.
7. Cu²⁺ ko 2 OH⁻ chahiye: 6 × 2 = **12 OH⁻**, aur **6 Cu(OH)₂** units. Fe³⁺ ko 3 OH⁻: 7 × 3 = **21 OH⁻**, aur **7 Fe(OH)₃** units. Jaanch: 6 Cu²⁺ ka charge +12, 12 OH⁻ ka −12, kul 0 ✅; 7 Fe³⁺ ka +21, 21 OH⁻ ka −21 ✅.
8. (a) **False** ❌: NH₄OH mein OH⁻ kam hain par fir bhi Cu²⁺ aur Fe³⁺ ke hydroxide bana dete hain (Part C). (b) **False** ❌: spectator net equation mein nahi likhe jaate. (c) **True** ✅: Fe³⁺ ka charge +3. (d) **True** ✅. (e) **False** ❌: paani mein OH⁻ nahi diya gaya, precipitate nahi banta.
9. Pale blue precipitate **Cu(OH)₂** ka rang hai, Fe(OH)₃ ka nahi (wo reddish-brown). Sahi conclusion: **Cu²⁺**. Net: **Cu²⁺ + 2OH⁻ → Cu(OH)₂↓**. Jaanch: atom Cu 1, O 2, H 2; charge 0 = 0 ✅.
10. **Ca²⁺ + 2OH⁻ → Ca(OH)₂↓**: **safed, thoda sa (slight)** precipitate. Jaanch: Ca 1, O 2, H 2 dono taraf; charge (+2) + 2 × (−1) = 0 ✅.
11. Table: **Kya daala:** NaOH ki kuch boondein. **Kya dikha:** reddish-brown precipitate. **Matlab:** Fe³⁺ maujood (Fe(OH)₃). Jaanch: Fe³⁺ + 3OH⁻ → Fe(OH)₃↓ ✅.
12. Tube **saaf** hona chahiye kyunki purana koi ion (jaise Cu²⁺ ke nishaan) galat clue de sakta hai. **Teacher** isliye dispense karta hai kyunki NaOH ka ghol dilute hone par bhi aankh aur skin ke liye **corrosive** hai. Bacha hua ghol **sink mein nahi phenkna**: teacher heavy-metal waste ko sambhalta hai.
13. Udaharan: teen ek jaise lunch box. Clue 1: ek par laal sticker. Clue 2: doosre ke dhakkan par ek chhota sa nishaan. Clue 3: teesre ke andar ek chhote kagaz par naam. Dost ek-ek clue dekh kar (kya dekha, matlab kya hua) har box ko uske maalik se milaayega, bina box khole. Koi chemical nahi chahiye.
14. Precipitate neeche baith jaata hai, to upar ka saaf ghol **dheere se nikaal liya** (decant) ya **filter paper** se chhaan liya. Sketch:
