# Topic 4: Ek Compound, Teen Bond: OH⁻, NaOH aur NH₄Cl

Ab tak humne bond ko ek-ek karke dekha: Class 9 mein ionic (de-le) aur covalent (baanto), is chapter mein polar covalent (barabar nahi baanta) aur coordinate (ek ne dono electron diye). Dikkat ye hai ki asli compounds aksar **sirf ek tarah ke bond se nahi bane hote**. Ek hi formula ke andar **do ya teen tarah ke bond** saath rehte hain, jaise ek bade model ke andar chhote-chhote models. Aaj hum yahi dekhenge: bond ginna aur batana ki **kaun sa bond kahan hai**.

## Block-set ka bada model

Mr. Barve apne bhatije Mandar ke saath ghar ke drawing room mein baithe the. Mandar ke paas ek building-block set tha aur usne ek bada ghar ka model banaya tha. Model ke andar ek chhoti si "gaadi" bhi thi, jo laal aur safed blocks ko pakka jodkar bani thi. Mandar ne model uthaya to gaadi model se alag hi nahi hui, wo ek **pakka tukda** thi.

Mandar ne dekha, "Chacha, ye gaadi to ek hi cheez ki tarah chipki hai, par poora ghar ke saath wo sirf halke se jodi gayi hai." Mr. Barve ne sir hilaya. "Bilkul sahi. Ab socho: ek **chhota set** (gaadi) kayi blocks se bana hai, aur wo chhota set **bade model ka ek hissa** hai. Model mein **do tarah ke jod** hain: gaadi ke **andar** ke jod pakke, aur gaadi aur ghar ke **beech** ka jod alag tarah ka."

"Chacha, kya compounds mein bhi aisa hota hai?" Mr. Barve muskuraye. "Haan! Aise hi kuch compounds mein ek **chhota atoms ka jhund** hota hai, jiske **andar** covalent bond pakke jude hote hain. Aur wo jhund poora ek **ion** ban jaata hai, jise bahar ke kisi ion se ionic bond jodta hai. Chalo, dekhte hain ki aisa kaun sa compound hai."

```
   Bada model (ek compound)
   =====================================================
      [ chhota set A ]    ======    [ chhota set B ]
      (laal + safed                 (akela peela block)
       blocks pakke jude)
   =====================================================
   set A ke ANDAR ke jod  : pakke, share kiye hue (covalent)
   set A aur set B ke BEECH ka jod : ulte charge ka aakarshan (ionic)
```

> **Figure 4.1** – Block-set ka analogy: ek chhote jhund ke andar ke jod aur bade model mein do hisson ke beech ka jod do alag kisam ke hote hain. Compound mein bhi ek hi formula ke andar covalent aur ionic dono bond ho sakte hain. Ye sirf ek analogy hai.

## Part A: Polyatomic ion kya hai?

Class 9 mein ion ka matlab tha **ek atom** jo electron de ya le: Na⁺, Cl⁻, Mg²⁺, O²⁻. Ye sab **ek-atom ke ion** the. Par ek aur tarah ka ion bhi hota hai: **atoms ka jhund jisne ek electron zyada le liya (ya ek electron kho diya)**. Is jhund ke atoms ek-doosre se **covalent bond** se jude hote hain, aur poore jhund par **ek hi charge** hota hai. Isko **polyatomic ion** kehte hain: "poly" = bahut, "atomic" = atoms wala.

Is jhund ke bahar jaakar wo **ek hi ion ki tarah** kaam karta hai: jab kisi aur ion se milta hai ya paani mein ghulta hai, to poora jhund saath rehta hai, tootkar alag nahi hota.

```
   Ek-atom ka ion          Polyatomic ion (atoms ka jhund)
   ---------------         --------------------------------
   Na⁺   Cl⁻   Mg²⁺        OH⁻  (2 atom: O, H)
   (andar koi bond nahi)   NH₄⁺ (5 atom: N, 4 H)
                           (andar covalent bond hain)
```

Is topic mein hum do polyatomic ion padhenge: **OH⁻** aur **NH₄⁺**. NH₄⁺ ko Topic 3 mein coordinate bond ke saath dekha tha, aur OH⁻ ke saath wo hamare do alag compounds (NaOH aur NH₄Cl) banayenge.

## Part B: Hydroxide ion OH⁻ kaise bana hai?

Ek **O** atom aur ek **H** atom milkar apne aap ek stable jhund nahi banate, kyunki **O** (2, 6) ko octet ke liye 2 electron chahiye aur H ke paas sirf 1. Agar H ek electron share kare to O ke paas 7 ho jaate hain (6 + 1), ek kam rah jaata hai.

Ab ek **extra electron** (kisi dusre atom se aaya hua) O ke paas aa jaaye to O ke paas ginti **8** ho jaati hai. Ye **ek extra electron** poore jhund ko **−1 charge** de deta hai. Is tarah bana **OH⁻**.

Dot-aur-cross diagram mein dekho. Hum O ke apne 6 electron **dot** se, H ke 1 electron **x** se, aur bahar se aaya extra electron **o** se dikhate hain.

```
                ..
          [  o. O .x H  ]⁻
                ..
```

> **Figure 4.2** – Hydroxide ion OH⁻. O ke apne 6 dots (upar 2, neeche 2, baayen 1, daayen 1), H ka x aur extra electron o. O ke aas-paas 4 jodi: upar, neeche, baayen (o aur ek dot) aur daayen (ek dot aur H ka x, yahi shared pair hai). Kul electrons 8 = 6 + 1 + 1.

Ginti: O ke paas **3 lone pairs** (6 electron) aur **1 shared pair** (2 electron) = **8** (octet). H ke paas shared pair ke 2 electron = **duplet**. Dash mein ise **[ O–H ]⁻** likhte hain, aur O par 3 lone pairs hain.

### Example 1. OH⁻ ki ginti

**Sawaal:** OH⁻ mein kul kitne electron hain (sirf baahari shell ke), aur ye kahan se aaye?

- O ke baahari electron: 6. H ke electron: 1. Extra electron (−1 charge ka kaaran): 1.
- Kul = 6 + 1 + 1 = **8**.
- Ye 8 electron 4 jodi mein baant gaye: 3 lone pairs (6 e) + 1 shared pair (2 e).

**Jaanch:** 6 + 2 = 8 O ke paas ✅, H ke paas 2 ✅, 6 + 1 + 1 = 8 = 6 + 2 ✅.

### Example 2. O–H bond kitna ek taraf?

Electronegativity: O = 3.5, H = 2.1. Farq = 3.5 − 2.1 = **1.4**. Topic 1 ki thumb rule se ye **polar covalent** bond hai: shared pair O ki taraf zyada khisakta hai, to O par δ⁻ aur H par δ⁺ (lekin poore jhund ka kul charge −1 hai, wo alag baat hai). Ye bond ionic nahi hai (farq itna bada nahi), isliye **O aur H alag-alag ion nahi bante**: OH⁻ ek hi jhund ki tarah rehta hai.

**Jaanch:** 3.5 − 2.1 = 1.4 ✅, aur 1.4 "lagbhag 0.5 se 1.7" ke beech hai, to polar covalent ✅.

## Part C: NaOH: ionic aur covalent saath

Ab OH⁻ ko ek **Na** atom ke saath milao. **Na** (2, 8, 1) ka ek baahari electron dena aasan hai (Chapter 6 ka Topic 2). Wo **ek electron OH jhund ko de deta hai**, jo upar wala "extra electron" ban jaata hai. Na ban jaata hai **Na⁺** aur jhund ban jaata hai **OH⁻**. Dono ulte charge ke hain, to ionic bond se jud jaate hain: **NaOH**.

```
   Na (2,8,1)       +      O-H  (7 electron: O 6 + H 1)

         Na  ----- ek electron ----->  [ O-H ]
         Na⁺                              OH⁻
         (2,8: 8 baahari)                 (8 electron, octet)

   Na⁺  :  OH⁻     <-- ulte charge ka aakarshan  (IONIC bond)
            O - H  <-- shared pair, polar          (COVALENT bond)
```

> **Figure 4.3** – NaOH ki banawat: Na ek electron OH jhund ko deta hai. Na⁺ aur OH⁻ ke beech ionic bond hai, aur OH⁻ ke andar O–H covalent bond hai. Ek hi formula, do kisam ke bond.

### Example 3. NaOH mein bond ginna (step method)

**Kadam 1 (ions dhundo):** NaOH → Na⁺ aur OH⁻. Charge balance: (+1) + (−1) = 0 ✅.
**Kadam 2 (har ion ke andar dekho):** Na⁺ ek atom hai, andar koi bond nahi. OH⁻ ke andar 1 **O–H covalent bond**.
**Kadam 3 (coordinate bond?):** O ke paas lone pairs hain, par H ko **koi aur** electron nahi chahiye (uska duplet poora hai) aur kisi ke paas khaali jagah nahi. Isliye **koi coordinate bond nahi**.
**Kadam 4 (ginti):**

| Bond ka naam | Kahan | Ginti (ek NaOH mein) |
|---|---|---|
| Ionic | Na⁺ aur OH⁻ ke beech | 1 |
| Covalent (O–H) | OH⁻ ke andar | 1 |
| Coordinate | koi nahi | 0 |

**Jaanch:** ions ka charge 0 ✅; OH⁻ ke andar 8 electron ginne se octet/duplet ✅. Atom ginti: Na 1, O 1, H 1 ✅. Mass: 23 + 16 + 1 = **40**.

### Example 4. Bond ka swabhav electronegativity se

Na aur O ka farq: 3.5 − 0.9 = **2.6**, bahut bada. Isliye **Na⁺ aur OH⁻ ke beech ionic** jaisa bond hona samajh aata hai (Chapter 6 Topic 4 wala "bahut bada farq"). Par O–H ka farq sirf **1.4** hai: wo covalent hi rehta hai. Do alag farq, do alag swabhav, ek hi formula mein.

**Jaanch:** 3.5 − 0.9 = 2.6 ✅ (bahut bada) aur 3.5 − 2.1 = 1.4 ✅ (beech ka). Isliye ek compound mein dono tarah ka swabhav milta hai.

**Ek aur baat (honest note):** NaOH ke crystal mein ek Na⁺ ke aas-paas kayi OH⁻ aur ek OH⁻ ke aas-paas kayi Na⁺ hote hain (Class 9 ka lattice). "1 ionic bond" ka matlab hai **ek formula unit ke liye ek Na⁺–OH⁻ ka joda**, poore crystal ki ginti nahi.

## Part D: NH₄Cl: teeno kisam ke bond ek saath

Ye topic ka sabse achha example hai. **NH₄Cl** (ammonium chloride) ek safed thos namak jaisa compound hai. Isme **teeno** bond milte hain: ionic, saadha covalent aur coordinate.

Pehle ye dekhte hain ki ye bana kaise. Ammonia gas (NH₃) aur hydrogen chloride (HCl) milkar NH₄Cl dete hain: NH₃ + HCl → NH₄Cl. (Dono gas zehreeli aur gala jalane wali hain: sirf padhne ke liye, kabhi ghar par nahi.) Atom ginti: N: 1 = 1, H: 3 + 1 = 4 = 4, Cl: 1 = 1 ✅.

Is reaction mein NH₃ ne H⁺ liya (Topic 3: N ka lone pair donor) aur NH₄⁺ bana. Cl se bana Cl⁻. Dono ulte charge ke ion, to ionic bond.

### NH₄⁺ ke andar kya hai?

NH₄⁺ mein **4 N–H bond** hain: **3 saadhe covalent** (N ne apne electron aur H ne apna electron diya) aur **1 coordinate** (N ke lone pair se, Topic 3). Charge +1 kyunki ek H⁺ jud gaya jiske paas koi electron nahi tha. Ye chaaron bond ek jaise hain (Topic 3), par **ginti ke liye** hum unhe do group mein baant lete hain taaki **kaun sa bond kaise bana** ye gina ja sake.

Electron ginti: N ke 5 + 4 H ke 4 = 9 electron, lekin charge +1 hai (ek kam), to **8** electron NH₄⁺ ke paas hain. Ye 8 electron **4 shared pairs** hain: N ke paas **octet** (4 jodi = 8), har H ke paas **duplet**.

Cl⁻: Cl ke 7 + 1 (lene wala) = **8** electron, **4 lone pairs**, octet.

```
                    H
                    :
         [ H ----   N  ---> H ]⁺      Cl⁻  (4 lone pairs)
                    :                 (8 electron)
                    H

   N : H  --->  3 saadhe covalent  (dash --)
   N ---> H :  1 coordinate        (tir -->, N se H ki taraf)
   NH₄⁺  :  Cl⁻  :  ionic bond
```

> **Figure 4.4** – NH₄Cl: NH₄⁺ ke andar 4 N–H bond (3 saadhe, 1 coordinate tir se) aur NH₄⁺ aur Cl⁻ ke beech ek ionic bond. NH₄⁺ ka kul charge +1, Cl⁻ ka −1, to NH₄Cl neutral. (Asli ion ka 3D aakaar aage ki class mein.)

### Example 5. NH₄Cl ke bond ginna

**Kadam 1:** NH₄Cl → NH₄⁺ aur Cl⁻. Charge: (+1) + (−1) = 0 ✅.
**Kadam 2:** NH₄⁺ ke andar N–H bond: kul 4. Cl⁻ ek-atom ion hai, andar koi bond nahi.
**Kadam 3:** In 4 mein se 1 bond coordinate hai (N ka lone pair H⁺ ko mila), baaki 3 saadhe covalent.
**Kadam 4:** ginti:

| Bond ka naam | Kahan | Ginti (ek NH₄Cl mein) |
|---|---|---|
| Ionic | NH₄⁺ aur Cl⁻ ke beech | 1 |
| Saadha covalent (N–H) | NH₄⁺ ke andar | 3 |
| Coordinate (N → H) | NH₄⁺ ke andar | 1 |
| **Kul bond** | | **5** |

**Jaanch:** NH₄⁺ mein 3 + 1 = 4 bond = 4 H ✅. Electron: NH₄⁺ ke 8 + Cl⁻ ke 8 = **16**, aur neutral atoms ke baahari electron N 5 + 4 H (4) + Cl 7 = **16** ✅.

### Example 6. Electronegativity se N–H aur ionic bond

N–H ka farq: 3.0 − 2.1 = **0.9**, to saadhe N–H bond polar covalent (N par δ⁻). NH₄⁺ ko Cl⁻ jodne wale bond mein dono ions poore hain, to wo ionic hai (ion-ion aakarshan, electronegativity ka sawaal nahi, kyunki Cl⁻ NH₄⁺ ke kisi atom ke saath electron share nahi kar raha).

**Jaanch:** 3.0 − 2.1 = 0.9 ✅, ye 0.5 se 1.7 ke beech hai to polar covalent ✅.

### Example 7. HCl paani mein: ek aur jhund wala ion

Topic 2 mein dekha tha ki HCl paani mein ions mein toot jaata hai. Ek aur tarah se likhein to ye **H₃O⁺** aur **Cl⁻** hain: H₃O⁺ (hydronium) bhi ek polyatomic ion hai, jo H₂O ne H⁺ lekar banaya (Topic 3). (HCl gas aur uska ghol dono tez hain: sirf padhne ke liye.) Count karein: H₃O⁺ ke andar 3 bond: 2 saadhe covalent O–H aur 1 coordinate O → H. Cl⁻ ke saath ionic bond: 1.

Electron ginti: O 6 + 3 H 3 = 9, charge +1 hai to 9 − 1 = **8** electron: 3 shared pairs (6 e) aur 1 lone pair (2 e). O ka octet poora ✅.

**Jaanch:** 3 bond = 3 H ✅, electron 8 ✅.

## Part E: Kisi bhi formula mein bond ginne ka tareeka

Ab hum seekhe hue ko ek **chaar-kadam ke niyam** mein likh lete hain.

1. **Ions dhundo.** Formula ko charge se todo. Metal ya NH₄ aam taur par positive ion banata hai, aur Cl, OH jaise hisse negative. Kul charge 0 hona chahiye.
2. **Har ion ke andar dekho.** Agar ion ek hi atom ka hai (Na⁺, Cl⁻) to andar koi bond nahi. Agar jhund hai (OH⁻, NH₄⁺) to andar covalent bond ginte hain: har dash = ek bond.
3. **Coordinate bond dhundo.** Kya kisi atom ne dono electron diye (lone pair donor) aur kisi ke paas khaali jagah thi (acceptor, jaise H⁺)? NH₄⁺ mein 1 aur H₃O⁺ mein 1 mil gaya.
4. **Ginti likho:** ionic kitne, saadhe covalent kitne, coordinate kitne.

```
   Formula --->  ions  --->  ion ke andar covalent  --->  coordinate?  --->  ginti
   NaOH         Na⁺ OH⁻       1 (O-H)                    koi nahi             ionic 1, cov 1, coord 0
   NH₄Cl        NH₄⁺ Cl⁻     4 (N-H)                    1 (N → H)            ionic 1, cov 3, coord 1
```

> **Figure 4.5** – Chaar-kadam wali ginti: har formula ke liye ions, ion ke andar ke covalent, coordinate bond aur phir ginti. Do examples saath mein.

### Bracket wale formula

Ca(OH)₂ ka matlab: **1 Ca** aur **2 OH jhund** (bracket ke andar ka poora jhund 2 baar). Atom ginti: Ca 1, O 2, H 2. Charge: Ca²⁺ aur do OH⁻: (+2) + 2 × (−1) = 0 ✅. Isliye bracket zaroori hai: Ca(OH)₂ aur CaOH₂ alag cheezein hain (CaOH₂ ka matlab sirf 1 O aur 2 H, jo OH jhund wala nahi). Is par practice mein dekhenge.

## Part F: Ye compounds ionic salt jaise kyun behave karte hain?

NaOH aur NH₄Cl dono **ions ke crystal** (lattice) se bane hain: Na⁺ aur OH⁻ ya NH₄⁺ aur Cl⁻ ek regular jaali mein. Isliye ye **ionic compounds** ke gun dikhate hain (Class 9: thos, kadak, ghol mein bijli). NaOH ek kadak safed thos hai jise pighlane ke liye kaafi garmi chahiye. (NH₄Cl garam karne par pighalta nahi, balki toot kar NH₃ aur HCl gas ban kar udta hai aur thande hisse par phir jud jaata hai: ye sirf padhne ke liye, ghar par kabhi garam nahi karna.) Sab bond ki tulna aur melting ke numbers Topic 5 mein ek table mein.

Par ek bahut zaroori baat: **paani mein ghulne par kya tootta hai?**

```
   NaOH  (paani mein)  --->   Na⁺   +   OH⁻
   NH₄Cl (paani mein)  --->   NH₄⁺  +   Cl⁻

   Ionic bond TOOTA (ions alag ho gaye).
   Ion ke ANDAR ke covalent bond (O-H, N-H) NAHI TOOTE:
   OH⁻ aur NH₄⁺ poore jhund ki tarah ghol mein ghoomte hain.
```

> **Figure 4.6** – Paani mein ionic bond toot-ta hai, ion ke andar ke covalent bond (O–H, N–H aur coordinate N → H) nahi. Isliye ghol mein Na⁺, OH⁻, NH₄⁺, Cl⁻ jaise **poore ions** milte hain.

Isi wajah se Chapter 4 (electrolysis) mein NaOH ko **strong electrolyte** kaha tha: lagbhag poora Na⁺ aur OH⁻ mein tootta hai. NH₄Cl bhi ek salt hai aur paani mein ions deta hai. NH₄OH (ammonium hydroxide) ko wahan **weak electrolyte** kaha tha, kyunki wo kam toot-ta hai, aur uske ions NH₄⁺ aur OH⁻ hain.

## Khud karke dekho: block se model

(Ghar par adult ke saath. Koi chemical nahi, sirf building blocks ya Lego.)

Do alag rang ke blocks lo: **laal** (O) aur **safed** (H). Unhe pakke jodo: ye ek **OH jhund** hai. Uspar ek chhoti chit lagao "−1". Ek **peela** block lo (Na) aur uspar chit "+1". Ab peele block ko OH jhund ke paas rakh do (halke se, ya ek chhote magnet se jodo): ye hai **NaOH**. Dhyan do: laal-safed ke beech ka jod pakka, peele aur laal-safed ke beech ka jod halka.

Ab NH₄Cl ke liye ek **hare** block (N) ke chaaron taraf 4 **safed** blocks jodo, aur ek safed ko **ek alag rang ke dhaage** (tir) se jodo ye dikhane ke liye ki wo coordinate hai. Poore jhund par chit "+1". Ek **neela** block (Cl) par "−1" lagao aur use halke se paas rakho. Ginti karo: kitne pakke jod, kitne tir, kitne halke jod? (Jawab: 3 pakke, 1 tir, 1 halka.)

## Aam galtiyan (bachke rehna)

**Galti: "Jis compound mein metal hai, wo purely ionic hai, aur kahin koi covalent bond nahi."**
Ye sach lagta hai kyunki NaCl, MgO aur Na₂O jaise Class 9 ke examples mein metal ke saath sirf ionic bond tha. Par ye **hamesha sahi nahi**. Demo teen kadam mein:

1. **NaOH ko lo.** Isme metal hai (Na). Ab ions todo: Na⁺ aur OH⁻. OH⁻ ke andar O aur H ke beech **share kiya hua electron-jodi** hai: ye covalent bond hai (Part B). To NaOH mein **1 ionic aur 1 covalent** bond hai. Purely ionic nahi.
2. **Na₂O se tulna karo** (Class 9 se). Na₂O mein 2 Na⁺ aur 1 O²⁻ hain. Dono ek-atom ke ion hain, to **andar koi covalent bond nahi**: ye sach mein **sirf ionic** hai. Fark kahan? Na₂O ke negative ion O²⁻ ek **akela atom** hai, jabki NaOH ke negative ion OH⁻ **atoms ka jhund** hai.
3. **Niyam:** metal wala compound sirf tab purely ionic hai jab uska negative ion bhi ek-atom ka ho (Cl⁻, O²⁻, S²⁻). Agar negative ya positive ion polyatomic hai (OH⁻, NH₄⁺), to uske **andar covalent bond** hote hain, aur poore compound mein **dono kisam** ke bond hote hain.

Ek chhota test: formula mein bracket ya jhund wala ion dikhe (jaise Ca(OH)₂, NaOH) to socho "andar covalent bond hai kya?" Haan hi hoga.

## Quick Recap

- **Polyatomic ion** = atoms ka jhund (andar covalent bond) jis par ek kul charge hai, aur jo ek ion ki tarah behave karta hai: OH⁻, NH₄⁺.
- **OH⁻**: O ke apne 6, H ka 1, aur ek extra electron = 8 electron. O par 3 lone pairs aur 1 shared pair (O–H). O–H ka farq 3.5 − 2.1 = 1.4, polar covalent.
- **NaOH** = Na⁺ + OH⁻: **1 ionic + 1 covalent**, coordinate 0. Na–O ka farq 2.6 (bahut bada), O–H ka 1.4.
- **NH₄Cl** = NH₄⁺ + Cl⁻: **1 ionic + 3 saadhe covalent (N–H) + 1 coordinate (N → H)**: teeno kisam.
- **Chaar-kadam ginti:** ions dhundo, andar ke covalent ginno, coordinate dhundo, ginti likho.
- Bracket wale formula mein ion jhund ek se zyada baar: Ca(OH)₂ mein 1 Ca aur 2 OH.
- Paani mein **sirf ionic bond** tootta hai, ion ke andar ke covalent bond nahi.
- "Metal wala compound purely ionic" hamesha sahi nahi: NaOH mein covalent bhi hai. Na₂O purely ionic hai.

## Practice Questions

(Sab sawaal paper par hal karne ke liye hain.)

Use these approximate values where needed: H 2.1, N 3.0, O 3.5, K 0.8, Ca 1.0.

1. Potassium hydroxide, KOH, is made of K⁺ and OH⁻. List the bonds in one formula unit (ionic, covalent, coordinate) with a count of each.
2. Draw the dot-and-cross diagram of the OH⁻ part of KOH. Count the electrons around O and around H. Which atom supplied the extra electron, and how many electrons does K have in its outer shell after it gives one away (K: 2, 8, 8, 1)?
3. Ca(OH)₂ contains one Ca²⁺ and two OH⁻. Show the charge balance. Count the ionic attractions and O–H covalent bonds in one formula unit, and also the atoms of Ca, O and H in the formula.
4. Ammonium hydroxide, NH₄OH, is made of NH₄⁺ and OH⁻. Count its ionic, ordinary covalent and coordinate bonds. Is the ionic bond one of the bonds you count inside NH₄⁺?
5. Ammonium nitrate, NH₄NO₃, contains NH₄⁺ and NO₃⁻. Using the charges, find the charge on NO₃⁻. Without drawing NO₃⁻, say how many ionic bonds per formula unit and how many N–H bonds, including coordinate, are in the NH₄⁺ part.
6. Find the electronegativity differences for K–O, Ca–O, O–H and N–H. Arrange them in decreasing order, and say which are ionic-like and which are polar covalent.
7. State True or False and correct the false ones: (a) In OH⁻, oxygen has one lone pair. (b) In KOH the potassium ion is joined to oxygen by a shared pair. (c) Ca(OH)₂ has no covalent bonds because Ca is a metal. (d) NH₄⁺ in NH₄NO₃ has 4 N–H bonds in all.
8. Solid KOH does not conduct electricity, but its water solution does. Explain which bond breaks when KOH dissolves, which bond stays, and why the solution conducts.
9. A student writes the formula of calcium hydroxide as CaOH₂. Find the atoms in CaOH₂ and in Ca(OH)₂, and explain why the bracket matters.
10. NH₄OH is called a weak electrolyte in Chapter 4. Write the ions it gives in water, and say which kind of bond is broken and which bonds stay intact inside those ions.
11. Describe a block-model that shows the bonds of KOH: which blocks are joined firmly and which loosely, with the sign tags.
12. Why does the O–H bond not break into separate H⁺ and O²⁻ ions when KOH dissolves in water? Use the electronegativity difference.

### Answers

1. KOH → K⁺ + OH⁻, charge (+1) + (−1) = 0 ✅. **Ionic: 1** (K⁺ aur OH⁻ ke beech), **saadha covalent: 1** (O–H, OH⁻ ke andar), **coordinate: 0**. Jaanch: formula mein K, O, H sab 1-1 ✅.
2. Dot diagram: O ke 6 dots, H ka x, extra electron o (Figure 4.2 jaisa). O ke paas 3 lone pairs (6 e) + 1 shared pair (2 e) = **8** ✅, H ke paas 2 ✅, kul 6 + 1 + 1 = 8 ✅. Extra electron **K** ne diya. K ke paas ab 2, 8, 8 hain, yaani baahari shell mein **8** electron ✅.
3. Ca²⁺ + 2 OH⁻: (+2) + 2 × (−1) = **0** ✅. **Ionic attractions: 2** (ek Ca²⁺ ke do OH⁻ partner), **O–H covalent bonds: 2**, coordinate 0. Atoms: Ca 1, O 2, H 2 ✅. Ca ke 2 baahari electron the, har OH⁻ ko 1-1 mila ✅.
4. NH₄⁺ ke andar 3 saadhe N–H aur 1 coordinate N → H; OH⁻ ke andar 1 O–H covalent; NH₄⁺ aur OH⁻ ke beech 1 ionic. **Ionic: 1, saadha covalent: 3 + 1 = 4 (N–H ×3 aur O–H ×1), coordinate: 1.** Nahi: ionic bond NH₄⁺ ke **andar** ke bonds mein shaamil nahi, wo ions ke **beech** ka bond hai. Jaanch: NH₄⁺ mein 4 bond = 4 H ✅.
5. Charge balance: NH₄⁺ (+1) + NO₃⁻ ka charge = 0, to NO₃⁻ ka charge **−1**. **Ionic bond: 1** per formula unit. NH₄⁺ mein **4 N–H bond**: 3 saadhe + 1 coordinate. Jaanch: 3 + 1 = 4 ✅ (NO₃⁻ ke andar ke bond ko hum yahan gine nahi rahe).
6. K–O: 3.5 − 0.8 = **2.7**; Ca–O: 3.5 − 1.0 = **2.5**; O–H: 3.5 − 2.1 = **1.4**; N–H: 3.0 − 2.1 = **0.9**. Decreasing: **K–O (2.7) > Ca–O (2.5) > O–H (1.4) > N–H (0.9)**. K–O aur Ca–O ionic-like (bahut bada farq), O–H aur N–H polar covalent. Jaanch: 2.7 > 2.5 > 1.4 > 0.9 ✅.
7. (a) **False** ❌: O par **3** lone pairs hain (6 e). (b) **False** ❌: K⁺ aur OH⁻ ke beech **ionic** bond hai, shared pair nahi. (c) **False** ❌: har OH⁻ ke andar O–H covalent bond hai, to 2 covalent bonds. (d) **True** ✅: 3 saadhe + 1 coordinate = 4.
8. Solid KOH mein K⁺ aur OH⁻ lattice mein jakde hain, free nahi, to bijli nahi chalti. Paani mein **ionic bond tootta hai**: K⁺ aur OH⁻ free hokar ghol mein ghoomte hain. **O–H covalent bond nahi tootta**: OH⁻ poore jhund ki tarah rehta hai. Free ions charge le jaate hain, isliye ghol bijli chalata hai (Chapter 4).
9. CaOH₂: Ca 1, O 1, H 2. Ca(OH)₂: Ca 1, O **2**, H 2. Bracket batata hai ki OH jhund **do baar** hai, to O bhi 2. Bina bracket ke ek O hi rehta hai aur charge balance bhi nahi banta (Ca²⁺ ke liye 2 OH⁻ chahiye). Jaanch: bracket wale mein charge +2 + 2 × (−1) = 0 ✅.
10. NH₄OH ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻ (⇌ isliye ki thoda hi toota hai, baaki NH₄OH hi rehta hai). Jo bond tootta hai wo NH₄⁺ aur OH⁻ ke beech ka **ionic** bond hai. **Bachte hain:** NH₄⁺ ke andar 3 saadhe N–H aur 1 coordinate N → H, aur OH⁻ ke andar 1 O–H covalent. Jaanch: atom ginti N 1, H 5, O 1 dono taraf ✅, charge (+1) + (−1) = 0 ✅.
11. Ek laal block (O) aur ek safed block (H) **pakke** jode jaate hain: ye OH jhund hai, uspar "−1" ka chit. Ek peela block (K) par "+1" ka chit, jise OH jhund ke paas **halke se** (ya magnet se) jodo. Pakka jod = O–H covalent. Halka jod = K⁺ aur OH⁻ ka ionic bond. Ginti: 1 pakka, 1 halka. Jaanch: formula KOH mein 1 K, 1 O, 1 H ✅.
12. O–H ka farq 3.5 − 2.1 = **1.4**, jo polar covalent ke beech wale hisse mein hai, ionic ke itna bada nahi. Shared pair O ki taraf zyada khisakta hai par poora O ke paas nahi jaata, to O aur H ke beech bond covalent hi rehta hai. Paani mein sirf K⁺–OH⁻ wala ionic bond tootta hai. Jaanch: 1.4 < 2 ✅.
