# Topic 5: Sab Bonds Ki Tulna: Gun Aur Wajah

Topic 1 se Topic 4 tak humne bond ke kai chehre dekhe: **polar covalent** (pair ek taraf jhuka hua), **molecule ki polarity** (shape ka khel aur paani mein ghulna), **coordinate bond** (ek hi ne diye dono electron) aur ek compound mein **teen bond** saath-saath. Class 9 mein ionic aur covalent ke gun ek table mein aaye the; aaj hum usi table ko **gehra** karenge. Ab hamare paas chaar bond ke naam hain, electronegativity ka farq hai, aur polar-nonpolar ki samajh hai. Is topic ka kaam: sab ko **ek master table** mein rakhna, aur har gun ke peeche ki **wajah** dhoondhna: kaun sa solid 801 °C par pighalta hai aur kaun sa gas -85 °C par bhi ubal jaata hai, kaun paani mein ghulta hai, aur kaun tootne par chatakta hai.

## Part A: Master table: chaar bond ek nazar mein

Pehle **bond** ki table: har bond kaise bana aur uska nishaan kya hai. (Electronegativity ke maan wahi: H 2.1, C 2.5, N 3.0, O 3.5, F 4.0, Cl 3.0, Na 0.9, Mg 1.2, sab lagbhag.)

| Baat | Ionic | Polar covalent | Non-polar covalent | Coordinate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kaise banta hai | Metal electron deta hai, non-metal leta hai | Do non-metal pair baante hain, par ek zyada kheenchta hai | Do ek jaise (ya lagbhag ek jaise) atom pair baante hain | Ek hi atom ke lone pair se dono electron aate hain, doosre ke paas khaali jagah hai |
| Electronegativity ka farq (thumb rule) | Bahut bada, lagbhag 2 ya zyada (1.7 se 2 ke beech dhundhli patti, Topic 1) | Lagbhag 0.5 se 1.7 | 0 se lagbhag 0.4 | Bante samay koi rule nahi; ban jaane ke baad ye pehchaan nahi hoti |
| Ikai | Ions ki lattice | Molecule | Molecule | Molecule ya ion ke andar ka link |
| Dot diagram mein | Charge wale ions | Shared pair, ek taraf jhuka | Shared pair, beech mein | Pair par arrow donor se acceptor |
| Example | NaCl, MgO | HCl, O–H of water | H₂, Cl₂, N₂, C–H of CH₄ (lagbhag) | NH₃ → H⁺ jo NH₄⁺ banata hai |

Teen baatein dhyan se:

- Ye **thumb rule** hai, deewar nahi: change dheere-dheere hota hai. HF ka farq 1.9 hai, to wo ionic line ke paas hai, phir bhi molecule hai (Topic 1).
- **Coordinate bond** ka matlab ye hai ki bond **kaise bana**, na ki wo kis alag cheez ka bana hai. Topic 3 ne dekha: NH₄⁺ ban jaane ke baad chaaron N–H ek jaise hain. Isliye gun (property) ke hisaab se coordinate bond **sirf ek covalent bond** hai, aur neeche ke gun covalent ke saath hi aate hain.
- Gun ek **compound** ke hote hain, ek bond ke nahi. NaCl ke gun ionic lattice se aate hain, HCl ke polar molecule se. NH₄Cl mein teeno bond hain (Topic 4), to uska gun **ionic lattice** se tay hota hai, kyunki NH₄⁺ aur Cl⁻ ka lattice hi to bana hai.

## Part B: Melting aur boiling point: kyun itna farq?

Pehle ek sawaal: kisi cheez ko pighlane ya ubalne ke liye humein **kya todna** padta hai?

- **Ionic compound** (jaise NaCl) ko pighlane ke liye **ion aur ion ke strong attraction** todna padta hai. Lattice mein har ion ko ulte charge ke ion **chaaron taraf** se pakde hue hain.
- **Covalent molecules** ko pighlane ya ubalne ke liye **molecule aur molecule ke beech ki kamzor pakad** todni hai. Molecule ke **andar** ke bond (H–Cl, C–H) wahi rehte hain: unhe todna nahi padta.

Class 9 mein hum ye kehte the ki ionic ka melting point ooncha aur covalent ka neecha. Ab wajah ko ek aur kadam gehra dekhte hain.

```
   IONIC (lattice)                     COVALENT (molecules ka dher)

   +  -  +  -                          CH4    CH4    CH4
   -  +  -  +                            CH4    CH4    CH4
   +  -  +  -
   -  +  -  +                          andar bond mazboot,
                                       molecule-molecule pakad bahut kam
   har ion ko chaaron taraf se
   ulte charge ke ion pakde hain
```

> **Figure 5.2** – Baayen: ionic lattice (4 rows aur 4 columns, yaani 16 ions: 8 positive aur 8 negative, har ek ke chaaron taraf ulte charge). Daayen: methane ke 6 alag molecules, jo sirf halke se pakde hain. (Sketch hai: ions ki asli doori aur molecules ka asli shape yahan nahi dikhaya.)

### Kai padarth, ek seedhi: lagbhag melting aur boiling

```
   2850 C   MgO melts           (ionic)
    801 C   NaCl melts          (ionic)
    186 C   sugar decomposes    (covalent)
    100 C   water boils        (covalent)
     78 C   ethanol boils      (covalent)
     60 C   wax melts          (covalent)
      0 C   water melts        (covalent)
    -33 C   ammonia boils      (covalent)
    -85 C   HCl boils          (covalent)
   -162 C   methane boils      (covalent)
```

> **Figure 5.3** – Upar se neeche garam se thanda (lagbhag maan). Dhyan: kuch jagah **melting** hai aur kuch jagah **boiling**, isliye har do line ki seedhi tulna nahi, par ek baat saaf dikhti hai: ionic padarth ko **pighlane** ke liye hi 801 °C aur 2850 °C chahiye, jabki kai covalent padarth **ubalne** par bhi 100 °C, 78 °C ya usse bahut neeche hi gas ban jaate hain.

### Example 1. NaCl aur HCl: itna bada farq?

**Sawaal:** NaCl lagbhag 801 °C par pighalta hai (solid se liquid) aur HCl lagbhag -85 °C par **ubalta** hai (liquid se gas). Dono ka gap kitna hai, aur kyun?

- Gap = 801 − (−85) = **886 °C**.
- NaCl mein Na⁺ aur Cl⁻ ki lattice hai. Pighlane ke liye har ion ko chaaron taraf ke ulte ion se alag hona padta hai: bahut energy.
- HCl molecule polar hai (Topic 1: farq 0.9), par molecule aur molecule ke beech ki pakad **halki** hai. Itni kam garmi (ya, itni kam thandak se bhi **zyada** garmi) mein hi molecules alag ho jaate hain.
- Phir bhi HCl ke andar H–Cl bond **mazboot** hai: wo todne ki zaroorat hi nahi padi, ubalne mein sirf molecule ek doosre se door gaye.

**Jaanch:** 801 + 85 = 886 ✅. Ek covalent molecule ka **boiling** point ek ionic padarth ke **melting** point se kahin neeche hai: ye lattice ki pakad ka saboot hai ✅.

### Example 2. MgO ka melting point NaCl se zyada

Class 9 mein hum MgO (lagbhag 2850 °C) ko NaCl (lagbhag 801 °C) se aage rakh chuke hain, kyunki Mg²⁺ aur O²⁻ par **bade charge** (2+ aur 2−) hain, aur NaCl ke ions par sirf 1+ aur 1−. Bade ulte charge ki pakad zyada, to lattice todna aur mushkil.

**Jaanch:** farq = 2850 − 801 = **2049 °C**: bahut bada ✅. (Isse gehra hisaab aage ki class mein; abhi charge ka tareeka kaafi.)

### Example 3. Cheeni lagbhag 186 °C par pighalte-pighalte **tootne** lagti hai

Cheeni covalent hai, to mombatti jaise wo bhi neeche pighalni chahiye? Pighalti kam hai, aur wo bhi **simple nahi**: cheeni lagbhag 186 °C par pighalne ke saath hi **tootna** (decompose) shuru kar deti hai. Cheeni ka molecule bada aur bahut atom wala hai: molecule ke andar ke kuch bond aise hain ki bahut garmi par molecule hi tootne lagta hai, aur bhuran bhura (caramel jaisa) padarth bachta hai.

**Jaanch:** cheeni ka lagbhag 186 °C, wax ke lagbhag 60 °C se 186 − 60 = **126 °C** zyada ✅. Dono covalent hain, par cheeni ke molecule mein bahut saare polar O–H hisse hain (wax non-polar hai), isliye cheeni ke molecule ek doosre ko zyada pakadte hain.

### Example 4. Wax lagbhag 60 °C par naram

Wax ke molecules lambe hain, par unke beech ki pakad kam. Isliye lagbhag 60 °C par pighalta hai, aur dheeme-dheeme naram padta hai: kadak se naram, phir liquid. Ek mombatti ki lau ki garmi se paas ka wax aasani se pighal jaata hai: isi se candle ki "beech ki jagah" bahti hai.

**Jaanch:** 60 °C ek ionic padarth (801 °C) se kahin neeche hai ✅.

### Example 5. Paani aur methane: dono covalent, par bahut alag

Methane (CH₄, C–H ka farq 0.4, lagbhag non-polar) lagbhag -162 °C par ubalta hai. Paani (H₂O, O–H ka farq 1.4, polar) 100 °C par. Gap = 100 − (−162) = **262 °C**.

Dono covalent molecules hain, phir bhi paani ka boiling point itna ooncha kyun? Ek wajah ye hai ki paani ke molecules **polar** hain (Topic 2), jinke beech pakad zyada hai; poori kahani ek khaas pakad ki hai, jiska naam **aage ki class mein** milega. Abhi itna yaad rakho: covalent compounds ke melting aur boiling point **alag-alag** hote hain, sab ek jaise nahi.

**Jaanch:** 100 + 162 = 262 ✅.

## Part C: Kadakpan, bhangurpan, bijli aur reaction ki raftaar

### Kadak par bhangur: ionic crystal

Namak ka crystal kadak hai, par hathode se maaro to **chatak** jaata hai: bend nahi hota. Wajah dekhte hain: lattice mein + ke saamne -, - ke saamne +. Agar kisi tarah upar ki row **ek ion aage khisak** jaaye, to ab + ke saamne + aur - ke saamne - aa jaate hain. Ek jaise charge **dhakelte** hain, aur crystal ki wo parat toot jaati hai.

```
   Pehle (theek):                 Ek ion khisakne ke baad:

   + - + -                           + - + -
   - + - +                         - + - +

   upar + ke neeche -               upar + ke neeche +  (dhakka)
                                    upar - ke neeche -  (dhakka)
```

> **Figure 5.4** – Ionic crystal ki do rows. Baayen: har ion ke saamne ulta charge. Daayen: upar ki row ek ion daayen khisak gayi to doosre aur teesre column mein + ke neeche +, aur - ke neeche - aa gaye: ek jaise charge ka dhakka, isliye crystal chatak jaata hai (brittle).

Wax ya wo covalent solid jo molecules ka dher hai, **naram** hai: molecules ek doosre par phisal sakte hain, kyunki wahan koi ek jaise charge ka dhakka nahi.

### Bijli: kaun, kab

| Padarth | Solid | Pighla hua | Paani mein ghola |
|---|---|---|---|
| NaCl (ionic) | Nahi chalata (ions jakde) | Chalata hai (ions free) | Chalata hai |
| Cheeni (covalent) | Nahi | Nahi (molecule) | Nahi (molecule ki tarah ghulti hai) |
| HCl (polar covalent) | Nahi (gas, molecule) | Nahi (molecule) | **Chalata hai** (paani ke saath ions bante hain, Topic 2) |
| Ethanol | Nahi | Nahi | Nahi |

Is table mein ek chhoti seekh hai: **ionic** ki bijli ke liye ions **free** hone chahiye. **HCl** covalent hai par paani mein ions **bana** deta hai (Chapter 4 ka HCl → H⁺ + Cl⁻ ka link yahi hai), isliye uska ghol bijli chalata hai, par HCl ka gas nahi. Ammonia ke saath ye aur bhi kam hota hai: Chapter 4 ke hisaab se uske ghol mein **bahut thode** ion bante hain, to bijli bahut kam.

### Reaction ki raftaar: ek jhalak

**Ionic ghol** mein ions pehle se **free** hote hain. Do aise ghol milte hi ions turant mil jaate hain, jaise acid aur base ka neutralisation (Chapter 1): H⁺ aur OH⁻ ek pal mein mil kar paani banate hain. **Covalent padarth** ka reaction aksar **dheema** hota hai: pehle puraane bond todne hote hain, phir naye jodne hote hain, aur ye aasan nahi. Isiliye mombatti apne aap hawa mein nahi jalti, use ek chingari chahiye. Ye baat **organic chemistry** wale chapter mein bahut kaam aayegi (sirf jhalak).

## Part D: Paani mein ghulna

Topic 2 mein humne ek rule dekha tha: **jaisa jaisa se ghulta hai** (like dissolves like). Polar aur ionic padarth paani (polar) mein ghulte hain, non-polar padarth non-polar solvent mein. Ab ek aur galatfehmi tootegi: "covalent matlab paani mein na ghulne wala" sach nahi hai.

| Padarth | Bond ka type | Paani mein | Bijli (ghol) | Wajah |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NaCl | Ionic | Ghulta hai | Chalata hai | Paani ke polar molecule Na⁺ aur Cl⁻ ko ghere lete hain (Topic 2) |
| Cheeni | Covalent, bahut polar O–H jaise hisse | Ghulti hai | Nahi | Polar molecule hai, paani se mel khaata hai, par ion nahi |
| Ethanol | Covalent, polar O–H wala | Poora mil jaata hai | Nahi | Polar molecule hai, paani se mel khaata hai |
| Ammonia | Polar covalent | Bahut zyada ghulti hai | Bahut kam | Polar molecule, paani se mel; thode hi ion bante hain |
| HCl | Polar covalent | Ghulti hai | Chalata hai | Paani ke saath ions banate hain |
| Tel, wax | Non-polar covalent | Nahi ghulte | Nahi | Non-polar hain: paani se mel nahi khaate |
| CCl₄ | Polar bond par non-polar molecule | Nahi ghulta | Nahi | Shape se symmetrical, to molecule non-polar (Topic 2) |

**Poison warning:** ammonia, HCl, CCl₄ aur ethanol ki yahan sirf baat hai: inhe kabhi ghar par mat banao, mat sungho. Ammonia aur HCl zehreeli aur jalane wale, CCl₄ zehreela, ethanol aag pakadne wala. Sab "sirf padhne ke liye".

## Part E: Chaar kadam ka decision flow

Ab sab ek jagah: kisi bhi compound ke liye **chaar sawaal** pooch kar bond ka naam aur gun likh do.

```
   KADAM 1   bond ke dono atomon ka electronegativity ka farq nikalo
               farq lagbhag 0 se 0.4          non-polar covalent
               farq lagbhag 0.5 se 1.7        polar covalent
               farq lagbhag 2 ya usse upar    ionic jaisa
                         v
   KADAM 2   kya ek metal aur ek non-metal hain?
               haan  --> ionic bond ki pushti    nahi --> covalent
                         v
   KADAM 3   kya koi polyatomic ion (NH4+, OH-) hai?
               haan  --> ion ke andar bhi bond dekho (Topic 4)
                         v
   KADAM 4   kya kisi atom ke lone pair se dono electron aaye aur doosre ke paas khaali jagah thi?
               haan  --> coordinate bond (donor --> acceptor)
```

> **Figure 5.5** – Chaar kadam ka decision flow. Ye ek **thumb rule** hai: sima par (jaise farq 1.7 aur 2 ke beech, Topic 1 ki dhundhli patti) ek line se zyada koi deewar nahi.

### Example 6. NaCl

- Kadam 1: Na 0.9, Cl 3.0. Farq = 3.0 − 0.9 = **2.1**: bahut bada.
- Kadam 2: Na metal, Cl non-metal: ionic ki pushti.
- Kadam 3: koi polyatomic ion nahi. Kadam 4: koi lone pair ka daan nahi.
- **Nateeja:** ionic. Isse hi **ooncha melting point** (801 °C), kadak aur brittle, paani mein ghulta hai, ghol bijli chalata hai.

**Jaanch:** 3.0 − 0.9 = 2.1 ✅.

### Example 7. HCl

- Kadam 1: H 2.1, Cl 3.0. Farq = 3.0 − 2.1 = **0.9**: polar covalent.
- Kadam 2: dono non-metal: covalent. Kadam 3 aur 4: nahi.
- **Nateeja:** polar covalent. Isliye **gas** (kamre ke taapman par), lagbhag -85 °C par ubalta hai, par paani mein ions bana deta hai.

**Jaanch:** 3.0 − 2.1 = 0.9 ✅ aur 0.9 ke ird-gird ke bond polar hi hain.

### Example 8. CH₄

- Kadam 1: C 2.5, H 2.1. Farq = **0.4**: lagbhag non-polar (thumb rule ki sima par). 
- Kadam 2: dono non-metal. Kadam 3 aur 4: nahi.
- **Nateeja:** covalent, lagbhag non-polar. **Gas**, lagbhag -162 °C par ubalta hai, paani mein nahi ghulta.

**Jaanch:** 2.5 − 2.1 = 0.4 ✅.

### Example 9. Ammonia aur coordinate bond ka kadam

- Kadam 1: N 3.0, H 2.1. Farq = 3.0 − 2.1 = **0.9**: polar covalent. Ammonia lagbhag -33 °C par ubalti hai.
- Kadam 4: N ke paas ek **lone pair** hai. Agar paas mein H⁺ (khaali jagah) mile, to wo pair dono electron deta hai: **coordinate bond**.

```
   H₃N:   +   H⁺    --->    [ H₃N → H ]⁺
   N par 1 lone pair        lone pair ab shared pair, arrow donor se acceptor
```

> **Figure 5.6** – Kadam 4 ka example (Topic 3 ka recall): ammonia + H⁺. Gintee: NH₃ mein 5 + 3 = 8 electron (4 pair: 3 bond aur 1 lone). NH₄⁺ mein bhi 8 electron (4 bond pair) kyunki H⁺ ka koi electron nahi, aur charge + poore ion par.

**Jaanch:** NH₄⁺: 5 + 4 − 1 = 8 electron = 4 pairs ✅. Ammonia ka pehle 1 lone pair, baad mein 0 ✅.

### Example 10. MgO

- Kadam 1: Mg 1.2, O 3.5. Farq = 3.5 − 1.2 = **2.3**: bahut bada.
- Kadam 2: metal aur non-metal: ionic. **Nateeja:** Mg²⁺ aur O²⁻, melting point lagbhag 2850 °C.

**Jaanch:** 3.5 − 1.2 = 2.3 ✅.

## Part G: Gun bond se, bond banawat se

Poori baat ek line mein: **bond tay karta hai ki kaun si cheez ek doosre ko kitni zor se pakadti hai, aur wahi pakad gun tay karti hai.** Ionic lattice ki pakad bahut zyada (ooncha melting, kadak, brittle). Covalent molecules ke beech pakad kam (neeche melting, naram ya gas). Polar molecule paani mein mil jaate hain, non-polar nahi.

Do baatein **sirf naam ke liye**, aage ki class mein poori:

- **Metallic bond:** dhaatuon ka apna bond, jisse wo bijli chalati hain aur chamakti hain.
- **Giant covalent solid:** diamond aur ret (sand) jaise padarth covalent hain par molecules ka dher nahi hote: atoms lagataar bond se jude hote hain, isliye bahut kadak aur ooncha melting point.

## Aam galtiyan (bachke rehna)

**Galti 1: "Saare covalent compound gas ya liquid hote hain, aur low melting point wale."**
Ye baat aadhi sachchi hai. Demo: cheeni covalent hai par **solid** hai aur lagbhag 186 °C par hi pighalti (aur tootne lagti) hai, wax 60 °C par pighalta hai: dono covalent solid hain, na gas na liquid. Ek aur ek-line sachchai: diamond aur ret jaise **giant covalent** solid covalent hone ke bawajood bahut kadak aur ooncha melting point wale hote hain (sirf naam, aage ki class mein). Isliye sahi rule: "zyadatar **molecular** covalent compounds ka melting point neecha hota hai, par har covalent ka nahi."

**Galti 2: "Saare covalent compounds paani mein nahi ghulte."**
Demo: kirana jar test. Cheeni (covalent) ghul gayi, ethanol (covalent) paani mein poora mil jaata hai, ammonia (covalent) bahut zyada ghulti hai, aur HCl (covalent) ghulkar ions bhi bana deta hai. Dusri taraf tel, wax aur CCl₄ (covalent) paani mein **nahi** ghulte. Matlab ghulna **bond ke naam** se nahi, bond ke **polar hone** se tay hota hai: polar covalent aur ionic paani mein ghulte hain, non-polar nahi. Sahi rule: "like dissolves like".

## Quick Recap

- **Chaar bond:** ionic (electron dena-lena), polar covalent (pair ek taraf jhuka), non-polar covalent (pair beech mein), coordinate (dono electron ek hi se).
- **Thumb rule** farq: lagbhag 0 se 0.4 non-polar, 0.5 se 1.7 polar, bahut bada ionic jaisa. Deewar nahi, dheere-dheere badalta hai.
- Gun **bond ki banawat** se aate hain: ionic lattice (ion ki zyada pakad) ya molecules ka dher (kam pakad).
- Lagbhag: NaCl 801 °C, MgO 2850 °C, cheeni 186 °C (tootne lagti hai), wax 60 °C, paani 0 aur 100 °C, ethanol 78 °C (ubalna), ammonia -33 °C, HCl -85 °C, methane -162 °C.
- Ionic: kadak, brittle (ek jaise charge ka dhakka), ghol bijli chalata hai. Covalent: naram ya gas, aksar bijli nahi, **par** HCl paani mein ions banata hai.
- Ghulna: polar aur ionic paani mein, non-polar nahi (cheeni, ethanol, ammonia ghulte hain: covalent hone se na-ghulna nahi aata).
- **Chaar kadam:** (1) farq, (2) metal aur non-metal, (3) polyatomic ion, (4) lone pair ka daan.

## Practice Questions

(Sab sawaal paper par hal karne ke liye hain. Use these approximate values: H 2.1, C 2.5, N 3.0, O 3.5, Cl 3.0, Na 0.9, Mg 1.2, Li 1.0.)

1. The approximate temperatures at which four substances change state are: MgO melts at 2850 °C, ethanol boils at 78 °C, ammonia boils at -33 °C and methane boils at -162 °C. Arrange the four in order of how strongly their particles hold together, from strongest to weakest, and state what must be overcome in each case.
2. Ethanol can be written CH₃–CH₂–OH. List all its bonds (count each kind), find the electronegativity difference of C–C, C–H, C–O and O–H, and classify each as polar or nearly non-polar.
3. Methane boils at about -162 °C and ammonia at about -33 °C. Find the gap in temperature. Is a larger gap a proof that the bond inside the molecule is stronger? Explain in two lines.
4. State True or False and correct the false ones: (a) A coordinate bond is a different, weaker bond once the ion has formed. (b) HCl is covalent, so a solution of HCl in water cannot conduct electricity. (c) Wax melts at a lower temperature than NaCl because its molecules are held weakly. (d) All covalent compounds are insoluble in water. (e) An ionic crystal is hard but brittle.
5. A student predicts that "ethanol will not mix with water because it is covalent". Use the kirana-jar rule to say whether the prediction is right and why.
6. NaCl does not conduct electricity as a solid but does when melted and when dissolved in water, whereas ethanol conducts in none of these states, and HCl gas does not conduct although its solution in water does. Explain each of these in one or two sentences.
7. A hammer strikes a crystal of NaCl and it shatters, while a lump of wax only gets dented. Explain the two outcomes using the idea of rows of particles.
8. Apply the four-step decision flow to water (H₂O) and to lithium chloride (LiCl, Li about 1.0): give the electronegativity difference of each bond and the bond type, and say whether step 3 or 4 applies.
9. Water boils at 100 °C and ethanol at 78 °C. Find the gap. Both are covalent molecules: write one sentence saying why such different covalent substances can have different boiling points, without using the word "ionic".
10. Why is a chemical reaction between two ionic solutions usually faster than a reaction between two covalent liquids? Give one reason from the lesson and one everyday example.
11. A kirana customer wants to know if wax, ammonia solution (water solution), NaCl and oil will dissolve in a glass of water. For each, predict "dissolves" or "does not dissolve" and name the bond type that explains it. (Ammonia solution is only a name here: it is never handled at home.)
12. Explain, in three lines, why the properties of NH₄Cl are decided by an ionic lattice even though it also contains covalent and coordinate bonds.

### Answers

1. Sabse strong se kamzor: **MgO** (ionic, 2850 °C) > **ethanol** (78 °C) > **ammonia** (-33 °C) > **methane** (-162 °C). MgO mein ions ki lattice todni hai: bahut strong ulte charge ki pakad. Baaki teen mein sirf **molecule aur molecule ke beech ki pakad** todni hai (bond ke andar ka bond nahi), aur wo kam hoti jaati hai. Jaanch: 2850 > 78 > -33 > -162 ✅.
2. CH₃–CH₂–OH mein 9 atom hain, to 8 bond: **5 C–H, 1 C–C, 1 C–O, 1 O–H** (5 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 8 ✅). Farq: C–C = 2.5 − 2.5 = **0.0** (non-polar), C–H = 2.5 − 2.1 = **0.4** (lagbhag non-polar), C–O = 3.5 − 2.5 = **1.0** (polar), O–H = 3.5 − 2.1 = **1.4** (polar). Jaanch: 0.0, 0.4, 1.0, 1.4 ✅.
3. Gap = -33 − (−162) = **129 °C**. Nahi, bond ke andar ki mazbooti ka saboot nahi: ubalne mein bond ke andar kuch nahi tootta, sirf molecule ek doosre se door jaate hain. Ammonia ke molecules polar hain (N–H, farq 3.0 − 2.1 = 0.9), to molecule-molecule pakad zyada, isliye -33 °C. Jaanch: 162 − 33 = 129 ✅.
4. (a) **False** ❌: coordinate bond ban jaane ke baad chaaron bond ek jaise hote hain (Topic 3). (b) **False** ❌: HCl ka ghol bijli chalata hai, kyunki paani mein H⁺ aur Cl⁻ bante hain (Topic 2). (c) **True** ✅. (d) **False** ❌: cheeni, ethanol, ammonia ghulte hain. (e) **True** ✅.
5. Prediction **galat** hai. Ethanol ka molecule polar hai (O–H ka farq 1.4), paani bhi polar: "jaisa jaise se" ghulta hai, isliye ethanol paani mein poora mil jaata hai. Covalent hona na-ghulne ka kaaran nahi: kaaran molecule ka non-polar hona hota hai (jaise tel).
6. NaCl solid mein Na⁺ aur Cl⁻ lattice mein jakde, to bijli nahi. Pighla ya ghola hua: ions **free**, to bijli chalti hai. Ethanol ke molecules neutral hain aur kisi halat mein ion nahi banate, to bijli nahi. HCl gas ke molecules neutral hain, par paani ke saath H⁺ aur Cl⁻ bana lete hain, to ghol mein ion free hone se bijli chalti hai.
7. NaCl ki lattice mein + ke saamne -: hathode se ek row khisakti hai, to + ke saamne + aur - ke saamne - aate hain, wo dhakelte hain aur crystal chatak jaata hai (brittle). Wax ke molecules sirf halke se pakde hain, aur ek doosre par phisal jaate hain: ek jaise charge ka dhakka nahi, isliye sirf dent padta hai.
8. **H₂O:** O–H, 3.5 − 2.1 = **1.4**, polar covalent; kadam 3 aur 4 lagu nahi (isolated molecule, aur koi lone pair daan nahi hota). **LiCl:** Li–Cl, 3.0 − 1.0 = **2.0**, lagbhag 2: ionic wali row; kadam 2: Li metal aur Cl non-metal, to ionic ki pushti (Li⁺ aur Cl⁻); koi polyatomic ion nahi aur koi lone pair daan nahi, to kadam 3 aur 4 lagu nahi. Jaanch: 3.5 − 2.1 = 1.4 ✅ aur 3.0 − 1.0 = 2.0 ✅.
9. Gap = 100 − 78 = **22 °C**. Dono covalent molecules hain, par har molecule ka size, shape aur polarity alag hai, isliye molecules ke beech ki pakad alag, to ubalne ka taapman alag. Jaanch: 100 − 78 = 22 ✅.
10. Ionic ghol mein ions pehle se **free** hote hain, to milte hi mil jaate hain (jaise acid aur base ka neutralisation, H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O, Chapter 1). Covalent padarth mein pehle bond todne aur phir naye jodne padte hain, jo dheeme hota hai: jaise mombatti ko hawa mein apne aap nahi jalti, ek chingari chahiye.
11. **Wax:** nahi ghulega (non-polar covalent). **Ammonia solution:** ghulti hai (polar covalent, polar paani se mel). **NaCl:** ghulega (ionic, paani ke polar molecule ions ko ghere lete hain). **Tel:** nahi ghulega (non-polar covalent). Sirf tel aur wax paani mein nahi ghulte.
12. NH₄Cl mein N–H bond (3 covalent, 1 coordinate) NH₄⁺ ke **andar** hain, aur ye bond mazboot rehte hain. NH₄⁺ aur Cl⁻ ek doosre se ulte charge se **lattice** mein jude hain. Ghulne par ye lattice tootti hai aur NH₄⁺ aur Cl⁻ free ho jaate hain, to uske gun (kadak crystal, paani mein ghulna, ghol mein bijli) ionic jaise hain. Jaanch: bond ke teeno naam hain, par gun lattice tay karti hai ✅.
