## Part A: Master method: chaar sawaal

Class 9 mein 3-step tareeka tha (metal ya non-metal, type, electron ginti). Is chapter ne use kaafi gehra kar diya: ab bond ke andar **barabar ya ek taraf** ka sawaal bhi hai, aur **ion ke andar** ke bond aur **coordinate** bond bhi. Chaar sawaal, isi krama mein:

**Step 1: Farq kitna?** Dono atomon ki electronegativity ka farq nikalo (agar value di ho). Thumb rule: lagbhag **0.4 ya usse kam**: non-polar covalent. Lagbhag **0.5 se 1.7**: polar covalent. **Usse bahut zyada**: ionic. Ye seema ek **thumb rule** hai, deewar nahi: badlaav dheere-dheere hota hai.

**Step 2: Metal ya non-metal?** Dono non-metal hon to baantna (covalent). Ek metal aur ek non-metal ho to dena-lena (ionic). Ye step tab bhi chalta hai jab electronegativity ki value na ho, aur step 1 ki jaanch bhi karta hai.

**Step 3: Formula mein ion hai?** Agar polyatomic ion (jaise NH₄⁺, OH⁻) hai to ion ke **bahar** ionic bond aur ion ke **andar** covalent bond (Topic 4).

**Step 4: Kisi ne akele dono electron diye?** Agar ek atom ki lone pair aur doosre ka khaali jagah mil gaye, to coordinate bond, aur arrow donor se acceptor ki taraf (Topic 3).

```
   formula diya
        v
   Step 1: EN farq?        0.4 ya kam: non-polar    0.5 se 1.7: polar    bahut zyada: ionic
        v
   Step 2: metal + non-metal?   haan: ionic        nahi: covalent
        v
   Step 3: polyatomic ion?      haan: ion ke bahar ionic, andar covalent
        v
   Step 4: lone pair donation?  haan: coordinate bond, arrow D -> A
        v
   jawab: bond kism + kaaran + electron ginti ki jaanch
```

> **Figure 6.2** – Master method ka flow: chaar sawaal, upar se neeche. Har jawab ke saath ek kaaran aur ek ginti ki jaanch zaroor likho.

**Ek niyam jo har puzzle mein lagega:** electron ginti do jagah hoti hai. **Kul electrons** = har atom ke valence electrons ka jod (ion ho to charge ke hisaab se ghatao ya badhao), aur har electron ek hi baar ginte hain. **Octet ki ginti** alag cheez hai: har atom ke lone pair ke electrons aur uske shared pairs ke **dono** electrons ginte hain, kyunki shared pair do atomon ke liye ginti mein aata hai.

## Part B: Bond Bingo ke rounds (hal kiye hue puzzles)

### Round 1. CaF₂: bahut bada farq

**Clue:** "Metal aur non-metal. Farq bahut bada."

- Step 1: Ca = 1.0, F = 4.0. Farq = 4.0 − 1.0 = **3.0**, bahut bada.
- Step 2: Ca metal, F non-metal: dena-lena.
- Ca ke baahari shell mein 2 electron hain, F ko 1 chahiye. To Ca **do F ko ek-ek** deta hai: **Ca²⁺ aur 2 F⁻**.

```
   Pehle:        ..                          ..
               : F .        x Ca x         . F :
                 ..                          ..
              (F: 7 e)     (Ca: 2 e)       (F: 7 e)

   Baad mein:    ..                         ..
             [ : F .x ]⁻     Ca²⁺      [ x. F : ]⁻
                 ..                         ..
```

> **Figure 6.3** – CaF₂: Ca (x) apne 2 baahari electron deta hai, har F ko ek-ek. Har F⁻ ke paas ab 8 electron (4 pairs), aur Ca²⁺ ki baahari shell mein bhi 8.

**Jaanch:** F⁻: 7 + 1 = 8 ✅. Ca: 20 − 2 = 18 electron bache, jo 2, 8, 8 hain ✅. Charge: (+2) + 2 × (−1) = 0 ✅. Ye ionic hai, aur kinare ke paas nahi: 3.0 bahut bada hai.

### Round 2. H₂S: dono non-metal, par farq chhota

**Clue:** "Dono non-metal. Farq 0.4."

- Step 2: S aur H dono non-metal: **baantna**.
- Step 1: S = 2.5, H = 2.1. Farq = 2.5 − 2.1 = **0.4**: thumb rule ki seema par, lagbhag non-polar. Itna zaroor ki S shared pair ko **thoda** zyada apni taraf rakhta hai, to S par δ⁻ aur H par δ⁺, par bahut halka.
- Dot diagram: S ke 6 electron, do H ke 1-1. S ko 2 aur chahiye, to **do pairs share**.

```
            ..
     H x  . S .  x H
            ..
```

> **Figure 6.4** – H₂S ke dots: S ke paas 2 lone pairs (upar aur neeche ke ".."), aur dono H ke saath ek-ek shared pair. Ye sirf bonds ki ginti dikhata hai: molecule ka asli tedha shape aur angle aage ki class mein.

**Jaanch:** kul electrons: 6 + 1 + 1 = **8**, aur figure mein: S ke 6 dots + 2 crosses = 8 ✅. Octet: S = 2 lone pairs (4 e) + 2 shared pairs (4 e) = 8 ✅. Har H = 2 ✅. (H₂S ek zehreeli gas hai jisme sadey ande jaisi badboo hoti hai: sirf padhne ke liye, koi prayog nahi.)

### Round 3. H₃O⁺: coordinate bond

**Clue:** "Ek acid paani mein ghula hai. Ek bond ke dono electron ek hi atom ke."

- Paani H₂O mein O ke paas **2 lone pairs** hain. H⁺ ke paas koi electron nahi, sirf khaali jagah.
- Step 4: O lone pair deta hai (**donor**), H⁺ le leta hai (**acceptor**): coordinate bond, arrow **O se H⁺ ki taraf**.

```
          ..                               ..
     H -- O -- H     +   H⁺    --->   H -- O -- H
          ..                               v
                                           H
```

> **Figure 6.5** – H₂O + H⁺ → H₃O⁺. Neeche wali lone pair arrow ban kar H⁺ ki taraf gayi: O ne dono electron diye. Baad mein teeno O–H bond ek jaise hain (Topic 3).

**Jaanch (kul electrons):** O ke 6 + teen H ke 3 = 9, par ion ka charge **+1** hai, matlab ek electron kam: 9 − 1 = **8**. Figure mein O ke paas 4 pairs (1 lone pair + 3 bonds) = 8 electron ✅. Equation: H₂O + H⁺ → H₃O⁺: H 2 + 1 = 3 ✅, O 1 ✅, charge 0 + 1 = +1 ✅. Chapter 1 ke "acid H⁺ deta hai" ka asli matlab yahi H₃O⁺ hai ([acids-bases-and-salts-part2.md](../acids-bases-and-salts/acids-bases-and-salts-part2.md)).

### Round 4. NH₄Cl: teeno bond ek compound mein

**Clue:** "Ek hi formula mein ionic, covalent aur coordinate teeno."

Step 3 se shuru: NH₄Cl = **NH₄⁺** aur **Cl⁻**.

| Jagah | Bond kism | Ginti |
|---|---|---|
| NH₄⁺ aur Cl⁻ ke beech | Ionic | 1 |
| NH₄⁺ ke andar, 3 N–H | Covalent (har H ne apna electron diya) | 3 |
| NH₄⁺ ke andar, 1 N→H | Coordinate (N ki lone pair, H⁺ khaali) | 1 |

**Jaanch:** NH₄⁺ ke andar kul 3 + 1 = 4 bond (N ka octet: 4 pairs = 8 electron). Kul electrons: N 5 + 4 H = 9, charge +1 isliye 9 − 1 = **8** ✅. Cl⁻: 7 + 1 = 8 (4 lone pairs) ✅. Charge: (+1) + (−1) = 0 ✅.

### Round 5. Bonds ko polarity ke krama mein lagao

**Sawaal:** Cl–Cl, S–H, H–Cl, Al–Cl, Mg–Cl aur Ca–F ko kam se zyada polar ke krama mein likho, aur har ek ka zone batao.

Shared constants: Cl 3.0, S 2.5, H 2.1, Al 1.5, Mg 1.2, Ca 1.0, F 4.0.

```
   Cl-Cl   0.0   (koi bar nahi)
   S-H     0.4   ####
   H-Cl    0.9   #########
   Al-Cl   1.5   ###############
   Mg-Cl   1.8   ##################
   Ca-F    3.0   ##############################
```

> **Figure 6.6** – Bonds ka electronegativity-farq (har # = 0.1). Farq jitna bada, shared pair utna ek taraf, aur Ca–F mein lagbhag poora ek taraf.

- **Cl–Cl (0.0):** non-polar. **S–H (0.4):** kinare par, lagbhag non-polar. **H–Cl (0.9)** aur **Al–Cl (1.5):** polar covalent zone mein. **Mg–Cl (1.8):** 1.7 se 2 ki dhundhli patti mein (Topic 1), ionic ki taraf; Mg metal hai (step 2), to MgCl₂ ko ionic hi maana jaata hai. **Ca–F (3.0):** pakka ionic.
- Krama: **Cl–Cl < S–H < H–Cl < Al–Cl < Mg–Cl < Ca–F**.

**Jaanch:** 3.0 − 3.0 = 0.0, 2.5 − 2.1 = 0.4, 3.0 − 2.1 = 0.9, 3.0 − 1.5 = 1.5, 3.0 − 1.2 = 1.8, 4.0 − 1.0 = 3.0, aur 0.0 < 0.4 < 0.9 < 1.5 < 1.8 < 3.0 ✅.

### Round 6. AlCl₃: jab do sanket takrayein

**Clue:** "Metal aur non-metal. Par farq sirf 1.5."

- Step 2 kehta hai: Al metal, Cl non-metal, to dena-lena, ionic.
- Step 1 kehta hai: 3.0 − 1.5 = **1.5**, yaani 0.5 se 1.7 ke polar covalent zone mein.

Dono sanket alag baat bol rahe hain. Ye ek **borderline bond** hai. Honest jawab: Al–Cl na pakka ionic hai na pakka covalent; thumb rule yahan do hisson mein baant jaata hai. Class 10 par itna kaafi hai ki aisa maamla pehchano, aur dono sanket likho. Aage ki class mein iska aur gehra karan aayega.

**Jaanch:** 3.0 − 1.5 = 1.5, aur 1.5 zone ki beech mein hai, aur 1.7 se 0.2 neeche ✅.

### Round 7. Paani mein kaun ghulega?

**Clue:** "Glass mein MgCl₂, ethanol aur candle wax ke tukde alag-alag daalo."

- **MgCl₂:** ionic (Round 5, farq 1.8). Paani ke molecule polar hain (Topic 2): unke δ⁻ siro Mg²⁺ ki taraf aur δ⁺ siro Cl⁻ ki taraf mudte hain, aur ions alag ho jaate hain. **Ghulta hai.**
- **Ethanol:** polar molecule hai (O–H wala hissa), paani ke saath **mil jaata hai**.
- **Wax:** non-polar, like dissolves like (Topic 2) ke hisaab se paani mein **nahi ghulta**.

Ek honest line: ye bhi thumb rule hai. Kuch ionic compounds (jaise CaF₂, jo fluorite naam ka khanij hai) paani mein bahut kam ghulte hain, uska karan aage ki class mein.

**Jaanch:** polar aur ionic paani mein, non-polar nahi: MgCl₂ ✅, ethanol ✅, wax ❌ (ghulta nahi) ✅.

### Round 8. Properties ko substances se milao

**Clue:** "Chaar cheezein hain: MgO, NaCl, HCl, wax. Chaar properties: (a) lagbhag −85 °C par ubalta hai, (b) lagbhag 60 °C par pighalta hai, (c) lagbhag 801 °C par pighalta hai, (d) lagbhag 2850 °C par pighalta hai."

- HCl: covalent molecules, kamzor khinchav, bahut kam ubaal-bindu: **(a)**.
- Wax: bade covalent molecules, naram, low melting: **(b)**.
- NaCl: ionic, Na⁺ aur Cl⁻ ke ek-ek charge: **(c)**.
- MgO: ionic, Mg²⁺ aur O²⁻ ke **bade charge**, zyada mazboot pakad: **(d)** (NaCl se bhi zyada).

**Jaanch:** −85 < 60 < 801 < 2850, aur covalent chhote melting wale, ionic bade wale, MgO sabse bada ✅.

### Round 9. HCl ka ghol aur sugar ka ghol

**Clue:** "Conductivity tester do glassi mein: ek mein HCl ka ghol, ek mein sugar (cheeni) ka. Ek mein bulb jalta hai, ek mein nahi."

- **HCl:** H–Cl bond polar hai (farq 0.9). Paani ke polar molecule Cl ko aur H ko khinchte hain, aur bond toot jaata hai: **ionisation**. Ek H paani ke O ki lone pair se jud kar **H₃O⁺** banta hai, aur Cl **Cl⁻** ban jaata hai.
- Equation: **HCl + H₂O → H₃O⁺ + Cl⁻**. Ye wahi HCl → H⁺ + Cl⁻ hai jo electrolysis chapter mein tha ([electrolysis.md](../electrolysis/electrolysis.md)), bas asli roop mein.
- Ghol mein **free ions** hain, to bulb **jalta hai**.
- **Sugar:** molecule ke andar bond polar hain, par paani sugar ke molecule ko **todta nahi**; wo poore molecule ke roop mein ghulta hai. Koi ion nahi, to bulb **nahi jalta**.

**Jaanch:** H 1 + 2 = 3 ✅, O 1 ✅, Cl 1 ✅, charge 0 = (+1) + (−1) ✅. (HCl ek tez acid hai: sirf padhne ke liye. Ghar par sirf namak ka ghol aur cheeni ka ghol, bade ke saath, kabhi chakhna nahi.)

```
   HCl ka ghol                              Sugar ka ghol

   H₃O⁺  Cl⁻  H₃O⁺  Cl⁻                    sugar molecule  (poora, toota nahi)
   Cl⁻  H₃O⁺  Cl⁻  H₃O⁺                    sugar molecule  (poora, toota nahi)
   (ions ghoomte hain)                      (ion koi nahi)

   bulb: jalta hai                          bulb: nahi jalta
```

> **Figure 6.7** – Do ghol ka mukabla: HCl ke ghol mein H₃O⁺ aur Cl⁻ ions (charge ko le jaane wale), sugar ke ghol mein sirf poore molecule. Isi liye pehla bijli chalata hai, doosra nahi.

Mugdha ke card par ab CaF₂, H₂S, MgCl₂ aur AlCl₃ ke cell kaam ho chuke the, **kaaran ke saath**. Baaki paanch cell (HI, KI, PH₃, CS₂, SiH₄) ke liye Mr. Mokashi ne kaha, "Wo tumhare Practice mein hain, wahan khud karo."

## Aam galtiyan (bachke rehna)

**Galti 1: "Dot diagram mein electrons do baar gin liye."**
Kul electrons ek hi baar ginte hain, par octet ki ginti mein shared pair dono atomon ke liye aata hai. Ye dono ek ho jaate hain. Demo: H₂S mein kul electrons 6 + 1 + 1 = **8**. Agar koi har bond ke dono electron S ke liye bhi gine aur H ke liye bhi alag se dot bana de, to drawing mein 6 + 2 + 2 = **10** dots ho jaate hain, jo 8 se zyada hain: ye galat hai. Sahi tareeka: kul 8 dots, jinme 2 shared pairs ke electron dono taraf ginti mein aate hain.

**Galti 2: "Ion ka charge electron ginti mein bhool gaye."**
Demo: H₃O⁺ ke liye O 6 + H 3 = 9 electron. Odd number aaya, aur 9 electron se octet nahi banta. Charge +1 yaani ek electron kam hai: 9 − 1 = **8**. Ab 4 pairs ban gaye. Isi tarah negative ion mein ek electron **jodna** padta hai (jaise Cl⁻: 7 + 1 = 8).

**Galti 3: "Coordinate bond ka arrow ulta bana diya."**
Arrow hamesha **donor se acceptor** ki taraf hota hai. Demo: NH₃ + H⁺ ke liye agar koi H⁺ → N banaye, to ye keh raha hai ki H⁺ ne electron diye. Par H⁺ ke paas koi electron hi nahi hai. Dono electron N ki lone pair se aaye, isliye sahi arrow **N → H⁺** hai.

**Galti 4: "δ⁻ us atom par hota hai jiski electronegativity kam hai."**
Ulta hai: shared pair ko jo zyada kheenchta hai, wo δ⁻ banta hai. Demo: H₂S mein S = 2.5 aur H = 2.1. Bada number S ka hai, to S par **δ⁻** aur H par δ⁺. Topic 1 ki seesaw yaad karo: jo taraf bhaari hai, kendra wahi taraf jhukta hai.

**Galti 5: "MgCl₂ mein Cl–Cl bond hai, kyunki Cl₂ likha hai."**
Formula mein subscript 2 ka matlab sirf ye hai ki **do Cl⁻ ion** hain, ek Mg²⁺ ke saath. Demo: MgCl₂ = Mg²⁺ + 2 Cl⁻. Charge: (+2) + 2 × (−1) = 0 ✅. Do Cl⁻ ek doosre se jude nahi hain; har ek Mg²⁺ ke paas hai. Cl₂ (gas) mein Cl–Cl bond hota hai, par MgCl₂ mein nahi.

## Quick Recap

- **4-step method:** (1) electronegativity ka farq, (2) metal ya non-metal, (3) polyatomic ion, (4) lone pair ka donation. Har jawab ke saath kaaran aur electron ginti.
- Thumb rule: 0.4 ya kam non-polar, 0.5 se 1.7 polar, bahut zyada ionic. Ye deewar nahi, dheeme badalti seema hai.
- **Borderline** maamle (jaise Al–Cl, 1.5) mein dono sanket likho, ek hi label thopo mat.
- **δ⁻** usi par jo zyada kheenchta hai. **Coordinate arrow** donor se acceptor.
- Kul electrons = valence ka jod ± charge, har electron ek baar. Octet ki ginti mein shared pair dono ke liye.
- **HCl** ka ghol ionisation se H₃O⁺ aur Cl⁻ banata hai (bijli chalata hai); **sugar** poora molecule ban kar ghulta hai (bijli nahi).
- MgCl₂ jaise formulas mein subscript ka matlab ion ki ginti hai, Cl–Cl bond nahi.

## Practice Questions

(Sab sawaal paper par hal karne ke liye hain. Use these values where needed: H 2.1, C 2.5, S 2.5, P 2.1, Cl 3.0, K 0.8, Br 2.8. Be about 1.5 (Chapter 6 table). Atoms of Group 17 go Cl, Br, I from top to bottom.)

1. CS₂ is a poisonous, flammable liquid (named only; nobody handles it). C has 4 and S has 6 valence electrons. (a) Find the C–S electronegativity difference and say whether the bond is polar. (b) Draw the dot diagram with two C=S double bonds and show that the total electron count matches.
2. In H–I, iodine is more electronegative than hydrogen but less electronegative than bromine (2.8). Without a number for iodine, say whether the H–I difference is bigger or smaller than 0.7, and name the atom that carries δ⁻.
3. KI is a white solid. Use the steps of the master method to say what kind of bond it has, even though no electronegativity value for iodine is given. What does the solid do on melting that a covalent solid like wax would not?
4. SiH₄ (named only: poisonous and flammable gas, no practice with it) is like CH₄: Si has 4 valence electrons. Show that 8 electrons in total are needed, draw the dot diagram, and check the octet on Si and the duplet on H.
5. (BeCl₂ is poisonous: named only.) In BeCl₂, find the electronegativity difference using Be about 1.5. Which atom carries δ⁻, and in which zone of the thumb rule is the bond?
6. PH₃ (phosphine, a poisonous gas: named only) has a lone pair on P. It can join an H⁺ to form PH₄⁺ (for example, from HI). (a) Write the equation PH₃ + H⁺ → PH₄⁺ and check atoms and charge. (b) Which atom is the donor and which the acceptor, and in which direction does the arrow point? (c) Show by counting that P in PH₄⁺ has 8 electrons around it.
7. The compound PH₄I is made of PH₄⁺ and I⁻. List every kind of bond in one formula unit and how many of each.
8. In PH₃ the three P–H bonds are arranged in a pyramid, like NH₃. Find the P–H difference. Is the PH₃ molecule polar? Compare with NH₃ (N–H is 0.9).
9. Predict, with reasons, which of KI and CS₂ dissolves in water. Then say which of the two solutions would light a conductivity tester bulb.
10. HI is a corrosive, poisonous gas (named only) whose polar molecules split in water like HCl does: HI + H₂O → H₃O⁺ + I⁻. Check atoms and charge in the equation. Will the bulb of a tester glow in an HI solution? Explain.
11. State True or False and correct the false ones: (a) KI contains covalent bonds inside. (b) The C=S bond in CS₂ is polar. (c) In BeCl₂ the Be atom carries δ⁻. (d) In PH₄⁺ the coordinate bond is weaker than the other three P–H bonds. (e) SiH₄ has a non-metal–non-metal covalent bond.
12. A student draws SiH₄ with 10 dots and says, "I counted the shared electrons for both atoms." Find the mistake and give the correct total.
13. A student writes the arrow in PH₃ + H⁺ as H⁺ → PH₃. What is wrong, and what is the correct direction and the reason?
14. Bond Bingo round. Clues: (a) an ionic solid that conducts when melted; (b) a gas whose central atom has one lone pair, three single bonds and all bonds with difference 0.0; (c) a gas whose polar molecules form H₃O⁺ and I⁻ in water; (d) a liquid with two C=S double bonds and no lone pair on the central atom. Match them to KI, HI, PH₃ and CS₂.

### Answers

1. (a) C = 2.5, S = 2.5, farq = **0.0**, bond **non-polar**. (b) S=C=S: har S par 2 lone pairs (4 e) aur C ke saath 2 shared pairs (4 e) = 8; C ke paas 4 shared pairs (8 e) = 8. Kul valence: 4 + 6 + 6 = **16**. Figure mein: lone pairs 4 (2 har S par) = 8 e, bonds 4 pairs = 8 e, kul 16 ✅. Dono bond ka farq zero hai, isliye CS₂ ke bond non-polar.
2. I, H se zyada electronegative hai (jaise I > 2.1) aur Br (2.8) se kam. To H–I farq 0 aur 0.7 ke beech hai: yaani **0.7 se chhota**. δ⁻ **iodine** par. Jaanch: I 2.1 se bada aur 2.8 se chhota, farq kabhi 0.7 se zyada nahi ✅.
3. Step 2: K metal, I non-metal, to **ionic** (K⁺ aur I⁻); I ki value ki zaroorat nahi padi. Step 1 ki jaanch: K = 0.8 bahut kam hai, to bada farq hona hi hai. Melt hone par ions **free** ho jaate hain, to molten KI **bijli chalata hai**; wax molecules ke roop mein rehta hai aur bijli nahi chalata.
4. Kul valence: 4 + 4 × 1 = **8** electron = 4 pairs. Si ke 4 bond, har ek mein 1 pair. Octet: Si ke paas 4 pairs × 2 = 8 ✅, har H ke paas 1 pair = 2 (duplet) ✅. Sab 8 electrons ek baar gine gaye.
5. Farq = 3.0 − 1.5 = **1.5**, **Cl** par δ⁻. 1.5, 0.5 se 1.7 ke beech: **polar covalent zone**. Jaanch: 3.0 − 1.5 = 1.5 ✅. (Be ka octet yahan poora nahi hota: ye aage ki class ki baat hai.)
6. (a) PH₃ + H⁺ → PH₄⁺: H 3 + 1 = 4 ✅, P 1 ✅, charge 0 + 1 = +1 ✅. (b) **Donor: P** (lone pair), **acceptor: H⁺** (khaali jagah), arrow **P → H⁺**. (c) P ke 5 + 4 H ke 4 = 9, charge +1 ke liye 9 − 1 = 8 electron = 4 bonds × 2 = 8 ✅.
7. **Ionic:** PH₄⁺ aur I⁻ ke beech 1. **Covalent:** 3 P–H. **Coordinate:** 1 P→H. Jaanch: ion ke andar 3 + 1 = 4 bond ✅ aur charge (+1) + (−1) = 0 ✅.
8. P–H farq = 2.1 − 2.1 = **0.0**, to bond non-polar. Is liye bonds par koi δ⁺ ya δ⁻ nahi aur koi bond-teer nahi, to PH₃ **bahut hi kam polar (lagbhag non-polar)** hai; P ki lone pair ki wajah se thodi si polarity rehti hai, uska hisaab aage ki class mein. NH₃ mein N–H = 3.0 − 2.1 = 0.9 hai, polar bonds aur pyramid shape, isliye NH₃ saaf polar hai, PH₃ se kahin zyada. Jaanch: 2.1 − 2.1 = 0.0, 3.0 − 2.1 = 0.9 ✅.
9. **KI** ghulega: ionic hai, paani ke polar molecule K⁺ aur I⁻ ko alag kar dete hain. **CS₂** non-polar hai (Q1), like dissolves like ke hisaab se paani mein nahi ghulta. Tester: **KI ka ghol** bulb jalayega (free ions), CS₂ ke saath ions ka sawaal hi nahi.
10. Atoms: H 1 + 2 = 3 ✅, I 1 ✅, O 1 ✅, charge 0 = (+1) + (−1) ✅. HI ke ghol mein H₃O⁺ aur I⁻ free hain, isliye bulb **jalega**. Ye HCl wali kahani ka doosra roop hai.
11. (a) **False** ❌: KI ionic hai, K⁺ aur I⁻ ke beech sirf ionic bond. (b) **False** ❌: farq 0.0 hai. (c) **False** ❌: Cl δ⁻, Be δ⁺. (d) **False** ❌: PH₄⁺ mein chaaron bond ek jaise hain, bante hi farq pehchana nahi ja sakta. (e) **True** ✅ (Si ko kabhi metalloid bhi kehte hain, par Si–H bond baantne wala covalent bond hi hai).
12. Kul valence electrons: 4 + 4 = **8**. 10 dots banana galat hai, kyunki ginti mein shared electrons ko do baar gin liya. Octet ki jaanch mein shared pair dono ke liye ginte hain, par drawing mein har electron ek baar hi banta hai.
13. Arrow **donor se acceptor** ki taraf hota hai. H⁺ ke paas koi electron hai hi nahi, to wo donor nahi ho sakta. Dono electron P ki lone pair se aaye, isliye sahi arrow **P → H⁺**.
14. (a) **KI**: ionic aur molten mein ions free. (b) **PH₃**: ek lone pair, 3 single bond, farq 0.0. (c) **HI**: polar molecule paani mein H₃O⁺ aur I⁻. (d) **CS₂**: 2 C=S double bond, C par lone pair nahi. Har clue ek hi ko milta hai: KI ke alawa koi ionic nahi, PH₃ ke alawa kisi par lone pair wala kendra nahi, HI akela jo ions deta hai, CS₂ akela double bond wala ✅.
