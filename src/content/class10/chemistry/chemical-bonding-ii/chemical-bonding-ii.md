# Topic 1: Polar Covalent Bond: Barabar Nahi, Phir Bhi Share

Class 9 mein humne bond ko do dabbon mein rakha tha: **ionic** (electron poora diya-liya) aur **covalent** (electron ka jodaa baanta). Phir Chapter 6 ke Topic 4 mein electronegativity aayi, aur ek chhoti si baat chhoot gayi thi: agar do atom jodaa baant rahe hain par **dono ki kheench barabar nahi**, to jodaa kahan rehta hai? Wahan likha tha ki iska naam aur asar **Chapter 7** mein aayega. Aaj wahi hai: ek aisa bond jo covalent bhi hai (baantna hai) aur barabar bhi nahi (kheench ka farq hai). Chaliye, ek park ki seesaw se shuru karte hain.

---

## Seesaw ka balance point

Itwaar ki shaam thi. Mr. Dongre apni bhanji Bhairavi ko society ke park mein le gaye the. Wahan ek lambi lakdi ki seesaw thi, aur uske do siron par do bachche baithe the: ek bhaari-bharkam bada ladka aur ek patli-dubli chhoti ladki. Seesaw ek taraf jhuk gayi: bhaari bachcha neeche, halka bachcha upar.

Bhairavi hansi, "Mama, ye to hona hi tha." Mr. Dongre ne kaha, "Theek hai. Ab socho: agar tum is seesaw ko beech se pakad ke **balance** karna chaho, to pivot (tikne ki jagah) kahan rakhoge?" Bhairavi ne socha. "Beech mein rakhoongi to bhaari wala neeche hi girega. Pivot ko **bhaari bachche ki taraf** khisakana padega."

"Shabaash!" Mr. Dongre ne kaha. "Yani do bachchon ka **balance point** na dono ke theek beech mein hai, na kisi ek ke upar: wo **bhaari bachche ke paas** hai. Ab ek chhoti si kalpana karo: do atom, beech mein shared electron ka jodaa. Agar dono atom ek jaise hain, to jodaa theek beech mein rehta hai. Par agar ek atom ki kheench **zyada** hai (yaani wo seesaw ka bhaari bachcha hai), to jodaa ka 'average jagah' us bhaari atom ki taraf khisak jaati hai. Phir bhi jodaa **baanta hua** hai: kisi ne use poora le nahi liya."

Bhairavi ne dheere se kaha, "To bond na poora barabar hai, na poora ek taraf. Beech ka kuch." "Bilkul," Mr. Dongre ne kaha. "Is beech wale bond ka naam hai **polar covalent bond**."

```
   1) Dono bachche barabar bhaari            2) Ek bachcha zyada bhaari

        A ------- ^ -------- A                    A ----- ^ ------------ B
               pivot beech mein                        pivot B ki taraf khisaka
   (balance point beech mein)               (balance point bhaari wale ke paas)

   Shared pair ki jagah bhi isi tarah:   dono atom barabar to beech mein,
   ek atom zyada kheenche to uski taraf khisakti hai.
```

> **Figure 1.1** – Seesaw ka balance point bhaari bachche ki taraf khisakta hai. Shared electron pair ki "average jagah" bhi zyada kheenchne wale atom ki taraf khisakti hai. (Ye sirf ek analogy hai: electron seesaw par baitha bachcha nahi hai, aur pair ek taraf khisakne par bhi dono atomon ke beech hi rehta hai.)

## Naye shabd

| Shabd | Matlab (simple bhasha mein) | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Non-polar covalent bond** | Aisa covalent bond jisme shared pair lagbhag theek beech mein rehta hai (dono atom ki kheench barabar ya lagbhag barabar) | F–F mein, C–H mein (bahut thoda farq) |

| **Partial charge** | Poori charge nahi, sirf ek chhota sa hissa: δ⁺ ya δ⁻ | δ⁺ aur δ⁻ |
| **Electronegativity ka farq** | Bond ke do atomon ki electronegativity ka bada minus chhota (Chapter 6, Topic 4), hamesha positive sankhya | H–Cl: 3.0 − 2.1 = 0.9 |

## Part A: Jab kheench barabar ho: non-polar covalent bond

Sabse pehle wo case dekhte hain jisme koi jhukaav nahi: **dono atom ek hi jaise** hon. Class 9 mein H₂ aur Cl₂ dekhe the, aaj F₂ lete hain (fluorine, ek zehreeli gas: sirf padhne ke liye, ise kabhi haath mat lagana). Iska diagram tumne Class 9 ki practice mein banaya tha; yahan sirf yaad karke aage badhte hain.

F ke 7 baahari electrons hain (2, 7). Octet ke liye 1 aur chahiye, to do F ek jodaa baantte hain. Diagram mein o se baayen F ke electron aur x se daayen F ke electron dikhaye hain.

```
           oo    xx
        oo F o x F xx          F-F
           oo    xx
```

> **Figure 1.2** – F₂ ka dot diagram: shared pair "o x" theek beech mein, aur har F par teen lone pair (6 electron). Dono taraf F hi hai, isliye kheench barabar aur jodaa beech mein.

**Ginti:** har F ke paas 3 lone pair (6 electron) aur 1 shared pair (2 electron) = **8**. Kul electron = 7 + 7 = 14, aur diagram mein 6 + 6 + 2 = 14 ✅.

### Example 1. F₂ ka farq

F = 4.0 aur F = 4.0. Farq = 4.0 − 4.0 = **0.0**. Kheench bilkul barabar, pair theek beech mein, na koi δ⁺ na δ⁻. Ye **non-polar covalent bond** hai.

**Jaanch:** ek hi tatva ke do atom, to farq hamesha 0.0 ✅.

**Thumb rule:** jab bond **ek hi tatva ke do atomon** ke beech ho, to wo non-polar hota hai. Bromine (Br₂, ek khatarnak, zehreeli liquid: sirf padhne ke liye) aur oxygen (O₂) mein bhi yahi hai. Aur Class 9 ka N₂ bhi: sab mein farq 0.0.

Dhyan do: non-polar covalent ka matlab **bond nahi hai** ya **baantna nahi hai** nahi hota. Baantna poora hai, bas kheench barabar hai.

## Part B: Jab kheench barabar na ho: polar covalent bond

Ab do alag atom lete hain: H aur Cl. Class 9 mein HCl ka dot diagram dekha tha (H ko 1 aur chahiye, Cl ko 1 aur). Wahi diagram hai, bas ab hum use **kheench ki nazar** se dekhenge. (HCl gas aur uska ghol zehreele aur jalane wale hain: sirf padhne ke liye.)

H = 2.1 aur Cl = 3.0. Cl ki kheench zyada hai, to shared pair Cl ki taraf khisak jaata hai.

```
              xx
        H o x Cl xx          pair Cl ki taraf khisaka hua
              xx
```

> **Figure 1.3** – HCl ka dot diagram: H ka 1 electron (o) aur Cl ka 1 electron (x) milkar shared pair banate hain; Cl par 3 lone pair (6 electron) hain. Ginti: H ke paas 2 (duplet), Cl ke paas 6 + 2 = 8 (octet) ✅.

**Ginti:** kul valence electron = 1 + 7 = 8, aur diagram mein 6 lone-pair electron + 2 shared = 8 ✅.

Ab pair Cl ke paas **zyada der** rehta hai, to:

- Cl par thodi **negative** charge aa jaati hai: **δ⁻**.
- H par thodi **positive** charge aa jaati hai: **δ⁺**, kyunki uska electron ab zyada dur Cl ki taraf hai.

Is baat ko hum aise likhte hain, aur ek **arrow** δ⁺ se δ⁻ ki taraf (pair ki kheench ki disha):

```
        δ+             δ-
        H ------------ Cl          dash diagram
        +------------>             arrow: kheench Cl ki taraf
```

> **Figure 1.4** – HCl mein partial charges. Arrow H (δ⁺) se Cl (δ⁻) ki taraf: pair Cl ki taraf kheencha ja raha hai. Arrow ki tail par chhota "+" H ke paas hai.

### Example 2. HCl: kitna jhukaav?

H = 2.1, Cl = 3.0. Farq = 3.0 − 2.1 = **0.9**. Ye "kuch jhukaav" wala farq hai, na 0 (barabar) aur na bahut bada (ionic). Cl zyada kheenchta hai, to **Cl: δ⁻, H: δ⁺**. Bond **polar covalent**.

**Jaanch:** 3.0 > 2.1 ✅ aur 3.0 − 2.1 = 0.9 ✅.

### δ ka matlab: poori charge nahi!

Ek zaroori baat: HCl mein Cl par **δ⁻** hai, **Cl⁻ nahi**. Cl⁻ matlab Cl ne ek poora electron le liya (NaCl jaisa). Yahan Cl ne sirf jodaa **thoda zyada paas** kheencha hai, kyunki jodaa ab bhi **dono ka** hai. Isliye HCl ek **molecule** hai (H–Cl judi hui), ions ka crystal nahi.

δ ki koi alag sankhya hum nahi likhte (uska naap aage ki class mein aayega). Hum sirf **sign** (kaun δ⁺, kaun δ⁻) aur farq ki **bada-chhota** ginti se kaam chalayenge.

### Negative end kaun? (3 step)

1. Bond ke dono atom ki electronegativity table se dekho (Chapter 6, Topic 4).
2. **Zyada** number wala atom δ⁻ (pair ko paas rakhta hai).
3. **Kam** number wala atom δ⁺. Arrow δ⁺ se δ⁻ ki taraf.

### Example 3. HF: sabse zyada jhukaav

H = 2.1 aur F = 4.0. Farq = 4.0 − 2.1 = **1.9**. F zyada kheenchta hai, to **F: δ⁻, H: δ⁺**. Ye HCl (0.9) se bahut zyada jhuka hua bond hai: F pair ko Cl se bhi zyada zor se kheenchta hai. HF ek khatarnak, zehreeli aur jalan karne wali gas/liquid hai (sirf padhne ke liye). Iska diagram aap Class 9 mein dekh chuke hain.

**Jaanch:** 4.0 − 2.1 = 1.9 ✅ aur 1.9 > 0.9 ✅.

## Part C: Referee: farq se bond ka swabhav

Chapter 6 ne **farq** ko ek referee ki tarah diya tha. Ab us referee ko ek table bana dete hain. Dhyan rahe: ye sirf **thumb rule** hai, koi sakht deewar nahi. Bond kabhi ek dabbe se doosre mein achanak kood nahi jaata: swabhav **dheere-dheere** badalta hai.

| Electronegativity ka farq | Pair ki jagah | Bond ka naam | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 se lagbhag 0.4 tak | Lagbhag beech mein | Non-polar covalent | F–F (0.0), C–H (0.4) |
| Lagbhag 0.5 se 1.7 tak | Saaf-saaf ek taraf | Polar covalent | H–Cl (0.9), O–H (1.4) |
| Lagbhag 1.7 se 2 ke aaspaas | Bahut zyada ek taraf (dhundhli patti) | Strongly polar, ionic ke kareeb | H–F (1.9) |
| Lagbhag 2 ya usse zyada | Lagbhag poora ek taraf | Ionic | Na–Cl (2.1), Mg–O (2.3) |

```
   farq:   0            0.4        0.9        1.9    2.1
           :............:..........:..........:......:.............
           F-F          C-H        H-Cl       H-F    Na-Cl
           barabar      lagbhag    saaf       bahut  lagbhag poora
                        barabar    ek taraf   zyada  ek taraf
   <--- non-polar ---><----- polar covalent ------><---- ionic --->
```

> **Figure 1.5** – Bond ka spectrum: farq badhne par bond non-polar covalent se polar covalent ki taraf, aur aakhir mein ionic ki taraf jaata hai. Beech ki sima-rekhaayein (0.4, 1.7) thumb rule hain, tez deewar nahi. Isliye H–F (1.9) ionic ki seema ke itna paas hone ke bavjood ek molecule hai, aur Na–Cl (2.1) uske bas thoda hi aage hai.

Ye bhi samajh lo ki Class 9 ka "metal plus non-metal = ionic, do non-metal = covalent" wala rule isi table ka **lagbhag** roop hai: metal ki electronegativity kam hoti hai (Chapter 6), to non-metal ke saath farq bada nikalta hai aur bond ionic hota hai. Par table **zyada baarik** hai: do non-metals ke beech bhi farq 0 se 1.9 tak ho sakta hai.

### Example 4. C–H: lagbhag barabar

CH₄ ke har C–H bond mein C = 2.5 aur H = 2.1. Farq = 2.5 − 2.1 = **0.4**. Ye table ki pehli row ki sima par hai. C thoda zyada kheenchta hai, par itna kam ki hum C–H bond ko aksar **lagbhag non-polar** maante hain. (Is par Topic 2 mein dobara baat hogi: CH₄ ke saath kya hota hai.)

**Jaanch:** 2.5 − 2.1 = 0.4 ✅ aur 0.4 pehli row mein ✅.

### Example 5. Na–Cl: bahut bada farq

Na = 0.9, Cl = 3.0. Farq = 3.0 − 0.9 = **2.1**. Ye bada farq hai: Cl pair ko lagbhag poora apni taraf kheench leta hai, aur Na ek electron dene wala ban jaata hai: **Na⁺ aur Cl⁻**. Ye Class 9 ki "de-le" kahani hai, aur ab number se bhi dikhti hai. Yahan δ ki zarurat nahi: ye ek **poori** charge wala ionic bond hai.

**Jaanch:** 3.0 − 0.9 = 2.1 ✅ aur 2.1 "lagbhag 2 ya usse zyada" wali row mein ✅.

### Example 6. Teen bond ka krama

**Sawaal:** HCl (0.9), HF (1.9) aur C–H (0.4) mein pair sabse zyada kis bond mein ek taraf khisakta hai? Krama likho (kam se zyada).

- Jahan farq bada, wahan jodaa zyada ek taraf.
- Krama: **C–H (0.4) < H–Cl (0.9) < H–F (1.9)**.

**Jaanch:** 0.4 < 0.9 < 1.9 ✅.

## Part D: Pehle atom dhoondho, phir arrow lagao

Bond polar hai ya nahi, ye farq batata hai; **arrow kis taraf** jaayega, ye dono numbers ki tulna batati hai. Dhyan rahe ki electronegativity ek **tulna** hai (Chapter 6): ek hi atom ek partner ke saath δ⁺ ho sakta hai aur kisi aur partner ke saath δ⁻. Isliye har bond mein dono number alag se dekho, "H hamesha positive" jaisa label mat lagao. Is topic ke bondon mein H (2.1) sab jagah kam kheenchne wala nikla, par aage dusre atomon ke saath ye badal sakta hai.

### Example 7. Kaun δ⁺ aur kaun δ⁻?

**Sawaal:** C–Cl bond mein kaun δ⁺ aur kaun δ⁻ hai? Farq kitna hai, aur bond kis row mein aata hai?

- C = 2.5, Cl = 3.0. Cl zyada, to **Cl: δ⁻**, **C: δ⁺**.
- Farq = 3.0 − 2.5 = **0.5**.
- Ye table ki doosri row ki **shuruaat** par hai: lagbhag polar covalent, par bahut halka.

**Jaanch:** 3.0 − 2.5 = 0.5 ✅. (C–Cl CCl₄ jaisi cheezon mein milta hai; CCl₄ zehreeli hai, sirf padhne ke liye.)

Dekha? C–H (0.4) aur C–Cl (0.5) ke farq mein bahut kam antar hai, par table ki line ne unhe do alag naam de diye. Isiliye hum bolte hain: line ek **thumb rule** hai, dono bond mein hi kuch na kuch jhukaav hai aur dono lagbhag ek jaisi kategory mein aate hain.

## Aam galtiyan (bachke rehna)

**Galti 1: "Do alag atom ke beech ka bond hamesha polar hota hai."**
Sach: bond ka swabhav **farq** par depend karta hai, "alag atom" hone par nahi. Demo: P (2.1) aur H (2.1) alag tatva hain, par farq **0.0** hai: PH₃ (phosphine, ek zehreeli gas: sirf padhne ke liye) ke P–H bond bilkul non-polar jaise hain. C (2.5) aur H (2.1) ka farq sirf **0.4** hai: lagbhag non-polar. S (2.5) aur H (2.1) ka farq bhi 0.4 hai. Ab sirf farq badhao: H–Cl (0.9) aur H–F (1.9): ye saaf polar hain. Matlab "alag atom" se bond polar nahi banta; **kheench ka farq** polar banata hai.

**Galti 2: "Polar covalent matlab ionic."**
Sach: polar covalent mein pair **ek taraf jhuka hua** hota hai par **baanta hua** hai; ionic mein pair lagbhag poora ek atom ke paas hai. Demo: HCl (farq 0.9, polar covalent) aur NaCl (farq 2.1, ionic) ko saath rakho. HCl ek molecule hai jo lagbhag **−85 °C** par ubalti hai (isliye room temperature par gas hai), jabki NaCl ek crystal hai jo lagbhag **801 °C** par pighalta hai. Alag swabhav, alag gun. Aur dhyan rahe: HF (1.9) itna polar hai ki ionic ki sima ke paas hai, phir bhi wo ek molecule hai, ek poora ion-crystal nahi. Polar covalent ek **covalent** bond hai, bas "tilted".

## Quick Recap

- **Non-polar covalent bond:** pair lagbhag beech mein (farq 0 se lagbhag 0.4). Ek hi tatva ke do atom (F₂, Br₂, O₂, N₂) mein farq 0.0.
- **Polar covalent bond:** pair zyada electronegative atom ki taraf khisakta hai (farq lagbhag 0.5 se 1.7), par baanta hua rehta hai. Zyada electronegative atom **δ⁻**, kam wala **δ⁺**, arrow δ⁺ se δ⁻.
- δ ka matlab **partial** (thodi) charge, poori ek charge nahi. Cl⁻ aur δ⁻ alag cheezein hain.
- Farq lagbhag 2 ya usse zyada ho to bond ionic jaisa (Na–Cl 2.1, Mg–O 2.3). Ye sab **thumb rule** hai: swabhav dheere-dheere badalta hai, isliye H–F (1.9) strongly polar covalent hai, ionic ki sima ke paas.
- Do alag atom ka bond hamesha polar nahi: C–H (0.4) aur P–H (0.0) lagbhag non-polar hain.
- Polar covalent aur ionic ek nahi: HCl lagbhag −85 °C par ubalta hai, NaCl lagbhag 801 °C par pighalta hai.

### Answers

1. C = 2.5, F = 4.0. Farq = 4.0 − 2.5 = **1.5**. F zyada, to **F: δ⁻, C: δ⁺**. 1.5 lagbhag 0.5 se 1.7 ke beech hai, to **polar covalent** (kaafi tez jhukaav). Jaanch: 4.0 − 2.5 = 1.5 ✅.
2. Br 7 + 7 = 14 valence electron. Diagram: har Br par 3 lone pair (6) + 1 shared pair (2) = 8, aur kul 6 + 6 + 2 = 14 ✅. Dono atom ek jaise (2.8 aur 2.8), farq 0.0, to pair beech mein: **non-polar**.
3. (a) P–S: 2.5 − 2.1 = 0.4, **non-polar (lagbhag)**. (b) S–O: 3.5 − 2.5 = 1.0, **polar covalent**. (c) Ca–Cl: 3.0 − 1.0 = 2.0, **ionic-like** (lagbhag 2: ionic wali row). (d) O=O: 3.5 − 3.5 = 0.0, **non-polar**. Jaanch: 0.4, 1.0, 2.0, 0.0 ✅.
4. C–N: N (3.0) > C (2.5), to **N: δ⁻**, farq 0.5. N–F: F (4.0) > N (3.0), to **F: δ⁻** aur N yahan **δ⁺**, farq 1.0. Matlab N ek partner ke saath δ⁻ aur doosre ke saath δ⁺ ho sakta hai: electronegativity ek tulna hai (Part D). N–F mein pair zyada ek taraf khisakta hai. Jaanch: 0.5 < 1.0 ✅.
5. S–Cl: 3.0 − 2.5 = 0.5; P–Br: 2.8 − 2.1 = 0.7; N–F: 4.0 − 3.0 = 1.0. Krama: **S–Cl (0.5) < P–Br (0.7) < N–F (1.0)**. Jaanch: 0.5 < 0.7 < 1.0 ✅.
6. (a) **False** ❌: P–H (0.0) aur C–H (0.4) alag atom hain par lagbhag non-polar. (b) **False** ❌: δ⁻ sirf thodi si negative charge hai, poora electron nahi gaya. (c) **True** ✅. (d) **True** ✅. (e) **False** ❌: polar covalent mein pair baanta hua hai, ionic mein lagbhag poora ek taraf.
7. Li (1.0) aur O (3.5) ka farq bahut bada hai, O lagbhag poora electron kheench leta hai. Isliye partial charge nahi, **poori charge**: Li⁺ aur O²⁻ (Li₂O mein). Ye ionic bond hai. Jaanch: 3.5 − 1.0 = 2.5 ✅.
8. O=O ke dono atom ek jaise (3.5 aur 3.5), farq 0.0. Double bond ka matlab do shared pair, par dono pair barabar kheenche jaate hain, to koi jhukaav nahi. Bond ki ginti (single, double) aur bond ki polarity do alag cheezein hain.
9. N = 3.0 aur Cl = 3.0. Farq = 3.0 − 3.0 = **0.0**. Do alag tatva hone ke bavjood kheench barabar, to **koi δ⁻ nahi** (aur koi δ⁺ nahi): bond non-polar.
10. **Galat.** Br = 2.8 aur Cl = 3.0: Cl zyada kheenchta hai, to Cl δ⁻ hai, Br δ⁺. Arrow δ⁺ (Br) se δ⁻ (Cl) ki taraf hona chahiye: **Br se Cl ki taraf**. (Farq sirf 0.2 hai, to jhukaav bahut halka: bond lagbhag non-polar.) Jaanch: 3.0 > 2.8 ✅, 3.0 − 2.8 = 0.2 ✅.
11. Al–O ka farq 2.0 hai, S–Cl ka 0.5. **Al–O ionic ke zyada paas** hai. Nahi, S–Cl ionic nahi: 0.5 polar covalent wali row ki shuruaat mein hai, pair baanta hua hai, bas thoda Cl ki taraf jhuka hai.
12. Pivot (balance point) = shared pair ki average jagah. Bahut bhaari bachcha = bahut zyada electronegative atom, jo pair ko apne paas kheench leta hai. Pivot jitna bhaari taraf, utna zyada polar bond.
13. X–Y ka farq 1.1 hai, X–Z ka 0.2. **X–Y zyada polar**, jabki X–Z ka pair beech ke paas hai. Jaanch: 1.1 > 0.2 ✅.
