# Topic 2: Electrolysis Kaise Hota Hai: Cell, Electrode aur Ion

Pichhle topic mein humne dekha ki jo padarth pighle ya ghule hone par **ion** de dete hain, wahi bijli chalate hain, aur ye bijli taar jaisi chupchaap nahi chalti: ghol mein **rasayanik badlav** bhi hota hai. Par wo badlav kya hai? Kaun si cheez kahan banti hai? Aur ye sab ek **battery** ke do sire jodne se kaise shuru ho jata hai? Aaj hum electrolysis ki poori **machine** kholenge: cell, uske electrode, ions ka safar, taar mein chalte electron, aur oxidation-reduction ki **electron wali nazar**, jiska zikr Class 9 mein "aage ki class mein" kehkar chhod diya tha.

## Do keelon ka jaadu

Kushagra ke school mein "Open Lab Day" tha. Uske chacha Mr. Phatak, jo bank mein kaam karte hain aur science ke bade shaukeen hain, bhatije ke saath lab mein ghoom rahe the. Ek table par science teacher (Madam) ne ek kaanch ke glass mein paani aur do chammach namak ghola. Phir do **saaf lohe ki keelein** (nails) glass mein latkayin, ek taar se keel ko **9 volt ki battery ke + sire** se aur doosri keel ko **battery ke − sire** se jod diya.

Madam ne pehle ek baat saaf kar di: "Ye **sirf teacher demo** hai. Battery 9 volt ki chhoti si hai, kamre mein hawa chal rahi hai, aur koi bachcha taar ya glass ko haath nahi lagayega. Aur ghar mein ye mat dohrana: ghar ki bijli (mains) se to kabhi nahi."

Taar jude hi dono keelon par **chhote-chhote bulbule** uthne lage. Ek keel par bahut, doosri par kam. Kuch der baad ek keel ke aas-paas paani dhundhla hara-bhura hone laga.

Kushagra ki aankhein chamak uthin. "Chacha! Bulbule kahan se aaye? Hamne to paani mein sirf namak dala tha!"

Mr. Phatak muskuraye. "Bulbule gas hain, Kushagra. Aur gas ka banna ek **naya padarth** banna hai, yaani **rasayanik badlav**. Battery ne paani aur namak ko **tod** diya. Isi ko kehte hain **electrolysis**. Aao, pehle ye samjhein ki glass ke andar bijli ne ye kaise kiya. Phir hum is demo ko dobara dekhenge, ab poori samajh ke saath."

## Part A: Electrolytic cell aur ions ka safar

Ek electrolytic cell mein sirf **teen cheezein** chahiye:

1. **Electrolyte:** ions wala ghol ya pighla namak (Topic 1 se).
2. **Do electrode:** jo ghol ke andar dube rehte hain.
3. **Battery (ya cell):** jo electron ko dhakelne ka kaam karti hai.

Class 9 Physics ([electricity-part7.md](../../../class9/physics/electricity/electricity-part7.md)) mein anode (+) aur cathode (−) ke naam aur namak ke paani ki electrolysis ki jhalak aa chuki hai; yahan hum electrode ke andar ki poori kahani dekhenge. Naam yaad rakhne ka ek aasaan niyam: **jo electrode battery ke + sire se juda hai wo anode (+) hai, aur jo − sire se juda hai wo cathode (−) hai.** Electrolytic cell mein anode positive aur cathode negative hota hai.

Ab ghol ke andar dekho. Electrolyte mein cation (+) aur anion (−) pehle se tairte rehte the. Battery lagte hi cathode par electron ki bhid ho jaati hai (wo negatively charged ho jata hai) aur anode par electron ki kami ho jaati hai (wo positive ho jata hai). **Vipreet charge ek doosre ko kheenchte hain**, isliye:

- **Cation (+) → cathode (−) ki taraf.** Yaad rakhne ka tareeka: **CAT**hode ke paas **CAT**ions.
- **Anion (−) → anode (+) ki taraf.**

Taar mein kya chalta hai? **Electron.** Taar dhatu ki hoti hai, aur dhatu mein electron aazad hote hain (metallic conduction, Topic 1). Electron anode se nikalkar taar ke raaste battery ke + sire mein jaate hain, battery unhe − sire se bahar dhakelti hai, aur wo taar ke raaste **cathode** tak pahunchte hain. Ghol ke andar electron **nahi** chalte: wahan bijli ko **ion** le jaate hain.

```
      e⁻ ka raasta (sirf taar mein): anode ---> battery ---> cathode

        +--------------[ (+)  BATTERY  (-) ]-------------+
        :                                                :
        ^ e⁻                                         e⁻  v
        :                                                :
        :                                                :
   +----#------------------------------------------------#----+
   :    #  ANODE (+)                       CATHODE (-)   #    :
   :    #                                                #    :
   :    #  <-- X⁻                                M⁺ -->  #    :
   :    #  <-- X⁻       ELECTROLYTE              M⁺ -->  #    :
   :    #  <-- X⁻                                M⁺ -->  #    :
   :    #                                                #    :
   :    #                                                #    :
   +----------------------------------------------------------+
```

> **Figure 2.1** – Electrolytic cell (sketch): battery ka + sira anode se aur − sira cathode se juda hai. Ghol ke andar cation M⁺ cathode (−) ki taraf aur anion X⁻ anode (+) ki taraf chalte hain. Electron sirf taar mein chalte hain: anode se battery ke raaste cathode tak. (Chitra mein 3 cation aur 3 anion hain, ginti barabar.)

Ek dilchasp baat: dono taraf ka "traffic" milkar ek **poora chakkar** banata hai. Taar mein electron, ghol mein ions, aur har electrode par ek **badlav**, jahan electron ion ko diye ya ion se liye jaate hain. Agar kisi electrode par ye badlav na ho, to chakkar toot jaayega aur bijli ruk jaayegi.

> **Note:** "Battery khud kaise bijli banati hai" ek alag cheez hai, ye Class 12 mein aayegi. Yahan hum battery ko bas "electron dhakelne wali machine" maante hain.

## Part B: Electrode par kya hota hai: reduction aur oxidation

**Cathode par:** wahan electron bhare hain aur cation aate hain. Cation electron **le leta hai** aur sada atom ban jaata hai:

- Pb²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Pb
- Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu
- Ag⁺ + e⁻ → Ag
- 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂

Electron **paane** ko kehte hain **reduction**. Isliye cathode par hamesha **reduction** hota hai.

**Anode par:** wahan electron ki kami hai, aur anion aate hain. Anion apne extra electron **de deta hai** aur sada atom (ya molecule) ban jaata hai:

- 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻
- 2Br⁻ → Br₂ + 2e⁻
- 4OH⁻ → 2H₂O + O₂ + 4e⁻

Electron **khone** ko kehte hain **oxidation**. Isliye anode par hamesha **oxidation** hota hai.

Yaad rakhne ke do tareeke:

- **OIL RIG:** **O**xidation **I**s **L**oss (of electrons), **R**eduction **I**s **G**ain.
- **AN OX, RED CAT:** **AN**ode par **OX**idation, **RED**uction **CAT**hode par.

Aur ek bahut zaroori niyam: **jitne electron anode chhodta hai, utne hi cathode ko milte hain.** Electron kahin ruk nahi sakte aur banaye bhi nahi jaate. Isliye dono half-equations mein electron ki ginti barabar karke hi hum poori equation likhte hain.

### Class 9 ki nazar aur electron ki nazar

Class 9 mein humne oxidation ko "oxygen **milna** (ya hydrogen jaana)" aur reduction ko "oxygen **jaana** (ya hydrogen milna)" kaha tha. Wahan ek sawaal us paribhasha se hal nahi hota tha: jab Mg ne O₂ se milkar MgO banaya, to **reduce kaun hua**? Class 9 ke aakhri hisse mein electron wali nazar ki sirf ek jhalak di thi ("aage ki class mein"). Ab use poora samajhte hain.

MgO ek ionic yaugik hai, Mg²⁺ aur O²⁻ se bana hua. Mg ne **2 electron khoye** aur O₂ ne **electron paaye**:

- Mg → Mg²⁺ + 2e⁻ (electron khoye: **oxidation**)
- O₂ + 4e⁻ → 2O²⁻ (electron paaye: **reduction**)

Dekho, Mg ko oxygen mila (Class 9 ki nazar se oxidation), aur electron ki nazar se bhi usne electron khoye: **dono nazar ek hi baat kehti hain.** Aur O₂ ne electron liye, isliye wo reduce hua aur oxidising agent bana. Class 9 wala sawaal ab poora hal ho gaya.

Isi tarah CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O mein CuO ka Cu²⁺ electron leta hai (Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu), yaani reduce hota hai, aur oxygen ka jaana wahi hai jo Class 9 ne kaha tha.

**Imaandaari ki baat:** sadharan reactions mein dono nazarein milti hain. Par kuch reactions mein na oxygen hota hai na hydrogen. Jaise anode par 2Br⁻ → Br₂ + 2e⁻: yahan oxygen ka lena-dena nahi, phir bhi bromide ne electron khoye, to ye **oxidation** hai. Isliye **electron wali nazar zyada badi aur zyada saaf hai**, aur electrolysis mein yahi kaam aati hai.

Aur dono hamesha **saath** hote hain: jo electron khota hai, uske electron koi doosra leta hai. Electrolysis mein ye "doosra" taar ke us paar baitha hai: anode par oxidation, cathode par reduction, aur dono ke beech taar mein electron ka safar.

## Part C: Inert aur active electrode

Sab electrode ek jaise nahi hote.

- **Inert electrode** (graphite, platinum): khud kuch nahi karta. Wo bas taar ki bijli ko ghol tak pahunchata hai aur wapas lata hai. Graphite sasta hai aur kaarbon hone ke bawajood bijli ka achchha chaalak hai.
- **Active electrode** (copper, silver, nickel): ye khud bhi reaction mein bhaag le sakta hai. Agar active metal **anode** hai, to wo khud electron chhodkar ion ban jaata hai aur ghol mein ghul jaata hai: Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻.

Ye dono baatein agle topic mein kaam aayengi. Abhi ke liye: **aaj ke saare cells mein electrode inert maane jaayenge, jab tak kuch aur na kaha jaaye.**

## Part D: Pighla lead bromide, sab kuch ek saath

Ab ek poora cell dekhte hain, jisme na paani hai na koi aur ion, sirf **ek hi namak**. **Lead bromide (PbBr₂)** ek thos, safed namak hai. Lagbhag 373 °C par ye pighal jaata hai aur uske ions aazad ho jaate hain: Pb²⁺ aur Br⁻.

> **Poison warning:** **Lead (Pb) aur uske yaugik zehreele hain, aur bromine (Br₂) zehreela hai: kamre ke taapman par laal-bhoora liquid, aur garmi mein laal-bhoori vapour, jo aankh, skin aur phephdon ko jalati hai.** Ye electrolysis **sirf padhne ke liye** hai. Ise school lab mein bhi sirf prashikshit teacher fume cupboard mein kar sakta hai. Bachche ise kabhi nahi karte, na gas ko sunghte hain.

Cell mein pighle PbBr₂ ke andar do **graphite** rod dale jaate hain (inert electrode), aur battery lagti hai.

```
       (+) taar ki taraf                    (-) taar ki taraf
            :                                     :
   Br₂  ^   #                                     #
 (laal-     #                                     #
  bhoori)   #                                     #
  vapour    #                                     #
        +---#-------------------------------------#----+
        :   #                                     #    :
        :   #  <-- Br⁻               Pb²⁺ -->     #    :
        :   #  ANODE (+)         CATHODE (-)      #    :
        :   #                                     #    :
        :   #        PbBr₂ (pighla)               #    :
        :                              Pb (pighli,     :
        :                              bhaari: neeche) :
        +----------------------------------------------+
                   ^  ^  ^   garmi (teacher, fume cupboard mein)
```

> **Figure 2.2** – Pighle PbBr₂ ka electrolysis (sirf padhne ke liye): anode (+) par Br⁻ se laal-bhoori bromine vapour banti hai jo oopar uthti hai; cathode (−) par Pb²⁺ se pighla lead banta hai jo bhaari hone ke karan neeche jama hota hai.

**Cathode par (reduction):** Pb²⁺ cathode par aata hai aur 2 electron leta hai:

Pb²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Pb

Lead is garmi par pighla hua hota hai. Wo bhaari hai (lagbhag 11 guna paani ke barabar), isliye bunde banke neeche jama hota hai.

**Anode par (oxidation):** Br⁻ anode par aata hai aur ek-ek electron chhodta hai. Do Br⁻ milkar ek Br₂ banate hain:

2Br⁻ → Br₂ + 2e⁻

Laal-bhoori bromine vapour oopar uthti hai.

**Dono ko jodne par:** cathode ko 2 electron chahiye, anode 2 de raha hai, ginti barabar hai. Poori equation:

PbBr₂ → Pb + Br₂

> **Jaanch:** Pighle PbBr₂ ke electrolysis mein cathode par lead (dhatu) banta hai aur anode par bromine (gas) banti hai. ✅

## Part E: Chapter 3 ki do kahaniyan ab samajh mein aati hain

Metallurgy chapter mein hum "bas dekho" kehkar do electrolysis dekh chuke the. Ab unki poori kahani pata hai. Dono ek hi niyam par chalti hain:

| Pighla electrolyte | Cathode (reduction) | Anode (oxidation) |
|---|---|---|
| NaCl (pighla) | Na⁺ + e⁻ → Na | 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻ |
| Al₂O₃ (cryolite mein ghula) | Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al | 2O²⁻ → O₂ + 4e⁻ |

Dono jagah **dhatu cathode par** aur **non-metal anode par** banta hai. (Alumina wale cell mein anode ka carbon oxygen se kyun jalta hai, ye [metallurgy-part5.md](../metallurgy/metallurgy-part5.md) mein hai; yahan use dohrana nahi hai.)

### Hamesha cathode par hi dhatu kyun?

Dhatu ke atom electron **dena** pasand karte hain, isliye ve **positive ion** (cation) banate hain: Na⁺, Mg²⁺, Al³⁺, Pb²⁺. Hydrogen bhi positive ion H⁺ banata hai. Aur positive ion kheeche jaate hain **negative cathode** ki taraf. Wahan unhe electron milte hain aur wo dhatu (ya hydrogen gas) ban jaate hain. Isliye **dhatu aur hydrogen hamesha cathode par** milte hain. Non-metals (Cl, Br, O) negative ion banate hain, isliye wo anode par jaate hain.

## Part F: Do keelon ka demo, ab samajh ke saath

Ab wapas Open Lab Day par. Madam ne battery lagayi thi: **cathode** (− se juda) keel par bahut bulbule uthe. Mr. Phatak ne Kushagra ko samjhaya:

"Dekho, glass ke ghol mein H⁺ (paani se) aur Na⁺ cation hain. Cathode par cation electron leta hai. Yahan hydrogen gas ke bulbule bante hain: 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂. (Namak ke Na⁺ ki jagah H⁺ kyun jeeta, ye hum agle topic mein dekhenge.)"

"Aur doosri keel?" Kushagra ne poochha.

"Wo **anode** hai, yahan oxidation hota hai. Isliye wahan bhi kuch bulbule bante hain, par kam. Aur dhyan se dekho: ye keel **lohe ki** hai, ek **active electrode**. Wo khud bhi electron chhod kar ghulne lagti hai (Fe → Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻). Ye Fe²⁺ ghol ke OH⁻ se milkar hari si dhundhli cheez banata hai jo hawa lagne par bhoori hone lagti hai: isiliye us ke aas-paas ka paani dhundhla hara-bhura ho jaata hai. Yaani anode ka zyadatar kaam keel ka ghulna hai, isliye wahan bulbule kam hain."

## Part G: Solved examples

Atomic masses: H 1, O 16, Cl 35.5, Zn 65, Br 80, Pb 207 (aur Mg 24, Cu 63.5).

**Example 1 (poori equation jodna):** Pighle PbBr₂ ke electrolysis ke liye half-equations likho, electron barabar karo aur poori equation likho.

- Cathode: Pb²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Pb (reduction). Anode: 2Br⁻ → Br₂ + 2e⁻ (oxidation).
- Electron: cathode ko 2, anode 2 deta hai: barabar.
- Jodne par: Pb²⁺ + 2Br⁻ → Pb + Br₂, yaani PbBr₂ → Pb + Br₂.

**Jaanch:** Atom: Pb 1 = 1, Br 2 = 2. Charge: left 2 + (−2) = 0, right 0 ✅. Mass: 207 + 2 × 80 = 367, aur 207 + 160 = 367 ✅.

**Example 2 (electron ginna):** Agar cathode par 0.5 mol lead bane, to kitne mol electron lage, kitna lead (grams mein) aur kitni bromine bani?

- Pb²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Pb: 1 mol Pb ke liye 2 mol electron. To 0.5 mol Pb ke liye 0.5 × 2 = **1 mol electron**.
- Lead: 0.5 × 207 = **103.5 g**.
- Anode ne bhi wahi 1 mol electron diye. 2Br⁻ → Br₂ + 2e⁻ mein 1 mol Br₂ ke liye 2 mol electron lagte hain, to 1 mol electron se 0.5 mol Br₂ bani: 0.5 × 160 = **80 g**.

**Jaanch:** Mass bachta hai: PbBr₂ = 0.5 × 367 = 183.5 g, aur 103.5 + 80 = 183.5 ✅.

**Example 3 (Mg + O₂, electron ki nazar):** 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO ko electron ki nazar se likho aur batao kaun oxidise, kaun reduce hua.

- Mg → Mg²⁺ + 2e⁻ (ise 2 se guna karo: 2Mg → 2Mg²⁺ + 4e⁻). O₂ + 4e⁻ → 2O²⁻.
- Jodne par: 2Mg + O₂ → 2Mg²⁺ + 2O²⁻, yaani 2MgO.
- **Mg oxidise** hua (electron khoye), **O₂ reduce** hua (electron paaye). Mg reducing agent, O₂ oxidising agent.

**Jaanch:** Charge: left 0; right 2 × (+2) + 2 × (−2) = 0 ✅. Electron: Mg ne 4 khoye, O₂ ne 4 paaye ✅.

**Example 4 (bina taar ka electron transfer):** Zn + Cu²⁺ → Zn²⁺ + Cu (jo Chapter 2 mein displacement tha). Kaun oxidise aur kaun reduce hua?

- Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻: electron khoye, **Zn oxidise**.
- Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu: electron paaye, **Cu²⁺ reduce**.
- Yahan electron zinc se seedhe copper ion tak jaate hain (beech mein taar nahi). Electrolysis mein wohi do kaam **alag-alag jagah** (anode aur cathode) par hote hain aur electron taar se jaate hain.

**Jaanch:** Charge: left 0 + 2 = +2, right +2 + 0 = +2 ✅. Atom: Zn 1 = 1, Cu 1 = 1 ✅.

## Aam galtiyan (bachke rehna)

**Galti 1: "Cathode positive hota hai, kyunki cation positive hain."** Nahi. Cathode **negative** hota hai (battery ke − se juda). Cation positive hain, aur vipreet charge kheenchte hain, isliye wo cathode ki taraf jaate hain. Dekho: do keelon wale demo mein hydrogen ke bulbule us keel par bane jo battery ke **− sire** se juda tha, kyunki H⁺ (positive) wahin gaya.

**Galti 2: "Electron ghol ke andar se hokar chalte hain."** Nahi. Ghol mein electron nahi, **ion** chalte hain. Electron to bas taar mein chalte hain. Soch lo ek relay race: taar mein electron daud rahe hain, ghol mein ions. Electrode wo jagah hai jahan **batten badalta hai**: cathode par electron taar se nikalkar cation ko diye jaate hain, anode par anion se electron taar ko diye jaate hain. Ginti karo: taar mein jitne electron cathode ki taraf gaye, utne hi cation ne cathode par liye. Agar wahi electron ghol se bhi nikal jaate, to taar mein bijli ka hisaab nahi baithta.

**Galti 3: "Oxidation ka matlab sirf oxygen milna hai."** Nahi. Wo to Class 9 ki shuruaati nazar thi. Dekho: 2Br⁻ → Br₂ + 2e⁻. Yahan oxygen hai hi nahi, par bromide ne electron khoye, isliye ye oxidation hai. Naya paimana: **oxidation = electron khona, reduction = electron paana.** Mg + O₂ wale example mein dono nazarein saath chalti hain, isliye hume pehle galatfahmi hoti hai.

**Galti 4: "Anode hamesha negative hota hai, jaise cell ka negative sira."** Electrolytic cell mein **anode positive** hota hai (battery ke + se juda). Dekho: pighle PbBr₂ mein Br⁻ negative ion hai, wo kheencha gaya anode ki taraf, aur vipreet charge hi kheenchte hain, isliye anode positive hi hona chahiye. (Battery khud ek alag cheez hai, ye Class 12 mein sikhoge.) Ek chhota test: **An**ion **An**ode par jaate hain, aur **Cat**ion **Cat**hode par.

## Quick Recap

- **Electrolysis:** electrolyte mein bijli guzarkar use rasayanik tareeke se todna.
- **Cell:** electrolyte + do electrode + battery. Battery ke **+** se juda electrode **anode**, **−** se juda **cathode**.
- **Cation (+) → cathode (−); anion (−) → anode (+).** Taar mein electron anode se battery ke raaste cathode tak; ghol mein bijli ions le jaate hain.
- **Cathode par reduction** (electron paana): Pb²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Pb. **Anode par oxidation** (electron khona): 2Br⁻ → Br₂ + 2e⁻.
- **OIL RIG:** Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain. Class 9 ki oxygen-hydrogen nazar sadharan cases mein isi se milti hai, par electron nazar zyada badi hai (2Br⁻ → Br₂ + 2e⁻ mein oxygen nahi).
- Anode par chhode electron = cathode par liye electron.
- **Inert electrode** (graphite, platinum) kuch nahi karta; **active electrode** (Cu, Ag, Ni) khud ghul sakta hai.
- Dhatu aur hydrogen **hamesha cathode** par, kyunki ve positive ion banate hain.
- Pighle PbBr₂: cathode par pighla lead, anode par laal-bhoori bromine. Dono zehreele: **sirf padhne ke liye.**

## Practice Questions

1. Define electrolysis. Name the three essential parts of an electrolytic cell and say which electrode is joined to the positive terminal of the battery.
2. Molten zinc chloride (ZnCl₂) is electrolysed using graphite electrodes (described only; chlorine is poisonous). (a) Which ion goes to which electrode? (b) Write the half-equation at each electrode and say whether it is oxidation or reduction. (c) Write the overall equation.
3. In the electrolysis of molten ZnCl₂ in Question 2, 195 g of zinc is formed (Zn 65, Cl 35.5). Find (a) the moles of zinc, (b) the moles of electrons that passed, (c) the mass of chlorine formed, and check that mass is conserved.
4. Molten potassium iodide (KI) is electrolysed with inert electrodes (described only). Write the half-equations (K⁺ + e⁻ → K at one electrode) and the overall equation. If 78 g of potassium is formed (K 39, I 127), find the moles of electrons and the mass of iodine formed (I₂ = 254 g per mol).
5. For each change, write whether it is oxidation or reduction and whether electrons are lost or gained: (a) Ni²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Ni, (b) Fe → Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻, (c) 2I⁻ → I₂ + 2e⁻, (d) Ag⁺ + e⁻ → Ag, (e) Cl₂ + 2e⁻ → 2Cl⁻.
6. Iron displaces nickel from a nickel sulphate solution: Fe + Ni²⁺ → Fe²⁺ + Ni. Write the two half-equations and say which species is oxidised and which is reduced. No oxygen or hydrogen is involved here. Which view of oxidation (oxygen view or electron view) can you use?
7. (A thinking puzzle only: the metal is not really made this way.) A molten chloride MCl₂ (M forms M²⁺ ions) is electrolysed. When 1 mol of electrons passes, 29.5 g of the metal M is deposited at the cathode. Use the atomic masses Mg 24, Ca 40, Fe 56, Ni 59, Cu 63.5, Zn 65, Pb 207 to name M. Which electrode does the metal form at?
8. Draw and label a cell for the electrolysis of molten zinc chloride with graphite electrodes. Show the battery with its + and − terminals, the anode, the cathode, the direction in which Zn²⁺ and Cl⁻ move, and the direction of electrons in the wire.
9. True or false, with a correction for each false one: (a) The anode of an electrolytic cell is negative. (b) Electrons travel through the molten salt from cathode to anode. (c) Cations move to the cathode. (d) Reduction means gain of electrons. (e) A graphite electrode is inert.
10. Dry zinc chloride crystals do not conduct, but molten zinc chloride does. Using ions, explain why.
11. Why is a metal always formed at the cathode and never at the anode in an electrolytic cell (with inert electrodes)? Use the idea of charge.
12. A student wants to try the electrolysis of molten lead bromide at home on the gas stove. Give two reasons why this is dangerous and what should be done instead to learn about it.

### Answers

1. **Electrolysis** ek electrolyte mein bijli guzarkar use rasayanik tareeke se todna hai. Teen hisse: electrolyte, do electrode, aur battery (ya cell). Battery ke **positive** sire se juda electrode **anode** hota hai.

2. (a) Zn²⁺ **cathode (−)** par jaata hai aur Cl⁻ **anode (+)** par. (b) Cathode: Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Zn (**reduction**). Anode: 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻ (**oxidation**). (c) ZnCl₂ → Zn + Cl₂. **Jaanch:** Atom: Zn 1 = 1, Cl 2 = 2. Charge: left 0, right 0 ✅. Electron: cathode ko 2, anode deta hai 2 ✅.

3. (a) Mole = 195 ÷ 65 = **3 mol** Zn. (b) Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Zn: 1 mol Zn ke liye 2 mol electron, to 3 × 2 = **6 mol electron**. (c) Anode par 6 mol electron chhode gaye, aur 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻ se 6 ÷ 2 = 3 mol Cl₂ bani, yaani 3 × 71 = **213 g**. **Jaanch:** ZnCl₂ = 65 + 71 = 136, 3 × 136 = 408 g; zinc + chlorine = 195 + 213 = 408 ✅.

4. Cathode: K⁺ + e⁻ → K. Anode: 2I⁻ → I₂ + 2e⁻. Electron barabar karne ke liye cathode ki equation ko 2 se guna karo: 2K⁺ + 2e⁻ → 2K. Jodne par: 2K⁺ + 2I⁻ → 2K + I₂, yaani 2KI → 2K + I₂. 78 g K = 78 ÷ 39 = 2 mol, aur har K ke liye 1 electron, to **2 mol electron**. Anode par 2 mol electron se 2 ÷ 2 = 1 mol I₂ bana: **254 g**. **Jaanch:** KI = 39 + 127 = 166, 2KI = 332; 78 + 254 = 332 ✅. Atom: K 2 = 2, I 2 = 2; charge: left 0, right 0 ✅.

5. (a) **Reduction**, electron gained (2). (b) **Oxidation**, electron lost (2). (c) **Oxidation**, electron lost (2). (d) **Reduction**, electron gained (1). (e) **Reduction**, electron gained (2). Yaad rakho: OIL RIG. Jahan electron equation ke **right** mein hain (e⁻ nikal rahe hain), wahan oxidation; jahan **left** mein hain (e⁻ le rahe hain), wahan reduction.

6. Oxidation: Fe → Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻. Reduction: Ni²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Ni. **Fe oxidise** hua (electron khoye), **Ni²⁺ reduce** hua (electron paaye). Yahan oxygen ya hydrogen nahi hai, isliye **electron wali nazar** hi chalegi. **Jaanch:** Atom: Fe 1 = 1, Ni 1 = 1. Charge: left 0 + 2 = +2, right +2 + 0 = +2 ✅.

7. 1 mol electron, aur M²⁺ + 2e⁻ → M mein 2 electron ek M ke liye: M ke mole = 1 ÷ 2 = 0.5 mol. Molar mass = 29.5 ÷ 0.5 = 59 g, jo **Ni** (nickel) ka hai (table mein sirf Ni 59 hai). Dhatu **cathode (−)** par banti hai. **Jaanch:** 0.5 × 59 = 29.5 ✅; baaki dhatuon ke 0.5 mol ka mass 12, 20, 28, 31.75, 32.5, 103.5 g banta (29.5 nahi) ✅. (Ye sirf ek soch ka sawaal hai; asli pighla NiCl₂ is tarah nahi kiya jaata.)

8. Chitra Figure 2.1 jaisa hona chahiye: battery ke **+** se juda graphite rod **anode**, **−** se juda **cathode**. Zn²⁺ ka teer **cathode ki taraf** jaata hai aur Cl⁻ ka teer **anode ki taraf**. Taar mein electron **anode se battery ke raaste cathode** ki taraf chalte hain. Cathode par Zn bane (neeche pighli dhatu), anode par Cl₂ gas (hari-peeli, zehreeli, sirf chitra mein likhna).

9. (a) **False** ❌. Anode **positive** hota hai (battery ke + se juda). (b) **False** ❌. Pighle namak mein electron nahi, **ion** chalte hain; electron sirf taar mein chalte hain, anode se cathode ki taraf. (c) **True** ✅. (d) **True** ✅. (e) **True** ✅. Graphite khud reaction nahi karta, sirf electron pahunchata hai.

10. Thos ZnCl₂ mein Zn²⁺ aur Cl⁻ ions apni jagah par jakde hue hain, ve ghoom nahi sakte, isliye bijli nahi chalti. Pighlane par ions aazad ho jaate hain aur cathode/anode ki taraf chal sakte hain, isliye bijli chalti hai.

11. Dhatu ke atom electron khokar **positive ion** (cation) banate hain. Positive ion negative electrode, yaani **cathode**, ki taraf kheencha jaata hai, aur wahan electron paakar dhatu banta hai. Anode par positive ion aate hi nahi, isliye dhatu wahan nahi banti.

12. (i) Lead aur uske yaugik **zehreele** hain, aur bromine ki gas bhi bahut zehreeli aur jalane wali hai, jo ghar ki rasoi mein hawa mein fail sakti hai. (ii) PbBr₂ lagbhag 373 °C par pighalta hai: itni garmi se jalne ka khatra hai. Iski jagah: kitab ya teacher ka **demo** dekhna (fume cupboard mein), chitra banana, ya Figure 2.2 jaisa paper par samajhna.
