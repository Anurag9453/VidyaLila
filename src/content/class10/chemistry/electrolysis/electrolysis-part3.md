# Topic 3: Selective Discharge: Kaun Pehle Ayega?

Topic 2 mein humne ek simple kahani dekhi: pighle PbBr₂ mein sirf **do** kism ke ions the, Pb²⁺ aur Br⁻, aur dono ko apni jagah milni hi thi. Par asli duniya mein ghol (solution) mein **kai** ions hote hain, aur paani khud bhi apne ions bhej deta hai. Ab sawaal ye hai: ek electrode par agar **kai** ions pahunch rahe hon, to **pehle kaun** apne electrons lega ya dega? Is topic mein hum wo **niyam** seekhte hain jisse kagaz par pehle hi bata diya jaata hai ki har electrode par kya banega. Aur ye niyam hi **chlor-alkali**, **copper ki refining** aur aage **electroplating** ki neev hain.

---

## Society ke swimming pool ka namak wala dabba

Mr. Sathaye ki society mein ek chhota sa swimming pool hai, aur wo us pool committee ke member hain. Ek Sunday unki bhatiji Nishka unke saath pool ke pump-room tak aayi. Nishka ne dekha: pipe par ek grey dabba laga hai jis par likha hai **"Salt Chlorinator"**, aur paas mein namak ke bore rakhe hain. "Chacha, ye namak yahan kya kar raha hai? Pool mein to chlorine daalte hain na?"

Mr. Sathaye ne dabbe ko door se dikhaya: "Dabba **khud chlorine banata hai**, namak se. Pool ka paani halka namkeen rakha jaata hai (lagbhag 3 se 4 gram namak har litre mein; lifeguard ise meter se naapta hai, chakhne ki koi zaroorat nahi). Ye paani dabbe ke andar se guzarta hai, jahan do plate lagi hain jinhe bijli di jaati hai. Yaani andar **electrolysis** chal rahi hai." Nishka ne turant soch liya: "To namak ke ions, Na⁺ aur Cl⁻, aur paani ke ions bhi... to plate par **kaun** pehle aata hai? Sodium dhatu to nahi banti hogi, wo to paani se ladta hai!" Mr. Sathaye hanse: "Bilkul sahi shak. Sodium kabhi nahi banta. Wahan **hydrogen** banta hai, aur anode par **chlorine** ya **oxygen**, ye kai baton par depend karta hai. Chal, wo niyam samjhte hain jisse ye pehle se pata chal jaata hai."

**Sirf padhne ke liye:** chlorine ek zehreeli gas hai (aankh, naak, phephdon ke liye khatarnak). Pool ka chlorinator band, sealed system hota hai aur bahut kam chlorine ek baar mein banata hai, jo turant paani mein ghul jaati hai. Hum is poore topic mein koi bhi electrolysis khud nahi karenge: har ek **sirf padhne ke liye** ya **school lab mein teacher ke saath (teacher demo)** hai, kam voltage ke cell se, bijli ke plug se kabhi nahi.

## Part A: Ek hi paani, kai ions

**Ek electrolyte ke ions ke alawa, paani bhi ions deta hai.** Shuddh paani bahut hi kam toot kar H⁺ aur OH⁻ deta hai: H₂O ⇌ H⁺ + OH⁻ (bahut, bahut kam). Isliye har **aqueous** (paani wale) ghol mein hamesha **do group** ke ions hote hain:

- electrolyte ke ions (jaise NaCl se Na⁺ aur Cl⁻),
- paani ke ions (H⁺ aur OH⁻).

Cathode par **Na⁺ aur H⁺** dono pahunchte hain. Anode par **Cl⁻ aur OH⁻** dono. Ab kisko pehle electron milega?

**Niyam:** jo ion discharge order mein **upar** (yaani **aasaan**) hai, wahi pehle discharge hota hai. Ye order wahi **reactivity series ulta padhna** hai. Wajah ek line mein: jo dhatu bahut reactive hai (K, Na, Ca), wo apne electrons chhodne mein bahut khush hai, isliye uska ion electron **wapas lene mein kanjoos** hai; aur jo dhatu kam reactive hai (Ag, Cu), uska ion electron lene ko taiyar hai. Reactivity series ki seedhi ka wo hissa [metals-and-non-metals-part4.md](../metals-and-non-metals/metals-and-non-metals-part4.md) mein hai; yahan bas use **ulta** padhna hai, aur hydrogen ko uski jagah par rakhna hai.

```
  CATIONS (cathode par, ek-ek karke)       ANIONS (anode par, ek-ek karke)

    sabse AASAANI SE discharge                sabse AASAANI SE discharge
              ^                                         ^
        Ag⁺   silver                              I⁻    iodide
        Cu²⁺  copper                              Br⁻   bromide
        H⁺    hydrogen    <== (paani se)          Cl⁻   chloride
        Pb²⁺  lead                                OH⁻   hydroxide (paani se)
        Sn²⁺  tin                              - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
        Ni²⁺  nickel                              NO₃⁻  nitrate  :  paani wale
        Fe²⁺  iron                                SO₄²⁻ sulphate :  ghol se nahi
        Zn²⁺  zinc                                              :  nikalte
        Al³⁺  aluminium                                         :  (OH⁻ nikalta hai)
        Mg²⁺  magnesium
        Ca²⁺  calcium
        Na⁺   sodium
        K⁺    potassium
              v
    sabse MUSHKIL se discharge
    (dilute paani wale ghol mein H⁺ ke baad wale ion ki jagah H₂ banta hai)
```

> **Figure 3.1** – Discharge order. Baayein: cations (reactivity series ulta, hydrogen apni jagah). Daayein: anions. Dilute paani wale (aqueous) ghol mein cathode par H⁺ ke neeche likhe ion ki jagah H⁺ discharge hota hai (H₂ banta hai), aur NO₃⁻ aur SO₄²⁻ anode par discharge nahi hote: unki jagah paani ka OH⁻ discharge hota hai.

**Cathode par kya hota hai (reduction):** Ag⁺ + e⁻ → Ag, Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu, 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂.
**Anode par kya hota hai (oxidation):** 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻, 2Br⁻ → Br₂ + 2e⁻, 4OH⁻ → 2H₂O + O₂ + 4e⁻.

Paani ke ions ki wajah se **K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al** jaisi dhatuein **aqueous** ghol ke electrolysis se kabhi nahi milti: wahan cathode par H⁺ aata hai aur H₂ banta hai. Isiliye Chapter 3 mein inke liye **pighla** compound lena pada tha ([metallurgy-part4.md](../metallurgy/metallurgy-part4.md)), jismein paani hi nahi hota.

**Ek imaandaar chhoot (concentration ka asar):** H⁺ ke theek neeche wale Pb²⁺, Sn²⁺ aur Ni²⁺ jab **apne hi namak ke gaadhe ghol** mein bahut zyada hon, to wo bhi cathode par jam sakte hain. Isi se tin plating aur nickel plating (Topic 4) chalti hai. Par Al³⁺, Mg²⁺, Ca²⁺, Na⁺ aur K⁺ kisi bhi paani wale ghol se kabhi nahi jamte. Is chapter ke prediction sawaalon mein, jab tak gaadhe ghol se plating ki baat na ho, H⁺ ke neeche wale ion ki jagah H₂ hi likhna hai.

## Part B: Teen baatein jo faisla karti hain

Discharge sirf order se nahi chunta. Teen baaton ko dekhna hai:

1. **Order mein jagah:** list mein upar wala (aasaan) ion pehle (Figure 3.1).
2. **Concentration (ghol kitna gaadha hai):** jab ek ion bahut zyada ho aur doosra bahut kam, to zyada wala "bheed ke dum par" discharge ho sakta hai. Sabse mashhoor example: **concentrated NaCl** anode par **Cl₂** deta hai, par **bahut dilute NaCl** anode par **O₂** deta hai. (Class 10 ke level par hum yahi simple niyam rakhte hain: gaadha ghol aur chloride ho to Cl₂; bahut dilute ho to O₂.)
3. **Electrode kaisa hai:** **inert** (graphite, platinum) sirf bijli deta hai. **Active** (jaise taamba) anode par ho to anion discharge hone ke bajaye **electrode khud ghul jaata hai**: Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻.

Ab in teeno ko paanch asli electrolysis par laga ke dekhte hain. Har ek mein: ions ki list, order se faisla, half-equations, kya dikhta hai, aur ghol ka kya hua.

## Part C: Paanch electrolysis, ek-ek karke

(Sab **sirf padhne ke liye** ya school lab mein teacher ke saath, kam voltage ke cell se.)

### 1. Acidified paani (dilute H₂SO₄), platinum electrodes

**Electrolyte aur electrode:** dilute H₂SO₄ (acidified water), inert platinum.

**Shuddh paani bijli ko bahut kam chalata hai** (kam ions), isliye paani mein thoda dilute H₂SO₄ milaya jaata hai. Ye acid sirf **ions deta hai**: ye khud nahi tootta.

- **Ions:** H⁺, SO₄²⁻ (acid se), aur H⁺, OH⁻ (paani se).
- **Cathode (–):** sirf H⁺ aata hai. 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ → 2H₂.
- **Anode (+):** SO₄²⁻ ya OH⁻? SO₄²⁻ discharge nahi hota; **OH⁻** hota hai. 4OH⁻ → 2H₂O + O₂ + 4e⁻.
- **Dono ko jodne par:** 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂ (paani toot gaya; acid ko kuch nahi hua).
- **Volume ratio:** H₂ : O₂ = **2 : 1**, kyunki 4 electrons par 2 H₂ aur 1 O₂ bante hain (Class 9 ka [water.md](../../../class9/chemistry/water/water.md) wala ratio ab electrons se samjha).
- **Ghol ka kya hua:** paani kam hua, isliye acid thoda **gaadha** ho jaata hai.

```
         (–) CATHODE                              (+) ANODE
     H₂ ki tube (2 hissa)                    O₂ ki tube (1 hissa)

        +---------+                            +---------+
        :         :                            :         :
        :         :                            :         :
        :   H₂    :                            :         :
        :   H₂    :                            :         :
        :   H₂    :                            :   O₂    :
        :   H₂    :                            :   O₂    :
     ~~~:~~~~~~~~~:~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~:~~~~~~~~~:~~~~
        :    I    :                            :    I    :
        :    I    :                            :    I    :
     ~~~~~~~~I~~~~~~~~~~~ dilute H₂SO₄ ~~~~~~~~~~~~I~~~~~~~~~~
              I   (platinum)                      I   (platinum)
              I                                   I
              +------------[ battery ]------------+
                         (–)          (+)
```

> **Figure 3.2** – Acidified paani ka cell (teacher demo): ulti tubes mein cathode par H₂ aur anode par O₂ jama hoti hai. Cathode wali tube mein gas ka hissa anode wali tube se do guna hai (4 pankti baayein, 2 pankti daayein: volume ratio 2 : 1). Students gas ko na collect karte hain, na jalaate hain; ye kaam sirf teacher ka hai.

### 2. Concentrated NaCl ghol (brine), graphite electrodes

**Electrolyte aur electrode:** concentrated NaCl solution, inert graphite.

- **Ions:** Na⁺, Cl⁻ (namak se), H⁺, OH⁻ (paani se).
- **Cathode (–):** Na⁺ ya H⁺? Order mein H⁺ upar hai, Na⁺ bahut neeche. **H⁺** discharge hota hai: 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂.
- **Anode (+):** Cl⁻ ya OH⁻? ghol **concentrated** hai aur Cl⁻ order mein OH⁻ se pehle: **Cl⁻** discharge hota hai: 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻.
- **Ghol mein bacha kya:** Na⁺ (kabhi discharge nahi hua) aur OH⁻ (paani ke H⁺ ke discharge hone se bacha). Yaani **NaOH** ban gaya, aur ghol **kshaariya (alkaline)** ho gaya.
- **Poora reaction:** 2NaCl + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + Cl₂ + H₂.
- **Kya dikhta hai:** cathode par bulbule (H₂, jalne wali gas), anode par peeli-hari gas (Cl₂, **zehreeli**; teacher demo mein bhi sirf fume cupboard mein, door se dekhna).

Ye wahi **chlor-alkali process** hai jiska naam [acids-bases-and-salts-part6.md](../acids-bases-and-salts/acids-bases-and-salts-part6.md) mein aaya tha: "chlor" (Cl₂) aur "alkali" (NaOH). Wahan sirf naam aur uses the; yahan ek-ek **ion ka faisla** hai. Teeno products: Cl₂ (anode), H₂ (cathode), NaOH (cathode ke paas ghol mein). Zyada detail aur uses Topic 5 mein.

### 3. Bahut dilute NaCl ghol, platinum electrodes

**Electrolyte aur electrode:** very dilute NaCl solution, inert platinum.

Ions wahi: Na⁺, Cl⁻, H⁺, OH⁻. Par ab **Cl⁻ bahut kam** hai, aur OH⁻ paani ka hai.

- **Cathode (–):** phir bhi **H⁺**: 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂.
- **Anode (+):** ab Cl⁻ ki bheed nahi, isliye **OH⁻** discharge hota hai: 4OH⁻ → 2H₂O + O₂ + 4e⁻.
- **Kul mila kar:** H₂ (cathode) aur O₂ (anode). Bilkul acidified paani jaisa.

**Seekh:** wahi namak, wahi electrode, par **concentration** badli to anode ka product badal gaya (Cl₂ se O₂). Isi liye "kya concentrated hai ya dilute?" hamesha pooch lete hain.

**Pool wale dabbe ke baare mein ek imaandari ki baat.** Pool ka paani **dilute** hai (3 se 4 g namak har litre), phir bhi chlorinator mein Cl₂ banta hai. Wajah: asli dabbe mein **khaas coating wale titanium electrodes** lagte hain jo Cl⁻ ko faayda dete hain, aur ye bahut kam matra mein banta hai. Hamara simple niyam graphite ya platinum jaise normal electrodes ke liye hai; asli plant ki baarikiyan **aage ki class mein**.

### 4. CuSO₄ ghol, **inert** (platinum ya graphite) electrodes

**Electrolyte aur electrode:** copper sulphate solution, inert electrodes.

- **Ions:** Cu²⁺, SO₄²⁻ (CuSO₄ se), H⁺, OH⁻ (paani se).
- **Cathode (–):** Cu²⁺ ya H⁺? Order mein **Cu²⁺** upar hai, wahi discharge hota hai: Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu. Cathode par **laal-bhura taamba** jamta hai.
- **Anode (+):** SO₄²⁻ discharge nahi hota; **OH⁻** hota hai: 4OH⁻ → 2H₂O + O₂ + 4e⁻. Anode par **O₂** ke bulbule.
- **Ghol ka kya hua:** Cu²⁺ kam hote gaye (neela rang halka padta hai). Paani ke OH⁻ gaye, to H⁺ aur SO₄²⁻ bache: **H₂SO₄** ban gaya. Ghol **amlin (acidic)** hota gaya.
- **Poora reaction:** 2CuSO₄ + 2H₂O → 2Cu + 2H₂SO₄ + O₂.

CuSO₄ ghol **swallow karne par nuksandeh** hai: koi student ise chhuye ya piye nahi; ye sirf teacher demo hai.

### 5. CuSO₄ ghol, **taambe (copper) ke** electrodes

**Electrolyte aur electrode:** copper sulphate solution, **active** copper electrodes.

- **Cathode (–):** wahi: Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu. Cathode **moti hoti** hai (taamba jamta hai).
- **Anode (+):** ab anode **khud taamba** hai. OH⁻ ya SO₄²⁻ ke discharge hone se pehle **anode hi** electron deta hai: Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻. Anode **ghulta** aur patla hota hai, koi O₂ nahi.
- **Ghol ka kya hua:** **kuch nahi**: jitna Cu²⁺ cathode par gaya, utna anode se aaya. **Neela rang waisa ka waisa**, concentration wahi, ghol acidic nahi hota.
- **Net:** taamba anode se cathode par **chala gaya**. Isi se Chapter 3 mein copper refining ([metallurgy-part6.md](../metallurgy/metallurgy-part6.md)) aur Topic 4 mein electroplating chalti hai.

```
   CELL (a): CuSO₄(aq), INERT electrodes           CELL (b): CuSO₄(aq), COPPER electrodes

     (–) cathode         (+) anode                   (–) cathode         (+) anode
        graphite         graphite                      Cu                  Cu

          I                 I                           I                  I
          I     ~~~~~~~     I                           I    ~~~~~~~~      I
      ~~~~I~~~~~ neela ~~~~~I~~~~                   ~~~~I~~~~~ neela ~~~~~~I~~~~
          I     ghol        I                           I     ghol         I
          I                 I                           I                  I
     Cu jamta (moti)    O₂ ke bulbule              Cu jamta (moti)    anode ghulta (patla)
                                                        
   Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu    4OH⁻ → 2H₂O + O₂ + 4e⁻     Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu    Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻

   ghol: neela rang HALKA padta hai,              ghol: neela rang WAISA KA WAISA,
   H₂SO₄ banta hai (acidic)                       concentration wahi
```

> **Figure 3.3** – CuSO₄ ghol ke do cell. (a) Inert electrodes: anode par O₂, ghol amlin aur halka. (b) Copper electrodes: anode ghulta hai, cathode par copper jamta hai, ghol ka rang aur concentration nahi badalte. Dono mein cathode par Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu hi hota hai; farq anode ka hai.

## Part F: Solved examples (electron gin kar)

Is chapter mein hum bijli ke charge ka hisaab nahi lagate (wo aage ki class mein): sirf **electrons gin kar**, jo mole se judte hain.

### Example 1: CuSO₄ ghol, inert electrodes: taamba, O₂ aur acid

**Sawaal:** Platinum electrodes ke saath CuSO₄ ghol mein se bijli guzri aur cathode par **0.2 mol Cu** jama hua. Kitne mole electrons lage? Anode par kitni O₂ bani (mol aur gram)? Aur kitna H₂SO₄ bana (gram)?

**Hal:**
- Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu: 1 mol Cu ke liye 2 mol electrons. Electrons = 0.2 × 2 = **0.4 mol**.
- Anode: 4OH⁻ → 2H₂O + O₂ + 4e⁻: 4 mol electrons par 1 mol O₂. 0.4 mol electrons par O₂ = 0.4 ÷ 4 = **0.1 mol**. Mass = 0.1 × 32 = **3.2 g**.
- 2CuSO₄ + 2H₂O → 2Cu + 2H₂SO₄ + O₂: 2 mol Cu ke saath 2 mol H₂SO₄. 0.2 mol Cu, to H₂SO₄ = **0.2 mol**. H₂SO₄ = 2 + 32 + 64 = 98 g/mol, to 0.2 × 98 = **19.6 g**.
- **Jaanch (mass conservation, 0.2 mol ke liye):** left: 0.2 mol CuSO₄ (0.2 × 159.5 = 31.9 g) + 0.2 mol H₂O (3.6 g) = 35.5 g. Right: Cu 12.7 g + H₂SO₄ 19.6 g + O₂ 3.2 g = 35.5 g ✅.

### Example 2: Acidified paani: kitna paani, kitni gas

**Sawaal:** Acidified paani ke electrolysis mein 90 g paani poori tarah toot gaya. H₂ aur O₂ ke mass, mole, aur electrons kitne? Volume ratio kya?

**Hal:**
- H₂O = 18 g/mol, to 90 ÷ 18 = **5 mol**.
- 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂: 5 mol paani se 5 mol H₂ aur 2.5 mol O₂.
- H₂ = 5 × 2 = **10 g**. O₂ = 2.5 × 32 = **80 g**.
- **Jaanch:** 10 + 80 = 90 g ✅.
- Electrons: har H₂ ke liye 2 electrons, 5 mol H₂ ke liye **10 mol electrons**. Anode se bhi 2.5 × 4 = 10 mol electrons ✅.
- Volume ratio H₂ : O₂ = 5 : 2.5 = **2 : 1** ✅.

### Example 3: Concentrated NaCl: NaOH kitna?

**Sawaal:** Concentrated NaCl (graphite electrodes) ke electrolysis mein cathode par **0.5 mol H₂** bani. Kitni Cl₂ bani? Kitna NaOH bana (gram)? Kitne electrons lage?

**Hal:**
- 2NaCl + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + Cl₂ + H₂: 1 mol H₂ ke saath 1 mol Cl₂ aur 2 mol NaOH.
- Cl₂ = **0.5 mol** = 0.5 × 71 = 35.5 g (**zehreeli, sirf padhne ke liye**).
- NaOH = 2 × 0.5 = **1.0 mol** = 1.0 × 40 = **40 g**. (NaOH ek tez kshaar hai, sirf padhne ke liye.)
- Electrons: 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂: 0.5 × 2 = **1.0 mol**. Anode se bhi 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻: 0.5 × 2 = 1.0 mol ✅.

## Aam galtiyan (bachke rehna)

**Galti 1: "Cathode par sodium banega, kyunki ghol mein Na⁺ hai."**
Maano Nishka ke dost ne kaha ki NaCl ke ghol se sodium nikaalenge. Par ghol mein cathode par do pehelwan hain: Na⁺ aur H⁺. Order mein H⁺ upar hai, wahi jeetta hai, to cathode par **H₂** banta hai. Sodium sirf **pighle** NaCl se milta hai (Chapter 3), jahan paani nahi hota.

**Galti 2: "Inert aur copper electrode ka CuSO₄ mein ek hi result hai."**
Do beaker rakhte hain, dono mein neela CuSO₄. Beaker (a) mein graphite, beaker (b) mein taamba. Thodi der baad (a) ka neela rang halka, anode par bulbule; (b) ka neela rang waisa ka waisa aur anode patla. Dono mein cathode par taamba jamta hai, magar sirf (a) mein O₂ aur acid banta hai.

**Galti 3: "SO₄²⁻ anode par Cl⁻ jaisa discharge hota hai."**
Dilute H₂SO₄ ka electrolysis karte ho to anode par SO₂ ya SO₃ nahi, **O₂** milti hai. SO₄²⁻ paani wale ghol mein discharge nahi hota; OH⁻ hota hai. Isiliye acid "sirf ions dene wala" hai.

**Galti 4: "Anode negative hota hai."**
Topic 2 yaad karo: electrolytic cell mein anode **positive** terminal se juda hai, aur wahi par anions **electron dete** hain (oxidation). Cathode negative hai aur wahi par reduction hota hai. Agar tum anode ko negative maanoge, to Cl⁻ ko galat electrode ki taraf bhejoge. Sahi ye hai: Cl⁻ (negative ion) anode (positive electrode) ki taraf jata hai, kyunki viprit charge khinchte hain.

## Quick Recap

- Aqueous ghol mein **do group ke ions**: electrolyte ke aur paani ke (H⁺, OH⁻).
- **Cation order** (aasaan pehle): Ag⁺, Cu²⁺, H⁺, Pb²⁺, Sn²⁺, Ni²⁺, Fe²⁺, Zn²⁺, Al³⁺, Mg²⁺, Ca²⁺, Na⁺, K⁺ (reactivity series ulta). **Anion order**: I⁻, Br⁻, Cl⁻, OH⁻; NO₃⁻ aur SO₄²⁻ paani wale ghol se discharge nahi hote.
- Teen baatein: **order**, **concentration**, **electrode ka kism** (inert ya active).
- Acidified paani (Pt): H₂ : O₂ = 2 : 1. Concentrated NaCl (graphite): H₂, Cl₂, NaOH. Dilute NaCl: H₂ aur O₂. CuSO₄ (inert): Cu aur O₂, ghol amlin. CuSO₄ (Cu electrodes): anode ghulta, ghol waisa ka waisa.
- Mass ke sawaal: **electrons gino** (Cu²⁺ ke liye 2, O₂ ke liye 4), phir mole ratio.
- Chlorine **zehreeli** hai; har electrolysis sirf padhne ke liye ya teacher demo.

## Practice Questions

Har sawaal mein electrolyte, uski concentration aur electrodes diye gaye hain. Saare electrolysis **sirf padhne ke liye ya teacher demo** hain.

1. Dilute calcium nitrate, Ca(NO₃)₂, solution is electrolysed using inert platinum electrodes. Name the ions present, the product at each electrode, and write the half-equations. State the volume ratio of the two gases.
2. Concentrated hydrochloric acid is electrolysed using graphite electrodes. Name the ions present and the product at each electrode, with half-equations. (Chlorine is poisonous: described only.)
3. Dilute silver nitrate solution is electrolysed using inert platinum electrodes. Give the product at each electrode, write the overall equation, and say how the solution changes. Then predict what changes if the platinum is replaced by silver electrodes.
4. Concentrated potassium iodide solution is electrolysed using graphite electrodes. Give the product at each electrode, the substance left in the solution, and the overall equation. If 76.2 g of iodine is formed, find the moles of electrons used, the mass of hydrogen and the mass of KOH formed. (I = 127.)
5. Concentrated copper(II) chloride solution is electrolysed using graphite electrodes. If 3.81 g of copper is deposited, find the moles of electrons, and the mass of chlorine formed. Why is this a described-only demonstration?
6. Dilute sulphuric acid is electrolysed with a copper anode and a platinum cathode. Give the product at each electrode in the first few minutes, and say what colour change is seen in the solution.
7. Dilute sodium sulphate solution is electrolysed using inert platinum electrodes. Give the products. If 0.5 mol of electrons pass, find the mass of each gas, and say how the concentration of Na₂SO₄ changes.
8. A dilute solution contains silver nitrate, copper(II) nitrate and zinc nitrate. It is electrolysed with platinum electrodes for a long time. It is known that Ag⁺ is discharged more easily than Cu²⁺, Cu²⁺ more easily than H⁺, and H⁺ more easily than Zn²⁺, and that NO₃⁻ is not discharged. Give the order of the products at the cathode, and the product at the anode.
9. Concentrated sodium bromide solution is electrolysed using graphite electrodes. Name the products at both electrodes and the substance left in solution. Which is discharged first from a concentrated mixture of NaBr and NaCl at the anode? (Bromine is poisonous: described only.)
10. Explain why very dilute NaCl solution gives oxygen at the anode while concentrated NaCl gives chlorine. Also explain why sodium metal can never be obtained by electrolysing an aqueous NaCl solution, and name the method used in Chapter 3.
11. State True or False and correct the false ones: (a) Concentrated NaCl solution gives sodium at the cathode. (b) The anode is the positive electrode in an electrolytic cell. (c) With copper electrodes in CuSO₄ solution the blue colour fades. (d) In dilute H₂SO₄ (platinum electrodes), SO₄²⁻ is discharged at the anode. (e) Electrons travel through the solution from the cathode to the anode.
12. As a paper task, arrange these cations in the order of discharge, easiest first: Zn²⁺, Ag⁺, Na⁺, H⁺, Ni²⁺. Then say which of them give way to H⁺ in a dilute aqueous solution, and which one can never be obtained from any aqueous solution.

### Answers

1. Ions: Ca²⁺, NO₃⁻ (Ca(NO₃)₂ se) aur H⁺, OH⁻ (paani se). Cathode par **H₂** (H⁺ Ca²⁺ se aasaan): 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ → 2H₂. Anode par **O₂** (NO₃⁻ discharge nahi hota): 4OH⁻ → 2H₂O + O₂ + 4e⁻. Volume ratio H₂ : O₂ = **2 : 1**. Overall 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂ (Ca(NO₃)₂ sirf ions deta hai).
2. Ions: H⁺, Cl⁻ (HCl se) aur H⁺, OH⁻ (paani se). Cathode: **H₂**: 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂. Anode: ghol **concentrated** hai aur Cl⁻ order mein OH⁻ se pehle, isliye **Cl₂**: 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻. Ghol dheere-dheere HCl se khaali hota jaata hai. Chlorine zehreeli hai, isliye sirf fume cupboard mein teacher demo.
3. Cathode par **Ag**: Ag⁺ + e⁻ → Ag. Anode par **O₂**: 4OH⁻ → 2H₂O + O₂ + 4e⁻ (NO₃⁻ discharge nahi hota). Overall: 4AgNO₃ + 2H₂O → 4Ag + 4HNO₃ + O₂. Ghol mein **HNO₃** banta hai, ghol **amlin** hota hai aur Ag⁺ kam hota hai. **Silver electrodes** ke saath: anode par silver ghulta hai (Ag → Ag⁺ + e⁻), koi O₂ nahi; cathode par Ag jamta hai; ghol ka concentration waisa ka waisa. Silver nitrate dag chhodta hai aur nuksandeh hai: sirf teacher ke saath.
4. Ions: K⁺, I⁻, H⁺, OH⁻. Cathode: **H₂** (K⁺ nahi). Anode: **I₂** (I⁻, OH⁻ se pehle). Ghol mein K⁺ aur OH⁻: **KOH**. Overall: 2KI + 2H₂O → 2KOH + H₂ + I₂. Numbers: I₂ = 76.2 ÷ 254 = **0.3 mol**. 2I⁻ → I₂ + 2e⁻: electrons = 0.3 × 2 = **0.6 mol**. H₂ = 0.3 mol = 0.3 × 2 = **0.6 g**. KOH = 2 × 0.3 = 0.6 mol; KOH = 39 + 16 + 1 = 56 g/mol, to 0.6 × 56 = **33.6 g**. Jaanch (mass conservation): KI 0.6 mol (0.6 × 166 = 99.6 g) + H₂O 0.6 mol (10.8 g) = 110.4 g; KOH 33.6 + H₂ 0.6 + I₂ 76.2 = 110.4 g ✅.
5. Cu = 3.81 ÷ 63.5 = **0.06 mol**. Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu: electrons = 0.06 × 2 = **0.12 mol**. Anode: 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻: 0.12 mol electrons par 0.06 mol Cl₂ = 0.06 × 71 = **4.26 g**. Jaanch: 0.06 mol CuCl₂ = 0.06 × 134.5 = 8.07 g = 3.81 + 4.26 ✅. Chlorine zehreeli hai: isliye sirf padhne ke liye; copper sulphate/chloride ghol bhi nuksandeh hai.
6. Anode **copper hai (active)**: Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ (anode ghulta hai, koi O₂ nahi). Shuru mein ghol mein Cu²⁺ nahi hai, isliye cathode (platinum) par H⁺ discharge hota hai: 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂. Ghol dheere-dheere **neela** hota jaata hai kyunki Cu²⁺ ghol mein aata rehta hai. (Aage chalkar Cu²⁺ bhi cathode par jama hone lagta hai, kyunki order mein wo H⁺ se upar hai.)
7. Ions: Na⁺, SO₄²⁻, H⁺, OH⁻. Cathode **H₂**, anode **O₂**. 0.5 mol electrons: H₂ = 0.5 ÷ 2 = 0.25 mol = **0.5 g**; O₂ = 0.5 ÷ 4 = 0.125 mol = 0.125 × 32 = **4 g**. Paani kam hota hai (2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂), Na₂SO₄ nahi, isliye Na₂SO₄ ka concentration **thoda badhta hai**. Jaanch: paani ghata = 0.25 mol × 18 = 4.5 g; H₂ + O₂ = 0.5 + 4 = 4.5 g ✅.
8. Cathode par pehle **Ag** (sabse aasaan), jab Ag⁺ khatam ho gaya to **Cu**, jab Cu²⁺ bhi khatam ho gaya to **H₂** (H⁺ Zn²⁺ se aasaan, isliye zinc kabhi nahi jamta). Anode par hamesha **O₂** (NO₃⁻ nahi, OH⁻ discharge). Order: Ag, phir Cu, phir H₂.
9. Ions: Na⁺, Br⁻, H⁺, OH⁻. Cathode: **H₂**. Anode: **Br₂** (Br⁻, OH⁻ se pehle): 2Br⁻ → Br₂ + 2e⁻. Ghol mein **NaOH**. Br⁻ aur Cl⁻ ke mixture mein **Br⁻ pehle** discharge hota hai (Br⁻ order mein Cl⁻ se upar). Bromine zehreeli, laal-bhura vapour: sirf padhne ke liye.
10. Bahut dilute NaCl mein Cl⁻ bahut kam hai, aur paani ka OH⁻ ab bheed mein jeet jaata hai: **O₂**. Concentrated mein Cl⁻ bahut hain aur order mein bhi pehle, isliye **Cl₂**. Aqueous NaCl mein cathode par H⁺ aur Na⁺ dono hain, H⁺ aasaan hai, to H₂; sodium kabhi nahi. Sodium **pighle NaCl** ke electrolysis se milta hai (Chapter 3), jahan paani aur uske H⁺ hi nahi hote.
11. (a) **False** ❌: cathode par H₂ banta hai, sodium nahi. (b) **True** ✅. (c) **False** ❌: rang waisa ka waisa rehta hai, kyunki jitna Cu²⁺ cathode par gaya utna anode se aaya. (d) **False** ❌: SO₄²⁻ nahi, OH⁻ discharge hota hai, O₂ milti hai. (e) **False** ❌: electrons **tar** mein chalte hain (anode se battery ke raaste cathode tak); ghol mein **ions** bijli le jaate hain.
12. Order (aasaan pehle): **Ag⁺, H⁺, Ni²⁺, Zn²⁺, Na⁺**. Dilute paani wale ghol mein **Ni²⁺, Zn²⁺ aur Na⁺** ki jagah H⁺ discharge hota hai (H₂ banta hai), kyunki H⁺ order mein unse upar hai; Ag⁺ aur H⁺ discharge hote hain. **Na⁺** kisi bhi paani wale ghol se kabhi nahi; Ni²⁺ apne gaadhe ghol se plating mein jam sakta hai (Part A ki chhoot, Topic 4).
