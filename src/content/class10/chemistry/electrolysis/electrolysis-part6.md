# Topic 6: Predict Karo, Phir Dekho: Practice aur Chapter Ka Saar

Pichhle topics mein humne electrolysis ke saare hisse alag-alag seekhe: cell, anode aur cathode, kaun sa ion pehle discharge hota hai, plating, refining aur chlor-alkali. Ab ek hi sawaal bacha hai, jo asli science ka sawaal hota hai: **koi bhi nayi electrolysis dikha do, kya tum pehle se bata sakte ho ki kya banega?** Is aakhri topic mein ek chaar kadam ka tareeka banayenge, aath puzzle hal karenge, galtiyon ko pakadna seekhenge, aur phir poore chapter ka saar ek table mein dekhenge.

## School ki science-day aur "Predict first, then see" ka stall

School ki science-day thi. **Mr. Thatte** apne bhatije **Tirth** ke school gaye the. Ek kone mein ek stall tha, jiske upar bada board laga tha: **"Predict first, then see"**. Mez par glass ke chhote cells rakhe the, jinhe 6 volt ki battery se lab ke teacher chalate the. Bacchon ko sirf door se dekhna tha, chhoona nahi. Har cell ke saamne ek card tha: kaun sa electrolyte, kitna ghol (dilute ya concentrated) aur kaun se electrode.

Rule simple tha: pehle paper par likho ki cathode aur anode par kya banega, phir teacher cell chalayega, aur tab jawab dekho. Sahi jawab par ek sitara milta tha.

Ek aunty ne card padha aur haath utha diya: "Dilute sodium sulphate hai, to sodium dhatu banegi cathode par!" Teacher ne cell chalaya, aur dono electrodes par bulbule dikhne lage, koi dhatu nahi. Aunty hans padin, par Tirth ne notebook mein turant likha, "Chacha, ye galti to maine bhi pichhli baar ki thi. Kya koi tareeka hai jisse sahi jawab *hamesha* nikle, bina ratte?"

Mr. Thatte muskuraye. "Hai, Tirth. Chaar kadam, aur wahi tareeka jo hum ek-ek karke seekhte aaye hain. Aao, bana dete hain, aur phir stall ke saare cards hal karte hain."

> **Safety:** is topic ke saare electrolysis **sirf padhne ke liye** ya **school lab mein teacher ke saath** wale hain, kam voltage ki cell ke saath, kabhi mains ke saath nahi. Chlorine, bromine, molten salts, silver aur concentrated acids hamesha teacher ke kaam hain. Students kuch bhi nahi chakhte aur gas collect ya jalate nahi.

## Part A: Chaar kadam ka tareeka

Koi bhi electrolysis ho, ye chaar kadam chalo. Ye kadam wahi baatein hain jo Topic 2 aur 3 mein alag-alag aayi thi: bas ek hi jagah par rakh di hain.

1. **Ions ki list banao.** Electrolyte se aaye cations aur anions likho. Agar electrolyte **paani mein ghula** hai, to paani ke **H⁺ aur OH⁻** bhi list mein jodo. Agar electrolyte **pighla (molten)** hai, to sirf uske apne ions hain, kyunki paani nahi hai.
2. **Chuno.** Cathode par cations mein se **discharge order mein sabse upar wala** (Ag⁺, Cu²⁺, H⁺, Pb²⁺ ... K⁺). Anode par pehle dekho **electrode kaisa hai**: agar active hai (Cu, Ag, Ni), to wahi ghulta hai. Agar inert hai, to anions mein se sabse aasaani se discharge hone wala (I⁻, Br⁻, Cl⁻, OH⁻), aur **concentration** dekho: bahut dilute chloride mein Cl⁻ ki jagah OH⁻ discharge hota hai. NO₃⁻ aur SO₄²⁻ ghol se kabhi discharge nahi hote.
3. **Half-equations likho.** Cathode par electron lagte hain (reduction), anode par electron nikalte hain (oxidation). Atom aur charge dono barabar karo.
4. **Kya dikhega aur ghol ka kya hua?** Bulbule, dhatu ki parat, rang, ya ghol acidic ya alkaline hua? Ye bhi likho.

```
    KADAM 1                 KADAM 2                  KADAM 3             KADAM 4
    Ions ki list     ====>  Cathode aur anode  ====> Half-equations ===> Kya dikhega,
    (paani ho to            par chuno:               (atom aur           ghol ka kya hua
    H⁺ aur OH⁻ bhi)         order + concentration    charge barabar)     (rang, gas, acid
    (molten ho to           + electrode ki kism                           ya alkali)
    sirf apne ions)
```

> **Figure 6.1** – Chaar kadam ka tareeka. Kadam 2 mein teen cheezein dekhi jaati hain: discharge order, concentration aur electrode ki kism (inert ya active).

### Ek nazar mein niyam

| Kya dekhna hai | Niyam |
|---|---|
| Cathode (ghol) | Cations mein sabse pehle jo order mein upar ho: Ag⁺, Cu²⁺, H⁺ ... Ghol mein H⁺ hamesha maujood hai, isliye Na⁺, K⁺, Mg²⁺, Zn²⁺, Al³⁺ kabhi discharge nahi hote, H₂ banta hai |
| Cathode (molten) | Paani nahi, H⁺ nahi: dhatu ka ion hi discharge hota hai, dhatu banti hai |
| Anode (inert) | I⁻, Br⁻, Cl⁻ (concentrated), phir OH⁻. NO₃⁻ aur SO₄²⁻ nahi: OH⁻ discharge hota hai |
| Anode (active) | Electrode khud ghulta hai (Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻), koi anion discharge nahi |
| Anode (molten) | Anion hi discharge hota hai (2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻, 2Br⁻ → Br₂ + 2e⁻) |

## Part B: Aath puzzle, poore hal ke saath

Har puzzle mein electrolyte, uska ghol aur electrode likhe hain, to jawab sirf niyam se nikalta hai.

### Puzzle 1: Concentrated KI solution, graphite electrodes

**Kadam 1:** ions: K⁺, I⁻ (electrolyte se) aur H⁺, OH⁻ (paani se).
**Kadam 2:** cathode par K⁺ aur H⁺ mein H⁺ order mein pehle: **H₂**. Anode par graphite inert hai; I⁻ aur OH⁻ mein I⁻ pehle: **iodine, I₂**.
**Kadam 3:** cathode: 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂. Anode: 2I⁻ → I₂ + 2e⁻.
**Kadam 4:** cathode par bulbule (gas H₂). Anode ke paas ghol bhoora hone lagta hai (iodine ghul kar). K⁺ aur OH⁻ ghol mein bache, to **KOH** bana: ghol **alkaline** hua (teacher phenolphthalein daale to cathode ke paas gulabi dikhta hai).
Overall: **2KI + 2H₂O → 2KOH + H₂ + I₂**.

**Jaanch:** atom: K 2 = 2, I 2 = 2, H 4 = 2 + 2 = 4, O 2 = 2 ✅. Charge dono taraf 0 ✅. Electron: cathode 2 aur anode 2 ✅. (Iodine harmful hota hai: teacher demo, sirf dekhna.)

### Puzzle 2: Concentrated HCl, graphite electrodes

**Kadam 1:** H⁺, Cl⁻ (acid se) aur paani ke H⁺, OH⁻.
**Kadam 2:** cathode: sirf H⁺ hai: **H₂**. Anode: Cl⁻ aur OH⁻ mein, ghol concentrated hai, to Cl⁻: **Cl₂**.
**Kadam 3:** 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂; 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻.
**Kadam 4:** dono taraf gas, barabar matra mein (1 : 1 volume mein, kyunki har H₂ ke saath ek Cl₂ ke liye 2 electron lagte hain). Chlorine hari-peeli, tez ghutan wali, **poisonous** gas: sirf padhne ke liye, fume cupboard mein teacher. Acid dheere-dheere kam concentrated hota jaata hai.
Overall: **2HCl → H₂ + Cl₂**.

**Jaanch:** H 2 = 2, Cl 2 = 2 ✅; electron 2 = 2 ✅; charge 0 = 0 ✅.

### Puzzle 3: Molten ZnCl₂ (lagbhag 290 °C se upar), graphite electrodes

**Kadam 1:** molten hai, paani nahi, isliye sirf **Zn²⁺ aur Cl⁻**. H⁺ ya OH⁻ ka sawaal hi nahi.
**Kadam 2:** cathode par Zn²⁺ ke siwa koi cation nahi, to **zinc dhatu**. Anode par **Cl₂**.
**Kadam 3:** Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Zn; 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻.
**Kadam 4:** cathode par pighla zinc ikattha hota hai, anode par chlorine gas. Ghol nahi hai, to ghol ka koi sawaal nahi.
Overall: **ZnCl₂ → Zn + Cl₂**.

**Jaanch:** Zn 1 = 1, Cl 2 = 2 ✅; charge: (+2) + 2(−1) = 0 ✅. Ye **sirf padhne ke liye** hai (garam molten salt aur poisonous Cl₂).

> **Ye puzzle kyun zaroori hai:** Puzzle 3 ka Zn molten se milta hai. Agar yahi ZnCl₂ paani mein hota, to cathode par H₂ banta (Zn²⁺ order mein H⁺ ke baad hai). Isi liye Chapter 3 mein Na, Mg aur Al **molten** yaugik se banaye gaye the, ghol se nahi.

### Puzzle 4: Dilute Na₂SO₄ solution, platinum electrodes

**Kadam 1:** Na⁺, SO₄²⁻ aur H⁺, OH⁻.
**Kadam 2:** cathode: Na⁺ aur H⁺ mein H⁺: **H₂**. Anode: SO₄²⁻ kabhi discharge nahi hota, to OH⁻: **O₂**.
**Kadam 3:** 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ → 2H₂; 4OH⁻ → 2H₂O + O₂ + 4e⁻.
**Kadam 4:** cathode par anode se **doguni** gas (2 : 1). Na₂SO₄ khud nahi badla, sirf paani toota: overall **2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂**. (Lab demo: teacher agar universal indicator daale to cathode ke paas ghol **neela-baingani** (alkaline) hota hai, kyunki H⁺ nikal gaya aur OH⁻ bacha, aur anode ke paas **laal** (acidic), kyunki OH⁻ nikal gaya aur H⁺ bacha. Hilaane par dono mil kar phir neutral ho jaate hain.)

**Jaanch:** atom: H 4 = 4, O 2 = 2 ✅; electron 4 = 4 ✅; charge 0 = 0 ✅. Aunty ka "sodium dhatu" isliye galat tha ki Na⁺ order mein H⁺ ke baad aata hai.

### Puzzle 5: Concentrated CuCl₂ solution, graphite electrodes

**Kadam 1:** Cu²⁺, Cl⁻ aur H⁺, OH⁻.
**Kadam 2:** cathode: Cu²⁺ order mein H⁺ se pehle: **Cu**. Anode: concentrated hai, to Cl⁻: **Cl₂**.
**Kadam 3:** Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu; 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻.
**Kadam 4:** cathode par laal-bhoori taambe ki parat, anode par chlorine ke bulbule. Ghol ka rang dheere-dheere halka hota hai, kyunki Cu²⁺ kam ho raha hai.
Overall: **CuCl₂ → Cu + Cl₂**.

**Jaanch:** Cu 1 = 1, Cl 2 = 2 ✅; charge (+2) + 2(−1) = 0 ✅. (Copper salt ke ghol ko chakhna nahi, harmful hai.)

### Puzzle 6: Wahi CuCl₂ solution, par ab copper electrodes

**Kadam 1:** Cu²⁺, Cl⁻ aur H⁺, OH⁻, sab wahi.
**Kadam 2:** cathode par wahi: **Cu**. Anode copper ka hai, active hai, to **anode khud ghulta hai**: Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻. Chlorine **nahi** banti.
**Kadam 3:** Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu; Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻.
**Kadam 4:** cathode ka mass badhta hai, anode ka utna hi ghat-ta hai. Ghol ka rang aur concentration **vaisa hi rehta hai**. Yahi sidha refining aur plating ka tareeka hai (Topic 4 aur 5).

**Jaanch:** electron 2 = 2 ✅; charge 0 = 0 ✅; anode se jitna Cu gaya, cathode par utna aaya, isliye kul Cu ka mass same ✅.

**Puzzle 5 aur 6 ka farq:** wahi ghol, wahi cathode, par electrode badalne se anode ka product Cl₂ se "Cu dissolves" ho gaya.

### Puzzle 7: AgNO₃ solution, platinum electrodes

**Kadam 1:** Ag⁺, NO₃⁻ aur H⁺, OH⁻.
**Kadam 2:** cathode: Ag⁺ order mein sabse upar: **Ag**. Anode: NO₃⁻ discharge nahi hota, to OH⁻: **O₂**.
**Kadam 3:** Ag⁺ + e⁻ → Ag (chaar baar, 4 electron ke liye); 4OH⁻ → 2H₂O + O₂ + 4e⁻.
**Kadam 4:** cathode par chamakdar safed-grey chaandi ki parat. Anode par O₂ ke bulbule. Anode par OH⁻ nikal gaya, to H⁺ aur NO₃⁻ bache: ghol mein **nitric acid** bana, ghol **acidic** hua.
Overall: **4AgNO₃ + 2H₂O → 4Ag + 4HNO₃ + O₂**.

**Jaanch:** atom: Ag 4 = 4, N 4 = 4, H 4 = 4, O: baayein 4 × 3 + 2 = 14, daayein 4 × 3 + 2 = 14 ✅. Electron: cathode 4 aur anode 4 ✅. (Silver nitrate zehreela hai aur skin par kaale daag chhodta hai: **sirf teacher demo ya padhna**.)

### Puzzle 8: Dilute H₂SO₄, copper electrodes

**Kadam 1:** H⁺, SO₄²⁻ aur paani ke H⁺, OH⁻.
**Kadam 2:** cathode: shuru mein sirf H⁺: **H₂**. Anode copper ka active hai, to **copper ghulta hai**, OH⁻ discharge nahi hota. (Ye sirf **shuru ke kuch minute** ka jawab hai: jab ghol mein Cu²⁺ jama ho jaata hai, to wo bhi cathode par jamne lagta hai, kyunki order mein Cu²⁺ H⁺ se upar hai.)
**Kadam 3:** 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂; Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻.
**Kadam 4:** cathode par bulbule, anode ka copper ghat-ta hai, aur ghol dheere-dheere **neela** ho jaata hai (Cu²⁺ ke saath SO₄²⁻ = CuSO₄). Overall: **Cu + H₂SO₄ → CuSO₄ + H₂**, jo bijli ki madad se hua.

**Jaanch:** Cu 1 = 1, H 2 = 2, S 1 = 1, O 4 = 4 ✅. Chapter 2 mein tumne dekha tha ki copper dilute acid se nahi milta: yahan bijli ne wo kaam kar diya jo acid akela nahi kar pata, kyunki electron ko battery kheench rahi hai ✅. (Acid ke saath teacher ka kaam.)

## Part C: Mixed problems, Chapter 2, 3 aur 4 jodkar

### Mixed 1: Do dhatu ke ions saath mein

**Sawaal:** ek dilute ghol mein CuSO₄ aur ZnSO₄ dono hain. Graphite electrodes se bijli guzari jaati hai. Cathode par kya, kis kram mein banega? Anode par kya?

**Hal:** ions: Cu²⁺, Zn²⁺, SO₄²⁻, H⁺, OH⁻. Cathode par cations mein order: Cu²⁺, phir H⁺, phir Zn²⁺. To **pehle Cu** jam jaata hai. Jab Cu²⁺ khatam ho jaata hai (neela rang gaayab), tab **H₂** banna shuru hota hai. **Zn²⁺ kabhi discharge nahi hota**, wo ghol mein hi rehta hai. Anode par hamesha **O₂** (SO₄²⁻ discharge nahi hota).

**Jaanch:** Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu aur 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂, dono mein 2 electron ✅. Yahi Chapter 2 ki displacement ka ulta hai: dhatu jo kam reactive hai wahi pehle utarti hai ✅.

### Mixed 2: Aluminium ghol se kyun nahi nikalta?

**Sawaal:** concentrated AlCl₃ solution graphite electrodes ke saath electrolyse karne se aluminium kyun nahi milta? Kya banta hai? Phir aluminium ke liye Chapter 3 ne kya kiya?

**Hal:** ions: Al³⁺, Cl⁻, H⁺, OH⁻. Cathode par Al³⁺ order mein H⁺ ke **bahut baad** hai, to **H₂** banta hai, Al nahi. Anode par concentrated hai, to **Cl₂**. Ghol mein Al³⁺ aur OH⁻ bachte hain (aluminium hydroxide ki safed parat jaisa kuch). Isi liye Chapter 3 mein aluminium **molten alumina** (cryolite mein ghula hua) ki electrolysis se banaya gaya: paani nahi, to H⁺ nahi ([metallurgy-part5.md](../metallurgy/metallurgy-part5.md)).

**Jaanch:** cathode: 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂; anode: 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻: electron 2 = 2 ✅.

### Mixed 3: Electron gino, mass nikalo (molten ZnCl₂)

**Sawaal:** Puzzle 3 ke cell mein 0.5 mol electron guzre. Kitna zinc aur kitni chlorine bani?

**Hal:** har Zn²⁺ ko 2 electron chahiye: Zn ke moles = 0.5 ÷ 2 = 0.25 mol, mass = 0.25 × 65 = **16.25 g**. Har Cl₂ banne mein 2 electron nikalte hain: Cl₂ ke moles = 0.5 ÷ 2 = 0.25 mol, mass = 0.25 × 71 = **17.75 g**.

**Jaanch:** 16.25 + 17.75 = 34.0 g, aur 0.25 mol ZnCl₂ ka mass = 0.25 × (65 + 71) = 34.0 g ✅. Dhatu aur gas ka kul mass, tootne wale yaugik ke barabar ✅.

### Mixed 4: Displacement aur electrolysis, ek hi baat do roop mein

**Sawaal:** Chapter 2 mein zinc ki patti CuSO₄ ke ghol mein daalne par taambe ki parat chadhi thi. Electrolysis mein bhi cathode par taamba chadhta hai. Dono mein kya same hai aur kya alag?

**Hal:** **Same:** dono mein Cu²⁺ electron leta hai: Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu. **Alag:** displacement mein electron seedhe zinc se aate hain (Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻), jabki electrolysis mein electron battery se wire ke raaste cathode tak aate hain. Zinc reactive hai, isliye wo khud electron dene ko taiyaar hai; kam reactive dhatu ko electron "bijli" se dene padte hain ([metals-and-non-metals-part4.md](../metals-and-non-metals/metals-and-non-metals-part4.md)).

**Jaanch:** Zn + Cu²⁺ → Zn²⁺ + Cu: charge baayein +2, daayein +2 ✅.

## Aam galtiyan (bachke rehna)

1. **Galti 1: "Molten NaCl se bhi hydrogen banta hai."**
   Ek chhota sa test: molten NaCl ke cell mein kaun-kaun se ions hain? Sirf Na⁺ aur Cl⁻. Paani hai hi nahi, to H⁺ aur OH⁻ bhi nahi. Isliye cathode par **sodium** banta hai, hydrogen nahi (Na⁺ + e⁻ → Na). Hydrogen tabhi aata hai jab ghol mein paani ho. Isi vajah se Chapter 3 mein Na molten NaCl se bana, brine se nahi.

2. **Galti 2: "Electrolytic cell mein anode negative hota hai."**
   Ek battery ka + terminal jis electrode se juda hai, uska naam hai **anode**. Anode **positive** hota hai aur negative anions (Cl⁻, OH⁻) ko apni taraf kheenchta hai. Cathode negative hota hai aur positive cations ko kheenchta hai. Yaad rakho: **Anode ko Anion, Cathode ko Cation**, aur anode battery ke + se jura hai.

3. **Galti 3: "Electron ghol mein tairte hain."**
   Socho ek relay race: wire mein electron daudte hain (anode se battery ke raaste cathode tak). Ghol mein electron nahi, **ions** daudte hain: cations cathode ki taraf, anions anode ki taraf. Electron cathode par ion ko milte hain aur anode par ion se nikalte hain: yahi dono taraf ke raaste ko jodta hai. Agar electron ghol se guzarte, to ghol kisi metal wire jaisa conductor hota aur electrolysis ke baad kuch change na hota.

4. **Galti 4: "Dilute aur concentrated NaCl se anode par ek hi gas banti hai."**
   Do cell sochkar dekho, dono mein graphite electrode. Concentrated NaCl mein Cl⁻ bahut hain, aur wo OH⁻ se pehle discharge hote hain: **Cl₂** banti hai. Bahut dilute NaCl mein Cl⁻ bahut kam hain aur paani ke OH⁻ discharge ho jaate hain: anode par **O₂**. Cathode par dono mein H₂. (Topic 3 ka pool chlorinator patle namak-paani se bhi Cl₂ isliye bana paata hai ki usme khaas coating wale electrodes lage hote hain; hamare simple niyam graphite aur platinum ke liye hain.)

5. **Galti 5: "Cathode par hamesha dhatu hi banti hai."**
   Puzzle 4 yaad karo: dilute Na₂SO₄ mein cathode par **H₂** aaya, koi dhatu nahi. Dhatu tabhi banti hai jab uska ion H⁺ se pehle discharge ho (Ag⁺, Cu²⁺) ya jab paani ho hi nahi (molten). Na, K, Mg, Zn, Al ghol se nahi nikalte.

6. **Galti 6: "Electrode badalne se product nahi badalta."**
   Puzzle 5 aur 6 saath rakho. Wahi concentrated CuCl₂ ghol. Graphite anode: **Cl₂** ke bulbule, ghol ka neela rang halka. Copper anode: koi gas nahi, **anode ghulta hai**, rang same. Cathode par dono mein taamba hai, par anode ki kahani poori badal gayi. Isliye "electrode ki kism" kadam 2 ka tisra sawaal hai.

## Quick Recap

- Chaar kadam: **(1)** ions ki list (ghol mein H⁺, OH⁻ bhi; molten mein sirf apne ions), **(2)** discharge order, concentration aur electrode ki kism se chuno, **(3)** half-equations (atom aur charge barabar), **(4)** kya dikhega aur ghol ka kya hua.
- Cathode (ghol): Ag⁺, Cu²⁺, H⁺ ... order mein pehle wala. Na⁺, K⁺, Mg²⁺, Zn²⁺, Al³⁺ ghol se discharge nahi hote, H₂ banta hai. Molten mein dhatu hi banti hai.
- Anode (inert): I⁻, Br⁻, Cl⁻ (concentrated), phir OH⁻. NO₃⁻ aur SO₄²⁻ ghol se nahi, O₂ banti hai. Active anode khud ghulta hai.
- Anode positive hai (battery ke + se juda), cathode negative. Electron wire mein chalte hain, ghol mein ions.
- Electron ginkar mass: Cu²⁺ ko 2 electron, Ag⁺ ko 1, Cl⁻ se Cl₂ ke liye 2, O₂ ke liye 4. Mole ratio se mass nikalo.
- Chlorine, bromine, molten salts, silver aur acids: sirf padhne ke liye ya teacher ke saath.

## Practice Questions

1. Molten calcium chloride, CaCl₂, is electrolysed using graphite electrodes. (a) List the ions present. (b) Name the product at each electrode and write the two half-equations and the overall equation. (c) If 1.2 mol of electrons pass through the cell, find the mass of calcium and of chlorine formed, and show that the total equals the mass of CaCl₂ decomposed.
2. Dilute nitric acid is electrolysed with platinum electrodes. List the ions present, name the product at each electrode, write the half-equations and state the ratio of the volumes of the two gases. What happens to the concentration of the acid as the electrolysis continues?
3. Dilute sodium hydroxide solution is electrolysed with platinum electrodes. (a) Name the products at the cathode and anode, and write the half-equations. (b) What happens to the concentration of the NaOH? (c) If 0.8 mol of electrons pass, find the mass of hydrogen and of oxygen formed, and the mass of water decomposed.
4. A concentrated solution of magnesium chloride is electrolysed with graphite electrodes. Name the products at the two electrodes and say what is left in the solution. Why can magnesium metal not be obtained this way? How was magnesium obtained in Chapter 3?
5. A solution of copper(II) nitrate is electrolysed in two cells. Use the rule that NO₃⁻ is never discharged from a solution. (a) Cell A uses graphite electrodes. Name the product at each electrode, and say what happens to the blue colour and to the acidity of the solution. (b) Cell B uses copper electrodes. Name the changes at each electrode and at the solution. (c) In cell A, 19.05 g of copper is deposited. Find the moles of electrons that passed and the mass of oxygen formed at the anode. (d) In cell B, 0.7 mol of electrons pass. Find the change in mass of each electrode.
6. Platinum electrodes are used in each of these solutions: (a) dilute potassium sulphate, (b) concentrated potassium chloride, (c) concentrated sodium iodide, (d) dilute potassium nitrate. Which give only hydrogen and oxygen in the ratio 2 : 1? Which give a halogen at the anode (name it)? In which does the solution become alkaline, and which compound causes it?
7. Find the mistake in each statement and correct it. (a) "Electrolysis of molten potassium bromide gives hydrogen at the cathode." (b) "Concentrated KCl solution gives potassium at the cathode." (c) "In the solution, electrons carry the current from the cathode to the anode." (d) "The anode is the negative electrode of an electrolytic cell."
8. A very dilute solution of potassium chloride is electrolysed with platinum electrodes. Use the rule that in a very dilute chloride solution OH⁻ from water is discharged instead of Cl⁻. Name the products at both electrodes and explain how this differs from concentrated KCl solution (Question 6b).
9. After the electrolysis of a concentrated KCl solution (Question 6b) for some time, a few drops of phenolphthalein are added near the cathode. What colour is seen, and why? Link your answer with Chapter 1 (acids, bases and salts).
10. For the cell of Question 1 (molten CaCl₂, graphite electrodes), describe the path of the electrons and the movement of ions. Which electrode is joined to the + terminal of the battery, and what is its name?
11. State whether each statement is true or false and correct the false ones. (a) In a molten electrolyte, hydrogen ions from water reach the cathode. (b) With an active anode, the anode itself dissolves. (c) Aluminium is obtained from concentrated aluminium chloride solution by electrolysis. (d) NO₃⁻ ions are discharged at the anode from a solution.
12. Using Figure 6.2, write the half-equations for row 4 (dilute KOH, platinum) and row 6 (concentrated CuBr₂, copper electrodes). Atoms and charges must balance.

### Answers

1. (a) Molten hai, paani nahi, to ions sirf **Ca²⁺ aur Cl⁻**. (b) Cathode: **Ca dhatu** (Ca²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Ca). Anode: **Cl₂** (2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻). Overall: **CaCl₂ → Ca + Cl₂**. Atom: Ca 1 = 1, Cl 2 = 2; charge (+2) + 2(−1) = 0. (c) Ca ke moles = 1.2 ÷ 2 = 0.6 mol, mass = 0.6 × 40 = **24.0 g**. Cl₂ ke moles = 1.2 ÷ 2 = 0.6 mol, mass = 0.6 × 71 = **42.6 g**. Jaanch: 24.0 + 42.6 = 66.6 g, aur 0.6 mol CaCl₂ = 0.6 × (40 + 71) = 66.6 g ✅.

2. Ions: H⁺, NO₃⁻ (acid se) aur H⁺, OH⁻ (paani se). Cathode: **H₂** (4H⁺ + 4e⁻ → 2H₂). Anode: NO₃⁻ discharge nahi hota, to OH⁻: **O₂** (4OH⁻ → 2H₂O + O₂ + 4e⁻). Volume ratio **H₂ : O₂ = 2 : 1**, overall 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂. Paani toota, acid nahi, to ghol **zyada concentrated** hota jaata hai. Jaanch: electron 4 = 4 ✅.

3. (a) Ions: Na⁺, OH⁻ aur H⁺, OH⁻. Cathode: **H₂** (4H⁺ + 4e⁻ → 2H₂), anode: **O₂** (4OH⁻ → 2H₂O + O₂ + 4e⁻). (b) Paani toota, NaOH nahi, to NaOH ka ghol **zyada concentrated** hota jaata hai. (c) H₂ ke moles = 0.8 ÷ 2 = 0.4 mol, mass = 0.4 × 2 = **0.8 g**. O₂ ke moles = 0.8 ÷ 4 = 0.2 mol, mass = 0.2 × 32 = **6.4 g**. Paani: 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂ mein 0.4 mol H₂ ke liye 0.4 mol H₂O = 0.4 × 18 = **7.2 g**. Jaanch: 0.8 + 6.4 = 7.2 g ✅.

4. Ions: Mg²⁺, Cl⁻, H⁺, OH⁻. Cathode: Mg²⁺ order mein H⁺ ke **bahut baad** hai, to **H₂** banta hai (2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂). Anode: ghol concentrated hai, to **Cl₂**. Ghol mein Mg²⁺ aur OH⁻ bache, to **Mg(OH)₂** ki safed, bahut kam ghulne wali parat ban jaati hai: MgCl₂ + 2H₂O → Mg(OH)₂ + Cl₂ + H₂. Atom: Mg 1 = 1, Cl 2 = 2, H 4 = 2 + 2 = 4, O 2 = 2. Magnesium dhatu isliye nahi milti: ghol mein H⁺ hai aur Mg²⁺ usse pehle discharge nahi hota. Chapter 3 mein Mg **molten MgCl₂** ki electrolysis se mila tha, jahan paani nahi hai.

5. (a) Cell A: cathode par **Cu** (Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu), anode par **O₂** (4OH⁻ → 2H₂O + O₂ + 4e⁻). Ghol ka neela rang halka hota hai aur ghol mein H⁺ aur NO₃⁻ bachte hain, to **nitric acid** banta hai: ghol **acidic** hota hai. Overall 2Cu(NO₃)₂ + 2H₂O → 2Cu + 4HNO₃ + O₂ (Cu 2 = 2, N 4 = 4, H 4 = 4, O 12 + 2 = 14 = 14). (b) Cell B: cathode par Cu jamta hai (Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu), anode ka copper ghulta hai (Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻), O₂ nahi banti, ghol ka neela rang aur concentration same rehte hain. (c) Cu ke moles = 19.05 ÷ 63.5 = 0.3 mol; electron = 0.3 × 2 = **0.6 mol**. O₂ ke moles = 0.6 ÷ 4 = 0.15 mol, mass = 0.15 × 32 = **4.8 g**. Jaanch: 0.3 mol Cu aur 0.15 mol O₂ ka anupaat 2 : 1, jaise overall equation mein ✅. (d) Cu ke moles = 0.7 ÷ 2 = 0.35 mol, mass = 0.35 × 63.5 = **22.225 g**: cathode ka mass **22.225 g badhta hai** aur anode ka utna hi **ghat-ta hai**.

6. (a) Ions K⁺, SO₄²⁻, H⁺, OH⁻: H₂ aur O₂. (b) K⁺, Cl⁻, H⁺, OH⁻, concentrated: cathode H₂, anode **Cl₂** (chlorine). (c) Na⁺, I⁻, H⁺, OH⁻: cathode H₂, anode **I₂** (iodine). (d) K⁺, NO₃⁻, H⁺, OH⁻: H₂ aur O₂. **Sirf H₂ aur O₂ (2 : 1):** (a) aur (d). **Halogen:** (b) chlorine, (c) iodine. **Alkaline:** (b) aur (c), kyunki cathode par H⁺ nikal gaya aur ghol mein K⁺ aur OH⁻ ya Na⁺ aur OH⁻ bache: **KOH** aur **NaOH**. (a) aur (d) mein ghol neutral hi rehta hai (paani toota, ghol thoda zyada concentrated).

7. (a) **Galat:** molten mein paani nahi, to H⁺ bhi nahi. Cathode par **potassium dhatu** banti hai (K⁺ + e⁻ → K), anode par bromine. (b) **Galat:** ghol mein K⁺ H⁺ ke baad aata hai. Cathode par **H₂** banta hai, K⁺ ghol mein rehta hai. (c) **Galat:** ghol mein ions chalte hain, electron nahi; electron wire mein anode se battery ke raaste cathode tak jaate hain. (d) **Galat:** electrolytic cell mein anode battery ke + se juda hai, wo **positive** hai.

8. Ions: K⁺, Cl⁻ (bahut kam), H⁺, OH⁻. Cathode: **H₂**. Anode: bahut dilute chloride mein OH⁻ discharge hota hai, to **O₂** (4OH⁻ → 2H₂O + O₂ + 4e⁻). Concentrated KCl (Question 6b) mein anode par **Cl₂** banti hai. Cathode par dono mein H₂ hai. To concentration ne sirf anode ka product badla.

9. **Gulabi** rang dikhta hai. Kaaran: cathode par H⁺ nikal gaya aur ghol mein K⁺ aur OH⁻ bache, yaani **KOH** (ek strong alkali) bana. Phenolphthalein alkali mein gulabi hota hai (Chapter 1).

10. Anode ek taraf battery ke **+ terminal** se juda hai, cathode **− terminal** se. Wire mein electron **anode se battery ke raaste cathode ki taraf** chalte hain. Pighle CaCl₂ mein **Ca²⁺ cathode ki taraf** aur **Cl⁻ anode ki taraf** chalte hain. Cathode par Ca²⁺ electron leta hai (Ca dhatu), anode par Cl⁻ electron chhodta hai (Cl₂), aur wahi electron wire mein jaate hain. **Positive terminal wala electrode anode** hai.

11. (a) **False** ❌: molten mein paani nahi, H⁺ nahi; wahan dhatu ka ion hi cathode par aata hai. (b) **True** ✅: active anode (jaise copper) khud ghulta hai aur anion discharge nahi hota. (c) **False** ❌: ghol mein H⁺ aata hai aur Al³⁺ order mein bahut baad hai, H₂ banta hai; aluminium **molten alumina** se banta hai (Chapter 3). (d) **False** ❌: NO₃⁻ ghol se discharge nahi hota; OH⁻ discharge hota hai aur O₂ banti hai.

12. Row 4 (dilute KOH, platinum): cathode **4H⁺ + 4e⁻ → 2H₂**, anode **4OH⁻ → 2H₂O + O₂ + 4e⁻**. Atom: cathode H 4 = 4; anode O 4 = 2 + 2, H 4 = 4; charge: cathode (+4) + (−4) = 0, anode (−4) = (−4). Row 6 (concentrated CuBr₂, copper electrodes): cathode **Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu**, anode **Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻**. Atom Cu 1 = 1; charge cathode (+2) + (−2) = 0, anode 0 = (+2) + (−2).

13. **Chlorine** (Cl₂, jaise concentrated HCl ya brine ya molten chloride ki electrolysis mein) aur **bromine** (Br₂, jaise molten ya concentrated bromide mein): dono poisonous hain. Teacher ye kaam **fume cupboard** mein, hawadaar jagah par karta hai, aur students door rehte hain. Molten lead bromide mein lead bhi poisonous hai. Paani ki electrolysis mein H₂ jalne wali gas hai aur O₂ aag ko tez karti hai: dono ka mixture phat bhi sakta hai, isliye students gas collect ya jalate nahi, sirf teacher ke saath dekhte hain.
