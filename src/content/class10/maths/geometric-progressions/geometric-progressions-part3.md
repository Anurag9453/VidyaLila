# Topic 3: GP Ko Pehchanne Ki Paheliyan: Do Terms Se Poora GP

Topic 2 mein tumhare paas ek mazboot formula aaya: aₙ = a rⁿ⁻¹. Wahan pehla term a aur common ratio r diye hote the, aur hum koi bhi term nikaal lete the. Ab sawaal **ulta** hai: a aur r nahi diye, bas **kuch khabrein** di hain (do terms, ya ek term aur ek rishta, ya teen numbers ke bare mein ek shart), aur humein **poora GP** pakadna hai. Is topic mein dekhenge ki do terms se r aur a kaise milte hain, jab r sirf ek nahi balki do ho sakte hain to kya karein, aur teen numbers ke GP mein hone ki pehchaan kya hai.

## Part A: Do terms se r aur a

GP ke do terms diye hain: aₘ aur aₖ, jahan m > k (m-wa term k-wa term se aage hai). Topic 2 ke formula se:

aₘ = a rᵐ⁻¹ aur aₖ = a rᵏ⁻¹.

Dono ko ek ko doosre se **divide** karo. a ≠ 0 aur r ≠ 0 hai (GP mein koi term zero nahi hota), isliye a kat jaata hai:

aₘ ÷ aₖ = (a rᵐ⁻¹) ÷ (a rᵏ⁻¹) = rᵐ⁻¹⁻⁽ᵏ⁻¹⁾ = **rᵐ⁻ᵏ**. (Class 9 ka law: aᵖ ÷ aᵠ = aᵖ⁻ᵠ.)

**Kyun?** k-wa term se m-wa term tak pahunchne mein r se kitni baar guna karna padta hai? Bas (m − k) baar: har kadam par ek r. To aₘ = aₖ × rᵐ⁻ᵏ, aur wahi baat divide karne par milti hai.

**Teen kadam:**
1. Bade number wale term ko chhote number wale term se divide karo: rᵐ⁻ᵏ = aₘ/aₖ.
2. Is se r nikalo (power (m − k) ki root). Agar (m − k) **odd** ho (3, 5, ...), to ek hi real r milta hai, aur r ka sign aₘ/aₖ ke sign jaisa hota hai. Even ho to Part B dekho.
3. Ab a nikalo: a = aₖ ÷ rᵏ⁻¹. Ya k = 2 ho to seedha a = a₂ ÷ r.

### Example 1: Saurav ki sheets

Sheet 2 ki width a₂ = 192, sheet 5 ki a₅ = 81. Yahan m = 5, k = 2, m − k = 3.
r³ = 81/192 = 27/64 (dono ko 3 se divide kiya), to r = 3/4. (Kyunki (3/4)³ = 27/64.)
a = a₂ ÷ r = 192 ÷ (3/4) = 192 × 4/3 = **256.**
Zoom r = 3/4 = **75%**, original naksha **256 mm** chauda. Sheets ki widths: 256, 192, 144, 108, 81, 60.75, 45.5625, 34.171875, ... To 8-vi sheet: a₈ = 256 × (3/4)⁷ = 256 × 2187/16384 = 2187/64 ≈ **34.17 mm.**
**Jaanch:** 256 × 3/4 = 192, × 3/4 = 144, × 3/4 = 108, × 3/4 = 81 ✅ (a₅ diye hue 81 se milta hai).
**Story check:** A3 sheet ki chhoti side 297 mm hoti hai, to 256 mm ka naksha usme aa jaata hai. 75% ek aam zoom hai, aur sab widths positive aur ghat rahi hain, jaisa copy ki copy mein hona chahiye.

```
sheet 1  ################################ 256 mm
sheet 2  ########################         192 mm
sheet 3  ##################               144 mm
sheet 4  ##############                   108 mm
sheet 5  ##########                       81 mm
```

> **Figure 3.1** – Saurav ki paanch sheets ki widths ke bars (1 hash lagbhag 8 mm). Har bar pichhle bar ka 3/4 hai: 256 se 192, phir 144, 108, 81. Bars pehle tezi se aur phir dheere-dheere chhote hote hain, kyunki har baar ghatne wali cheez khud chhoti ho chuki hai.

### Example 2: negative ratio

Ek GP mein a₃ = 10 aur a₆ = −270. Yahan m − k = 3.
r³ = −270/10 = −27, to **r = −3.** (Kyunki (−3)³ = −27: odd power mein sign wahi rehta hai, isliye r negative.)
a = a₃ ÷ r² = 10 ÷ 9 = **10/9.**
GP: 10/9, −10/3, 10, −30, 90, −270, ...
**Jaanch:** 10/9 × (−3) = −10/3, × (−3) = 10 ✅, aur 10 × (−3) = −30, × (−3) = 90, × (−3) = −270 ✅.

### Example 3: fraction wale terms

Ek GP mein a₂ = −4/25 aur a₅ = 5/16. Yahan m − k = 3.
r³ = (5/16) ÷ (−4/25) = (5/16) × (−25/4) = −125/64, to **r = −5/4.** (Kyunki (−5/4)³ = −125/64.)
a = a₂ ÷ r = (−4/25) ÷ (−5/4) = (−4/25) × (−4/5) = **16/125.**
GP: 16/125, −4/25, 1/5, −1/4, 5/16.
**Jaanch:** 16/125 × (−5/4) = −4/25, × (−5/4) = 1/5, × (−5/4) = −1/4, × (−5/4) = 5/16 ✅.

## Part B: Jab power ka farak even ho: r ke do jawab

Ab m − k **even** ho, jaise 2 ya 4. Tab rᵐ⁻ᵏ ek even power hai, aur even power hamesha positive hoti hai (jaise (−2)² = 4 aur 2² = 4). Isliye:
- aₘ/aₖ **positive** hona chahiye. Agar negative ho to koi real r hai hi nahi.
- Positive ho to r **do** hote hain: **+** aur **−** wala. Dono GP bante hain.

### Example 4: a₂ = 50 aur a₄ = 8

m − k = 2. r² = 8/50 = 4/25, to **r = 2/5 ya r = −2/5.**
a = a₂ ÷ r: r = 2/5 ho to a = 50 × 5/2 = **125.** r = −2/5 ho to a = 50 ÷ (−2/5) = **−125.**
Do GP: **125, 50, 20, 8, ...** aur **−125, 50, −20, 8, ...**
**Jaanch:** pehla: 125 × 2/5 = 50, × 2/5 = 20, × 2/5 = 8 ✅. Doosra: −125 × (−2/5) = 50, × (−2/5) = −20, × (−2/5) = 8 ✅.

```
r = 2/5, a = 125
a1                          |######################### 125
a2                          |##########                50
a3                          |####                      20
a4                          |##                        8
r = -2/5, a = -125
a1 #########################|                          -125
a2                          |##########                50
a3                      ####|                          -20
a4                          |##                        8
```

> **Figure 3.2** – Dono GPs ke bars (1 hash = 5 ke aas-paas). Beech wali seedhi line shunya (0) hai: daayen bars positive terms, baayen bars negative terms. Dhyan do: a₂ aur a₄ dono GP mein ek jaise (50 aur 8) hain, jo hamare diye hue terms hain, par a₁ aur a₃ ke sign ulte hain.

Dekha? **Dono** GP diye hue a₂ = 50 aur a₄ = 8 ko poora karte hain. Do terms "ek hi doori ke even farak" par hon to ye do jawab milte hain, kyunki r ka sign a₂ aur a₄ ke beech do baar lagta hai aur kat jaata hai.

**Story check ka kaam:** agar kahani batati hai ki ye lambaiyan hain (jaise sheets ki width), to negative r chhod do. Agar kahani sirf "ek GP" kehti hai (koi sign ki shart nahi), to dono sahi hain.

**Jab koi GP milta hi nahi:** a₂ = 5 aur a₄ = −20 do to r² = −20/5 = −4. Koi real number apne aap se guna karke negative nahi ho sakta, to **aisa GP exist hi nahi karta.** Even power ke farak par aₘ aur aₖ hamesha ek hi sign ke hote hain.

## Part C: Ek term aur ek rishta

Kabhi do terms nahi milte, ek term (ya do) ke saath ek **rishta** diya hota hai. Tab bhi plan wahi: rishte se r nikalo, phir a.

### Example 5: a₄ ka a₁ se rishta

Ek GP mein a₄ = (64/27) × a₁ aur a₁ + a₂ = 63. Pehla term a, ratio r hai.
a₄ = a r³, to a r³ = (64/27) a. a ≠ 0, isliye a se divide karo: r³ = 64/27, **r = 4/3.**
Ab a₁ + a₂ = a + a r = a(1 + 4/3) = a × 7/3 = 63, to **a = 63 × 3/7 = 27.**
GP: **27, 36, 48, 64, ...**
**Jaanch:** 27 × 4/3 = 36, × 4/3 = 48, × 4/3 = 64 ✅. Rishta: a₄ = 64 aur (64/27) × 27 = 64 ✅. Sum: 27 + 36 = 63 ✅.

### Example 6: do jodon ka ratio

Ek GP mein a₂ + a₃ = 720 aur a₃ + a₄ = 576. Likho: a₂ + a₃ = a₂(1 + r) aur a₃ + a₄ = a₂ r (1 + r) (har term mein ek r aur aa gaya). Doosre ko pehle se divide karo:
(a₃ + a₄) ÷ (a₂ + a₃) = r, kyunki a₂(1 + r) dono mein common hai (aur 720 ≠ 0, to a₂(1 + r) ≠ 0).
**r = 576/720 = 4/5.**
a₂(1 + 4/5) = 720, to a₂ × 9/5 = 720, **a₂ = 400.** Phir a = a₂ ÷ r = 400 × 5/4 = **500.**
GP: **500, 400, 320, 256, ...**
**Jaanch:** 400 + 320 = 720 ✅ aur 320 + 256 = 576 ✅. Ratio: 400/500 = 4/5 aur 320/400 = 4/5 ✅.

## Part D: Har m-wa term bhi GP banata hai

GP a, ar, ar², ar³, ... se har doosra term uthao: a₁, a₃, a₅, ... Kya ye bhi GP hain?

**Proof.** Koi bhi term aₖ lo aur usse m kadam aage ka term aₖ₊ₘ. Part A ka divide wala tareeka (bade index k + m, chhota index k) lagao:
aₖ₊ₘ ÷ aₖ = r^((k + m) − k) = **rᵐ.**
Ye ratio k par nirbhar hi nahi karta, har jodi ke liye **wahi rᵐ** hai. Isliye aₖ, aₖ₊ₘ, aₖ₊₂ₘ, ... ek GP hai jiska ratio **rᵐ** hai. Khaas baat m = 2: a₁, a₃, a₅, ... GP hai, ratio **r².**

### Example 7: GP 243, −162, 108, ...

Yahan a = 243 aur r = −162/243 = −2/3. Pehle saat terms: 243, −162, 108, −72, 48, −32, 64/3.
- Har doosra (a₁, a₃, a₅, a₇): 243, 108, 48, 64/3. Ratios 108/243 = 4/9, 48/108 = 4/9, (64/3)/48 = 4/9. Aur r² = (−2/3)² = **4/9**, wahi ratio.
- Baaki doosre (a₂, a₄, a₆): −162, −72, −32. Ratios (−72)/(−162) = 4/9 aur (−32)/(−72) = 4/9. Yahan bhi r² = **4/9.**
- Har teesra (a₁, a₄, a₇): 243, −72, 64/3. Ratios (−72)/243 = −8/27 aur (64/3)/(−72) = −8/27, aur r³ = (−2/3)³ = **−8/27.**
**Jaanch:** 243 × 4/9 = 108, × 4/9 = 48, × 4/9 = 64/3 ✅, aur 243 × (−8/27) = −72, × (−8/27) = 64/3 ✅.

```
term       a1    a2    a3    a4    a5    a6    a7
value     243  -162   108   -72    48   -32  64/3
set A       ^           ^           ^           ^
set B             ^           ^           ^
set C       ^                 ^                 ^
```

> **Figure 3.3** – GP 243, −162, 108, ... ke saat terms, aur teen alag-alag chunav (^ se dikhaye): set A = a₁, a₃, a₅, a₇ (ratio 4/9), set B = a₂, a₄, a₆ (ratio 4/9) aur set C = a₁, a₄, a₇ (ratio −8/27). Dhyan do: r negative hai par r² positive, isliye har doosre term ka sign ek jaisa rehta hai (243, 108, 48, 64/3 sab positive; −162, −72, −32 sab negative).

## Part E: Teen numbers GP mein hain? Geometric mean wali shart

Teen numbers a, b, c GP mein hain to b/a = c/b (dono ratio r hain). Cross-multiply karo: **b² = ac.** Ulta bhi sach hai: agar a, b, c **non-zero** hain aur b² = ac, to b ko a se divide karo (b/a) aur c ko b se divide karo (c/b); b² = ac se b/a = c/b nikalta hai, yaani ratio barabar, to GP.

**Shart:** **b² = ac** ⇔ **a, b, c GP mein** (teeno non-zero ho). Zero se dhyan rakho: 0, 0, 5 mein b² = 0 aur ac = 0 × 5 = 0, to b² = ac ban jaata hai, par 0, 0, 5 GP nahi hai (ratio 0/0 ka matlab hi nahi). GP mein koi term zero hota hi nahi.

Isi shart se **geometric mean** ka naam aata hai: b ko a aur c ka **GM** kehte hain (Part F).

### Example 8: teen numbers ki jaanch

(a) 12, 30, 75: b² = 30² = 900, ac = 12 × 75 = 900 ✅ barabar, to GP hai. **Jaanch:** 30/12 = 5/2 aur 75/30 = 5/2 ✅, r = 5/2.
(b) 5, 12, 28: b² = 144, ac = 5 × 28 = 140. 144 ≠ 140, to **GP nahi.** **Jaanch:** 12/5 = 2.4 aur 28/12 = 2.33..., barabar nahi ✅.
(c) −2, 6, −18: b² = 36, ac = (−2)(−18) = 36 ✅, to GP hai. **Jaanch:** 6/(−2) = −3 aur (−18)/6 = −3 ✅, r = −3.

### Example 9: x nikalo (linear equation banti hai)

x + 5, 2x + 6, 4x + 5 GP mein hain. Shart b² = ac:
(2x + 6)² = (x + 5)(4x + 5)
4x² + 24x + 36 = 4x² + 25x + 25. Dono taraf 4x² hai, wo kat gaya:
24x + 36 = 25x + 25, to **x = 11.**
Terms: 11 + 5 = 16, 22 + 6 = 28, 44 + 5 = 49. **Valid-value check:** koi term zero nahi, to GP: **16, 28, 49**, ratio 7/4.
**Jaanch:** 16 × 7/4 = 28, × 7/4 = 49 ✅.

### Example 10: x nikalo (quadratic banti hai, do jawab)

x + 2, x + 7, 2x − 10 GP mein hain. b² = ac:
(x + 7)² = (x + 2)(2x − 10)
x² + 14x + 49 = 2x² − 6x − 20
0 = x² − 20x − 69. Quadratic formula (Chapter 3): D = 400 + 276 = 676 = 26², to x = (20 ± 26)/2, **x = 23 ya x = −3.**
**Valid-value check:** har x par teeno terms dekho, aur dhyan rakho ki koi term zero ho gaya to wo x reject (GP mein zero nahi hota).
x = 23: 25, 30, 36. x = −3: −1, 4, −16. Dono mein koi term zero nahi, to dono GP hain.
**Jaanch:** x = 23: 25 × 6/5 = 30, × 6/5 = 36 ✅. x = −3: −1 × (−4) = 4, 4 × (−4) = −16 ✅ (ratio −4).
**Story check:** agar sawaal kehta "x ek natural number hai", to −3 reject, sirf x = 23. Bina shart ke dono sahi.

## Part F: GM daalna, aur AM aur GM ka comparison

**GM daalna.** Do **positive** numbers a aur c ke beech ek number b aisa rakho ki a, b, c GP ho. Part E ki shart: b² = ac, to **b = √(ac).** Chunki b = −√(ac) bhi b² = ac ko poora karta hai, ek aur GP (a, −√(ac), c) bhi banta hai; par do positive numbers ka "GM" matlab positive √(ac) hota hai.

### Example 11: 4 aur 196 ka GM

ac = 4 × 196 = 784, √784 = 28 (28² = 784), to **GM = 28.** GP: 4, 28, 196, ratio 7.
**Jaanch:** 28/4 = 7 aur 196/28 = 7 ✅. (Doosra GP 4, −28, 196, ratio −7, bhi chalta hai, par GM positive 28 hai.)

### Example 12: do GM daalna

16 aur 250 ke beech do GM daalo (poori list 4 terms ki hogi). a = 16, a₄ = 250. Part A ke tareeke se: r³ = 250/16 = 125/8, to **r = 5/2.**
Beech ke terms: 16 × 5/2 = **40** aur 40 × 5/2 = **100.** List: 16, 40, 100, 250.
**Jaanch:** 100 × 5/2 = 250 ✅ (aakhri term diye hue 250 se milta hai).

```
16 --x 5/2--> 40 --x 5/2--> 100 --x 5/2--> 250
```

> **Figure 3.4** – 16 se 250 tak teen kadam, har kadam par 5/2 se guna. Beech ke do number 40 aur 100 do GM hain. Teen kadam isliye ki 4 terms ke beech teen jump hote hain: r³ = 250/16.

**AM aur GM.** Chapter 4 mein AM = (a + c)/2 dekha tha. Ab dono ko saath rakhte hain:

| a | c | AM = (a + c)/2 | GM = √(ac) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 49 | 25 | 7 |
| 6 | 24 | 15 | 12 |
| 8 | 50 | 29 | 20 |
| 17 | 17 | 17 | 17 |

Teen number-jodiyon mein AM, GM se **bada** hai. Chauthi jodi mein dono number barabar hain, to dono mean bhi barabar hain. **Observation:** do positive numbers ka AM kabhi GM se chhota nahi hota, aur barabar tabhi jab dono number barabar ho. (Ye abhi sirf dekhi hui baat hai, iska proof aage ki class mein aata hai.)

```
 1     7                 25                      49
-+-----+-----------------+-----------------------+--
 a     GM                AM                      c
```

> **Figure 3.5** – 1 aur 49 ke beech GM = 7 aur AM = 25 number line par (1 dash = 1 unit). AM bilkul beech mein hai (1 aur 49 se 24-24 door), jabki GM chhote number ke paas khisak gaya hai, kyunki GP mein 1, 7, 49 ko ek hi factor 7 se guna karke jaate hain, jodkar nahi.

## Part G: Teen numbers a/r, a, ar

Jab sawaal **teen numbers GP mein, sum aur product** ke hon, to unhe **a/r, a, ar** likho. Faayda: teeno ka product (a/r) × a × (ar) = **a³**, r kat jaata hai, to a seedha mil jaata hai. Phir sum se r.

(Chaar numbers ke liye isi soch se a/r³, a/r, ar, ar³ likhte hain, jinka ratio r² hota hai.)

### Example 13: product 3375, sum 49

a³ = 3375, to a = 15 (15³ = 3375: 15 × 15 = 225, 225 × 15 = 3375). Teen numbers: 15/r, 15, 15r. Sum 49:
15/r + 15 + 15r = 49. Dono taraf r se guna karo: 15 + 15r + 15r² = 49r, to 15r² − 34r + 15 = 0.
D = 34² − 4 × 15 × 15 = 1156 − 900 = 256 = 16². r = (34 ± 16)/30, to **r = 50/30 = 5/3 ya r = 18/30 = 3/5.**
r = 5/3: 15/r = 9, to **9, 15, 25.** r = 3/5: 15/r = 25, to **25, 15, 9.**
Dono r ek hi set dete hain, bas ulte kram mein: 5/3 aur 3/5 ek doosre ke **reciprocal** hain, aur GP ko ulta padhne par ratio reciprocal ho jaata hai. Isliye answer **9, 15, 25** (ya ulta 25, 15, 9) ek hi hai.
**Jaanch:** sum 9 + 15 + 25 = 49 ✅. Product 9 × 15 = 135, 135 × 25 = 3375 ✅. GP: 15/9 = 5/3 aur 25/15 = 5/3 ✅.

## Part H: AP aur GP ek saath

### Example 14: AP mein teen numbers, kuch jodne par GP

Teen numbers AP mein hain aur unka sum 48 hai. Unme kramshah 4, 8 aur 14 jodne par naye teen numbers GP mein aate hain. Numbers kaun se?
AP ko m − h, m, m + h likho (Chapter 4 wala tareeka; yahan m AP ka beech wala number hai, Part A ke position wale m se alag): sum 3m = 48, to m = 16. Numbers: 16 − h, 16, 16 + h.
Jodne ke baad: (16 − h + 4), (16 + 8), (16 + h + 14) = **20 − h, 24, 30 + h.** GP hain, to b² = ac:
24² = (20 − h)(30 + h)
576 = 600 + 20h − 30h − h² = 600 − 10h − h²
h² + 10h − 24 = 0, (h + 12)(h − 2) = 0, to **h = 2 ya h = −12.**
h = 2: AP 14, 16, 18 aur naye numbers 18, 24, 32. h = −12: AP 28, 16, 4 aur naye numbers 32, 24, 18.
**Jaanch:** 18 × 4/3 = 24, × 4/3 = 32 ✅, aur 32 × 3/4 = 24, × 3/4 = 18 ✅. Dono AP ka sum: 14 + 16 + 18 = 48 ✅ aur 28 + 16 + 4 = 48 ✅.
**Story check:** dono jawab poore, kuch shart nahi toh dono sahi: **14, 16, 18** ya **28, 16, 4.**

**Ek sundar baat:** agar teen non-zero numbers a, b, c **AP bhi** hain **aur GP bhi**, to wo teeno **barabar** hote hain. Kyun? AP: 2b = a + c. GP: b² = ac. To (a − c)² = (a + c)² − 4ac = (2b)² − 4b² = 0. Isliye a − c = 0, yaani a = c, aur 2b = 2a, b = a. (Jaise 7, 7, 7: d = 0 aur r = 1.)

## Aam galtiyan (bachke rehna)

**Galti 1: Teen numbers ko a, ar, ar³ maan lena (power 2 chhod dena)**

GP ke terms ki powers 0, 1, 2, 3, ... **ek-ek kadam** se badhti hain: a, ar, ar², ar³. Ek bachche ne GP 54, 36, 24 ko a, ar, ar³ likha. Pehle do terms se a = 54, ar = 36, to r = 36/54 = 2/3. Ab teesra term wo likhta hai ar³ = 54 × (2/3)³ = 54 × 8/27 = **16**, jabki GP ka asli teesra term **24** hai.
**Ulta udaharan:** GP 54, 36, 24 mein 36/54 = 2/3 aur 24/36 = 2/3, to r = 2/3 theek hai. Teesra term ar² = 54 × 4/9 = **24**, bilkul sahi. Galat form ne term ko ek kadam aage (4-wa term jaisa) kheench diya.
Kyun? Teesra term pehle term se **do** kadam aage hai, to r² chahiye. ar³ teen kadam aage ka hai (a₄). Isi liye a/r, a, ar likhte hain (powers −1, 0, 1: ek-ek kadam) ya a, ar, ar² (0, 1, 2).

**Galti 2: b² = ac se GM nikaalna jab ac negative ho**

Ek bachcha 4 aur −9 ke beech GM daalta hai: "ac = 4 × (−9) = −36, to GM = 6" (sign chhod diya). List banti hai: 4, 6, −9. **Ulta udaharan:** ratios 6/4 = 3/2 aur −9/6 = −3/2, barabar nahi, to **ye GP nahi hai.** Sahi baat: b² = ac = −36 ka matlab hai b² = −36, aur koi real number apne aap se guna karke negative nahi ho sakta, to **4 aur −9 ke beech GM exist hi nahi karta.**
Kyun? **b² = ac ke liye ac > 0 chahiye**, yaani a aur c ek hi sign ke hon. Ye wahi baat hai jo Part B mein dikhi thi: GP mein ek term chhodkar aage ka term (do kadam door) hamesha **ek hi sign** ka hota hai, kyunki ratio r² positive hai.

## Quick Recap

- Do terms aₘ aur aₖ (m > k) se: **rᵐ⁻ᵏ = aₘ/aₖ**, phir **a = aₖ ÷ rᵏ⁻¹.** Kyun: k-wa se m-wa term tak (m − k) baar r se guna hota hai.
- (m − k) **odd** ho to ek hi real r, jiska sign aₘ/aₖ jaisa hota hai. (m − k) **even** ho to aₘ/aₖ positive chahiye aur **r = ±** root: do GP, ya kahani ke hisaab se ek. Negative ratio par koi GP nahi.
- Ek term aur ek rishta ho to rishte se r ya a nikalo; jodon (a₂ + a₃) aur (a₃ + a₄) ka ratio seedha r deta hai.
- Har m-wa term wali list (aₖ, aₖ₊ₘ, aₖ₊₂ₘ, ...) GP hai, ratio **rᵐ.** Har doosra term: ratio r².
- Non-zero a, b, c GP mein ⇔ **b² = ac.** x dhundhne mein linear ya quadratic banti hai: har jawab par terms check karo (koi term zero na ho).
- Do positive numbers ka **GM = √(ac)**; GM daalte waqt r ki power = (terms ki ginti − 1). Observation: AM ≥ GM, barabar sirf barabar numbers par.
- Teen numbers GP mein, sum aur product diya ho: **a/r, a, ar**, product a³. r aur 1/r wahi set ulte kram mein dete hain.
- AP aur GP ek saath: m − h, m, m + h likho, jodne ke baad b² = ac. Jo teen numbers AP aur GP dono hon, wo barabar hote hain.
- **Galti 1:** a, ar, ar³ nahi: teesra term ar² hai. **Galti 2:** b² = ac mein ac > 0 chahiye, warna GM nahi.

## Practice Questions

1. In a GP the 2nd term is 1080 and the 5th term is 625. Find the common ratio and the first term, and write the first five terms.

2. In a mosaic, each square frame has a side that is a fixed fraction of the side of the previous (bigger) frame. The 2nd frame has side 567 mm and the 4th frame has side 343 mm. Find the common ratio (do a story check), the side of the first frame, and the side of the 3rd frame.

3. In a GP the 2nd term is −540 and the 5th term is 2500. Find the first term and the common ratio.

4. In a GP, the 4th term is 343/27 times the 1st term, and the sum of the 1st and 2nd terms is 150. Find the first three terms.

5. In a GP, the sum of the 2nd and 3rd terms is 1224 and the sum of the 3rd and 4th terms is 1377. Find the first three terms.

6. For the GP 3125, −2500, 2000, ...: (a) write the 1st, 3rd, 5th and 7th terms and show that they form a GP; what is its ratio? (b) What is the ratio of the GP formed by the 2nd, 5th and 8th terms? (c) Explain in a line why each ratio is a power of the original r.

7. Find the geometric mean of 13 and 468 and write the three-term GP. Write also the second possible GP. Compare the GM with the AM of 13 and 468.

8. The expressions x + 4, 3x + 6, 9x + 2 are three consecutive terms of a GP. Find x and the terms.

9. The expressions x + 4, x + 1, 2x − 6 are three consecutive terms of a GP. Find all possible values of x and the corresponding GPs. If x must be a natural number, which value remains?

10. Three numbers are in GP, their sum is 185 and their product is 216000. Find the numbers.

11. Three numbers are in AP and their sum is 66. When 7, 8 and 14 are added to them respectively, the new numbers are in GP. Find the original numbers.

12. State True or False, with a reason: (a) If b² = ac for three numbers a, b, c, they are always in GP. (b) The 2nd, 4th, 6th and 8th terms of a GP are in GP with ratio r². (c) For two positive numbers, the GM is never larger than the AM. (d) A GP can have its 6th term divided by its 2nd term equal to −16. (e) If the 7th term divided by the 3rd term of a GP is 16/81, then r must be 2/3. (f) A geometric mean can be inserted between 5 and −20.

### Answers

1. m − k = 3. r³ = 625/1080 = 125/216, to **r = 5/6.** a = 1080 ÷ (5/6) = 1080 × 6/5 = **1296.** Terms: **1296, 1080, 900, 750, 625.** Jaanch: 1296 × 5/6 = 1080, × 5/6 = 900, × 5/6 = 750, × 5/6 = 625 ✅.

2. m − k = 2. r² = 343/567 = 49/81 (dono ko 7 se divide karo: 49/81), to r = 7/9 ya −7/9. Pehla frame: a₂ ÷ r = 567 × 9/7 = 729 (ya −729 agar r negative). **Story check:** frame ki side negative nahi ho sakti, to r = −7/9 reject. **r = 7/9**, pehle frame ki side **729 mm**, aur 3rd frame ki side 567 × 7/9 = **441 mm.** Jaanch: 729 × 7/9 = 567, × 7/9 = 441, × 7/9 = 343 ✅.

3. m − k = 3. r³ = 2500/(−540) = −125/27, to **r = −5/3** (odd power, sign wahi). a = −540 ÷ (−5/3) = 540 × 3/5 = **324.** GP: 324, −540, 900, −1500, 2500. Jaanch: 324 × (−5/3) = −540, × (−5/3) = 900, × (−5/3) = −1500, × (−5/3) = 2500 ✅.

4. a r³ = (343/27) a, a ≠ 0, to r³ = 343/27, **r = 7/3.** a(1 + 7/3) = 150, a × 10/3 = 150, **a = 45.** Terms: **45, 105, 245.** Jaanch: 45 + 105 = 150 ✅. Rishta: a₄ = 245 × 7/3 = 1715/3 aur (343/27) × 45 = 15435/27 = 1715/3 ✅.

5. a₂(1 + r) = 1224 aur a₂ r (1 + r) = 1377, to r = 1377/1224 = **9/8** (dono ko 153 se divide karo: 9/8). a₂ × 17/8 = 1224, to a₂ = 1224 × 8/17 = 576, aur a = 576 ÷ 9/8 = **512.** Terms: **512, 576, 648.** Jaanch: 576 + 648 = 1224 ✅. a₄ = 648 × 9/8 = 729 aur 648 + 729 = 1377 ✅.

6. r = −2500/3125 = −4/5. (a) a₁, a₃, a₅, a₇ = 3125, 2000, 1280, 4096/5 (= 819.2). Ratios 2000/3125 = 16/25, 1280/2000 = 16/25, (4096/5)/1280 = 16/25. GP hai, ratio **16/25.** (b) a₂, a₅, a₈ = −2500, 1280, −16384/25 (= −655.36). Ratios 1280/(−2500) = −64/125 aur (−16384/25)/1280 = −64/125. Ratio **−64/125.** (c) a₁ se a₃ tak 2 kadam hain, to r² = (−4/5)² = 16/25; a₂ se a₅ tak 3 kadam, to r³ = (−4/5)³ = −64/125 (Part D). Jaanch: 3125 × 16/25 = 2000 ✅ aur (−4/5)³ = −64/125 ✅.

7. ac = 13 × 468 = 6084 = 78², to **GM = 78.** GP: **13, 78, 468** (r = 6). Doosra GP: **13, −78, 468** (r = −6). AM = (13 + 468)/2 = 240.5, jo GM 78 se bada hai (Part F ki observation ke saath mel). Jaanch: 78/13 = 6, 468/78 = 6 ✅.

8. b² = ac: (3x + 6)² = (x + 4)(9x + 2). 9x² + 36x + 36 = 9x² + 38x + 8. 9x² kat gaya: 36x + 36 = 38x + 8, to 28 = 2x, **x = 14.** Terms: 14 + 4 = 18, 42 + 6 = 48, 126 + 2 = 128: **18, 48, 128**, ratio 8/3. Jaanch: 18 × 8/3 = 48, 48 × 8/3 = 128 ✅.

9. b² = ac: (x + 1)² = (x + 4)(2x − 6). x² + 2x + 1 = 2x² + 2x − 24, to x² = 25, **x = 5 ya x = −5.** x = 5: 9, 6, 4 (r = 2/3). x = −5: −1, −4, −16 (r = 4). Dono mein koi term zero nahi. Natural number ki shart ho to **x = 5** (story check). Jaanch: 9 × 2/3 = 6, × 2/3 = 4 ✅; −1 × 4 = −4, × 4 = −16 ✅.

10. a³ = 216000, to a = 60 (60³ = 216000). 60/r + 60 + 60r = 185, r se guna: 60 + 60r + 60r² = 185r, to 60r² − 125r + 60 = 0, 5 se divide: 12r² − 25r + 12 = 0. D = 625 − 576 = 49, r = (25 ± 7)/24, **r = 4/3 ya 3/4.** Numbers: **45, 60, 80** (ya ulta 80, 60, 45). Jaanch: 45 + 60 + 80 = 185 ✅. 45 × 60 = 2700, 2700 × 80 = 216000 ✅. 60/45 = 4/3 = 80/60 ✅.

11. AP: m = 66/3 = 22, numbers 22 − h, 22, 22 + h. Jodne ke baad: 29 − h, 30, 36 + h. GP: 900 = (29 − h)(36 + h) = 1044 − 7h − h², to h² + 7h − 144 = 0. D = 49 + 576 = 625 = 25², h = (−7 ± 25)/2, **h = 9 ya h = −16.** h = 9: AP **13, 22, 31**, naye numbers 20, 30, 45. h = −16: AP **38, 22, 6**, naye numbers 45, 30, 20. Jaanch: 20 × 3/2 = 30, × 3/2 = 45 ✅; sum 13 + 22 + 31 = 66 ✅ aur 38 + 22 + 6 = 66 ✅.

12. (a) **False** ❌. 0, 0, 9 mein b² = 0 = ac, par ye GP nahi (ratio 0/0 ka matlab nahi); shart non-zero numbers ki hai. (b) **True** ✅. Har doosra term, har m-wa term jaisa, ratio r² (Part D). (c) **True** ✅. Part F ki observation: AM ≥ GM, barabar tabhi jab numbers barabar. (d) **False** ❌. Yahan power ka farak 4 hai, to r⁴ = −16, aur even power negative nahi hoti (Part B). (e) **False** ❌. Power ka farak 4, to r⁴ = 16/81, aur r = 2/3 ya −2/3 dono chalte hain (Part B). (f) **False** ❌. ac = 5 × (−20) = −100 < 0, to b² = −100 impossible (Galti 2).
