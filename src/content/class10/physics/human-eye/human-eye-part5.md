# Topic 5: Telescope Aur Camera: Door Ki Cheez Paas

Topic 4 mein humne **chhoti** cheez ko badi dikhaya: loupe, aur objective-eyepiece wala microscope. Wahan objective cheez ke bilkul paas rakha tha. Ab ulta sawaal: **cheez bahut door ho, Chaand ho ya taara**, to? Wahan objective ko cheez ke paas le jaana mumkin hi nahi. Tab bhi do lens ka wahi jod kaam aata hai, bas lens ki ginti aur f ka hisaab alag hota hai. Is topic mein **telescope**, uska **aaina wala (reflecting) roop**, aur phir **camera**.

---

## Terrace par chaand aur ek chhota telescope

Garmiyon ki shaam thi. Mr. Bendre ne almaari se ek lamba peetal ka tube nikala aur terrace par tripod par jama diya: ek chhota **refracting telescope**, jiska aage ka lens lagbhag 80 mm chauda tha. Unki bhanji Maitreyi bahut utsuk thi. "Mama, kya ye sach mein chaand ko paas laa dega?"

Mr. Bendre ne tube ko ashtami ke aadhe chaand ki taraf modkar focus kiya (Suraj ki taraf **kabhi nahi**, ye unki pehli shart thi). Maitreyi ne aankh lagayi aur uchhal padi: "Mama! Gadde! Saaf gol gadde, aur kinaron par parchhaiyan!" Phir usne thodi der baad bhaunhein sikodi: "Par Mama, teen baatein ajeeb hain. **Pehli:** chaand ulta kyun dikh raha hai? **Doosri:** is tube mein do lens hain, aage ka lens bada, tube bahut lamba, aur peeche ka lens bahut chhota. Aisa kyun? **Teesri:** chaand to bada hua, par wo taara dekho, wo bada hua hi nahi, wahi chamakti bindi hai. Phir bhi aasmaan mein itne taare dikh rahe hain jo aankh se dikhte hi nahi the. Telescope ne asal mein kiya kya?"

Mr. Bendre muskuraye. "Teeno sawal ek hi jawab se jude hain. Pehle dekhte hain ki do lens kaise milte hain, phir 'Wapas terrace par' mein hum teeno sawal solve karenge."

## Class 9 ka recall

Class 9 mein convex lens ke liye cheez **bahut door (infinity par)** ho to image **F₂ par, real, ulti aur point jaisi chhoti** banti hai (Position 1), aur cheez **F₁ par** ho to image **infinity par** jaati hai (Position 5), dono [light-refraction-part6.md](../../../class9/physics/light-refraction/light-refraction-part6.md) mein. Wahin likha tha ki microscope aur telescope "aage ki class mein" aayenge: wo aaj hai. Topic 4 mein hum ye bhi dekh chuke hain ki eyepiece asal mein ek magnifying glass hai, aur cheez ko F par rakhne par M = D ÷ f aata hai. Class 9 ([light-reflection-part6.md](../../../class9/physics/light-reflection/light-reflection-part6.md)) mein concave mirror ke liye bhi parallel rays F par milti hain.

**Sign convention (Class 9 wala, hum yahi lenge):** light left se right ko jaati hai. Real object ke liye u negative. Convex lens ka f positive. Real image (doosri taraf) ka v positive, virtual image (object ki hi taraf) ka v negative.

## Part A: Telescope kaam kaise karta hai

### Do lens ki zimmedari

Taare ya Chaand se aati roshni itni door se aati hai ki uski kirnein hamare tube tak **lagbhag parallel** pahunchti hain. Chaand ke har kinare se aati kirnein ek parallel bundle ki tarah aati hain. Do kinaron ke bundle ek-doosre se thode jhuke hote hain, aur unke beech ka kon **θ** hai.

**Stage 1: objective.** Lamba f_o wala convex lens. Cheez infinity par hai, to Class 9 ki Position 1 ke hisaab se image **F par, real aur ulti**. Lens ke bilkul beech se jaane wali kiran seedhi nikalti hai, to F par image ki unchai **h' = f_o × θ** hoti hai (chhote kon ke liye). Ye **intermediate image I** hai.

**Stage 2: eyepiece.** Chhota f_e wala convex lens, **magnifying glass ki tarah**, I ko dekhta hai. Agar I ko eyepiece ke **F par** rakha jaaye (Position 5), to final image **infinity par** jaati hai: kirnein parallel nikalti hain aur aankh relaxed rehti hai. Ab kirnein ek **bade kon θ'** par nikalti hain: θ' = h' ÷ f_e.

Dono ko jodo: θ' ÷ θ = (f_o × θ ÷ f_e) ÷ θ. To

> **M = f_o ÷ f_e**   (normal adjustment: final image infinity par)

Is setting mein I objective ka F₂ bhi hai aur eyepiece ka F₁ bhi: dono lens ke focus ek hi jagah milte hain (**common focus**). Isliye dono lens ke beech ki doori

> **tube ki lambai L = f_o + f_e**

> **Figure 5.1** – Refracting telescope, normal adjustment (schematic: f_o = 16 column, f_e = 4 column, to M = 4). Chaand ke ek kinare se aati teen parallel kirnen objective par aati hain; har ek 4 column chalne par 1 row neeche jaati hai (slope ¼). I eyepiece ke F par hai, isliye eyepiece se teeno kirnen **parallel** niklti hain, par ab har ek 1 column chalne par 1 row **upar** jaati hai (slope 1): kon 4 guna, aur disha **ulti**. Final image infinity par hai, aankh relaxed.

**Ghar par adult ke saath (apne telescope jaisa lens-jod):** do convex lens lo, ek lamba f wala (lagbhag 50 cm ke aas-paas, kisi purane chashme ya magnifier ka jod chalega) aur ek chhote f ka magnifier. Khidki se door ki imarat ya ped dekhne ke liye bada lens aankh se door aur chhota aankh ke paas rakhkar dono ke beech ki doori aage-peeche karo. Ek jagah par door ki imarat **ulti aur badi** saaf dikhegi. (**Suraj ki taraf kabhi nahi dekhna**, aur Suraj ki roshni lens se kisi cheez par focus nahi karna.)

### Example 1: Maitreyi ka telescope

Maitreyi ke telescope ka objective f_o = 70 cm, eyepiece f_e = 7 cm. Normal adjustment mein M, tube ki lambai aur I ki jagah nikalo.

Sign ke saath: objective: f_o = +70 cm, cheez infinity par (u = −∞).
1/v = 1/f_o + 1/u = 1/(70 cm) + 0, to v = **+70 cm** (real, objective ke peeche 70 cm par, ulti).
Eyepiece: f_e = +7 cm. I ko eyepiece ke F par rakhna hai, to u = −7 cm. 1/v = 1/(7 cm) − 1/(7 cm) = 0, to v = ∞.
M = f_o ÷ f_e = 70 cm ÷ 7 cm = **10**.
Lambai L = f_o + f_e = 70 cm + 7 cm = **77 cm**.

Jaanch: I objective se 70 cm par aur eyepiece se 7 cm par hai, 70 + 7 = 77 cm ✅. Image ulti (objective ki real image) ✅. Chaand ke kinaron ke beech ka kon ab 10 guna.

### Example 2: Eyepiece badal diya

Mr. Bendre ne eyepiece badalkar f_e = 3.5 cm wala lagaya. Wahi objective. M aur L?

M = 70 ÷ 3.5 = **20**. L = 70 + 3.5 = **73.5 cm**.
Jaanch: eyepiece ki f aadhi hui to M doguna (10 se 20) ✅, aur tube thoda chhota (77 se 73.5 cm) ✅.

**To phir f_e aur chhota karke M badhate kyun nahi jaate?** Bahut chhoti f ka eyepiece ek chhota, bahut curved lens hota hai (Topic 4: f jitni chhoti, lens utna chhota aur curved), aur M zyada hone par wahi roshni bade kon mein faili hui, to image **halki aur dhundhli** lagti hai. Isliye M badhane ka doosra tareeka hai **f_o lamba** karna: yahi wajah hai ki telescope ka objective lamba f ka hota hai.

### Example 3: Image D par chahiye to (focusing)

Wahi Example 1 wala telescope (f_o = 70 cm, f_e = 7 cm). Maitreyi ko aaram hai ki final image infinity par ho. Agar koi final image aankh ke D = 25 cm par chahe to eyepiece kitna khisakana padega?

Eyepiece ke liye v = −25 cm (virtual, I ki taraf), f_e = +7 cm.
1/u = 1/v − 1/f = 1/(−25 cm) − 1/(7 cm) = −0.04 − 0.1429 = −0.1829 cm⁻¹, to **u = −5.47 cm**.
I ab eyepiece se 5.47 cm par chahiye, pehle 7 cm par thi. Eyepiece ko I ki taraf (objective ki taraf) **7 − 5.47 = 1.53 cm** khisakana hoga. Nayi lambai = 70 + 5.47 = **75.47 cm**.

Jaanch: 1/v − 1/u = −0.04 + 0.1829 = 0.1429 cm⁻¹ = 1/(7 cm) ✅. Image virtual (v negative) aur D par ✅. Isi khisakne ko hum telescope ka **focusing knob** kehte hain: bahut chhoti doori, phir bhi image saaf ya dhundhli.

## Part B: Bada objective kyun? Roshni ka hisaab

Part A ne bataya ki M kya karta hai: **kon badhana**. Par Maitreyi ka dusra sawaal bacha tha: taare bade nahi hue, to zyada taare kaise dikhne lage?

Taare itne door hain ki unka kon itna chhota hota hai ki M kitna bhi ho, wo **bindi hi rehte hain**. Par unki roshni bahut kamzor hoti hai. Telescope ka objective ek bada gol chhed hai, jo aankh ke pupil (Topic 1: 2 se 8 mm) se kai guna zyada roshni **ikattha** karke aankh mein bhej deta hai. Roshni ikatthi karna kitna hoga? Gol chhed ke liye roshni uske **area** ke anupaat mein hoti hai, aur area (diameter) ke varg ke anupaat mein:

> **roshni ka anupaat = (objective ka diameter ÷ pupil ka diameter) ka varg**

### Example 4: 80 mm ka objective

Maitreyi ka objective 80 mm ka hai. Andhere mein uski aankh ka pupil lagbhag 8 mm tak khulta hai. Telescope aankh se kitni guna zyada roshni ikatthi kar sakta hai?

Diameter ka anupaat = 80 mm ÷ 8 mm = 10. Roshni ka anupaat = 10 × 10 = **100**. Yaani aankh ke akele pupil se lagbhag **100 guna** roshni (sheeshe mein thodi roshni kho jaati hai, isliye asal mein kuch kam).
Jaanch: area ∝ d², (80)² ÷ (8)² = 6400 ÷ 64 = 100 ✅.

Isi liye astronomers ko **bada objective** chahiye: dhundhle taare, galaxy aur door ke planets tabhi dikhte hain. M kam bhi ho to chalega, roshni zyada honi chahiye. Aur isi liye bade telescope bahut jyada kimat ke hote hain: bada, saaf, shuddh kaanch ya mirror banana kathin hai.

## Part C: Ulti image aur seedha dekhne waale telescope

**Astronomer ulti image se pareshan kyun nahi?** Antariksh mein "upar" aur "neeche" hota hi nahi. Chaand ke gadde ulte dikhein ya seedhe, kisi ko fark nahi padta. Isliye astronomical telescope mein final image ulti hi chhodte hain: ek lens bachta hai, roshni ka nuksaan bhi kam hota hai.

**Zameen ki cheezein dekhni hon** (pahaad par ek mandir, ya ek panchhi) to ulti image kaam ki nahi. Tab **terrestrial telescope** banta hai: ya to ek teesra lens lagakar image ko dobara ulta (yaani seedha) kiya jaata hai, ya **prism** se. **Binoculars** ek hi ghar mein do chhote telescope hain, har aankh ke liye ek. Unke andar total reflecting prism (Class 9, [light-refraction-part4.md](../../../class9/physics/light-refraction/light-refraction-part4.md)) light ko modkar image seedhi karte hain, aur tube ko chhota bhi karte hain. Binoculars par likha "8 × 40" ka matlab: **M = 8**, objective ka diameter **40 mm**. Part B ke hisaab se uski roshni pupil (8 mm) se (40 ÷ 8) ka varg = **25 guna**.

**Galileo ne** lagbhag 1609 mein pehli baar telescope ko aasmaan ki taraf kiya aur Chaand ke gadde aur Jupiter ke chaar bade chaand dekhe. Tab se astronomy badal gayi.

## Part D: Reflecting telescope: lens ki jagah mirror

Bade telescope mein objective ka lens banana kathin hota hai: lens ko kaanch ke poore mote hisse mein ek-sa shuddh rakhna padta hai, aur use sirf **kinaare se** pakda ja sakta hai, to bade lens apne vazan se jhukne lagte hain. Isliye bade telescope mein objective ki jagah ek bada **concave mirror** lagta hai (Class 9, [light-reflection-part6.md](../../../class9/physics/light-reflection/light-reflection-part6.md)): taare se aati parallel kirnein mirror se laut kar **F par** milti hain, aur wahan I banti hai.

Wahi F par taare ki image hai; wahan eyepiece ya sensor rakhte hain. Mirror ki sateh yahan sirf aakar dikhane ke liye bahut kam kheenchi gayi hai.

Reflecting telescope ke teen fayde: (1) mirror ko **peeche se** bhi sahara de sakte hain, isliye bahut bada ban sakta hai, (2) uski ek hi sateh shuddh banani padti hai (lens mein do surfaces aur poora kaanch), (3) mirror mein sab rang ek hi jagah milte hain, jabki lens mein alag rang thodi alag jagah par milte hain (lens ki ek kami, aage ki class mein; isko **colour error** kehte hain). Ek chhota **second mirror** kirnein ek taraf mod deta hai, kyunki F par dekhne wale ka sir aate hue light rok dega. Eyepiece ya camera wahi lagta hai, aur M phir bhi **f_o ÷ f_e** (f_o ab mirror ki f).

## Part E: Camera: door ki cheez ki chhoti image

Camera bhi door ki cheez ki **real image** banata hai, par yahan hum image ko bada nahi karte, balki **sensor par saaf utaarte** hain. Topic 1 mein camera aur aankh ki tulna ho chuki hai (aperture, shutter, sensor ke saath), to hum yahan sirf lens ke hisaab par jaate hain.

Camera ka lens ek convex lens (ya lens ka set) hai. Door ki cheez Class 9 ki **Position 2** mein hoti hai (2F₁ se bahut door), to image **F₂ aur 2F₂ ke beech, real, ulti aur chhoti** banti hai, aur sensor wahan rakha jaata hai. Figure 5.3 mein ye saaf hai.

**Aperture** (kitna chhed) tay karta hai ki kitni roshni jaaye: bada chhed, zyada roshni, jaise aankh ka pupil (Part B ka area-wala hisaab yahan bhi). **Shutter** tay karta hai ki kitni der. Camera ko saaf karne ke liye cheez ki doori ke hisaab se lens ko **thoda sa** aage-peeche karte hain (Topic 1: aankh mein lens aakar badalta hai, camera mein khisakta hai).

### Example 5: Camera ka focus

Ek camera ke lens ki f = 40 mm hai. Ek 1.5 m lambi cheez lens se 2.04 m par hai. Sensor lens se kitni doori par hona chahiye, image kitni badi hogi, aur lens ko "door" wali setting se kitna khisakana padega?

Sign ke saath: f = +40 mm, u = −2.04 m = −2040 mm.
1/v = 1/f + 1/u = 1/(40 mm) − 1/(2040 mm) = (51 − 1) ÷ 2040 mm⁻¹ = 50/2040 mm⁻¹, to **v = +40.8 mm** (real, doosri taraf).
m = v ÷ u = (+40.8 mm) ÷ (−2040 mm) = **−0.02** (ulti, 50 guna chhoti).
Image ki unchai = 1.5 m × 0.02 = 0.03 m = **30 mm**.
Bahut door ki cheez ke liye (infinity) v = f = 40 mm. To lens ko sirf 40.8 − 40 = **0.8 mm** aage khisakana padega.

Jaanch: 1/v − 1/u = 1/(40.8) + 1/(2040) = 0.024510 + 0.000490 = 0.025 mm⁻¹ = 1/(40 mm) ✅. v positive, m negative: real, ulti ✅. Image F₂ (40 mm) aur 2F₂ (80 mm) ke beech (40.8 mm) ✅. Ek millimetre se bhi kam khisakna: camera ka focusing itna sookshm hai.

**Phone ka camera** bahut chhota lens rakhta hai, aur zyadatar ek hi tay f ka. Uska "zoom" aksar **digital** hota hai: image ka beech wala hissa kaatkar bada dikhaya jaata hai, isse naya detail nahi aata. (Kuch phone mein alag lambi f ka doosra lens bhi hota hai, jo asli optical zoom jaisa kaam karta hai.) Telescope aur camera ek-doosre se milte hain: telescope ke objective ki real image I par agar **sensor** rakh do, to wahi astronomer ka camera hai.

**Projector** Position 4 mein kaam karta hai (slide F₁ aur 2F₁ ke beech): camera ka ulta, kyunki cheez paas aur image door, badi (Class 9, [light-refraction-part6.md](../../../class9/physics/light-refraction/light-refraction-part6.md)).

## Wapas terrace par

**Pehla sawaal: chaand ulta kyun?** Objective ek convex lens hai, jo door ki cheez ki **real, ulti** image I banata hai. Eyepiece I ko sirf bada dikhata hai (magnifying glass ki tarah), seedha nahi karta. To final image ulti hi rahi (Figure 5.1). Antariksh mein koi "upar" nahi, isliye ye theek hai.

**Doosra sawaal: do lens aur lamba tube kyun?** M = f_o ÷ f_e. M zyada chahiye aur f_e chhota nahi kar sakte (lens chhota, curved aur image halki), to **f_o lamba** karte hain: Maitreyi ke telescope mein 70 cm, f_e = 7 cm, M = 10. Tube ki lambai L = f_o + f_e = 77 cm (Example 1).

**Teesra sawaal: taare bade kyun nahi hue, par zyada dikhe?** Telescope kon badhata hai: Chaand ke gadde alag-alag dikhne lage, kyunki unke beech ka kon 10 guna hua. Taara itna door hai ki uska kon pehle se hi bahut chhota hai, 10 guna hone par bhi bindi hai. Par objective 80 mm ka chhed hai, aankh ke 8 mm pupil se lagbhag 100 guna zyada roshni (Example 4): dhundhle taare ab dikhne lage.

Maitreyi ne tube ko apni taraf thoda ghumaya, aur khushi se ek aakhri baar chaand ke gadde ko dekha: "Mama, aaj ke baad main kabhi nahi bolungi ki telescope sirf cheez bada karta hai."

## Aam galtiyan (bachke rehna)

**Galti: "Telescope ka kaam cheez ko bada karna hai"**

Demonstration 1: Maitreyi ke telescope ka M = 10. Chaand ke do gadde jo aankh se ek hi dhabbe jaise dikhte hain, telescope mein 10 guna bade kon par alag dikhte hain: yahan M kaam aaya. Par ek taara jiska kon bahut hi chhota hai, 10 guna hone par bhi bindi: yahan M ne kuch nahi badla. Phir bhi taara pehle se zyada chamakdaar dikha, kyunki **80 mm ka objective aankh ke 8 mm pupil se (80 ÷ 8) ka varg = 100 guna roshni** laaya.

Demonstration 2: do telescope, dono M = 10, ek ka objective 40 mm, doosre ka 80 mm. Taare ke liye kaun behtar? Roshni ka anupaat (80 ÷ 40) ka varg = **4**. Doosra telescope usi taare ko 4 guna zyada roshni deta hai, dhundhle taare dikhata hai, jabki M dono ka ek jaisa. Aur ulta: ek chhota objective aur bahut bada M (bahut chhota f_e) image ko dhundhla kar deta hai.

**Sahi:** telescope ke **do kaam** hain: (1) **kon badhana** (M = f_o ÷ f_e), jisse bade pind ka vistaar alag dikhe, (2) **roshni ikatthi karna** (objective ke diameter ka varg), jisse dhundhli cheezein dikhein. Chhote taare ko "bada" karna telescope ka kaam nahi.

## Quick Recap

- **Astronomical telescope:** objective (lamba f_o, bada diameter) door ki cheez ki real, ulti image **common focus** par banata hai; eyepiece (chhota f_e) usse magnifying glass ki tarah dekhta hai.
- **Normal adjustment:** final image infinity par, aankh relaxed. **M = f_o ÷ f_e**, **L = f_o + f_e**. Final image cheez ke hisaab se ulti.
- M badhane ke liye f_o lamba karo; bahut chhota f_e lens chhota, curved aur image halki banata hai. Final image D par chahiye to eyepiece thoda khisakta hai (focusing).
- **Roshni ikatthi karna** objective ke diameter ke **varg** par nirbhar hai. 80 mm vs 8 mm pupil: lagbhag 100 guna.
- Astronomer ko ulti image se fark nahi. **Terrestrial telescope** aur **binoculars** (prism) image seedhi karte hain. "8 × 40": M = 8, objective 40 mm.
- **Reflecting telescope:** objective ki jagah bada concave mirror, taare ki image F par. Bade telescope isi liye reflecting hote hain: mirror ko peeche se sahara, ek sateh shuddh, colour error nahi. Bharat ke naam: GMRT (radio), Devasthal, Hanle. Hubble aur James Webb orbiting telescope hain.
- **Camera:** convex lens, aperture, shutter, sensor. Door ki cheez ki **real, ulti, chhoti** image F₂ aur 2F₂ ke beech, sensor par. Focus ke liye lens thoda aage-peeche. Phone ka digital zoom naya detail nahi laata.
- Telescope ka kaam sirf bada karna nahi: **kon badhana aur roshni ikatthi karna**.

## Practice Questions

Take D = 25 cm. Use the sign convention from the lesson.

1. A telescope has an objective of focal length 90 cm and an eyepiece of focal length 6 cm. In normal adjustment, find its magnifying power and the length of the tube.
2. A telescope has f_o = 1.2 m and f_e = 4 cm. Find the magnifying power and the distance between the lenses in normal adjustment (in cm).
3. A telescope in normal adjustment has a magnifying power of 25 and a tube length of 78 cm. Find f_o and f_e.
4. A 60 cm long telescope in normal adjustment has a magnifying power of 11. Find the focal lengths of the two lenses.
5. Find the power in dioptres of the objective and of the eyepiece of the telescope in Question 1. Which one is more powerful? How does the eyepiece compare with the whole human eye (+60 D)?
6. For the telescope of Question 2, the eyepiece is moved until the final image is formed at the near point (D = 25 cm). By how much, and in which direction, is the eyepiece moved? Find the new distance between the lenses.
7. The main mirror of a large reflecting telescope is about 3.6 m wide. (a) How many times more light does it gather than an eye with a pupil of 8 mm? (b) How many times more than a 2 m mirror?
8. A tower 42 m tall is 7 km away. Find the angle it makes at the eye (use angle = height ÷ distance, in radians). What angle does the telescope of Question 1 make it appear to subtend in normal adjustment?
9. A reflecting telescope has a concave mirror of focal length 3 m and an eyepiece of focal length 1.5 cm. Find its magnifying power in normal adjustment. At what distance from the mirror is the star's image formed, and on which side of the mirror?
10. A camera lens has a focal length of 30 mm. A statue 2.7 m tall stands 3.03 m from the lens. Find the image distance, the magnification and the image height. How far is the lens moved from its "distant object" setting?
11. A pair of binoculars is marked "10 × 50". Say what the two numbers mean, and find how many times more light the objective collects than an eye with a pupil of 8 mm.
12. Say true or false and give a one-line reason.
    (a) The objective of an astronomical telescope has a short focal length.
    (b) In normal adjustment the final image is at infinity.
    (c) The magnifying power of a telescope depends on the diameter of its objective.
    (d) A reflecting telescope uses a concave mirror as its objective.
    (e) Digital zoom adds new detail to a photograph.
    (f) The final image in an astronomical telescope is erect.
13. A student says, "My telescope has M = 100, so every star will look like a big shining disc." Explain what is wrong, and say which change would help him see a faint star.
14. Why are big optical telescopes built on high mountains such as Hanle in Ladakh, and why is it better still to put one in orbit?
15. Explain in three or four lines why a camera and a slide projector are "opposite" instruments, using the object and image positions.

### Answers

1. M = f_o ÷ f_e = 90 cm ÷ 6 cm = **15**. L = f_o + f_e = 90 + 6 = **96 cm**.
   Jaanch: 15 × 6 = 90 ✅

2. f_o = 1.2 m = 120 cm. M = 120 ÷ 4 = **30**. L = 120 + 4 = **124 cm** (1.24 m).
   Jaanch: 30 × 4 = 120 ✅

3. L = f_o + f_e = f_e × M + f_e = f_e (M + 1) = 78 cm, to f_e = 78 ÷ 26 = **3 cm**, aur f_o = 25 × 3 = **75 cm**.
   Jaanch: 75 + 3 = 78 ✅, 75 ÷ 3 = 25 ✅

4. L = f_e (M + 1) = 60 cm, to f_e = 60 ÷ 12 = **5 cm**, f_o = 11 × 5 = **55 cm**.
   Jaanch: 55 + 5 = 60 ✅, 55 ÷ 5 = 11 ✅

5. Objective: f_o = 90 cm = 0.9 m, P = 1 ÷ 0.9 = **+1.11 D**. Eyepiece: f_e = 6 cm = 0.06 m, P = 1 ÷ 0.06 = **+16.7 D**. Eyepiece zyada powerful hai (16.7 D ≫ 1.11 D: lagbhag 15 guna, aur M = 15 hi tha). Poori aankh ki power lagbhag +60 D hai, jo eyepiece se kaafi zyada hai.
   Jaanch: P_e ÷ P_o = 16.67 ÷ 1.11 = 15 = M ✅

6. Eyepiece: f_e = +4 cm, v = −25 cm (virtual). 1/u = 1/v − 1/f = −1/25 − 1/4 = −0.04 − 0.25 = −0.29 cm⁻¹, to u = **−3.45 cm**. Normal adjustment mein I eyepiece se 4 cm par thi, ab 3.45 cm par chahiye. Eyepiece ko **4 − 3.45 = 0.55 cm objective ki taraf** (I ki taraf) khisakana hoga. Nayi doori = 124 − 0.55 = **123.45 cm**.
   Jaanch: 1/v − 1/u = −0.04 + 0.29 = 0.25 cm⁻¹ = 1/(4 cm) ✅

7. (a) Diameter ka anupaat = 3.6 m ÷ 8 mm = 3600 mm ÷ 8 mm = 450. Roshni ka anupaat = 450² = **2,02,500** (lagbhag 2 lakh guna). (b) (3.6 ÷ 2)² = 1.8² = **3.24 guna**.
   Jaanch: 202,500 = 450 × 450 ✅

8. Angle = 42 m ÷ 7000 m = **0.006 rad**. Telescope ke saath θ' = M × θ = 15 × 0.006 = **0.09 rad**.
   Jaanch: 0.09 ÷ 0.006 = 15 ✅ (rad mein, kam kon ke liye)

9. M = f_o ÷ f_e = 3 m ÷ 1.5 cm = 300 cm ÷ 1.5 cm = **200**. Parallel kirnein mirror se laut kar F par milti hain, to image mirror se **3 m** door, mirror ke **saamne wali taraf** (jahan se roshni aayi) banti hai.
   Jaanch: 200 × 1.5 = 300 cm ✅

10. f = +30 mm, u = −3.03 m = −3030 mm. 1/v = 1/30 − 1/3030 = (101 − 1) ÷ 3030 = 100/3030 mm⁻¹, to **v = +30.3 mm**. m = 30.3 ÷ (−3030) = **−0.01**. Image ki unchai = 2.7 m × 0.01 = 0.027 m = **27 mm** (ulti). Lens infinity setting (v = 30 mm) se 30.3 − 30 = **0.3 mm** aage.
    Jaanch: 1/30.3 + 1/3030 = 0.033003 + 0.000330 = 0.033333 = 1/30 ✅

11. "10 × 50": **M = 10**, objective ka diameter **50 mm**. Roshni ka anupaat = (50 ÷ 8)² = 6.25² = **39.06** (lagbhag 39 guna).
    Jaanch: 6.25 × 6.25 = 39.0625 ✅

12. (a) **False** ❌. Objective ka f lamba hota hai (M = f_o ÷ f_e).
    (b) **True** ✅. Aankh relaxed rehti hai.
    (c) **False** ❌. M sirf f_o ÷ f_e hai; diameter roshni ki matra badalta hai.
    (d) **True** ✅. Taare ki image F par.
    (e) **False** ❌. Digital zoom bas ek hissa kaatkar bada karta hai.
    (f) **False** ❌. Objective ki image ulti hai aur eyepiece seedha nahi karta.

13. M = 100 sirf **kon** 100 guna karta hai. Taara itna door hai ki uska kon pehle se bahut chhota hai, to 100 guna hone par bhi wo bindi hi rehta hai, disc nahi banta. Zyada M se image halki aur dhundhli ho jaati hai. Dhundhla taara dekhne ke liye **objective ka diameter** badhao (bada chhed, zyada roshni), M badhane ki zaroorat nahi.

14. Oonche pahaad par hawa patli aur sukhi hoti hai, aur shahar ki roshni aur dhool kam hoti hai, to aasmaan zyada andhera aur saaf. Orbit mein telescope hawa ki parat ke upar hai, to hawa ki dhundh ya dhool roshni ko nahi rokti.

15. Camera: cheez **bahut door** (2F₁ se door), image F₂ aur 2F₂ ke beech, **real, ulti, chhoti**, sensor par. Projector: slide **paas** (F₁ aur 2F₁ ke beech), image 2F₂ ke peeche, **real, ulti, badi**, screen par. Cheez aur image ki jagah badal gayi (Class 9 ka Position 2 aur Position 4). Isliye ek door ko chhota karke ek chhote sensor par laata hai, doosra chhote slide ko bada karke door screen par.
