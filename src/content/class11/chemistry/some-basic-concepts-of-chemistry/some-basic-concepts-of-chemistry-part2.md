# Topic 2: Laws of Chemical Combination aur Dalton's Atomic Theory

Topic 1 mein humne dekha ki chemistry mein sahi naap-tol aur significant figures kitne zaroori hain. 18th aur 19th sadi ke vaigyanikon ne jab aise hi pakke taraazuon se chemical reactions ka vazan aur gas volumes naapne shuru kiye, to unhein paanch zabardast niyam mile — jinhein **Laws of Chemical Combination** kehte hain. Inmein se chaar niyam (Conservation of Mass, Definite Proportions, Gay-Lussac aur Avogadro) tum Class 9 aur 10 mein padh chuke ho. Aaj hum unhein ek jagah jodenge, paanchvein bade niyam **Law of Multiple Proportions** ko poori gehraai se seekhenge, aur **Dalton's Atomic Theory** ki Class 11 wali imaandar jaanch karenge: ye theory kahan chamak gayi aur kahan adhooori reh gayi!

## Gulzar aur pottery workshop ke laal-kaale copper oxides

Gulzar apne Papa ke dost ke pottery glaze workshop mein gaya tha, jahan mitti ke bartan par chamakdar rang (glaze) chadhaya jaata hai. Shelf par do alag-alag band dibbon mein do powder rakhe the: ek **chamakdar laal (Cu₂O)** aur doosra **gehra kaala (CuO)**.

Gulzar ne label padha aur chaunk gaya: "Uncle, dono dibbon par likha hai ki ye sirf **copper aur oxygen** se bane hain! Par Class 9 mein humne padha tha ki ek compound mein tatvon ka ratio fixed hota hai. To copper aur oxygen milkar do bilkul alag rang ke compounds kaise bana sakte hain?"

Workshop wale uncle muskuraye: "Shabaash Gulzar, yahi sawaal 1803 mein John Dalton ke dimaag mein aaya tha! Jab wahi do tatva ek se zyada compounds banayein — jaise laal copper(I) oxide (Cu₂O) aur kaala copper(II) oxide (CuO) — to unke andar oxygen ke mass mein koi ajeeb-o-gareeb number nahi hota, balki ek **simple whole-number ratio (1 : 2)** nikalta hai! Isi ko **Law of Multiple Proportions** kehte hain." Chalo, paanchon niyamon aur Dalton ke siddhant ko khol kar dekhte hain.

## Part A: Paanchon Laws of Chemical Combination Ek Saath

Class 9 aur 10 ki kadi ko jodte hue paanchon niyamon ka naqsha dekho:

1. **Law of Conservation of Mass (Antoine Lavoisier, 1789):** Chemical reaction mein kul mass na banta hai na nasht hota hai (Class 9 [atoms-and-molecules.md](../../../class9/chemistry/atoms-and-molecules/atoms-and-molecules.md)). *(Class 11 note: nuclear reactions mein thoda mass energy mein badal sakta hai, par aam chemical reactions mein ye badlaav naapne se bhi chhota hota hai.)*
2. **Law of Definite (Constant) Proportions (Joseph Proust, 1799):** Koi bhi shuddh compound chahe kisi bhi srot se mile, usmein uske tatvon ka mass ratio hamesha ek hi hota hai (Class 9 [atoms-and-molecules-part2.md](../../../class9/chemistry/atoms-and-molecules/atoms-and-molecules-part2.md)).
3. **Law of Multiple Proportions (John Dalton, 1803):** **Naya Class 11 core niyam** — niche Part B mein poori gehraai se!
4. **Gay-Lussac's Law of Gaseous Volumes (1808):** Samaan taapman aur dabao (same T and P) par jab gases reaction karti hain, to unke volumes ek simple whole-number ratio mein hote hain (Class 10 [mole-concept-and-stoichiometry.md](../../../class10/chemistry/mole-concept-and-stoichiometry/mole-concept-and-stoichiometry.md)).
5. **Avogadro's Law (1811):** Samaan T aur P par sabhi gases ke barabar volumes mein molecules ki ginti barabar hoti hai (Class 10 [mole-concept-and-stoichiometry.md](../../../class10/chemistry/mole-concept-and-stoichiometry/mole-concept-and-stoichiometry.md)).

## Part B: Law of Multiple Proportions (Dalton, 1803) — Gehraai Se

Class 9 ([atoms-and-molecules-part2.md](../../../class9/chemistry/atoms-and-molecules/atoms-and-molecules-part2.md), Part G) mein is niyam ka sirf naam aaya tha. Ab iska poora ganit seekhte hain.

**Niyam:** Jab do tatva A aur B aapas mein milkar **do ya do se zyada alag compounds** banate hain, to tatva A ke ek **fixed mass** ke saath judne wale tatva B ke alag-alag masses aapas mein ek **simple whole-number ratio** mein hote hain.

### Sabse zaroori kadam: Ek tatva ka mass FIX karo!
Raw percentage ya alag-alag sample masses ko seedha compare mat karo! Pehle **1 g** (ya kisi ek suvidhajanak fixed mass, jaise 14 g ya 31 g) of Element A ke saath Element B ka kitna mass juda hai, ye nikaalo — phir un B ke masses ka ratio lo.

### Example 1. Gulzar ke pottery workshop ke do copper oxides (Cu₂O aur CuO)

**Sawaal:** Lab analysis mein laal copper oxide (Compound 1) mein **88.81% Cu** aur **11.19% O** mila, jabki kaale copper oxide (Compound 2) mein **79.87% Cu** aur **20.13% O** mila. Dikhao ki ye data Law of Multiple Proportions ka paalan karta hai.

**Hal:**
- **Step 1 (Copper ka mass 1.00 g fix karo):**
  - Compound 1 (Cu₂O) mein: 88.81 g Cu ke saath 11.19 g O juda hai.
    To **1.00 g Cu** ke saath juda O = 11.19 ÷ 88.81 = **0.1260 g O**.
  - Compound 2 (CuO) mein: 79.87 g Cu ke saath 20.13 g O juda hai.
    To **1.00 g Cu** ke saath juda O = 20.13 ÷ 79.87 = **0.2520 g O**.
- **Step 2 (Fixed 1.00 g Cu ke liye O ke masses ka ratio lo):**
  - O (Compound 1) : O (Compound 2) = 0.1260 : 0.2520 = **1 : 2**!

**Jaanch:** Cu₂O mein 2 Cu (127 g) ke saath 16 g O, aur CuO mein 2 Cu (127 g) ke saath 32 g O. Ratio 16 : 32 = 1 : 2 (exact simple whole-number ratio) ✅.

---

### Example 2. Nitrogen ke 5 Oxides — Law of Multiple Proportions ka sabse sundar saboot *(sirf padhne ke liye; NO, NO₂ aur N₂O₅ zehreele/corrosive gases hain)*

Nitrogen aur oxygen milkar **paanch** alag oxides banate hain: N₂O, NO, N₂O₃, NO₂ aur N₂O₅. Chalo **14 g Nitrogen** ko fix karke sabmein Oxygen ka mass dekhte hain:

| Compound | Formula | 28 g N ke saath O | **14 g N (Fixed) ke saath O** | Ratio (O ka) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nitrous oxide | N₂O | 16 g | **8 g** | **1** |
| Nitric oxide | NO | 32 g | **16 g** | **2** |
| Dinitrogen trioxide | N₂O₃ | 48 g | **24 g** | **3** |
| Nitrogen dioxide | NO₂ | 64 g | **32 g** | **4** |
| Dinitrogen pentoxide | N₂O₅ | 80 g | **40 g** | **5** |

```
   14 g Nitrogen (FIXED) ke saath Oxygen ka mass:

   N₂O  : [ 8 g ]                                   --> 1 hissa
   NO   : [ 8 g ][ 8 g ]                            --> 2 hisse
   N₂O₃ : [ 8 g ][ 8 g ][ 8 g ]                     --> 3 hisse
   NO₂  : [ 8 g ][ 8 g ][ 8 g ][ 8 g ]              --> 4 hisse
   N₂O₅ : [ 8 g ][ 8 g ][ 8 g ][ 8 g ][ 8 g ]       --> 5 hisse

   Ratio = 8 : 16 : 24 : 32 : 40  =  1 : 2 : 3 : 4 : 5
```

> **Figure 2.1** – 14 g nitrogen ke saath paanchon nitrogen oxides mein oxygen 8 g ke poora-ank gunaj (1, 2, 3, 4, 5) mein judti hai — yahi Law of Multiple Proportions hai.

**Jaanch:** Sabhi 5 oxides mein 8 g se bhaag dene par exact 1 : 2 : 3 : 4 : 5 milta hai ✅.

### Example 3. Phosphorus ke do Chlorides (PCl₃ aur PCl₅) *(sirf padhne ke liye; dono corrosive aur toxic hain)*

**Sawaal:** Phosphorus chlorine ke saath do chlorides banata hai. Pehle chloride mein **31.0 g P** ke saath **106.5 g Cl** judta hai, aur doosre chloride mein **31.0 g P** ke saath **177.5 g Cl** judta hai. Ratio nikaalo aur batao ye kaun sa niyam dikhata hai.

**Hal:**
- Yahan phosphorus ka mass pehle se hi **31.0 g fixed** hai!
- Chlorine ke masses ka ratio = 106.5 : 177.5.
- Dono ko 35.5 (1 mol Cl ka mass) se bhaag do:
  - 106.5 ÷ 35.5 = **3**
  - 177.5 ÷ 35.5 = **5**
- Simple whole-number ratio = **3 : 5** → **Law of Multiple Proportions** siddh hota hai!

**Jaanch:** 3 × 35.5 = 106.5 g (PCl₃) aur 5 × 35.5 = 177.5 g (PCl₅); ratio 3 : 5 ✅.

### Law of Reciprocal Proportions (Jeremias Richter, 1792) — Poora Ganit

Jab do alag-alag tatva **A** aur **B** alag-alag kisi teesre tatva **C ke fixed mass** se judte hain, to unke masses ka jo ratio (A : B) hota hai, jab A aur B khud aapas mein judkar compound banayenge, to unka mass ratio **ya to wahi hoga ya uska simple whole-number multiple** hoga!

```
                      C (Carbon — 12 g FIXED)
                        /                 \
               CH₄     /                   \    CO₂
             (4 g H)  /                     \  (32 g O)
                     v                       v
                    H  --------------------->  O
                         H₂O (2 g H : 16 g O)

   Ratio with fixed 12 g C:  H : O = 4 : 32 = 1 : 8
   Direct ratio in H₂O:      H : O = 2 : 16 = 1 : 8  (BILKUL BARABAR!)
```

> **Figure 2.2** – Law of Reciprocal Proportions ka triangle: C ke fixed 12 g ke saath H aur O ka ratio (1 : 8) bilkul wahi hai jo H aur O ke seedhe compound H₂O (1 : 8) mein milta hai.

### Example 4. Law of Reciprocal Proportions — Sulphur, Hydrogen aur Oxygen *(H₂S, SO₂ zehreele gases: sirf paper calculation)*

**Sawaal:** H₂S mein **5.88% H** aur **94.12% S** hai; H₂O mein **11.11% H** aur **88.89% O** hai; aur SO₂ mein **50.0% S** aur **50.0% O** hai. Siddh karo ki ye data Law of Reciprocal Proportions ka paalan karta hai.

**Hal:**
- **Step 1 (Teesre tatva H ka mass 1.00 g FIX karo):**
  - H₂S mein: 1.00 g H ke saath juda S = 94.12 ÷ 5.88 = **16.0 g S**.
  - H₂O mein: 1.00 g H ke saath juda O = 88.89 ÷ 11.11 = **8.0 g O**.
  - Isliye fixed 1.00 g H ke liye **S : O = 16.0 : 8.0 = 2 : 1**.
- **Step 2 (Direct compound SO₂ mein S : O dekho):**
  - SO₂ mein S : O = 50.0 : 50.0 = **1 : 1**.
- **Step 3 (Dono ratios ki tulna):**
  - (2/1) ÷ (1/1) = **2 : 1** — ek simple whole-number multiple! Isliye **Law of Reciprocal Proportions** siddh hota hai!

**Jaanch:** H₂S (2 g H : 32 g S → 1 : 16), H₂O (2 g H : 16 g O → 1 : 8) → S : O = 2 : 1; SO₂ mein 32 : 32 = 1 : 1; ratio of ratios = 2 : 1 ✅.

*(Berzelius banaam Avogadro ka Itihas: Berzelius ne kaha tha ki barabar volume gases mein barabar ATOMS hote hain — par usse H + Cl → 2 HCl mein aadha atom toot-ta dikha, jo Dalton ke khilaaf tha! 1811 mein **Avogadro** ne "atoms" ki jagah **"molecules"** shabd rakha aur poori chemistry bacha li!)*

## Part C: Dalton's Atomic Theory — Class 11 Ki Imaandar Jaanch

Class 9 ([atoms-and-molecules-part3.md](../../../class9/chemistry/atoms-and-molecules/atoms-and-molecules-part3.md)) mein tumne Dalton ke postulates padhe the. Ab Class 11 mein dekhte hain ki Dalton ki theory **kahan kamyaab rahi** aur **kahan fail ho gayi**:

### Dalton's Theory Kahan Chamak Gayi (Successes)
1. **Law of Conservation of Mass:** Chemical reaction mein atoms sirf apni jagah badalte (rearrange hote) hain — koi atom banta ya mita nahi. Isliye kul mass barabar rehta hai.
2. **Law of Definite Proportions:** Har compound mein atoms ki sankhya aur prakaar fixed hote hain, isliye mass ratio fixed hota hai.
3. **Law of Multiple Proportions:** Kyunki atoms aadhe ya paune mein nahi toot-te, isliye fixed atoms of A ke saath B ke 1, 2, 3 ya 5 poora-ank atoms hi jud sakte hain! Isliye mass ratio hamesha simple whole numbers mein aata hai.

### Dalton's Theory Kahan Fail Ho Gayi (Limitations — Class 11 Key Points)
1. **Gay-Lussac's Law of Gaseous Volumes nahi samjha paayi:** Dalton maanta tha ki gases akele atoms (H, Cl) ke roop mein rehti hain. Is hisaab se 1 vol H + 1 vol Cl se 1 vol HCl banna chahiye tha, jabki prayog mein **2 vol HCl** banta hai! Iska hal **Avogadro (1811)** ne diya: **atom aur molecule alag hain** — hydrogen aur chlorine diatomic molecules (H₂, Cl₂) hain: H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl!
2. **Atom ab "indivisible" nahi raha:** Baad mein electron, proton aur neutron ki khoj hui (jo hum agle chapter mein padhenge).
3. **Isotopes ne "ek tatva ke sabhi atoms ka mass same" galat sabit kiya:** ³⁵Cl aur ³⁷Cl dono chlorine hain, par mass alag hai.
4. **Isobars ne "alag tatvon ke atoms ka mass hamesha alag" galat sabit kiya:** ⁴⁰Ar aur ⁴⁰Ca alag tatva hain, par mass number dono ka 40 hai.
5. **Atoms aapas mein kyun judte hain (chemical bonding ka kaaran):** Dalton ne nahi bataya ki H₂O mein 2 H aur 1 O kyun judte hain, 5 H kyun nahi.
6. **Non-stoichiometric compounds:** Kuch thos (jaise Fe₀.₉₅O) mein crystal defects ke kaaran exact whole-number ratio nahi hota.

## Aam galtiyan (bachke rehna)

1. **Galti 1: Law of Multiple Proportions mein raw percentages ko seedha divide kar dena.**
   Example 1 mein ek student ne Cu₂O ke 11.19% O aur CuO ke 20.13% O ko seedha bhaag de diya: 20.13 ÷ 11.19 = 1.80 (jo 2 nahi aaya!) aur likh diya "niyam fail ho gaya!"
   *Sahi baat:* 100 g Cu₂O mein 88.81 g Cu hai, jabki 100 g CuO mein sirf 79.87 g Cu hai — Cu ka mass fixed nahi tha! Pehle **1 g Cu** ke liye O nikaalo (0.1260 g vs 0.2520 g), tab exact **1 : 2** milega.

2. **Galti 2: Law of Definite Proportions aur Law of Multiple Proportions ko ghalat-malat kar dena.**
   Ek student ne nadi ke paani (H₂O) aur baarish ke paani (H₂O) mein H : O = 1 : 8 ko "Law of Multiple Proportions" likh diya!
   *Sahi baat:* **Ek hi compound** (H₂O) alag jagahon se → **Law of Definite Proportions**. **Do alag compounds** (H₂O aur H₂O₂) wahi do tatvon se → **Law of Multiple Proportions**.

3. **Galti 3: Ye maan lena ki Dalton ne Gay-Lussac ka niyam samjha diya tha.**
   Dalton ne Gay-Lussac ke gas-volume data ko khud sweekar nahi kiya tha kyunki use "aadha atom" tootne ka dar lagta tha. **Avogadro** ne polyatomic molecules (H₂, O₂, Cl₂) ki kalpana karke Gay-Lussac aur Dalton ke beech ka jhagda suljhaya!

## Quick Recap

- **Paanch Laws of Chemical Combination:** Conservation of Mass (Lavoisier), Definite Proportions (Proust), **Multiple Proportions (Dalton)**, Gaseous Volumes (Gay-Lussac), aur Avogadro's Law.
- **Law of Multiple Proportions:** Do tatva A aur B jab kai compounds banayein, to A ke **fixed mass** ke saath judne wale B ke masses simple whole-number ratio mein hote hain (hamesha pehle ek tatva ka mass fix karo!).
- **Dalton's Theory:** Laws 1, 2 aur 3 ko bakhubi samjhati hai, par Gay-Lussac's gas volumes, subatomic particles, isotopes (³⁵Cl/³⁷Cl), isobars (⁴⁰Ar/⁴⁰Ca), chemical bonding aur non-stoichiometric solids (Fe₀.₉₅O) nahi samjha paati.

## Practice Questions

1. Tin forms two oxides, SnO and SnO₂. In one experiment, **1.19 g of tin** combines with **0.16 g of oxygen** to form the first oxide, and **1.19 g of tin** combines with **0.32 g of oxygen** to form the second oxide. Show that these data illustrate the Law of Multiple Proportions.
2. Sulphur forms two oxides (paper problem only; SO₂ and SO₃ are toxic corrosive gases). In the first oxide, **3.20 g of sulphur** combines with **3.20 g of oxygen**; in the second oxide, **3.20 g of sulphur** combines with **4.80 g of oxygen**. Calculate the simple whole-number ratio of oxygen masses combining with a fixed mass of sulphur.
3. Iron forms two chlorides, FeCl₂ and FeCl₃. In FeCl₂, **2.80 g of iron** combines with **3.55 g of chlorine**; in FeCl₃, **2.80 g of iron** combines with **5.325 g of chlorine**. Verify the Law of Multiple Proportions using these masses.
4. Carbon forms two oxides (paper problem only; CO is a deadly colourless, odourless poison). Oxide X contains **42.86% carbon** and **57.14% oxygen**, whereas Oxide Y contains **27.27% carbon** and **72.73% oxygen**. Fix the mass of carbon at **1.00 g** and verify the Law of Multiple Proportions.
5. Lead forms two oxides (paper problem only; lead compounds are poisonous): **2.07 g of lead** combines with **0.16 g of oxygen** in the yellow oxide (PbO) and with **0.32 g of oxygen** in the brown oxide (PbO₂). What is the mass ratio of oxygen per fixed mass of lead? Which law is illustrated?
6. Nitrogen and hydrogen react to form ammonia: N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) → 2NH₃(g). If **40 mL of N₂** reacts completely with **120 mL of H₂** at constant temperature and pressure, what volume of NH₃ gas is formed? Explain how Avogadro's Law resolves why 1 volume of N₂ + 3 volumes of H₂ gives 2 volumes of NH₃ (and not 4 volumes).
7. Clearly distinguish between the **Law of Definite Proportions** and the **Law of Multiple Proportions** with one pair of real chemical examples.
8. State any **four major limitations** of Dalton's Atomic Theory that led chemists to develop modern atomic theory.
9. A student checks two samples of table salt (NaCl) — one mined from rock salt and one prepared in a lab — and finds 39.3% Na in both. Another student compares H₂O (11.1% H) and H₂O₂ (5.9% H). Identify which law each student is verifying.
10. State whether each statement is True or False, and correct the false ones:
    (a) Dalton's Atomic Theory successfully explained Gay-Lussac's Law of Gaseous Volumes.
    (b) The discovery of isotopes (such as ³⁵Cl and ³⁷Cl) contradicted Dalton's postulate that all atoms of an element have identical mass.
    (c) To test the Law of Multiple Proportions from percentage data, you can divide the two percentages of oxygen directly without fixing the other element's mass.
    (d) Non-stoichiometric solids such as Fe₀.₉₅O do not strictly obey the Law of Definite Proportions.
11. Ammonia (NH₃) contains **82.35% N** and **17.65% H**; water (H₂O) contains **88.89% O** and **11.11% H**; and nitrous oxide (N₂O) contains **63.64% N** and **36.36% O**. Fix the mass of hydrogen at **1.00 g** and verify the **Law of Reciprocal Proportions**.
12. Explain clearly why **Berzelius's hypothesis** ("equal volumes of gases contain equal numbers of atoms") conflicted with Dalton's Atomic Theory, and how **Avogadro's Law** resolved the conflict using the reaction H₂(g) + Cl₂(g) → 2HCl(g).

### Answers

1. Tin ka mass dono oxides mein pehle se **1.19 g fixed** hai.
   Oxygen ke masses ka ratio = 0.16 g : 0.32 g = **1 : 2** (ek simple whole-number ratio).
   Ye **Law of Multiple Proportions** ko siddh karta hai.
   Jaanch: 0.32 ÷ 0.16 = 2 (SnO vs SnO₂) ✅.

2. Sulphur ka mass dono oxides mein **3.20 g fixed** hai.
   Oxygen ke masses ka ratio = 3.20 g : 4.80 g. Dono ko 1.60 se bhaag do → **2 : 3**.
   Jaanch: 2 × 1.60 = 3.20 (SO₂) aur 3 × 1.60 = 4.80 (SO₃) ✅.

3. Iron ka mass dono chlorides mein **2.80 g fixed** hai.
   Chlorine ke masses ka ratio = 3.55 g : 5.325 g. Dono ko 1.775 se bhaag do → **2 : 3** (kyunki 3.55 ÷ 1.775 = 2 aur 5.325 ÷ 1.775 = 3).
   Simple whole-number ratio 2 : 3 milne se Law of Multiple Proportions siddh hota hai.
   Jaanch: 3.55 / 5.325 = 2 / 3 ✅.

4. Pehle **1.00 g carbon** fix karo:
   - Oxide X (CO): 42.86 g C ke saath 57.14 g O → 1.00 g C ke saath O = 57.14 ÷ 42.86 = **1.333 g O**.
   - Oxide Y (CO₂): 27.27 g C ke saath 72.73 g O → 1.00 g C ke saath O = 72.73 ÷ 27.27 = **2.667 g O**.
   - Fixed 1.00 g C ke liye O ka ratio = 1.333 : 2.667 = **1 : 2**!
   Jaanch: 2.667 ÷ 1.333 = 2.00 (CO vs CO₂) ✅.

5. Fixed 2.07 g lead ke saath oxygen ka ratio = 0.16 g : 0.32 g = **1 : 2**. Ye **Law of Multiple Proportions** dikhata hai.
   Jaanch: 0.32 ÷ 0.16 = 2 ✅.

6. Gay-Lussac's Law se volume ratio N₂ : H₂ : NH₃ = 1 : 3 : 2.
   40 mL N₂ + 120 mL H₂ → **80 mL NH₃** banega.
   Avogadro's Law ke anusar barabar volumes mein barabar molecules hote hain: 1 molecule N₂ + 3 molecules H₂ → 2 molecules NH₃. Kyunki N₂ aur H₂ **diatomic molecules** hain, 2 N atoms aur 6 H atoms milkar theek 2 NH₃ molecules banate hain (atom toot-te nahi, sirf molecule toot-te hain).
   Jaanch: 40 : 120 : 80 = 1 : 3 : 2 ✅.

7. **Law of Definite Proportions:** Ek hi shuddh compound mein (chahe jahan se mile) tatvon ka mass ratio fixed hota hai (jaise shuddh H₂O mein H : O hamesha 1 : 8).
   **Law of Multiple Proportions:** Do tatva jab do ya zyada alag compounds banayein (jaise H₂O aur H₂O₂), to ek tatva ke fixed mass (2 g H) ke saath doosre tatva ke masses (16 g O aur 32 g O) simple whole-number ratio (1 : 2) mein hote hain.

8. Dalton's Atomic Theory ki chaar badi seemayein: (1) Gay-Lussac's Law of Gaseous Volumes nahi samjha paayi; (2) Subatomic particles (electron, proton, neutron) ki khoj ke baad atom indivisible nahi raha; (3) Isotopes (ek hi tatva ke alag mass wale atoms) aur Isobars (alag tatvon ke same mass number wale atoms) nahi samjha paayi; (4) Atoms aapas mein kyun judte hain (bonding) aur non-stoichiometric compounds (Fe₀.₉₅O) ki vyakhya nahi kar paayi.

9. Pehla student (dono jagah se ek hi compound NaCl) → **Law of Definite Proportions**.
   Doosra student (wahi H aur O se do alag compounds H₂O aur H₂O₂) → **Law of Multiple Proportions**.

10. (a) **False** ❌. Dalton ise nahi samjha paaya tha; Avogadro ne polyatomic molecules ke idea se ise suljhaya.
    (b) **True** ✅. ³⁵Cl aur ³⁷Cl dono chlorine ke atoms hain par unke masses alag hain.
    (c) **False** ❌. Pehle ek tatva ka mass fix karna zaroori hai, phir doosre tatva ke masses ka ratio liya jaata hai.
    (d) **True** ✅. Fe₀.₉₅O jaise non-stoichiometric thos exact whole-number ratio mein nahi hote.

11. 1.00 g H fix karo:
    - NH₃ mein N = 82.35 ÷ 17.65 = **4.666 g N** (exact 14/3 g).
    - H₂O mein O = 88.89 ÷ 11.11 = **8.000 g O**.
    - Fixed 1 g H ke liye N : O = 4.666 : 8.000 = **7 : 12** (≈ 0.5833).
    - Direct N₂O mein N : O = 63.64 : 36.36 = **7 : 4** (= 1.750).
    - Dono ratios ka anupaat = 1.750 ÷ 0.5833 = **3 : 1** (simple whole number!) → Law of Reciprocal Proportions siddh hua!
    Jaanch: (7/4) ÷ (7/12) = 12/4 = 3 : 1 ✅.

12. Berzelius ke mutabik 1 vol H + 1 vol Cl → 2 vol HCl ka matlab 1 atom H + 1 atom Cl → 2 compound atoms HCl, yaani 1 HCl banane mein **½ atom H + ½ atom Cl** lagega — jo Dalton ke indivisible atom ke khilaaf tha! Avogadro ne kaha ki barabar volume mein barabar **molecules** hote hain (H₂, Cl₂ diatomic hain), isliye 1 molecule H₂ + 1 molecule Cl₂ → 2 molecules HCl bina kisi atom ko tode ban jaata hai!
