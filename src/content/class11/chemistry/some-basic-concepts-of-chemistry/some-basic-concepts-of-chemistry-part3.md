# Topic 3: Average Atomic Mass, Mole Concept, Combustion Analysis aur Molecular Formula

Topic 2 mein humne dekha ki isotopes ki khoj ne Dalton ke "ek tatva ke sabhi atoms ka mass same" wale niyam ko badal diya. Class 9 aur 10 mein tumne atomic mass, molecular mass, mole (6.022 × 10²³), percentage composition aur seedhe percentage se empirical formula nikaalna seekha tha. Class 11 mein hum in sab ko **teen naye asli lab tools** tak le jaate hain: (1) isotopes ke data se **Average Atomic Mass** aur ulta **% abundance** nikaalna, (2) **Combustion Analysis** (compound ko jalakar bane CO₂ aur H₂O ke mass se C, H aur O nikaalna), aur (3) **Hydrated salts** ka empirical formula nikaalna!

## Ehsaan aur science museum ki periodic table ka 10.81 wala raaz

Ehsaan apni Didi ke saath school ke science museum gaya tha, jahan deewar par ek badi periodic table aur mass spectrometer ka poster laga tha. Ehsaan ne dhyan se dekha aur apni Didi se poochha, "Didi, Boron ke nucleus mein protons aur neutrons to poori ginti mein hote hain — ya to 10 honge ya 11. Phir is wall chart par Boron ka atomic mass **10.81 u** aur Chlorine ka **35.45 u** decimal mein kyun likha hai? Kya koi 0.81 neutron bhi hota hai?"

Didi hans padin: "Bilkul nahi Ehsaan! Koi aadha-adhoora neutron nahi hota. Prakriti mein Boron ke do tarah ke atoms (**isotopes**) milte hain: halka **¹⁰B** lagbhag 20% aur bhaari **¹¹B** lagbhag 80%. Jab tum lab mein ek chutki Boron uthate ho, to usmein dono isotopes ka yahi mishran hota hai — aur inka **weighted average** theek **10.81 u** aata hai!"

Ehsaan ko baat turant chamak gayi: periodic table ka atomic mass kisi ek atom ka nahi, balki prakriti ke saare isotopes ka **weighted average** hota hai. Chalo iska ganit aur Class 11 ka formula-detective method seekhte hain.

## Part A: Unified Mass (u), Average Atomic Mass aur Isotopic Abundance

Class 9 ([atoms-and-molecules-part4.md](../../../class9/chemistry/atoms-and-molecules/atoms-and-molecules-part4.md)) mein humne ¹²C standard dekha tha. Class 11 mein iski pakki sankhya yaad rakho:
- **1 u (unified mass ya Dalton, Da) = (1/12) × mass of one ¹²C atom = 1.66056 × 10⁻²⁴ g.**
- **Mole ki aadhunik paribhasha (2019 SI standard):** Ek mole wo matra hai jismein theek **6.02214076 × 10²³** kan (atoms, molecules ya ions) hon. *(Aam hisaab mein hum **6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹** use karte hain.)*
- **Mole, Mass aur Formula Mass** ka basic pul tum Class 9 ([atoms-and-molecules-part5.md](../../../class9/chemistry/atoms-and-molecules/atoms-and-molecules-part5.md) aur [atoms-and-molecules-part6.md](../../../class9/chemistry/atoms-and-molecules/atoms-and-molecules-part6.md)) mein pakka kar chuke ho: n = m ÷ M aur N = n × Nₐ.

### Average Atomic Mass ka Formula
Agar kisi tatva ke do isotopes hain jinke atomic masses m₁, m₂ aur percentage abundances p₁%, p₂% hain (jahan p₁ + p₂ = 100):

**Average Atomic Mass = (p₁ × m₁ + p₂ × m₂) ÷ 100**

*(Teen ya zyada isotopes ke liye isi tarah p₃ × m₃ jodte jaao aur 100 se bhaag do!)*

### Example 1. Ehsaan ke wall chart ka Boron (10.81 u) — Forward Calculation

**Sawaal:** Prakriti mein Boron ke do isotopes milte hain: **¹⁰B** (mass = 10.013 u, abundance = 19.9%) aur **¹¹B** (mass = 11.009 u, abundance = 80.1%). Boron ka average atomic mass nikaalo.

**Hal:**
- Average atomic mass = (19.9 × 10.013 + 80.1 × 11.009) ÷ 100
- 19.9 × 10.013 = 199.2587
- 80.1 × 11.009 = 881.8209
- Jod = 199.2587 + 881.8209 = 1081.0796
- 100 se bhaag dene par = **10.8108 u ≈ 10.81 u**!

**Jaanch:** 10.81 u, 10.013 aur 11.009 ke beech hai aur bhaari isotope ¹¹B (80.1%) ke kaafi paas hai ✅.

---

### Example 2. Ulta Sawaal — Average Atomic Mass se Isotopes ka % Abundance (Copper)

**Sawaal:** Copper ke do prakritik isotopes hain: **⁶³Cu** (mass = 62.93 u) aur **⁶⁵Cu** (mass = 64.93 u). Agar copper ka average atomic mass **63.55 u** hai, to dono isotopes ka percentage abundance nikaalo.

**Hal:**
- Maan lo **⁶³Cu** ka abundance = **x%** hai. To **⁶⁵Cu** ka abundance = **(100 − x)%** hoga.
- Formula lagao:
  [x × 62.93 + (100 − x) × 64.93] ÷ 100 = 63.55
- Dono taraf 100 se guna karo:
  62.93x + 6493 − 64.93x = 6355
- −2.00x = 6355 − 6493 = −138
- x = 138 ÷ 2.00 = **69.0%**
- Isliye **⁶³Cu = 69.0%** aur **⁶⁵Cu = 100 − 69.0 = 31.0%**!

**Jaanch:** (69 × 62.93 + 31 × 64.93) ÷ 100 = (4342.17 + 2012.83) ÷ 100 = 6355 ÷ 100 = 63.55 u ✅.

## Part B: Combustion Analysis se Empirical aur Molecular Formula

Class 10 ([mole-concept-and-stoichiometry-part3.md](../../../class10/chemistry/mole-concept-and-stoichiometry/mole-concept-and-stoichiometry-part3.md)) mein tumhe tatvon ka seedha percentage diya hota tha aur tum 5-step method se empirical formula aur n = M ÷ EFM se molecular formula nikaalte the.

Par asli organic chemistry lab mein machine seedha "%" nahi deti — wo compound ko excess oxygen mein jalati hai (**Combustion Analysis**, *sirf padhne ke liye / lab instrument demo*) aur sirf do cheezein tolti hai:
1. Kitne gram **CO₂** bana (KOH tube mein absorb hokar) → isse sample ka **poora Carbon** mil jaata hai!
2. Kitne gram **H₂O** bana (anhydrous CaCl₂ tube mein absorb hokar) → isse sample ka **poora Hydrogen** mil jaata hai!
3. Agar compound mein **Oxygen** bhi ho, to uska mass **difference (ghataav)** se milta hai (kyunki combustion mein bahar se extra O₂ di gayi thi!):

```
   Sample (m_sample g) + Excess O₂  --->  CO₂ (m_CO₂ g)  +  H₂O (m_H₂O g)
                                              :                 :
                                              v                 v
   Mass of C  = m_CO₂ × (12 / 44)             :                 :
   Mass of H  = m_H₂O × (2 / 18)              <-----------------+
   Mass of O  = m_sample − (Mass of C + Mass of H)
                (Sirf tab jab C + H ka jod m_sample se kam ho!)
```

> **Figure 3.1** – Combustion Analysis ka pul: CO₂ ke mass se C (12/44 hissa), H₂O ke mass se H (2/18 hissa), aur bacha hua mass O deta hai. Iske baad wahi Class 10 wala mole-ratio method lagakar empirical aur molecular formula mil jaata hai.

### Example 3. Combustion Analysis — C, H aur O wala Compound (Lactic Acid)

**Sawaal:** Ek organic compound (jismein C, H aur O hain) ke **0.450 g** sample ko poori tarah jalane par **0.660 g CO₂** aur **0.270 g H₂O** bana. Agar compound ka molar mass **90 g/mol** hai, to iska empirical aur molecular formula nikaalo.

**Hal:**
- **Step 1 (C, H aur O ka mass nikaalo):**
  - Mass of C = 0.660 × (12 ÷ 44) = 0.660 × (3 ÷ 11) = **0.180 g**
  - Mass of H = 0.270 × (2 ÷ 18) = 0.270 ÷ 9 = **0.030 g**
  - Mass of O = 0.450 − (0.180 + 0.030) = 0.450 − 0.210 = **0.240 g**
- **Step 2 (Har tatva ke moles nikaalo):**
  - Moles of C = 0.180 ÷ 12 = **0.015 mol**
  - Moles of H = 0.030 ÷ 1 = **0.030 mol**
  - Moles of O = 0.240 ÷ 16 = **0.015 mol**
- **Step 3 (Sabse chhote mole 0.015 se sabko bhaag do):**
  - C : H : O = (0.015 ÷ 0.015) : (0.030 ÷ 0.015) : (0.015 ÷ 0.015) = **1 : 2 : 1**
  - **Empirical Formula = CH₂O**
- **Step 4 (Molecular Formula):**
  - Empirical Formula Mass (CH₂O) = 12 + 2 + 16 = **30 g/mol**
  - n = Molar mass ÷ Empirical formula mass = 90 ÷ 30 = **3**
  - **Molecular Formula = (CH₂O)₃ = C₃H₆O₃**

**Jaanch:** C₃H₆O₃ ka molar mass = 3(12) + 6(1) + 3(16) = 36 + 6 + 48 = 90 g/mol ✅. 0.450 g = 0.005 mol C₃H₆O₃ → 0.015 mol CO₂ (0.660 g) aur 0.015 mol H₂O (0.270 g) ✅.

---

### Example 4. Combustion Analysis — Sirf Hydrocarbon (Fractional Multiplier ×2)

**Sawaal:** Ek gaseous hydrocarbon ke **0.580 g** sample ke complete combustion se **1.760 g CO₂** aur **0.900 g H₂O** mila. Agar molar mass **58 g/mol** hai, to empirical aur molecular formula nikaalo. *(Sirf padhne ke liye; hydrocarbons jalansheel gases hain.)*

**Hal:**
- Mass of C = 1.760 × (12 ÷ 44) = **0.480 g**
- Mass of H = 0.900 × (2 ÷ 18) = **0.100 g**
- Check for O: 0.480 + 0.100 = 0.580 g (poora sample C aur H se bana hai, O = 0 g!).
- Moles: C = 0.480 ÷ 12 = **0.040 mol**; H = 0.100 ÷ 1 = **0.100 mol**.
- Sabse chhote mole (0.040) se bhaag do: C = 1, H = 0.100 ÷ 0.040 = **2.5**.
- 2.5 poori sankhya nahi hai → dono ko **2** se guna karo → C : H = **2 : 5**.
- **Empirical Formula = C₂H₅** (Empirical mass = 24 + 5 = 29 g/mol).
- n = 58 ÷ 29 = **2** → **Molecular Formula = C₄H₁₀** (butane).

**Jaanch:** C₄H₁₀ ka molar mass = 48 + 10 = 58 g/mol ✅. 0.580 g = 0.010 mol C₄H₁₀ → 0.040 mol CO₂ (1.760 g) aur 0.050 mol H₂O (0.900 g) ✅.

### Example 5. Hydrated Salt ka Formula Nikaalna (Epsom Salt, MgSO₄·xH₂O)

**Sawaal:** Ek hydrated magnesium sulphate salt (MgSO₄·xH₂O) ke analysis mein **48.78% anhydrous MgSO₄** aur **51.22% paani (H₂O)** mila. x ka maan aur poora formula nikaalo. *(Mg = 24, S = 32, O = 16, H = 1)*

**Hal:**
- Molar masses: MgSO₄ = 24 + 32 + 64 = **120 g/mol**; H₂O = **18 g/mol**.
- 100 g salt mein:
  - Moles of MgSO₄ = 48.78 ÷ 120 = **0.4065 mol**
  - Moles of H₂O = 51.22 ÷ 18 = **2.8456 mol**
- Ratio H₂O : MgSO₄ = 2.8456 ÷ 0.4065 = **7.00**!
- Isliye x = **7**, aur formula **MgSO₄·7H₂O** hai!

**Jaanch:** MgSO₄·7H₂O ka total mass = 120 + 7(18) = 120 + 126 = 246 g/mol. % H₂O = (126 ÷ 246) × 100 = 51.22% ✅.

## Part C: Gases ka Molar Volume (STP) aur Vapour Density (VD)

Gases ko tolne ke bajay unka volume naapna aasaan hota hai! Avogadro's Law se humein do behad kaam ke shortcuts milte hain:
1. **Molar Volume at STP:**
   - **Old STP (0 °C = 273.15 K, 1 atm):** 1 mol of any ideal gas = **22.4 L**
   - **New IUPAC STP (0 °C = 273.15 K, 1 bar):** 1 mol of any ideal gas = **22.7 L**
   *(Sawaal mein jo STP value di ho — 22.4 L ya 22.7 L — wahi lagao; agar na di ho to classic 22.4 L/mol use karke bracket mein likh do!)*
2. **Vapour Density (VD):** Kisi gas ka density ÷ H₂ gas ka density (same T, P par):
   > **Molar Mass (M) = 2 × Vapour Density (VD)**

### Example 6. Vapour Density aur STP Volume ka Sawaal

**Sawaal:**
(a) Ek gaseous organic compound ka empirical formula **CH₂** hai aur uski **Vapour Density = 21** hai. Uska molecular formula nikaalo.
(b) **5.60 L of O₂ gas** at STP (22.4 L/mol) mein kul kitne **moles**, kitne **molecules** aur kitne **oxygen atoms** honge?

**Hal:**
- (a) Molar mass M = 2 × VD = 2 × 21 = **42 g/mol**.
  - Empirical formula mass (CH₂) = 12 + 2 = **14 g/mol**.
  - n = 42 ÷ 14 = **3** → **Molecular Formula = (CH₂)₃ = C₃H₆** (propene).
- (b) n(O₂) = 5.60 ÷ 22.4 = **0.25 mol**.
  - O₂ molecules = 0.25 × 6.022 × 10²³ = **1.5055 × 10²³ molecules**.
  - Har O₂ molecule mein 2 O atoms → O atoms = 2 × 1.5055 × 10²³ = **3.011 × 10²³ atoms**.

**Jaanch:** C₃H₆ = 36 + 6 = 42 g/mol = 2 × 21 ✅; 0.25 × 22.4 = 5.60 L ✅.

## Aam galtiyan (bachke rehna)

1. **Galti 1: Isotopes ka seedha (unweighted) average le lena.**
   Boron ke ¹⁰B (10.013 u) aur ¹¹B (11.009 u) ka average nikaalte waqt ek student ne (10.013 + 11.009) ÷ 2 = 10.51 u likh diya!
   *Sahi baat:* Dono isotopes barabar (50%-50%) nahi hain! ¹¹B 80.1% hai, isliye hamesha **abundance-weighted average** (p₁m₁ + p₂m₂) ÷ 100 nikala jaata hai, jisse 10.81 u aata hai.

2. **Galti 2: Combustion analysis mein H₂O se H nikaalte waqt 1/18 se guna kar dena.**
   Ek student ne 0.270 g H₂O mein H ka mass 0.270 × (1 / 18) = 0.015 g nikaal diya!
   *Sahi baat:* Ek H₂O molecule mein **DO (2) H atoms** hote hain (mass = 2 g, kul 18 g mein). Isliye H ka mass hamesha **m_H₂O × (2 / 18)** = 0.030 g hota hai!

3. **Galti 3: Combustion analysis mein Oxygen ka mass CO₂ aur H₂O ke oxygen ko jodkar nikaal dena.**
   CO₂ aur H₂O mein jo oxygen aayi hai, uska bada hissa **bahar se di gayi excess O₂ gas** se aaya hai!
   *Sahi baat:* Sample ka apna Oxygen hamesha **difference** se nikaalo: m_O = m_sample − (m_C + m_H).

4. **Galti 4: Multiplier n nikaalte waqt molar mass aur empirical mass ko ulta rakh dena.**
   n = Empirical mass ÷ Molar mass rakhne par fraction (jaise 1/3) aa jaata hai.
   *Sahi baat:* Molecular formula hamesha empirical formula ka whole-number multiple (1, 2, 3...) hota hai, isliye hamesha **n = Molar mass ÷ Empirical formula mass** ≥ 1 hota hai.

## Quick Recap

- **1 u = 1.66056 × 10⁻²⁴ g** (¹²C ke 1/12वें hisse ka mass); **Nₐ = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹**.
- **Average Atomic Mass** = (p₁m₁ + p₂m₂ + ...) ÷ 100; reverse mein x% aur (100 − x)% maankar abundance nikalti hai.
- **Combustion Analysis:** m_C = m_CO₂ × (12/44), m_H = m_H₂O × (2/18), aur m_O = m_sample − (m_C + m_H).
- **Hydrated salt (Salt·xH₂O):** x = moles of H₂O ÷ moles of anhydrous salt.

## Practice Questions

1. Naturally occurring chlorine consists of **75.77% ³⁵Cl** (atomic mass 34.97 u) and **24.23% ³⁷Cl** (atomic mass 36.97 u). Calculate the average atomic mass of chlorine to two decimal places.
2. Bromine has two naturally occurring isotopes, **⁷⁹Br** (mass 79.00 u) and **⁸¹Br** (mass 81.00 u). If the average atomic mass of bromine is **79.90 u**, find the percentage abundance of each isotope.

4. Complete combustion of **0.460 g** of an organic compound containing C, H and O yields **0.880 g of CO₂** and **0.540 g of H₂O**. If its molar mass is **46 g/mol**, determine its empirical and molecular formulae.
5. Complete combustion of **0.720 g** of a pure hydrocarbon gives **2.200 g of CO₂** and **1.080 g of H₂O**. Its molar mass is **72 g/mol**. Find its empirical and molecular formulae.
6. A **0.550 g** sample of a C–H–O compound on complete combustion gives **1.320 g of CO₂** and **0.270 g of H₂O**. If its molar mass is **110 g/mol**, find its empirical and molecular formulae.
7. Hydrated zinc sulphate (ZnSO₄·xH₂O) contains **56.10% anhydrous ZnSO₄** and **43.90% water** by mass. Find the value of x and write the complete formula. (Zn = 65, S = 32, O = 16, H = 1)
8. Hydrated barium chloride (BaCl₂·xH₂O; paper problem only, barium salts are poisonous) loses **14.75%** of its mass as water vapour when heated completely to anhydrous BaCl₂. Find x. (Ba = 137, Cl = 35.5, H = 1, O = 16)
9. Using 1 u = 1.66056 × 10⁻²⁴ g, calculate the mass in grams of (a) **one single atom of ¹²C** (12 u), and (b) **one single molecule of NH₃** (17 u).
10. State whether each statement is True or False, and correct the false ones:
    (a) In combustion analysis of a C–H–O compound, the mass of oxygen in the sample is found by adding the oxygen present in CO₂ and H₂O.
    (b) The average atomic mass of an element with two isotopes always lies between the masses of the two isotopes.
    (c) If a compound's empirical formula is C₂H₃O and its molar mass is 86 g/mol, its molecular formula is C₄H₆O₂.
    (d) One mole of O₂ gas and one mole of O₃ gas contain the same number of molecules, but different numbers of oxygen atoms.
11. An organic compound has the empirical formula **CH₂O** and a **vapour density of 90**. Determine its molar mass and molecular formula.
12. Calculate the number of **moles, molecules and total atoms** present in **11.2 L of NH₃ gas** at STP (molar volume = 22.4 L/mol).

### Answers

1. Average atomic mass of Cl = (75.77 × 34.97 + 24.23 × 36.97) ÷ 100
   = (2649.6769 + 895.7831) ÷ 100 = 3545.46 ÷ 100 = **35.45 u**.
   Jaanch: 35.45 u, 34.97 ke zyada paas hai kyunki ³⁵Cl ~76% hai ✅.

2. Maan lo ⁷⁹Br = x%, ⁸¹Br = (100 − x)%.
   [79x + 81(100 − x)] ÷ 100 = 79.90 → 79x + 8100 − 81x = 7990 → −2x = −110 → x = **55.0%**.
   Isliye **⁷⁹Br = 55.0%** aur **⁸¹Br = 45.0%**.
   Jaanch: (55 × 79 + 45 × 81) ÷ 100 = (4345 + 3645) ÷ 100 = 79.90 u ✅.

3. Average atomic mass of Ne = (90.0 × 20.00 + 1.0 × 21.00 + 9.0 × 22.00) ÷ 100
   = (1800 + 21 + 198) ÷ 100 = 2019 ÷ 100 = **20.19 u**.
   Jaanch: 90% + 1% + 9% = 100%, aur 20.19 u, 20 ke bahut paas hai ✅.

4. m_C = 0.880 × (12/44) = **0.240 g** → n_C = 0.240 ÷ 12 = **0.020 mol**.
   m_H = 0.540 × (2/18) = **0.060 g** → n_H = 0.060 ÷ 1 = **0.060 mol**.
   m_O = 0.460 − (0.240 + 0.060) = **0.160 g** → n_O = 0.160 ÷ 16 = **0.010 mol**.
   Sabse chhote 0.010 se bhaag: C : H : O = **2 : 6 : 1** → Empirical formula = **C₂H₆O** (mass = 24 + 6 + 16 = 46 g/mol).
   n = 46 ÷ 46 = 1 → Molecular formula = **C₂H₆O**.
   Jaanch: 0.460 g = 0.010 mol C₂H₆O → 0.020 mol CO₂ (0.880 g) aur 0.030 mol H₂O (0.540 g) ✅.

5. m_C = 2.200 × (12/44) = **0.600 g** → n_C = 0.600 ÷ 12 = **0.050 mol**.
   m_H = 1.080 × (2/18) = **0.120 g** → n_H = 0.120 ÷ 1 = **0.120 mol**.
   (0.600 + 0.120 = 0.720 g → koi O nahi.)
   Ratio C : H = 0.050 : 0.120 = 1 : 2.4 → dono ko **5** se guna karo → **5 : 12**.
   Empirical formula = **C₅H₁₂** (mass = 60 + 12 = 72 g/mol); n = 72 ÷ 72 = 1 → Molecular formula = **C₅H₁₂**.
   Jaanch: 0.720 g = 0.010 mol C₅H₁₂ → 0.050 mol CO₂ (2.200 g) aur 0.060 mol H₂O (1.080 g) ✅.

6. m_C = 1.320 × (12/44) = **0.360 g** → n_C = 0.360 ÷ 12 = **0.030 mol**.
   m_H = 0.270 × (2/18) = **0.030 g** → n_H = 0.030 ÷ 1 = **0.030 mol**.
   m_O = 0.550 − (0.360 + 0.030) = **0.160 g** → n_O = 0.160 ÷ 16 = **0.010 mol**.
   Ratio C : H : O = 0.030 : 0.030 : 0.010 = **3 : 3 : 1** → Empirical formula = **C₃H₃O** (mass = 36 + 3 + 16 = 55 g/mol).
   n = 110 ÷ 55 = **2** → Molecular formula = **C₆H₆O₂**.
   Jaanch: C₆H₆O₂ = 72 + 6 + 32 = 110 g/mol; 0.550 g = 0.005 mol → 0.030 mol CO₂ (1.320 g) aur 0.015 mol H₂O (0.270 g) ✅.

7. Molar masses: ZnSO₄ = 65 + 32 + 64 = **161 g/mol**; H₂O = **18 g/mol**.
   100 g mein: n(ZnSO₄) = 56.10 ÷ 161 = **0.3484 mol**; n(H₂O) = 43.90 ÷ 18 = **2.4389 mol**.
   x = 2.4389 ÷ 0.3484 = **7.00** → Formula = **ZnSO₄·7H₂O**.
   Jaanch: 161 + 7(18) = 287 g/mol; (126 ÷ 287) × 100 = 43.90% ✅.

8. Anhydrous BaCl₂ = 100 − 14.75 = **85.25%**. Molar mass BaCl₂ = 137 + 71 = **208 g/mol**.
   n(BaCl₂) = 85.25 ÷ 208 = **0.4099 mol**; n(H₂O) = 14.75 ÷ 18 = **0.8194 mol**.
   x = 0.8194 ÷ 0.4099 = **2.00** → Formula = **BaCl₂·2H₂O**.
   Jaanch: 208 + 36 = 244 g/mol; (36 ÷ 244) × 100 = 14.75% ✅.

9. (a) 1 atom ¹²C = 12 × 1.66056 × 10⁻²⁴ g = **1.9927 × 10⁻²³ g**.
   (b) 1 molecule NH₃ = 17 × 1.66056 × 10⁻²⁴ g = **2.8230 × 10⁻²³ g**.
   Jaanch: 1.9927 × 10⁻²³ × 6.022 × 10²³ = 12.00 g (1 mol ¹²C) ✅.

10. (a) **False** ❌. Oxygen ka mass sample ke kul mass mein se C aur H ka mass ghata kar (m_sample − m_C − m_H) nikala jaata hai.
    (b) **True** ✅. Weighted average hamesha sabse chhote aur sabse bade isotope mass ke beech hota hai.
    (c) **True** ✅. C₂H₃O ka mass = 24 + 3 + 16 = 43; n = 86 ÷ 43 = 2 → C₄H₆O₂.
    (d) **True** ✅. Dono mein 6.022 × 10²³ molecules hain, par O₂ mein 2 Nₐ aur O₃ mein 3 Nₐ oxygen atoms hain.

11. Molar mass M = 2 × VD = 2 × 90 = **180 g/mol**.
    Empirical formula mass (CH₂O) = 12 + 2 + 16 = 30 g/mol.
    n = 180 ÷ 30 = **6** → **Molecular Formula = C₆H₁₂O₆** (glucose).
    Jaanch: 6 × 30 = 180 = 2 × 90 ✅.

12. n(NH₃) = 11.2 ÷ 22.4 = **0.50 mol**.
    Molecules = 0.50 × 6.022 × 10²³ = **3.011 × 10²³ molecules**.
    Har NH₃ mein 4 atoms (1 N + 3 H) → Total atoms = 4 × 3.011 × 10²³ = **1.2044 × 10²⁴ atoms**.
    Jaanch: 0.50 mol × 4 = 2.00 mol atoms = 1.2044 × 10²⁴ atoms ✅.
