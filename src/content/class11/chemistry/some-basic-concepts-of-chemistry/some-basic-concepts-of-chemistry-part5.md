## Part A: Poore Chapter Ka Master Pul (Sab Raaste Mole Se Guzarte Hain)

Koi bhi multi-step chemistry problem dekh kar ghabraao mat — bas ye dekho ki **kahan se shuru karna hai** aur **moles tak kaise pahunchna hai**:

```
   Mass % + Density -----> [ Molarity (M) & Molality (m) ]
                                       :
                                   n = M × V(L)
                                       :
                                       v
   Combustion (CO₂, H₂O) -> [  M O L E S  (n)  ] <---- Mass / Molar Mass (m ÷ M)
                                       :
                         Balanced Equation & Limiting Reagent
                                       :
                                       v
                        Product Mass / % Yield / % Purity
```

> **Figure 5.1** – Poore chapter ka master pul: chahe data combustion analysis ka ho, solution molarity (M × V) ka ho, ya mass ka — sabse pehle **moles (n)** nikaalo, phir balanced equation aur limiting reagent lagao, aur ant mein maanga gaya mass, purity ya yield nikaalo.

## Part B: Shraddha Ke Teen Multi-Concept Lab Dossiers (Solved Examples)

### Example 1 (Dossier 1). Combustion Analysis + Limiting Reagent Ek Saath *(NaOH reaction sirf padhne ke liye)*

**Sawaal:**
(a) Ek organic monobasic acid (C, H, O wala) ke **0.600 g** sample ke complete combustion se **0.880 g CO₂** aur **0.360 g H₂O** mila. Agar iska molar mass **60 g/mol** hai, to iska molecular formula nikaalo.
(b) Ab isi acid (CH₃COOH) ke **30.0 g** ko **16.0 g NaOH** (molar mass 40 g/mol) ke saath reaction karaya gaya: CH₃COOH + NaOH → CH₃COONa + H₂O. Kaun limiting reagent hai, kitne gram CH₃COONa (molar mass 82 g/mol) banega, aur excess reactant kitna bachega?

**Hal:**
- **(a) Formula (Topic 3 ka tool):**
  - m_C = 0.880 × (12 ÷ 44) = **0.240 g** → n_C = 0.240 ÷ 12 = **0.020 mol**
  - m_H = 0.360 × (2 ÷ 18) = **0.040 g** → n_H = 0.040 ÷ 1 = **0.040 mol**
  - m_O = 0.600 − (0.240 + 0.040) = **0.320 g** → n_O = 0.320 ÷ 16 = **0.020 mol**
  - Ratio C : H : O = 0.020 : 0.040 : 0.020 = **1 : 2 : 1** → Empirical formula = **CH₂O** (mass = 30 g/mol).
  - n = 60 ÷ 30 = **2** → Molecular formula = **C₂H₄O₂** (acetic acid, CH₃COOH).
- **(b) Limiting Reagent (Topic 4 ka tool):**
  - n(CH₃COOH) = 30.0 ÷ 60 = **0.50 mol**; n(NaOH) = 16.0 ÷ 40 = **0.40 mol**.
  - Balanced equation mein ratio 1 : 1 hai → chhota mole **0.40 mol (NaOH)** hai, isliye **Limiting Reagent = NaOH**.
  - CH₃COONa banega = 0.40 mol → Mass = 0.40 × 82 = **32.8 g**.
  - Bacha hua CH₃COOH = 0.50 − 0.40 = 0.10 mol → Mass = 0.10 × 60 = **6.0 g**.

**Jaanch:** Part (a): 0.600 g = 0.010 mol C₂H₄O₂ → 0.020 mol CO₂ (0.880 g) + 0.020 mol H₂O (0.360 g) ✅. Part (b): 0.40 mol H₂O = 7.2 g; kul mass pehle 30.0 + 16.0 = 46.0 g; baad mein 32.8 + 7.2 + 6.0 = 46.0 g ✅.

### Example 2 (Dossier 2). Impure Limestone + Molar HCl Solution → Percentage Purity *(sirf teacher demo / paper calculation)*

**Sawaal:** Ek **12.5 g impure limestone** sample (jismein CaCO₃ ke saath bina reaction karne wali mitti mili hai) ko poori tarah reaction karane ke liye **100 mL of 2.0 M HCl** laga:
CaCO₃(s) + 2HCl(aq) → CaCl₂(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g)↑
Sample mein shuddh CaCO₃ ka **Percentage Purity** nikaalo. *(Ca = 40, C = 12, O = 16)*

**Hal:**
- **Step 1 (HCl ke moles nikaalo — Volume ko LITRE mein badlo!):**
  - V = 100 mL = **0.100 L**.
  - n(HCl) = M × V(L) = 2.0 mol/L × 0.100 L = **0.20 mol HCl**.
- **Step 2 (Balanced equation se shuddh CaCO₃ ke moles):**
  - 2 mol HCl se 1 mol CaCO₃ reaction karta hai.
  - n(CaCO₃) = 0.20 ÷ 2 = **0.10 mol**.
- **Step 3 (Shuddh CaCO₃ ka mass aur % Purity):**
  - Molar mass of CaCO₃ = 40 + 12 + 48 = **100 g/mol**.
  - Shuddh CaCO₃ ka mass = 0.10 × 100 = **10.0 g**.
  - Percentage Purity = (10.0 ÷ 12.5) × 100 = **80.0%**.

**Jaanch:** 12.5 g ka 80.0% = 10.0 g = 0.10 mol CaCO₃ → 0.20 mol HCl chahiye = 2.0 M × 0.100 L ✅.

### Example 3 (Dossier 3). Mass % + Density se Molarity (M), Molality (m) aur Mole Fraction (χ) Ek Saath!

**Sawaal:** Ek aqueous urea solution (NH₂CONH₂, molar mass 60 g/mol) **10.0% w/w** hai aur uski density **1.02 g/mL** hai. Is solution ki (a) Molarity (M), (b) Molality (m) aur (c) Urea ka Mole Fraction (χ_urea) nikaalo.

**Hal:**
**Golden trick: 100 g solution maano!**
- 100 g solution mein: Solute (urea) = **10.0 g**; Solvent (paani) = 100 − 10.0 = **90.0 g = 0.090 kg**.
- Moles of urea = 10.0 ÷ 60 = **1/6 mol ≈ 0.1667 mol**.
- Moles of water = 90.0 ÷ 18 = **5.00 mol**.
- **(a) Molarity (M):**
  - 100 g solution ka Volume = Mass ÷ Density = 100 g ÷ 1.02 g/mL = 98.04 mL = **0.09804 L**.
  - M = n_urea ÷ V(L) = (1/6) ÷ (100 / 1020) = 1020 ÷ 600 = **1.70 M** (exact!).
- **(b) Molality (m):**
  - m = n_urea ÷ W_solvent(kg) = (1/6) ÷ 0.090 = 10 ÷ 5.4 = **1.85 mol/kg (1.85 m)**.
- **(c) Mole Fraction (χ_urea):**
  - χ_urea = (1/6) ÷ (1/6 + 5) = 1 ÷ 31 = **0.0323** (aur χ_water = 30 ÷ 31 = **0.9677**).

**Jaanch:** M = (10 × % w/w × d) ÷ M_solute shortcut se: (10 × 10.0 × 1.02) ÷ 60 = 102 ÷ 60 = 1.70 M ✅. χ_urea + χ_water = 1/31 + 30/31 = 1 ✅.

### Example 4 (Dossier 4). Two-Step Sequential Reaction + STP Gas Volume *(sirf padhne ke liye)*

**Sawaal:** Industrial sulphuric acid banane mein do steps lagte hain:
- Step 1: S(s) + O₂(g) → SO₂(g)
- Step 2: 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2SO₃(g)
Agar **16.0 g shuddh sulphur (32 g/mol)** ko excess O₂ mein chalaya jaaye (100% yield maankar), to Step 2 ke ant mein STP (22.4 L/mol) par kitne **litre SO₃ gas** banegi?

**Hal:**
- n(S) = 16.0 ÷ 32 = **0.50 mol**.
- Step 1 (1 S → 1 SO₂): **0.50 mol SO₂** bana.
- Step 2 (2 SO₂ → 2 SO₃, matlab 1 : 1): **0.50 mol SO₃** bana!
- STP Volume = 0.50 × 22.4 = **11.2 L SO₃(g)**!

**Jaanch:** Atom conservation (S): 0.50 mol S → 0.50 mol SO₃ = 11.2 L at STP ✅.

## Aam galtiyan (bachke rehna)

1. **Galti 1: Solution stoichiometry mein n = M × V lagate waqt volume ko mL mein hi rakh dena.**
   Example 2 mein agar student n(HCl) = 2.0 × 100 = 200 mol likh de, to uska CaCO₃ ka mass 10,000 g (10 kg!) aa jaayega jabki poora patthar hi 12.5 g ka tha!
   *Sahi baat:* Molarity mol/L mein hoti hai, isliye **n = M × V(L)** mein mL ko hamesha 1000 se bhaag dekar litre (0.100 L) banao! (Ya seedha millimoles, mmol, mein kaam karo.)

2. **Galti 2: Impure sample ke kul mass ko shuddh reactant maan kar stoichiometry laga dena.**
   Example 2 mein 12.5 g patthar mein mitti (impurities) bhi hai jo HCl se reaction nahi karti. Agar tum 12.5 ÷ 100 = 0.125 mol CaCO₃ maan loge, to HCl ka data bekaar ho jaayega aur purity kabhi nahi niklegi!
   *Sahi baat:* Impurity wale sawaalon mein hamesha **doosre pure reactant (jaise HCl) ya bane product (CO₂)** se ulta chal kar asli shuddh reactant ka mass nikaalo, aur phir kul sample mass se bhaag do!

## Quick Recap

- Har multi-step lab problem mein **Moles (n)** beech ka pul hai: mass se m ÷ M, solution se M × V(L), aur combustion se CO₂/H₂O ke ratios se moles tak pahuncho.
- **Percentage Purity** = (Asli shuddh mass ÷ Kul impure sample mass) × 100.
- **Mass % + Density** wale sawaalon mein **100 g solution** maankar M, m aur χ teeno ek saath aasani se nikal jaate hain (shortcut: **M = 10 × (% w/w) × d ÷ M_solute**).

## Practice Questions

1. A **15.0 g** sample of impure magnesium carbonate (MgCO₃, molar mass 84 g/mol) reacts completely with **150 mL of 2.0 M HCl** according to MgCO₃(s) + 2HCl(aq) → MgCl₂(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g). (Teacher demo calculation.) Calculate the percentage purity of the MgCO₃ sample.
2. An aqueous solution of NaOH (molar mass 40 g/mol; paper calculation, corrosive) is **20.0% w/w** and has a density of **1.20 g/mL**. Calculate both its **Molarity (M)** and its **Molality (m)**.
3. Complete combustion of **0.880 g** of an organic ester (C, H, O) produces **1.760 g of CO₂** and **0.720 g of H₂O**. Its molar mass is **88 g/mol**. Determine its empirical and molecular formulae.
4. Aluminium reacts with dilute HCl (teacher demo; H₂ gas is flammable): 2Al(s) + 6HCl(aq) → 2AlCl₃(aq) + 3H₂(g). If **10.8 g of Al** (27 g/mol) is added to **200 mL of 3.0 M HCl**, identify the limiting reagent, calculate the mass of H₂ gas produced, and find the mass of excess Al left unreacted.
5. Gallium has two naturally occurring isotopes: **⁶⁹Ga** (68.93 u) and **⁷¹Ga** (70.93 u). If the average atomic mass of gallium is **69.73 u**, calculate the percentage abundance of each isotope.
6. A lab bottle shows a solvent density of **0.789 g/cm³** at **30.0 °C**. Using dimensional analysis, express this density in **kg/m³** (to 3 significant figures), and convert **30.0 °C** to both **°F** and **K**.
7. Two oxides of nitrogen (paper problem only; toxic gases) are analysed: Oxide 1 has **1.40 g of N** combined with **1.60 g of O**; Oxide 2 has **1.40 g of N** combined with **3.20 g of O**. Which Law of Chemical Combination is verified, and what is the simple ratio of oxygen masses?
8. Calculate the answer to the proper number of significant figures:
   (a) (12.45 + 3.2) ÷ 2.50
   (b) Round 7.85 and 7.75 each to 2 significant figures using the even-digit rule.
9. A student dissolves **9.00 g of glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆, 180 g/mol)** in **180 g of water (18 g/mol)**. Calculate (a) the molality of the solution, and (b) the mole fraction of glucose.
10. What volume of **6.0 M H₂SO₄** stock solution (teacher demo only; always add acid slowly to water) is needed to prepare **500 mL of 0.30 M H₂SO₄**?
11. When **20.0 g of impure calcium oxide (CaO, 56 g/mol)** reacts with excess water (CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂, 74 g/mol), **22.2 g of pure Ca(OH)₂** is obtained. Assuming 100% yield for the pure CaO, calculate the percentage purity of the CaO sample.
12. State whether each statement is True or False, and correct the false ones:
    (a) In the formula M = (10 × % w/w × d) ÷ M_solute, the density d must be in g/mL.
    (b) Molarity and Molality are always numerically equal for every aqueous solution.
    (c) If a 10.0 g impure sample contains 8.5 g of pure reactant, its percentage purity is 85.0%.
    (d) In solution stoichiometry, 250 mL of 0.40 M HCl contains 0.10 mol of HCl.

### Answers

1. n(HCl) = 2.0 mol/L × 0.150 L = **0.30 mol**.
   Equation se 2 mol HCl → 1 mol MgCO₃, isliye n(MgCO₃) = 0.30 ÷ 2 = **0.15 mol**.
   Shuddh MgCO₃ ka mass = 0.15 × 84 = **12.6 g**.
   Percentage purity = (12.6 ÷ 15.0) × 100 = **84.0%**.
   Jaanch: 84% of 15.0 g = 12.6 g = 0.15 mol MgCO₃ → 0.30 mol HCl ✅.

2. 100 g solution maano: NaOH = 20.0 g (20.0 ÷ 40 = **0.50 mol**); Solvent (paani) = 80.0 g = **0.080 kg**.
   - Volume of 100 g solution = 100 ÷ 1.20 = 83.33 mL = **0.08333 L**.
   - **Molarity M** = 0.50 ÷ (100 / 1200) = **6.0 M** (shortcut: 10 × 20.0 × 1.20 ÷ 40 = 6.0 M).
   - **Molality m** = 0.50 ÷ 0.080 = **6.25 mol/kg (6.25 m)**.
   Jaanch: 6.25 × 0.080 = 0.50 mol = 20.0 g NaOH ✅.

3. m_C = 1.760 × (12/44) = **0.480 g** → n_C = 0.480 ÷ 12 = **0.040 mol**.
   m_H = 0.720 × (2/18) = **0.080 g** → n_H = 0.080 ÷ 1 = **0.080 mol**.
   m_O = 0.880 − (0.480 + 0.080) = **0.320 g** → n_O = 0.320 ÷ 16 = **0.020 mol**.
   Ratio C : H : O = **2 : 4 : 1** → Empirical formula = **C₂H₄O** (mass = 24 + 4 + 16 = 44 g/mol).
   n = 88 ÷ 44 = **2** → Molecular formula = **C₄H₈O₂**.
   Jaanch: 0.880 g = 0.010 mol C₄H₈O₂ → 0.040 mol CO₂ (1.760 g) + 0.040 mol H₂O (0.720 g) ✅.

4. n(Al) = 10.8 ÷ 27 = **0.40 mol**; n(HCl) = 3.0 × 0.200 = **0.60 mol**.
   Ratio (Moles ÷ Coeff): Al = 0.40 ÷ 2 = 0.20; HCl = 0.60 ÷ 6 = **0.10** (chhota!).
   - **Limiting reagent = HCl**.
   - H₂ moles = 0.10 × 3 = **0.30 mol** → Mass of H₂ = 0.30 × 2 = **0.60 g**.
   - Al kharch = 0.10 × 2 = 0.20 mol → Bacha Al = 0.40 − 0.20 = 0.20 mol = 0.20 × 27 = **5.4 g**.
   Jaanch: 0.20 mol Al ko 0.60 mol HCl chahiye (2 : 6 = 1 : 3) ✅.

5. Maan lo ⁶⁹Ga = x%, ⁷¹Ga = (100 − x)%.
   [68.93x + 70.93(100 − x)] ÷ 100 = 69.73 → −2.00x = 6973 − 7093 = −120 → x = **60.0%**.
   Isliye **⁶⁹Ga = 60.0%** aur **⁷¹Ga = 40.0%**.
   Jaanch: (60 × 68.93 + 40 × 70.93) ÷ 100 = (4135.8 + 2837.2) ÷ 100 = 69.73 u ✅.

6. Density = 0.789 (g/cm³) × (1 kg/1000 g) × (10⁶ cm³/1 m³) = **789 kg/m³** (3 sig figs).
   30.0 °C: °F = (9/5)(30.0) + 32 = **86.0 °F**; K = 30.0 + 273.15 = **303.15 K**.
   Jaanch: 789 ÷ 1000 = 0.789 g/cm³ aur (5/9)(86.0 − 32) = 30.0 °C ✅.

7. Nitrogen ka mass 1.40 g fixed hai; oxygen ka ratio = 1.60 : 3.20 = **1 : 2** → **Law of Multiple Proportions** (NO aur NO₂).
   Jaanch: 3.20 ÷ 1.60 = 2 ✅.

8. (a) Pehle bracket: 12.45 + 3.2 = 15.65 → 1 decimal place par **15.6** (even-digit rule: 6 even hai!).
   Ab 15.6 ÷ 2.50 = 6.24 → 3 sig figs mein **6.24** (agar bina intermediate rounding ke 15.65 ÷ 2.50 = 6.26 → 3 sig figs mein 6.26; dono tareeke teacher ko dikha sakte ho).
   (b) 7.85 → 8 even hai → **7.8**; 7.75 → 7 odd hai → **7.8**.
   Jaanch: Dono even digit 8 par ruke ✅.

9. n(glucose) = 9.00 ÷ 180 = **0.050 mol**; n(water) = 180 ÷ 18 = **10.0 mol**; W_solvent = 0.180 kg.
   (a) Molality m = 0.050 ÷ 0.180 = **0.278 mol/kg (0.278 m)**.
   (b) χ_glucose = 0.050 ÷ (0.050 + 10.0) = 0.050 ÷ 10.05 = **0.00498** (lagbhag 0.0050).
   Jaanch: 0.278 × 0.180 = 0.050 mol ✅.

10. M₁ V₁ = M₂ V₂ → 6.0 × V₁ = 0.30 × 500 → V₁ = 150 ÷ 6.0 = **25.0 mL** stock H₂SO₄ (ise dhyan se paani mein milakar 500 mL banaya jaata hai — teacher demo).
    Jaanch: 6.0 × 25 = 150 = 0.30 × 500 ✅.

11. n(Ca(OH)₂) = 22.2 ÷ 74 = **0.30 mol**.
    1 : 1 ratio se shuddh CaO = 0.30 mol = 0.30 × 56 = **16.8 g**.
    Percentage purity = (16.8 ÷ 20.0) × 100 = **84.0%**.
    Jaanch: 84% of 20.0 g = 16.8 g = 0.30 mol CaO → 0.30 mol Ca(OH)₂ = 22.2 g ✅.

12. (a) **True** ✅. Is shortcut mein density g/mL mein rakhi jaati hai.
    (b) **False** ❌. Molarity (per L solution) aur Molality (per kg solvent) aam taur par alag hoti hain; sirf bahut dilute aqueous solutions (jahan d ≈ 1 g/mL) mein lagbhag barabar dikhti hain.
    (c) **True** ✅. (8.5 ÷ 10.0) × 100 = 85.0%.
    (d) **True** ✅. 0.40 mol/L × 0.250 L = 0.10 mol.
