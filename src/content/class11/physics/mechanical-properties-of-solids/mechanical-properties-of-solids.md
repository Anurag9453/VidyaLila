# Elasticity, Plasticity, Stress & Strain

> **Pichle chapter se:** [Gravitation](../gravitation/gravitation-part6.md) mein humne planets aur satellites ka physics padha — aur Chapter 6 tak humne solids ko perfectly rigid (bina shape badle) maana tha. Par asli duniya mein har solid thoda stretch, compress, ya bend hota hai! Is chapter mein hum solids ke isi **Elastic Behavior** ko samjhenge!

---

## Lajpat ka Suspension-Bridge Cable Test

Lajpat ek suspension-bridge inspector hai. Ek din usne ek student se poocha: "Batao, **Steel** zyada elastic hai ya **Rubber**?" Student ne turant kaha, "Rubber, kyunki woh zyada khinchta hai!"

Lajpat muskura kar bole, "Physics mein **Elasticity** ka matlab zyada khinchna NAHI, balki **deforming force hatane par apna asli shape wapas paane ki taqat (restoring force)** hota hai! Same stretch ke liye steel rubber se hazaaron guna zyada restoring force lagata hai — isliye **Steel is MUCH more elastic than Rubber**!"

---

## Naye Shabd (Key Terms)

| Shabd | Matlab (Simple Hinglish) |
|---|---|
| Elasticity | Deforming force hatane par body ka apna original shape aur size wapas paane ka gun (jaise quartz, steel) |
| Plasticity | Deforming force hatane par shape wapas na aana — permanent deformation reh jana (jaise gili mitti, putty, wax) |
| Stress (σ) | Internal restoring force per unit cross-sectional area: **Stress = F / A** (SI unit: **N m⁻² = Pascal (Pa)**; dimension **[M L⁻¹ T⁻²]**) |
| Strain (ε) | Fractional change in dimension: **Change / Original** (jaise ΔL/L, ΔV/V, Δx/L) — **dimensionless & unitless!** |

---

## 1. Three Types of Stress & Strain

| Type | Cause (Deforming Force) | Stress Formula | Strain Formula |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Longitudinal** (Tensile / Compressive) | Length ke along pull ya push | **F / A** | **ΔL / L** |
| **Shearing** (Tangential) | Top surface ke parallel tangential force (shape tilt hota hai, volume same!) | **F_t / A** | **θ ≈ Δx / L** (radians) |
| **Hydraulic / Volume** | Chaaron taraf se uniform fluid pressure P | **P = ΔF / A** | **- ΔV / V** |

## Solved Examples

### Example 1: Tensile Stress & Strain in a Bridge Wire

A steel wire of length 4 m and cross-sectional area 2 × 10⁻⁶ m² is stretched by 2 mm (2 × 10⁻³ m) when a 40 kg load hangs from it (g = 10 m/s²). Find the tensile stress and tensile strain.

**Solution:**
- Force F = m g = 40 × 10 = 400 N.
- Tensile Stress = F / A = 400 / (2 × 10⁻⁶) = **2 × 10⁸ N/m² (200 MPa)**.
- Tensile Strain = ΔL / L = (2 × 10⁻³) / 4 = **5 × 10⁻⁴** (no unit!).

Jaanch:
- Jaanch: 400 / 2 = 200 × 10⁶ = 2 × 10⁸ Pa; 2/4 = 0.5 × 10⁻³ = 5 × 10⁻⁴ ✅

---

### Example 2: Shearing Stress & Shearing Strain on a Rubber Block

A rubber cube of side 10 cm (0.10 m) has its bottom face fixed. A tangential force of 500 N applied to its top face shifts the top face horizontally by 2 mm (0.002 m). Find the shearing stress and shearing strain.

**Solution:**
- Area of top face A = 0.10 × 0.10 = 0.01 m².
- Shearing Stress = 500 / 0.01 = **5 × 10⁴ N/m² (50 kPa)**.
- Shearing Strain θ = Δx / L = 0.002 / 0.10 = **0.02** (rad).

Jaanch:
- Jaanch: 500 / 0.01 = 50,000 Pa; 2 mm / 100 mm = 0.02 ✅

---

### Example 3: Hydraulic Volume Strain

A solid metal sphere of volume 2000 cm³ is lowered into a deep ocean trench where water pressure decreases its volume by 1.5 cm³. Find the magnitude of its volume strain.

**Solution:**
Volume Strain = |ΔV| / V = 1.5 / 2000 = **7.5 × 10⁻⁴**.

Jaanch:
- Jaanch: Same units (cm³ / cm³) cancel cleanly => dimensionless ✅

---

### Example 4: Breaking Force vs Wire Diameter

A wire of radius r can support a maximum load (breaking force) of 400 N without snapping. What maximum load can another wire of the **same material** with radius 2r support?

**Solution:**
Breaking Stress (σ_max = F_max / A) is a **material property** (independent of length or thickness)!
Since A = π r² => F_max = σ_max × π r² ∝ r².
Doubling radius increases area by 2² = 4× => **F_max' = 4 × 400 = 1600 N**.

Jaanch:
- Jaanch: Area becomes 4×, so breaking force becomes 4× ✅

## Aam Galtiyan (3 Common Mistakes)

### Galti 1: Rubber ko steel se zyada elastic bol dena
- **Galat:** Rubber aasani se khinch jata hai toh woh zyada elastic hai!
- **Sahi:** Elasticity = restoring force ka virodh (stiffness). **Steel is much more elastic than rubber!**

### Galti 2: Strain ki unit "m" ya "mm" likh dena
- **Galat:** Strain = 5 × 10⁻⁴ m.
- **Sahi:** Strain = ΔL / L (metre / metre) — yeh **pure dimensionless, unitless number** hai!

### Galti 3: Breaking stress ko wire ki length ya thickness par depend maanna
- **Galat:** Lambi taar jaldi tootegi toh uska breaking stress kam hoga!
- **Sahi:** **Breaking Stress** sirf material par depend karta hai (length/area par nahi); haan, **Breaking Force = Breaking Stress × Area** area par depend karta hai!

---

## Quick Recap

- **Stress:** F / A (Pa = N/m²); **Strain:** ΔL/L, Δx/L, ΔV/V (unitless!).
- **Elasticity:** Ability to regain original shape (Steel > Rubber).
- **Breaking Stress:** Pure material property; **Breaking Force ∝ Area (r²)**.

## Practice Questions

*(Take g = 10 m/s² unless stated otherwise.)*

### Question 1
A wire of cross-sectional area 4 mm² (4 × 10⁻⁶ m²) supports a load of 80 N. Find the tensile stress in the wire.

**Solution:**
Stress = 80 / (4 × 10⁻⁶) = **2 × 10⁷ N/m² (20 MPa)**.

Jaanch:
- Jaanch: 80 / 4 = 20 × 10⁶ = 2 × 10⁷ Pa ✅

---

### Question 2
A 2.5 m long copper wire stretches by 1.25 mm under a load. Find the longitudinal strain.

**Solution:**
Strain = (1.25 × 10⁻³) / 2.5 = **5 × 10⁻⁴**.

Jaanch:
- Jaanch: 1.25 / 2.5 = 0.5 => 0.5 × 10⁻³ = 5 × 10⁻⁴ ✅

---

### Question 3
A steel wire of breaking stress 8 × 10⁸ N/m² has cross-sectional area 5 × 10⁻⁶ m². Find the maximum mass it can support safely without breaking.

**Solution:**
F_max = σ_max × A = (8 × 10⁸)(5 × 10⁻⁶) = 4000 N.
Max mass m = F_max / g = 4000 / 10 = **400 kg**.

Jaanch:
- Jaanch: 400 kg × 10 = 4000 N; 4000 / (5×10⁻⁶) = 8 × 10⁸ Pa ✅

---

### Question 4
A metal plate of thickness 5 cm and top area 0.04 m² is sheared by a tangential force of 2000 N, shifting the top face by 0.5 mm. Find the shearing stress and shearing strain.

**Solution:**
- Shearing stress = 2000 / 0.04 = **5 × 10⁴ N/m²**.
- Shearing strain = 0.5 mm / 50 mm = **0.01**.

Jaanch:
- Jaanch: 0.5 / 50 = 1/100 = 0.01 ✅

---

### Question 5
A wire is cut into two equal halves. How do its breaking stress and breaking force change?

**Solution:**
Both material and cross-sectional area A remain unchanged => **Both breaking stress and breaking force remain UNCHANGED!**

Jaanch:
- Jaanch: Neither σ_max nor A depends on length L ✅

---

### Question 6
A hollow concrete pillar has outer area 0.5 m² and inner hollow area 0.3 m². If it supports a compressive load of 4 × 10⁵ N, find the compressive stress in the concrete.

**Solution:**
Effective material area A = 0.5 - 0.3 = 0.2 m².
Stress = (4 × 10⁵) / 0.2 = **2 × 10⁶ N/m² (2 MPa)**.

Jaanch:
- Jaanch: Only the actual solid concrete area carries the load ✅

---

### Question 7
A wire of diameter 2 mm can hold a maximum weight of 100 N. What maximum weight can a wire of the same material with diameter 6 mm hold?

**Solution:**
Diameter becomes 3× => Area becomes 3² = 9× => **F_max = 9 × 100 = 900 N**.

Jaanch:
- Jaanch: F_max ∝ d² => 9 × 100 = 900 N ✅

---

### Question 8
A 10 litre (0.01 m³) block of rubber is compressed in a hydraulic chamber until its volume decreases by 5 cm³ (5 × 10⁻⁶ m³). Find the volume strain.

**Solution:**
Volume strain = (5 × 10⁻⁶) / (10⁻²) = **5 × 10⁻⁴**.

Jaanch:
- Jaanch: 5 cm³ / 10,000 cm³ = 5 × 10⁻⁴ ✅

---

### Question 9
Find the minimum cross-sectional area of a steel cable (breaking stress 5 × 10⁸ Pa) needed to lift a 2000 kg elevator with a safety factor of 5 (working stress = breaking stress / 5).

**Solution:**
Allowed working stress = (5 × 10⁸) / 5 = 10⁸ Pa.
Load F = 2000 × 10 = 2 × 10⁴ N.
A_min = F / σ_work = (2 × 10⁴) / 10⁸ = **2 × 10⁻⁴ m² (2 cm²)**.

Jaanch:
- Jaanch: 2 × 10⁻⁴ × 10⁸ = 20,000 N = 2000 kg-wt ✅

---

### Question 10
A tensile stress of 1.5 × 10⁷ Pa produces a strain of 0.0001 in a 3 m wire. Find the elongation ΔL in mm.

**Solution:**
ΔL = strain × L = 0.0001 × 3 m = 3 × 10⁻⁴ m = **0.3 mm**.

Jaanch:
- Jaanch: 0.3 mm / 3000 mm = 10⁻⁴ ✅

---

### Question 11
State whether the following statements are True or False:
(a) Stress has the same SI unit and dimensional formula as Pressure.
(b) A perfectly plastic body completely regains its original shape once the deforming force is removed.

**Solution:**
- (a) **True** ✅ — Both are Force / Area = N/m² = Pa, [M L⁻¹ T⁻²].
- (b) **False** ❌ — Perfectly **elastic** body regains its shape; perfectly **plastic** body retains permanent deformation.

Jaanch:
- Jaanch: Definitions of stress, elasticity, and plasticity confirmed ✅

---

### Question 12
A 1 m long wire of radius 1 mm is stretched by a 31.4 N force (take π = 3.14). Find the tensile stress in the wire.

**Solution:**
A = π r² = 3.14 × (10⁻³)² = 3.14 × 10⁻⁶ m².
Stress = 31.4 / (3.14 × 10⁻⁶) = **10⁷ N/m² (10 MPa)**.

Jaanch:
- Jaanch: 31.4 / 3.14 = 10 => 10 × 10⁶ = 10⁷ Pa ✅
