## 1. Carbocation Rearrangement in Acid-Catalysed Dehydration

Jab alcohol ka acid-catalysed dehydration carbocation intermediate se guzarta hai, toh agar bagal ke carbon se **1,2-Hydride shift (H⁻)** ya **1,2-Methyl shift (CH₃⁻)** ya **Ring expansion** (4 → 5 ya 5 → 6 membered ring) se **zyada stable carbocation** ban sakta ho, toh rearrangement hamesha hota hai!
- **Example (Neopentyl alcohol):** (CH₃)₃C–CH₂–OH —(H⁺, Δ)→ [(CH₃)₃C–CH₂⁺ (1°)] —(1,2-methyl shift)→ (CH₃)₂C⁺–CH₂CH₃ (3°) → **(CH₃)₂C=CH–CH₃ (2-Methylbut-2-ene, major!)**.
- **Ring expansion:** Cyclobutylmethanol + H⁺/Δ → cyclopentyl carbocation → **Cyclopentene (major!)**.

---

## 2. Pinacol–Pinacolone Rearrangement

Vicinal diols (1,2-diols) jab acid (H₂SO₄) ke saath heat kiye jate hain, toh dehydration + 1,2-shift hokar **ketone (ya aldehyde)** banta hai!
- **Classic Pinacol:** (CH₃)₂C(OH)–C(OH)(CH₃)₂ —(H⁺, Δ)→ **(CH₃)₃C–CO–CH₃ (Pinacolone, 3,3-dimethylbutan-2-one)** + H₂O.
- **Step 1:** Protonation & loss of H₂O from the carbon that forms the **MORE STABLE carbocation** (e.g., Ph₂C⁺ > (CH₃)₂C⁺).
- **Step 2 (Migratory Aptitude):** From the adjacent carbon, the group with higher **electron density (migratory aptitude)** shifts to the carbocation:
  **–H > –Ar (p-MeO-Ph > Ph > p-NO₂-Ph) > 3° alkyl > 2° alkyl > 1° alkyl > –CH₃**.
  *(Caution: First decide WHICH –OH leaves to give the more stable carbocation; THEN apply migratory aptitude among the groups on the OTHER carbon!)*

## 3. Fries Rearrangement & Claisen Rearrangement

1. **Fries Rearrangement (Phenolic Ester → Hydroxyketone):**
   Phenyl acetate (C₆H₅–O–COCH₃) + anhydrous **AlCl₃**:
   - **Low temperature (~298 K / 25°C):** **p-Hydroxyacetophenone** (major — thermodynamically less hindered, para).
   - **High temperature (~433 K / 160°C):** **o-Hydroxyacetophenone** (major — stabilised by intramolecular H-bonding!).
   *(Note: Opposite of many reactions — here low temp gives para, high temp gives ortho!)*
2. **Claisen Rearrangement (Allyl Phenyl Ether → o-Allylphenol):**
   C₆H₅–O–CH₂–CH=CH₂ —(heat, 473 K, [3,3]-sigmatropic shift)→ **2-Allylphenol (ortho)**!
   - Agar dono ortho positions blocked hon (e.g., 2,6-dimethylallyl phenyl ether), toh double rearrangement hokar **4-allylphenol (para)** banta hai!

## 4. Excess Hot HI Cleavage & Victor Meyer Test

### (a) Cold 1 eq. HI vs Hot Excess HI on Ethers:
- **Aliphatic ether (R–O–R') + Excess Hot HI:** Dono alkyl groups iodide ban jate hain!
  CH₃–O–C₂H₅ + 2HI (excess, Δ) → **CH₃–I + C₂H₅–I + H₂O**.
- **Anisole (C₆H₅–O–CH₃) + Excess Hot HI:** Phir bhi sirf **C₆H₅–OH + CH₃–I** hi milta hai (phenol HI ke saath iodobenzene **KABHI nahi** banata!).
- **Cyclic ether (THF) + Excess Hot HI:** Ring opens to **1,4-diiodobutane** (I–(CH₂)₄–I) + H₂O!

### (b) Victor Meyer Test (RBC Trick for 1°, 2°, 3° Alcohols):
Sequence: Alcohol —(P/I₂)→ R–I —(AgNO₂)→ R–NO₂ —(HNO₂: NaNO₂/HCl)→ —(NaOH)→ Colour!
- **1° Alcohol:** Forms nitrolic acid → **Blood RED colour** with NaOH (**R**).
- **2° Alcohol:** Forms pseudonitrol → **BLUE colour** (**B**).
- **3° Alcohol:** Does not react with HNO₂ → **COLOURLESS** (**C**).
*(Mnemonic: **1°–2°–3° = R–B–C** = Red, Blue, Colourless!)*

## Deep Solved Problems

### Problem 1: Predicting Pinacol–Pinacolone Product (Unsymmetrical Diol)
Predict the major product when **1,1-diphenyl-2-methylpropane-1,2-diol**, Ph₂C(OH)–C(OH)(CH₃)₂, is heated with dilute H₂SO₄.

**Solution:**
- **Step 1 (Which –OH leaves?):** Leaving from C1 gives **Ph₂C⁺–C(OH)(CH₃)₂** (stabilised by resonance over TWO phenyl rings!), far more stable than (CH₃)₂C⁺. So C1 loses –OH!
- **Step 2 (Which group migrates from C2?):** C2 holds two –CH₃ groups → a **methyl group migrates** to C1!
- **Product:** **Ph₂C(CH₃)–CO–CH₃** (3,3-diphenylbutan-2-one)!

Jaanch: Resonance-stabilised Ph₂C⁺ forms first; methyl shifts from C2 to C1; C2 becomes C=O ✅

---

### Problem 2: Dehydration with Ring Expansion
Predict the major alkene formed when **1-cyclobutylethanol** (c-C₄H₇–CH(OH)–CH₃) is heated with conc. H₂SO₄.

**Solution:**
- Loss of H₂O gives secondary carbocation adjacent to strained 4-membered cyclobutane ring.
- **Ring expansion (4 → 5 membered ring)** relieves massive angle strain and forms a 2° cyclopentyl carbocation (2-methylcyclopentyl cation), followed by a 1,2-hydride shift to the **3° 1-methylcyclopentyl cation**!
- Loss of H⁺ (Zaitsev) gives **1-methylcyclopentene** as the major product!

Jaanch: 4-ring → 5-ring (strain relief) + 2° → 3° shift → trisubstituted 1-methylcyclopentene ✅

---

### Problem 3: Excess Hot HI with Diphenyl Ether vs Benzyl Phenyl Ether
Predict the products when heated with **excess hot HI**:
(a) Diphenyl ether (C₆H₅–O–C₆H₅)
(b) Benzyl phenyl ether (C₆H₅–CH₂–O–C₆H₅)

**Solution:**
(a) **Diphenyl ether:** Both C–O bonds are sp² aryl–O bonds with partial double-bond character → **NO reaction!**
(b) **Benzyl phenyl ether:** Only the sp³ benzylic C–O bond breaks (SN1) → **C₆H₅–CH₂–I (benzyl iodide) + C₆H₅–OH (phenol)**. Excess HI cannot convert phenol to iodobenzene!

Jaanch: Aryl–O bonds are immune to HI cleavage even in hot excess HI ✅

---

### Problem 4: Claisen Rearrangement with Blocked Ortho Positions
What is the major product when **allyl 2,6-dimethylphenyl ether** is heated to 473 K?

**Solution:**
First [3,3]-sigmatropic shift moves the allyl group to C2 (forming a non-aromatic dienone because C2 already has a –CH₃ group). A second [3,3]-Cope shift moves the allyl group to the open **para (C4)** position, followed by tautomerisation to restore aromaticity → **4-allyl-2,6-dimethylphenol**!

Jaanch: Both ortho positions blocked → rearrangement proceeds cleanly to para position ✅

---

### Problem 5: Identifying Alcohol via Victor Meyer Test
An alcohol C₃H₈O gives a **blue** colouration in the Victor Meyer test. Write its structure and the intermediate pseudonitrol formed.

**Solution:**
In Victor Meyer test, **R–B–C** = 1° Red, **2° Blue**, 3° Colourless.
So the alcohol is **2°**: **Propan-2-ol (CH₃–CH(OH)–CH₃)**.
Sequence: CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ → CH₃CH(I)CH₃ → CH₃CH(NO₂)CH₃ —(HNO₂)→ **(CH₃)₂C(NO)(NO₂)** (2-nitro-2-nitrosopropane, **pseudonitrol** — blue!).

Jaanch: 2° nitroalkane has 1 α-H → reacts with HNO₂ to form blue pseudonitrol ✅

## Advanced Practice Questions

1. Predict the major alkene formed on acid-catalysed dehydration of **3,3-dimethylbutan-2-ol**.
2. Predict the major product of Pinacol–Pinacolone rearrangement of **Ph–CH(OH)–CH(OH)–CH₃** with H₂SO₄.
3. What are the major products of Fries rearrangement of phenyl acetate with AlCl₃ at: (a) 298 K, (b) 433 K?
4. Write the products of heating **tetrahydrofuran (THF)** with excess hot HI.
5. Why does **diphenyl ether (C₆H₅–O–C₆H₅)** fail to react even with boiling concentrated HI?
6. Predict the Victor Meyer test colours for: (a) Butan-1-ol, (b) Butan-2-ol, (c) 2-Methylpropan-2-ol.
7. Arrange –H, –CH₃, –C₆H₅, and –p-C₆H₄OCH₃ in decreasing order of migratory aptitude in Pinacol rearrangement.
8. Predict the major product when **allyl phenyl ether** (labelled with ¹⁴C at the terminal =CH₂) undergoes Claisen rearrangement at 473 K — which carbon attaches to the ortho position of the ring?
9. Why does **o-hydroxybenzoic acid (salicylic acid, pKₐ = 2.98)** have much higher acidity than **p-hydroxybenzoic acid (pKₐ = 4.58)**?
10. Convert **1-methylcyclohexanol** to **1-methylcyclohexene** vs **methylenecyclohexane** — which is major on heating with conc. H₂SO₄?

---

### Solutions

1. 3,3-Dimethylbutan-2-ol → 2° carbocation → 1,2-methyl shift → 3° carbocation → **2,3-dimethylbut-2-ene** (tetrasubstituted alkene, major!).
2. Leaving –OH from Ph-bearing carbon gives resonance-stabilised **Ph–CH⁺–CH(OH)–CH₃**; then **–H** (higher migratory aptitude than –CH₃) migrates from C2 → **Ph–CH₂–CO–CH₃ (1-phenylpropan-2-one)**!
   Jaanch: Benzylic carbocation forms first; hydride shifts over methyl ✅
3. (a) At 298 K (low temp): **p-Hydroxyacetophenone** (major).
   (b) At 433 K (high temp): **o-Hydroxyacetophenone** (major).
4. **I–CH₂–CH₂–CH₂–CH₂–I (1,4-diiodobutane)** + H₂O.
5. Both C–O bonds are strong sp² aryl–O bonds with partial double-bond character due to resonance, so neither bond can be cleaved by I⁻.
6. (a) Butan-1-ol (1°): **Blood red**; (b) Butan-2-ol (2°): **Blue**; (c) 2-Methylpropan-2-ol (3°): **Colourless** (R–B–C!).
7. Decreasing migratory aptitude (more electron-rich migrates faster):
   **–p-C₆H₄OCH₃ > –C₆H₅ > –H > –CH₃** (note: in alkyl vs H, H > CH₃; electron-rich aryl often rivals/exceeds H depending on system, and definitely p-MeO-Ph > Ph > CH₃!).
8. In a [3,3]-sigmatropic Claisen rearrangement, the allyl chain **inverts** — the **terminal ¹⁴CH₂ carbon** forms the new C–C bond to the ortho position of the benzene ring!
9. **Ortho-effect (intramolecular H-bonding in conjugate base):** The salicylate anion (o-HO–C₆H₄–COO⁻) is strongly stabilised by a **6-membered intramolecular H-bond** between –OH and –COO⁻, making salicylic acid ~40× stronger than p-hydroxybenzoic acid!
10. **1-Methylcyclohexene** (trisubstituted endocyclic alkene, Zaitsev product) is the major product!
