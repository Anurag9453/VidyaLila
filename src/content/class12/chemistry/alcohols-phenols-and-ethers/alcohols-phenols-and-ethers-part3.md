# Physical Properties and Acidic Nature of Alcohols and Phenols

> **Pichle topic mein:** Humne [Preparation of Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers](alcohols-phenols-and-ethers-part2.md) padhe. Ab hum inki physical properties (boiling point, water solubility, H-bonding) aur **Acidic Strength of Alcohols vs Phenols** (resonance, inductive effect, aur ortho vs para nitrophenol) ka poora logic samjhenge!

---

Ankita ne ek data chart dekha jo use ajeeb laga: **Ethanol (CH₃CH₂OH)** aur **Methoxymethane (CH₃–O–CH₃)** ka molecular mass bilkul BARABAR (46 g mol⁻¹) hai — par ethanol **78°C** par ubalta hai, jabki methoxymethane **−25°C** par hi gas ban jata hai! 103°C ka farq! Doosre chart mein **Phenol** NaOH mein ghul jata hai (acidic!), par ethanol NaOH mein salt nahi banata!

Dono sawalon ke peeche do superstar concepts hain: **Hydrogen Bonding** aur **Resonance Stabilisation of Phenoxide Ion**!

---

## Naye Shabd (Key Terms)

| Term | Hinglish Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Intermolecular Hydrogen Bonding** | Alag-alag molecules ke –OH groups ke beech ka strong electrostatic attraction (high BP aur water solubility ka kaaran) |
| **Intramolecular Hydrogen Bonding** | Ek hi molecule ke andar do groups (jaise o-nitrophenol mein –OH aur –NO₂) ke beech H-bond (is se BP ghat jata hai → steam volatile!) |
| **Phenoxide Ion (C₆H₅–O⁻)** | Phenol se H⁺ nikalne par bana conjugate base, jo benzene ring par resonance se behad stable hota hai |
| **pKₐ Value** | Acid strength ka logarithmic measure (pKₐ = −log Kₐ): **Jitna chhota pKₐ, utna zyada strong acid!** |

---

## 1. Physical Properties (Boiling Point & Solubility)

1. **Boiling Points (BP):**
   - **Alcohols & Phenols >> Ethers ≈ Alkanes** (comparable molar mass): Kyunki alcohols aur phenols strong **intermolecular H-bonds** banate hain (ethers mein O par koi H nahi hai → no self H-bonding!).
   - Example (M = 46 g mol⁻¹): **Ethanol (351 K) >> Methoxymethane (248 K) > Propane (231 K)**.
   - Carbon chain badhne par BP badhta hai (van der Waals forces ↑); **branching badhne par BP GHAT-TA hai** (surface area ↓): Butan-1-ol (391 K) > Butan-2-ol (373 K) > 2-Methylpropan-2-ol (356 K).
2. **Solubility in Water:**
   - Lower alcohols (methanol, ethanol, propanol) aur lower ethers paani ke saath **H-bonds** bana lete hain, isliye paani mein **miscible/soluble** hote hain!
   - Jaise-jaise hydrophobic alkyl chain badi hoti hai, solubility ghat-ti jati hai.
3. **ortho-Nitrophenol vs para-Nitrophenol (Classic Exam Question!):**
   - **o-Nitrophenol** mein –OH aur –NO₂ paas-paas hote hain → **intramolecular H-bonding** (apne hi andar ring banata hai, doosre molecules se nahi judta) → **low BP, steam volatile!**
   - **p-Nitrophenol** mein –OH aur –NO₂ door hote hain → **intermolecular H-bonding** (molecules aapas mein jud kar lambe chains banate hain) → **high BP, less volatile!**
   - Isliye dono isomers ko **steam distillation** se aasani se alag kar sakte hain!

## 2. Acidic Nature of Alcohols

Alcohols active metals (Na, K, Al) ke saath H₂ gas release karte hain:
2R–OH + 2Na → 2R–O⁻Na⁺ + H₂↑

### Acidity Order of Alcohols: **1° > 2° > 3°** (and **H₂O > Alcohols**!)
- **Kyun 1° > 2° > 3°?** Alkyl groups (**–CH₃, –C₂H₅**) **electron-releasing (+I effect)** hote hain! Jitne zyada alkyl groups honge, oxygen par electron density utni badhegi → O–H bond ki polarity kam hogi aur alkoxide ion (R–O⁻) destabilise hoga!
  - Order: **CH₃OH > 1° (CH₃CH₂OH) > 2° ((CH₃)₂CHOH) > 3° ((CH₃)₃COH)**.
- **Kyun Water > Alcohols (except CH₃OH)?** Alkoxide (R–O⁻) mein +I group negative charge badhata hai (stronger base than OH⁻!), isliye R–O⁻Na⁺ + H₂O → R–OH + NaOH! (Yaani paani alcohol se stronger acid hai!)
- Alcohols **Brønsted bases** bhi hote hain (oxygen ke lone pairs proton accept kar sakte hain).

## 3. Acidic Nature of Phenols (Why Phenol >> Alcohol!)

Phenol (pKₐ ≈ 10) ethanol (pKₐ ≈ 15.9) se lagbhag **10⁶ (1 million) times stronger acid** hai! Isliye phenol **NaOH** ke saath react karke sodium phenoxide bana leta hai (par **NaHCO₃** se CO₂ nahi nikalta kyunki phenol carbonic acid H₂CO₃ se weak hai — yeh carboxylic acid se phenol ko distinguish karne ka test hai!):
C₆H₅–OH + NaOH → C₆H₅–O⁻Na⁺ + H₂O

### Two Reasons Why Phenol Is Much More Acidic Than Alcohol:
1. **sp²-Hybridised Carbon in Phenol:** Ring ka sp² carbon zyada electronegative hota hai (33.3% s-character vs 25% in sp³) → electron pair ko apni taraf khinch kar O–H bond ko zyada polar banata hai.
2. **Resonance Stabilisation of Phenoxide Ion (MAIN REASON!):**
   - Alkoxide ion (R–O⁻) mein negative charge sirf ek oxygen atom par phansa (**localised**) rehta hai → unstable!
   - **Phenoxide ion (C₆H₅–O⁻)** mein negative charge benzene ring ke ortho aur para carbons par **delocalise** ho jata hai (5 resonance structures, aur koi charge separation nahi!) → behad stable!
   - *(Note: Khud phenol mein bhi resonance hai, par usmein charge separation (+ on O, − on C) hota hai, jabki phenoxide ion mein koi charge separation nahi hota — isliye phenoxide ion phenol se bhi zyada stabilised hai!)*

### Effect of Substituents on Acidity of Phenols:
1. **Electron-Withdrawing Groups (EWG: –NO₂, –CN, –X)** disperse the negative charge of phenoxide ion → **INCREASE acidity** (especially at **ortho and para** positions via −R resonance effect!).
   - **2,4,6-Trinitrophenol (Picric acid, pKₐ = 0.71)** itna strong acid hai ki NaHCO₃ ke saath bhi brisk CO₂ effervescence deta hai!
2. **Electron-Donating Groups (EDG: –CH₃, –OCH₃)** intensify the negative charge on phenoxide ion → **DECREASE acidity**!
   - Cresols (methylphenols) phenol se **kam acidic** hote hain!

**Master Acidity Order (Must Memorise!):**
**2,4,6-Trinitrophenol (Picric acid) > 2,4-Dinitrophenol > p-Nitrophenol > o-Nitrophenol > m-Nitrophenol > Phenol > m-Cresol > p-Cresol > o-Cresol > H₂O > Ethanol**

*(Why p-nitrophenol > o-nitrophenol? Because o-nitrophenol has intramolecular H-bonding which holds onto its H⁺ slightly more tightly!)*

## Solved Examples

### Example 1: Arranging Compounds in Increasing Order of Acidity
Arrange the following in increasing order of acid strength:
Propan-1-ol, 2,4,6-trinitrophenol, 3-nitrophenol, 3,5-dinitrophenol, phenol, 4-methylphenol.

**Solution:**
- Aliphatic alcohol (**Propan-1-ol**) is weakest (no resonance).
- **4-Methylphenol** has EDG (–CH₃) → weaker than phenol.
- **Phenol** is the reference.
- **3-Nitrophenol** (one –NO₂ at meta, −I only) < **3,5-Dinitrophenol** (two –NO₂ at meta, stronger −I) < **2,4,6-Trinitrophenol** (three –NO₂ at o,o,p, massive −R and −I!).
Increasing order:
**Propan-1-ol < 4-Methylphenol < Phenol < 3-Nitrophenol < 3,5-Dinitrophenol < 2,4,6-Trinitrophenol**.

Jaanch: Alcohol < EDG-phenol < Phenol < 1-EWG < 2-EWG < 3-EWG (o,o,p) ✅

---

### Example 2: Separating o-Nitrophenol and p-Nitrophenol
How can a mixture of ortho-nitrophenol and para-nitrophenol be separated? Explain the principle.

**Solution:**
By **steam distillation**!
- **o-Nitrophenol** forms **intramolecular H-bonds** (within the same molecule), so intermolecular attractions are weak → lower boiling point → **steam volatile** (distils over first with steam).
- **p-Nitrophenol** forms **intermolecular H-bonds** between neighbouring molecules → higher boiling point → **less volatile** (remains in the distillation flask).

Jaanch: Intramolecular H-bond → lower BP (steam volatile); Intermolecular H-bond → higher BP ✅

---

### Example 3: Explaining Boiling Point Order
Arrange pentan-1-ol, n-butane, pentanal, and ethoxyethane in increasing order of boiling points. Give reason.

**Solution:**
All four have comparable molar masses (~58–88 g mol⁻¹), so intermolecular forces decide BP:
- **n-Butane** (non-polar, weak van der Waals only) — lowest BP.
- **Ethoxyethane** (weakly polar dipole–dipole, no H-bonding).
- **Pentanal** (stronger C=O dipole–dipole attraction, no self H-bonding).
- **Pentan-1-ol** (strong intermolecular **H-bonding**) — highest BP!
Increasing order: **n-Butane < Ethoxyethane < Pentanal < Pentan-1-ol**.

Jaanch: van der Waals < weak dipole < strong C=O dipole < H-bonding ✅

## Aam Galtiyan (Common Mistakes)

1. **o-Nitrophenol ko p-Nitrophenol se zyada acidic bol dena:**
   - *Galat:* –NO₂ ortho par zyada paas hai toh o-nitrophenol sabse zyada acidic hoga.
   - *Sahi:* **p-Nitrophenol (pKₐ 7.1) > o-Nitrophenol (pKₐ 7.2)**! Ortho par **intramolecular H-bonding** H⁺ ke release ko thoda rok leti hai!

2. **Phenol ko NaHCO₃ ke saath CO₂ dene wala acid bol dena:**
   - *Galat:* Phenol acidic hai toh NaHCO₃ se brisk effervescence dega.
   - *Sahi:* Simple phenol **NaHCO₃ se react NAHI karta** (kyunki phenol H₂CO₃ se weak hai); sirf **NaOH** se react karta hai! (Exception: Picric acid NaHCO₃ se react karta hai!)

3. **3° alcohol ko 1° alcohol se zyada acidic bol dena:**
   - *Galat:* 3° alcohol sabse zyada acidic hai.
   - *Sahi:* 3° alcohol mein teen +I alkyl groups oxygen par electron density badhate hain → **3° alcohol sabse KAM acidic** hota hai (**1° > 2° > 3°**)!

## Quick Recap

- **BP:** Alcohols/Phenols (H-bonding) >> Ethers ≈ Alkanes; branching ↓ BP.
- **o- vs p-Nitrophenol:** o-nitrophenol = intramolecular H-bond (steam volatile); p-nitrophenol = intermolecular H-bond (higher BP).
- **Alcohol acidity:** H₂O > 1° > 2° > 3° (+I effect destabilises alkoxide).
- **Phenol acidity:** Phenol >> H₂O > Alcohol (resonance-stabilised phenoxide ion + sp² C). EWG (–NO₂) ↑ acidity; EDG (–CH₃) ↓ acidity.
- **p-Nitrophenol > o-Nitrophenol > m-Nitrophenol > Phenol > Cresols**.

## Practice Questions

1. Explain why ethanol (M = 46) has a much higher boiling point (351 K) than methoxymethane (M = 46, BP = 248 K).
2. Why are lower alcohols soluble in water, while higher alcohols are insoluble?
3. Arrange 1° (ethanol), 2° (propan-2-ol), and 3° (2-methylpropan-2-ol) alcohols in decreasing order of acidic strength. Give reason.
4. Explain why phenol is more acidic than ethanol (give two reasons).
5. Why does phenol react with aqueous NaOH, whereas ethanol does not?
6. Explain why ortho-nitrophenol is more steam-volatile than para-nitrophenol.
7. Arrange phenol, p-cresol, and p-nitrophenol in increasing order of pKₐ value! (Careful: higher pKₐ = weaker acid!)
8. Why is p-nitrophenol slightly more acidic than o-nitrophenol?
9. Why does simple phenol NOT give effervescence with NaHCO₃, whereas 2,4,6-trinitrophenol (picric acid) does?
10. Arrange propan-1-ol, 2-methylpropan-2-ol, and butan-1-ol in increasing order of boiling point.

---

### Answers

1. Ethanol molecules associate through strong **intermolecular hydrogen bonding** (between O–H groups), requiring much more heat energy to separate them. Methoxymethane has no O–H bond, so it cannot form intermolecular H-bonds.
2. Lower alcohols form **hydrogen bonds** with water molecules. As the non-polar hydrophobic hydrocarbon chain grows longer, it disrupts water's H-bonding network and decreases solubility.
3. Decreasing acidic strength: **Ethanol (1°) > Propan-2-ol (2°) > 2-Methylpropan-2-ol (3°)**. More electron-releasing (+I) alkyl groups increase electron density on oxygen, reducing O–H bond polarity and destabilising the alkoxide ion.
4. (i) The **phenoxide ion (C₆H₅O⁻)** is strongly stabilised by **resonance** delocalisation of negative charge into the benzene ring (whereas ethoxide's charge is localised on oxygen). (ii) Phenol's **sp² carbon** is more electronegative than ethanol's sp³ carbon, increasing O–H polarity.
5. Phenol (pKₐ ≈ 10) is a stronger acid than water (pKₐ ≈ 15.7), so NaOH readily deprotonates it to sodium phenoxide + H₂O. Ethanol (pKₐ ≈ 15.9) is weaker than water, so it does not form sodium ethoxide with aqueous NaOH.
6. **o-Nitrophenol** has **intramolecular H-bonding** (weak intermolecular forces → low BP → steam volatile), whereas **p-nitrophenol** has **intermolecular H-bonding** (molecular association → high BP → less volatile).
7. Acid strength order: p-nitrophenol > phenol > p-cresol. Since **smaller pKₐ = stronger acid**, increasing **pKₐ** order is:
   **p-Nitrophenol < Phenol < p-Cresol**.
   Jaanch: pKₐ values: p-nitrophenol (7.1) < phenol (10.0) < p-cresol (10.2) ✅
8. In o-nitrophenol, **intramolecular hydrogen bonding** between the adjacent –OH and –NO₂ groups makes proton (H⁺) release slightly harder than in p-nitrophenol.
9. Simple phenol (pKₐ 10) is weaker than carbonic acid H₂CO₃ (pKₐ 6.4), so it cannot displace CO₂ from NaHCO₃. In picric acid (pKₐ 0.71), three –NO₂ groups at o,o,p make it far stronger than H₂CO₃, so it briskly liberates CO₂ from NaHCO₃!
10. Increasing BP: **2-Methylpropan-2-ol (356 K) < Propan-1-ol (370 K) < Butan-1-ol (391 K)** (branching lowers BP of tert-butanol even below straight-chain propan-1-ol!).
