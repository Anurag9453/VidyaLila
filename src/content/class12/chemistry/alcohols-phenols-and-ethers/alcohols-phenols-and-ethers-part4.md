## Naye Shabd (Key Terms)

| Term | Hinglish Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Esterification** | Alcohol/Phenol + Carboxylic acid (ya acid anhydride/chloride) → Ester + H₂O (aspirin synthesis isi ka example hai) |
| **PCC (Pyridinium Chlorochromate)** | Mild oxidising agent jo **1° alcohol ko sirf aldehyde tak** rok deta hai (acid tak nahi le jata!) |
| **Kolbe's Reaction** | Sodium phenoxide + CO₂ (400 K, 4–7 atm), phir H⁺ → **Salicylic acid (2-hydroxybenzoic acid)** |
| **Reimer–Tiemann Reaction** | Phenol + CHCl₃ + aq. NaOH (340 K), phir H⁺ → **Salicylaldehyde (2-hydroxybenzaldehyde)** (intermediate: dichlorocarbene :CCl₂) |
| **Benzoquinone** | Phenol ke Na₂Cr₂O₇ + H₂SO₄ oxidation se bana conjugated diketone |

## 1. Chemical Reactions of Alcohols

### (a) Esterification & Acetylation (O–H Bond Cleavage)
- R–OH + R'–COOH —(H⁺, reversible)→ R'–COOR + H₂O (conc. H₂SO₄ catalyst; water removed to push forward).
- **Aspirin synthesis:** Salicylic acid + (CH₃CO)₂O —(H⁺)→ **Acetylsalicylic acid (Aspirin)** + CH₃COOH.

### (b) Acid-Catalysed Dehydration to Alkenes (C–O Bond Cleavage)
- **Ethanol:** CH₃CH₂OH —(conc. H₂SO₄, 443 K)→ CH₂=CH₂ + H₂O.
- **2° Alcohol:** 85% H₃PO₄ at 440 K → alkene.
- **3° Alcohol:** 20% H₃PO₄ at 358 K → alkene (easiest!).
- **Ease of dehydration order:** **3° > 2° > 1°** (most stable carbocation forms fastest!).
- **3-Step Mechanism (Ethanol → Ethene):**
  1. Fast protonation: CH₃CH₂OH + H⁺ ⇌ CH₃CH₂–O⁺H₂
  2. Slow C–O cleavage (RDS): CH₃CH₂–O⁺H₂ → CH₃CH₂⁺ + H₂O
  3. Fast β-proton loss: CH₃CH₂⁺ → CH₂=CH₂ + H⁺

### (c) Oxidation & Catalytic Dehydrogenation (Hot Cu at 573 K)
| Alcohol Type | Mild Oxidiser (PCC / CrO₃) | Strong Oxidiser (KMnO₄ / K₂Cr₂O₇-H⁺) | Hot Cu at 573 K |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1° (R–CH₂OH)** | **Aldehyde (R–CHO)** | **Carboxylic acid (R–COOH)** | **Aldehyde (R–CHO)** + H₂ |
| **2° (R₂CH–OH)** | **Ketone (R₂C=O)** | **Ketone (R₂C=O)** (vigorous breaks C–C) | **Ketone (R₂C=O)** + H₂ |
| **3° (R₃C–OH)** | No reaction | Vigorous → C–C cleavage (smaller acid/ketone) | **Alkene** (dehydration!) + H₂O |

## 2. Chemical Reactions of Phenols

–OH group benzene ring ko **strongly activate** karta hai (+R resonance effect) aur **ortho/para-directing** hota hai!

### (a) Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution (EAS) — Solvent Matters!
1. **Nitration:**
   - **Dilute HNO₃ (298 K):** **o-Nitrophenol + p-Nitrophenol** (separated by steam distillation).
   - **Conc. HNO₃:** **2,4,6-Trinitrophenol (Picric acid)** (poor yield due to oxidation; better made via phenol → phenol-2,4-disulphonic acid → conc. HNO₃!).
2. **Bromination (SOLVENT EFFECT — SUPER IMPORTANT!):**
   - **Br₂ in CS₂ or CHCl₃ (low-polarity solvent, 273 K):** Monobromination → **p-Bromophenol (major)** + o-bromophenol (no FeBr₃ catalyst needed!).
   - **Br₂ in Water (polar solvent):** Phenol ionises to hyper-active phenoxide ion → **2,4,6-Tribromophenol (white precipitate!)**!

### (b) Two Superstar Named Reactions of Phenol:
1. **Kolbe's Reaction (Phenol → Salicylic Acid):**
   C₆H₅–OH —(NaOH)→ C₆H₅–ONa —(1. CO₂, 2. H⁺)→ **2-Hydroxybenzoic acid (Salicylic acid)**
2. **Reimer–Tiemann Reaction (Phenol → Salicylaldehyde):**
   C₆H₅–OH + CHCl₃ + aq. NaOH → [substituted benzal chloride intermediate] —(NaOH, then H⁺)→ **2-Hydroxybenzaldehyde (Salicylaldehyde)**
   *(Electrophile attacking the ring is **dichlorocarbene, :CCl₂**!)*

### (c) Zinc Dust Distillation & Oxidation of Phenol:
- **With Zinc dust (Δ):** **C₆H₅–OH + Zn —(Δ)→ C₆H₆ (Benzene) + ZnO** (removes –OH cleanly!).
- **With Na₂Cr₂O₇ / H₂SO₄:** Phenol oxidises to **Benzoquinone (p-benzoquinone)**!

## 3. Chemical Reactions of Ethers (Cleavage by HX & EAS of Anisole)

### (a) Cleavage of C–O Bond by HI / HBr (Reactivity: HI > HBr > HCl)
1. **Simple 1° / 2° Mixed Ethers (SN2 mechanism):**
   **I⁻ attacks the SMALLER (less sterically hindered) alkyl group!**
   - CH₃–O–CH₂CH₃ + HI (cold) → **CH₃–I** + **CH₃CH₂–OH**!
2. **When ONE group is Tertiary (3°) or Benzylic (SN1 mechanism!):**
   **3° carbocation forms first, so I⁻ goes to the TERTIARY group!**
   - **(CH₃)₃C–O–CH₃ + HI → (CH₃)₃C–I + CH₃–OH**! *(Exam trap #1!)*
3. **Alkyl Aryl Ethers (Anisole + HI):**
   The Ar–O bond has partial double-bond character (resonance) and NEVER breaks!
   - **C₆H₅–O–CH₃ + HI → C₆H₅–OH (Phenol) + CH₃–I** (ALWAYS Phenol + Alkyl iodide, never iodobenzene!).

### (b) Electrophilic Substitution of Anisole (C₆H₅–OCH₃)
–OCH₃ is **o,p-directing and activating** (para is always major!):
- **Br₂ in ethanoic acid (no FeBr₃ needed!):** **p-Bromoanisole (90%, major)** + o-bromoanisole.
- **Friedel–Crafts alkylation (CH₃Cl / anhyd. AlCl₃):** **4-Methoxytoluene (major)** + 2-methoxytoluene.
- **Friedel–Crafts acylation (CH₃COCl / anhyd. AlCl₃):** **4-Methoxyacetophenone (major)**.
- **Nitration (conc. HNO₃ + conc. H₂SO₄):** **4-Nitroanisole (major)** + 2-nitroanisole.

## Solved Examples

### Example 1: Predicting HI Cleavage Products of Three Ethers
Predict the products of reacting 1 mole of cold HI with:
(a) CH₃–CH₂–O–CH₃
(b) (CH₃)₃C–O–CH₂–CH₃
(c) C₆H₅–O–CH₂–CH₃

**Solution:**
(a) Both groups are 1° → **SN2**: I⁻ attacks smaller methyl group → **CH₃–I + CH₃CH₂–OH**.
(b) One group is 3° → **SN1**: stable (CH₃)₃C⁺ forms first → **(CH₃)₃C–I + CH₃CH₂–OH**.
(c) Aryl–O bond cannot break (partial double bond) → **C₆H₅–OH (phenol) + CH₃CH₂–I**.

Jaanch: SN2 (smaller → I), SN1 (3° → I), Aryl (Ar–OH always preserved!) ✅

---

### Example 2: Phenol to Aspirin (2-Step Synthesis)
Write the two-step synthesis of **aspirin** starting from **phenol**.

**Solution:**
- **Step 1 (Kolbe's reaction):** C₆H₅–OH —(1. NaOH, 2. CO₂, 3. H⁺)→ **2-Hydroxybenzoic acid (Salicylic acid)**.
- **Step 2 (Acetylation):** Salicylic acid + (CH₃CO)₂O —(H⁺)→ **2-Acetoxybenzoic acid (Aspirin)** + CH₃COOH.

Jaanch: Phenolic –OH gets acetylated to –OCOCH₃ while –COOH remains intact ✅

---

### Example 3: Vapour-Phase Action of Hot Copper (573 K)
What happens when vapours of (a) propan-1-ol, (b) propan-2-ol, and (c) 2-methylpropan-2-ol are passed over heated copper at 573 K?

**Solution:**
- (a) 1° alcohol → **dehydrogenation** → **Propanal (CH₃CH₂CHO)** + H₂.
- (b) 2° alcohol → **dehydrogenation** → **Propanone (CH₃COCH₃)** + H₂.
- (c) 3° alcohol (no α-H!) → **dehydration** → **2-Methylpropene ((CH₃)₂C=CH₂)** + H₂O.

Jaanch: 1° → aldehyde, 2° → ketone, 3° → alkene ✅

## Aam Galtiyan (Common Mistakes)

1. **(CH₃)₃C–O–CH₃ + HI mein SN2 lagakar CH₃I + (CH₃)₃COH bana dena:**
   - *Galat:* Chhota group CH₃ hai toh CH₃I banega.
   - *Sahi:* Jaise hi **3° ya benzylic group** dikhe, mechanism **SN1** mein switch ho jata hai → **(CH₃)₃C–I + CH₃OH** banta hai!

2. **Phenol + Br₂/H₂O aur Phenol + Br₂/CS₂ ke products mix kar dena:**
   - *Galat:* Dono mein p-bromophenol banta hai.
   - *Sahi:* **Br₂ / H₂O (polar)** → **2,4,6-tribromophenol (white ppt)**; **Br₂ / CS₂ (non-polar)** → **p-bromophenol (major)**!

3. **Kolbe aur Reimer–Tiemann ke products ulta kar dena:**
   - *Galat:* Kolbe se salicylaldehyde banta hai.
   - *Sahi:* **Kolbe (CO₂)** → **Salicylic acid (–COOH)**; **Reimer–Tiemann (CHCl₃/NaOH)** → **Salicylaldehyde (–CHO)**!

## Quick Recap

- **Alcohols:** Dehydration ease **3° > 2° > 1°**; PCC stops 1° alcohol at **aldehyde**; hot Cu (573 K): 1° → aldehyde, 2° → ketone, 3° → alkene.
- **Phenols:** Br₂/H₂O → 2,4,6-tribromophenol (white ppt); Br₂/CS₂ → p-bromophenol; **Kolbe** (NaOH/CO₂/H⁺) → salicylic acid; **Reimer–Tiemann** (CHCl₃/NaOH/H⁺) → salicylaldehyde; Zn dust → benzene.
- **Ethers + HI:** 1°/2° → SN2 (I⁻ to smaller R); 3°/benzylic → SN1 (I⁻ to 3°/benzyl); Anisole → **Phenol + CH₃I**.

## Practice Questions

4. Explain why bromination of phenol in water gives 2,4,6-tribromophenol, whereas in CS₂ at 273 K it gives mainly p-bromophenol.
5. What happens when phenol is heated with: (a) zinc dust, (b) Na₂Cr₂O₇ + conc. H₂SO₄?
6. Predict the products of reacting HI (1 mole) with: (a) C₆H₅–O–CH₃, (b) (CH₃)₃C–O–CH₃, (c) CH₃–O–CH₂CH₂CH₃.
7. Why does anisole react with HI to give phenol and iodomethane rather than iodobenzene and methanol?
8. Complete the reactions of anisole with: (a) Br₂ in ethanoic acid, (b) CH₃COCl / anhyd. AlCl₃.
9. Name the electrophile generated in the Reimer–Tiemann reaction.
10. Convert: (a) Phenol to salicylic acid, (b) Phenol to benzene, (c) Propan-2-ol to propanone.

---

### Answers

1. Step 1: CH₃CH₂OH + H⁺ ⇌ CH₃CH₂–O⁺H₂ (protonation).
   Step 2: CH₃CH₂–O⁺H₂ → CH₃CH₂⁺ + H₂O (slow, RDS: carbocation).
   Step 3: CH₃CH₂⁺ → **CH₂=CH₂** + H⁺ (deprotonation).
2. KMnO₄ is a strong oxidising agent that oxidises propan-1-ol all the way to propanoic acid. **PCC** is a mild, anhydrous oxidising agent that stops oxidation cleanly at **propanal**.
3. (a) **Kolbe:** C₆H₅OH —(NaOH)→ C₆H₅ONa —(1. CO₂, 2. H⁺)→ **2-hydroxybenzoic acid (salicylic acid)**.
   (b) **Reimer–Tiemann:** C₆H₅OH + CHCl₃ + aq. NaOH —(then H⁺)→ **2-hydroxybenzaldehyde (salicylaldehyde)**.
4. In **polar water**, phenol ionises into the highly activating **phenoxide ion (C₆H₅O⁻)**, causing rapid trisubstitution to white **2,4,6-tribromophenol**. In **low-polarity CS₂**, ionisation is suppressed and Br₂ polarisation is weaker, giving clean monobromination (**p-bromophenol**).
5. (a) **Benzene (C₆H₆)** + ZnO.
   (b) **p-Benzoquinone** (conjugated diketone).
6. (a) **C₆H₅OH + CH₃I**
   (b) **(CH₃)₃C–I + CH₃OH** (SN1 via 3° carbocation!)
   (c) **CH₃I + CH₃CH₂CH₂OH** (SN2 at smaller methyl!)
7. Because the **C(aryl)–O bond** in anisole has **partial double-bond character** (resonance) and an sp² carbon, making it much stronger than the sp³ O–CH₃ bond; hence only O–CH₃ cleaves to give phenol + CH₃I.
8. (a) **4-Bromoanisole (major)** + 2-bromoanisole.
   (b) **4-Methoxyacetophenone (major)** + 2-methoxyacetophenone.
9. **Dichlorocarbene (:CCl₂)** (neutral electron-deficient species formed from CHCl₃ + OH⁻).
10. (a) Phenol —(1. NaOH, 2. CO₂, 3. H⁺ — Kolbe)→ **Salicylic acid**.
    (b) Phenol + Zn dust —(Δ)→ **Benzene** + ZnO.
    (c) Propan-2-ol —(CrO₃ or Cu / 573 K)→ **Propanone**.
