# Commercially Important Alcohols, Uses and Identification Tests

## Naye Shabd (Key Terms)

| Term | Hinglish Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Wood Spirit (Methanol, CH₃OH)** | Carbon monoxide + H₂ ke catalytic hydrogenation se bana toxic alcohol (chhoti maatra se bhi blindness, badi maatra se death ho sakti hai!) |
| **Fermentation** | Yeast ke enzymes (invertase + zymase) dwara complex sugars ka anaerobic breakdown hokar ethanol + CO₂ banana |
| **Denatured Alcohol** | Industrial ethanol jismein CuSO₄ (colour) aur pyridine (bad smell) milakar use peene ke liye unfit bana diya gaya ho |
| **Lucas Reagent** | Concentrated HCl + anhydrous ZnCl₂ ka mixture jo **1°, 2°, aur 3° alcohols** ko turbidity ke time se distinguish karta hai |
| **Iodoform Test** | I₂ + NaOH ke saath **yellow precipitate of CHI₃** dene wala test (sirf **CH₃–CH(OH)–** group wale alcohols dete hain!) |

## 1. Two Commercially Important Alcohols

### (a) Methanol (CH₃OH — "Wood Spirit")
- **Manufacture:** Catalytic hydrogenation of carbon monoxide (water gas / syngas) at high pressure and temperature:
  **CO + 2H₂ —(ZnO–Cr₂O₃ catalyst, 200–300 atm, 573–673 K)→ CH₃OH**
- **Properties & Hazard:** Colourless liquid (BP 337 K), miscible with water, **highly poisonous** (even small amounts cause optic nerve damage/blindness; large amounts are fatal).
- **Uses:** Solvent for paints and varnishes, and mainly for making **formaldehyde (HCHO)**.

### (b) Ethanol (C₂H₅OH — "Grain Alcohol")
- **Manufacture (Fermentation of Molasses / Sugarcane):**
  - Step 1 (Invertase enzyme in yeast):
    **C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁ (Sucrose) + H₂O —(invertase)→ C₆H₁₂O₆ (Glucose) + C₆H₁₂O₆ (Fructose)**
  - Step 2 (Zymase enzyme in yeast):
    **C₆H₁₂O₆ —(zymase)→ 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂↑**
  - Fermentation anaerobic (oxygen ke bina) hoti hai; agar air ghus jaye toh ethanol **ethanoic acid (vinegar)** mein oxidise ho jata hai!
  - Jab ethanol concentration ~14% pahunch jati hai, zymase ka action ruk jata hai.
- **Denaturation of Alcohol:** Industrial ethanol ko drinking se rokne ke liye **CuSO₄** (neela rang) aur **pyridine** (gandi badboo) milate hain.
- **Uses:** Solvent in paint industry, antiseptic/hand sanitisers, fuel additive (power alcohol), organic synthesis.

## 2. Identification & Distinction Tests (Exam Goldmine!)

### (a) Lucas Test — Distinguishing 1°, 2°, and 3° Alcohols
**Reagent:** Concentrated HCl + anhydrous ZnCl₂ (**Lucas Reagent**).
**Principle:** Alcohol + HCl → **insoluble alkyl chloride (R–Cl)**, jo solution mein **turbidity (dhundhlapan)** paida karta hai! Reaction carbocation (SN1) ke through jati hai (reactivity: **3° > 2° > 1°**).

| Alcohol Type | Observation with Lucas Reagent (Room Temp) | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **Tertiary (3°)** (e.g., 2-methylpropan-2-ol) | **Immediate turbidity** (turant!) | Very stable 3° carbocation forms instantly |
| **Secondary (2°)** (e.g., propan-2-ol) | **Turbidity appears in ~5 minutes** | Moderately stable 2° carbocation |
| **Primary (1°)** (e.g., ethanol, propan-1-ol) | **No turbidity at room temperature** (only on heating!) | Unstable 1° carbocation |

*(Special note: **Benzyl alcohol** aur **allyl alcohol** 1° hone ke bawajood resonance-stabilised carbocation banate hain, isliye Lucas test mein **turant turbidity** dete hain!)*

### (b) Distinguishing Alcohols vs Phenols
| Test | Phenol (C₆H₅OH) | Aliphatic Alcohol (e.g., Ethanol) |
|---|---|---|
| **Neutral FeCl₃ solution** | **Violet / purple colouration** | No colour change |
| **Bromine water (Br₂/H₂O)** | **White precipitate** (2,4,6-tribromophenol) | No precipitate |
| **Blue litmus / aq. NaOH** | Turns blue litmus red; dissolves in NaOH | Neutral to litmus; does not react with NaOH |

### (c) Iodoform Test (For CH₃–CH(OH)– Group!)
**Reagent:** I₂ + NaOH (warm).
**Positive result:** **Yellow precipitate of Iodoform (CHI₃)** with antiseptic smell!
- **Positive alcohols:** **Ethanol (CH₃CH₂OH)** (ONLY 1° alcohol that gives iodoform test!), **Propan-2-ol (CH₃CH(OH)CH₃)**, **Butan-2-ol**, **1-Phenylethanol**.
- **Negative alcohols:** **Methanol (CH₃OH)**, **Propan-1-ol**, **Pentan-3-ol**, **2-Methylpropan-2-ol**.

## Solved Examples

### Example 1: Chemical Distinction Between Three Alcohol Pairs
Give ONE simple chemical test to distinguish between:
(a) Ethanol and Methanol
(b) Propan-1-ol and Propan-2-ol
(c) Ethanol and Phenol

**Solution:**
(a) **Iodoform test (I₂ + NaOH, warm):** **Ethanol** (has CH₃–CH(OH)– unit) gives a **yellow precipitate of CHI₃**; **Methanol** gives NO yellow precipitate.
(b) **Iodoform test** OR **Lucas test:**
   - With I₂ + NaOH: **Propan-2-ol** gives **yellow CHI₃ ppt**; **Propan-1-ol** gives NO ppt.
   - With Lucas reagent: **Propan-2-ol (2°)** gives turbidity in ~5 min; **Propan-1-ol (1°)** gives no turbidity at room temp.
(c) **Neutral FeCl₃ test (or Br₂ water):** **Phenol** gives a **violet colour** with neutral FeCl₃ (and white ppt with Br₂ water); **Ethanol** gives NO reaction.

Jaanch: Each test produces a clear, unmistakable visual difference ✅

---

### Example 2: Explaining Why Fermentation Must Be Anaerobic
Why is air excluded during the fermentation of molasses to ethanol?

**Solution:**
If air (O₂) enters the fermentation vat, aerobic bacteria (like *Acetobacter*) oxidise the produced **ethanol into ethanoic acid (CH₃COOH)**, spoiling the yield and turning the alcohol into vinegar.

Jaanch: Anaerobic condition preserves C₂H₅OH; aerobic oxidation gives CH₃COOH ✅

---

### Example 3: Identifying an Unknown Alcohol from Lucas & Iodoform Tests
An alcohol **A** (C₄H₁₀O) gives turbidity with Lucas reagent in about 5 minutes and forms a yellow precipitate with I₂ + NaOH. Identify **A**.

**Solution:**
- Turbidity in ~5 minutes → **2° alcohol**.
- Out of the four isomers of C₄H₁₀O, the ONLY 2° alcohol is **Butan-2-ol (CH₃–CH(OH)–CH₂CH₃)**.
- Check Iodoform test: Butan-2-ol contains the **CH₃–CH(OH)–** group → gives yellow CHI₃ ppt! Confirmed!

Jaanch: Formula CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH₃ = C₄H₁₀O (2°, has CH₃CH(OH)–) ✅

## Aam Galtiyan (Common Mistakes)

1. **Methanol ko iodoform test mein positive bol dena:**
   - *Galat:* Methanol aur ethanol dono primary alcohols hain toh dono iodoform denge.
   - *Sahi:* **Ethanol ekmatra 1° alcohol** hai jo iodoform test deta hai! Methanol mein CH₃–CH(OH)– group nahi hai → **NO iodoform!**

2. **Phenol ko Lucas reagent se test kar dena:**
   - *Galat:* Phenol ko distinguish karne ke liye Lucas test use karna.
   - *Sahi:* Phenol ko alcohol se distinguish karne ke liye **Neutral FeCl₃ (violet colour)** ya **Br₂/H₂O (white ppt)** use karo!

3. **Zymase aur Invertase ke kaam ulta kar dena:**
   - *Galat:* Zymase sucrose ko glucose mein todta hai.
   - *Sahi:* **Invertase** = Sucrose → Glucose + Fructose; **Zymase** = Glucose/Fructose → Ethanol + CO₂!

## Quick Recap

- **Methanol:** CO + 2H₂ —(ZnO–Cr₂O₃, 200–300 atm, 573–673 K)→ CH₃OH (highly toxic).
- **Ethanol:** Sucrose —(invertase)→ Glucose + Fructose —(zymase)→ 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂; denatured with CuSO₄ + pyridine.
- **Lucas Test (conc. HCl + anhyd. ZnCl₂):** 3° instant turbidity; 2° ~5 min; 1° none at room temp.
- **Phenol Tests:** Neutral FeCl₃ → violet; Br₂/H₂O → white ppt.
- **Iodoform Test (I₂ + NaOH):** Yellow CHI₃ ppt for **CH₃–CH(OH)–** alcohols (ethanol, propan-2-ol, butan-2-ol).

## Practice Questions

1. Write the balanced chemical equation and conditions for the industrial manufacture of methanol.
2. Write the two enzymatic reactions involved in preparing ethanol by fermentation of sucrose (molasses).
3. What is denatured alcohol? Name the substances commonly added to denature ethanol.
4. What is Lucas reagent? Explain how it distinguishes between 1°, 2°, and 3° alcohols.
5. Why does 2-methylpropan-2-ol give immediate turbidity with Lucas reagent, whereas butan-1-ol does not?
6. Give a simple chemical test to distinguish between:
   (a) Phenol and Benzyl alcohol
   (b) Pentan-2-ol and Pentan-3-ol
   (c) Butan-1-ol and 2-Methylpropan-2-ol
7. Name the ONLY primary alcohol that gives a positive iodoform test. Write its structure.
8. Why does benzyl alcohol (a 1° alcohol) give immediate turbidity in the Lucas test?
9. State two major industrial uses each of methanol and ethanol.
10. An alcohol C₃H₈O gives no turbidity with Lucas reagent at room temperature and fails the iodoform test. Identify the alcohol.

### Answers

1. **CO + 2H₂ —(ZnO–Cr₂O₃ catalyst, 200–300 atm, 573–673 K)→ CH₃OH**.
2. Step 1: C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁ + H₂O —(invertase)→ C₆H₁₂O₆ (glucose) + C₆H₁₂O₆ (fructose).
   Step 2: C₆H₁₂O₆ —(zymase)→ 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂↑.
3. **Denatured alcohol** is commercial ethanol rendered unfit for human consumption by adding poisonous/foul-smelling substances — typically **copper sulphate (CuSO₄)** (for blue colour) and **pyridine** (for foul smell).
4. **Lucas reagent** = conc. HCl + anhydrous ZnCl₂. It converts alcohols to insoluble R–Cl (turbidity): **3°** gives immediate turbidity, **2°** gives turbidity in ~5 minutes, and **1°** gives no turbidity at room temperature.
5. Because 2-methylpropan-2-ol (3°) forms a very stable **3° carbocation** instantly via SN1, whereas butan-1-ol (1°) cannot easily form an unstable 1° carbocation at room temperature.
6. (a) **Neutral FeCl₃ test:** **Phenol** gives violet colour; **benzyl alcohol** gives no colour.
   (b) **Iodoform test (I₂ + NaOH):** **Pentan-2-ol** (has CH₃CH(OH)–) gives yellow CHI₃ ppt; **pentan-3-ol** gives no ppt.
   (c) **Lucas test:** **2-Methylpropan-2-ol (3°)** gives immediate turbidity; **butan-1-ol (1°)** gives no turbidity at room temp.
7. **Ethanol (CH₃–CH₂–OH)** — it is the only 1° alcohol containing the CH₃–CH(OH)– structural unit!
8. Because benzyl alcohol forms a **resonance-stabilised benzyl carbocation (C₆H₅–CH₂⁺)**, which is as stable as a 3° carbocation, causing rapid SN1 reaction with Lucas reagent.
9. **Methanol:** Solvent for paints/varnishes, manufacture of formaldehyde (HCHO). **Ethanol:** Solvent in paint/pharma industries, fuel additive / hand sanitiser.
10. Isomers of C₃H₈O alcohols are propan-1-ol (1°) and propan-2-ol (2°). No Lucas turbidity + negative iodoform → **Propan-1-ol (CH₃CH₂CH₂OH)**.
    Jaanch: Propan-2-ol would be positive in both Lucas (~5 min) and iodoform ✅
