## Mixed Solved Examples

### Example 1: Four Classic Multi-Step Conversions
Convert:
(a) Phenol → Anisole
(b) Ethanol → Propan-2-ol
(c) Cumene → Salicylaldehyde
(d) Propan-2-ol → 2-Methylpropan-2-ol

**Solution:**
(a) **Phenol → Anisole:**
C₆H₅–OH —(NaOH)→ C₆H₅–ONa —(CH₃–Br, Williamson)→ **C₆H₅–O–CH₃ (Anisole)**

(b) **Ethanol → Propan-2-ol:**
- Step 1 (Mild oxidation): CH₃CH₂OH —(PCC)→ CH₃CHO (ethanal)
- Step 2 (Grignard +1 C): CH₃CHO —(1. CH₃MgBr/dry ether, 2. H₂O/H⁺)→ **CH₃–CH(OH)–CH₃ (Propan-2-ol)**

(c) **Cumene → Salicylaldehyde:**
- Step 1 (Cumene process): C₆H₅–CH(CH₃)₂ —(1. O₂, 2. H⁺/H₂O)→ C₆H₅–OH (phenol) + CH₃COCH₃
- Step 2 (Reimer–Tiemann): C₆H₅–OH —(1. CHCl₃ + aq. NaOH, 2. H⁺)→ **2-Hydroxybenzaldehyde (Salicylaldehyde)**

(d) **Propan-2-ol → 2-Methylpropan-2-ol:**
- Step 1 (Oxidation): CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ —(CrO₃ or Cu/573 K)→ CH₃COCH₃ (propanone)
- Step 2 (Grignard +1 C): CH₃COCH₃ —(1. CH₃MgBr/dry ether, 2. H₂O/H⁺)→ **(CH₃)₃C–OH (2-Methylpropan-2-ol)**

Jaanch: Every conversion cleanly shifts functional group or adds +1 carbon via Grignard ✅

### Example 2: Unknown Compound Deduction (Ether + HI + Lucas + Iodoform)
An ether **X** (C₅H₁₂O) on heating with 1 mole of HI yields an alkyl iodide **Y** and an alcohol **Z**. **Z** gives a positive iodoform test and turbidity with Lucas reagent in ~5 minutes. **Y** on treatment with aqueous KOH gives methanol. Identify X, Y, and Z.

**Solution:**
- **Y** + aq. KOH → CH₃OH → **Y = CH₃–I (iodomethane)**!
- Remaining carbons for alcohol **Z**: C₅ − C₁ = **C₄H₁₀O**.
- **Z** gives Lucas turbidity in ~5 min (**2° alcohol**) and positive iodoform test (**CH₃CH(OH)–**) → **Z = Butan-2-ol (CH₃–CH(OH)–CH₂CH₃)**!
- Combining CH₃– and sec-butyl via –O–: **X = 2-Methoxybutane (CH₃–O–CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₃)**.

Jaanch: 2-Methoxybutane (C₅H₁₂O, 1°/2° ether) + HI (SN2 at smaller methyl!) → CH₃I + Butan-2-ol ✅

## Aam Galtiyan (Mixed-Problem Traps)

1. **Phenol se Anisole banane mein NaOH step bhool jana:**
   - *Galat:* C₆H₅OH + CH₃Br → C₆H₅OCH₃.
   - *Sahi:* Phenol weak nucleophile hai; pehle **NaOH** daal kar **sodium phenoxide (C₆H₅ONa)** banao, phir CH₃Br dalo!

2. **Ethanol se Propan-2-ol banane mein KMnO₄ se oxidise kar dena:**
   - *Galat:* Ethanol + KMnO₄ → Ethanal.
   - *Sahi:* KMnO₄ seedha acetic acid bana dega! Ethanal par rokne ke liye **PCC** ya **Cu / 573 K** use karo!

## Quick Recap

- **Adding +1 carbon to alcohols:** 1° alcohol → (PCC) → aldehyde → (CH₃MgBr/H⁺) → 2° alcohol; 2° alcohol → (CrO₃) → ketone → (CH₃MgBr/H⁺) → 3° alcohol!
- **Phenol hub:** From cumene / diazonium → branches to benzene (Zn), salicylic acid (Kolbe), salicylaldehyde (Reimer–Tiemann), anisole (NaOH/CH₃Br), picric acid (conc. HNO₃).

## Practice Questions

1. Convert: (a) Propene to propan-1-ol, (b) Benzyl chloride to benzyl alcohol, (c) Ethylmagnesium chloride to propan-1-ol.
2. Convert: (a) Phenol to 2,4,6-tribromophenol, (b) Phenol to picric acid, (c) Phenol to aspirin.
3. Convert: (a) Anisole to phenol, (b) Anisole to 4-bromoanisole, (c) Ethanol to ethene.
4. Arrange in increasing order of acidic strength: Ethanol, Water, Phenol, p-Nitrophenol, p-Cresol.
5. Give ONE chemical test to distinguish between:
   (a) Phenol and Ethanol
   (b) Propan-1-ol and 2-Methylpropan-2-ol
   (c) Methanol and Ethanol
6. Predict the major products when the following ethers are treated with 1 mole of cold HI:
   (a) C₆H₅–CH₂–O–CH₃ (benzyl methyl ether)
   (b) CH₃–CH₂–O–CH(CH₃)₂
7. Explain why tert-butyl ethyl ether is prepared from sodium tert-butoxide and bromoethane, NOT from sodium ethoxide and tert-butyl bromide.
8. How will you separate a mixture of o-nitrophenol and p-nitrophenol? Why does the method work?
9. Complete the reaction sequence:
   CH₃CH₂OH —(conc. H₂SO₄, 443 K)→ **A** —(1. B₂H₆, 2. H₂O₂/OH⁻)→ **B** —(PCC)→ **C**.
10. Why does phenol have a higher boiling point (455 K) than toluene (384 K) despite having nearly identical molar masses (94 vs 92)?

---

### Answers

1. (a) CH₃CH=CH₂ —(1. B₂H₆/THF, 2. H₂O₂/OH⁻)→ **CH₃CH₂CH₂OH**.
   (b) C₆H₅CH₂Cl + aq. NaOH → **C₆H₅CH₂OH** + NaCl.
   (c) CH₃CH₂MgCl + HCHO —(1. dry ether, 2. H₂O/H⁺)→ **CH₃CH₂CH₂OH**.
2. (a) Phenol + Br₂/H₂O → **2,4,6-Tribromophenol (white ppt)**.
   (b) Phenol + conc. HNO₃ → **2,4,6-Trinitrophenol (picric acid)** (or via sulphonation + conc. HNO₃).
   (c) Phenol —(Kolbe: NaOH, CO₂, H⁺)→ Salicylic acid —((CH₃CO)₂O / H⁺)→ **Aspirin**.
3. (a) C₆H₅OCH₃ + HI (Δ) → **C₆H₅OH** + CH₃I.
   (b) C₆H₅OCH₃ + Br₂ (CH₃COOH) → **4-Bromoanisole (major)**.
   (c) CH₃CH₂OH —(conc. H₂SO₄, 443 K)→ **CH₂=CH₂** + H₂O.
4. Increasing acidic strength: **Ethanol < Water < p-Cresol < Phenol < p-Nitrophenol**.
5. (a) **Neutral FeCl₃:** Phenol → violet colour; Ethanol → no colour.
   (b) **Lucas reagent:** 2-Methylpropan-2-ol (3°) → instant turbidity; Propan-1-ol (1°) → no turbidity at room temp.
   (c) **Iodoform test (I₂ + NaOH):** Ethanol → yellow CHI₃ ppt; Methanol → no ppt.
6. (a) Benzylic group forms stable benzyl carbocation (SN1!) → **C₆H₅–CH₂–I + CH₃OH**.
   (b) 1°/2° ether (SN2 at less hindered ethyl!) → **CH₃CH₂–I + (CH₃)₂CH–OH**.
   Jaanch: Benzylic = SN1 (I⁻ to benzyl); 1°/2° = SN2 (I⁻ to 1° ethyl) ✅
7. Williamson synthesis proceeds by **SN2**; with a 3° halide (tert-butyl bromide), the strong base ethoxide causes **elimination** to give 2-methylpropene instead of ether. Using 3° alkoxide + 1° halide (bromoethane) gives clean SN2 ether formation.
8. By **steam distillation**: o-nitrophenol has intramolecular H-bonding (low BP, steam volatile) and distils over first, leaving behind p-nitrophenol (intermolecular H-bonding, high BP).
9. **A = CH₂=CH₂ (ethene)**; **B = CH₃CH₂OH (ethanol)**; **C = CH₃CHO (ethanal)**.
10. Phenol molecules form strong **intermolecular hydrogen bonds** through their –OH groups, whereas non-polar toluene molecules only have weak van der Waals forces.

| Topic | Key Reactions / Formulas | Must-Remember Fact |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Structure & Naming | R–OH, Ar–OH, R–O–R' (alkoxyalkane) | C–O–H = 108.9°, C–O–C = 111.7°, Phenol C–O = 136 pm (resonance) |
