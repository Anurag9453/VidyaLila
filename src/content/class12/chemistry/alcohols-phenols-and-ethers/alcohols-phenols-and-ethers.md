# Classification, Nomenclature and Structures of Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers

> **Pichle chapter mein:** Humne [Haloalkanes and Haloarenes — Mixed Conversions & Wrap-up](../haloalkanes-and-haloarenes/haloalkanes-and-haloarenes-part5.md) mein dekha ki kaise haloalkanes aqueous KOH ke saath alcohols banate hain. **Class 11 bridge:** Functional group priorities aur basic IUPAC rules hum [Organic Chemistry: Basic Principles and Techniques](../../../class11/chemistry/organic-chemistry-basic-principles-and-techniques/organic-chemistry-basic-principles-and-techniques.md) mein padh chuke hain — ab hum oxygen-containing organic compounds: **Alcohols, Phenols aur Ethers** shuru kar rahe hain!

---

Amrita ek herbal perfumery aur household store mein gayi thi jahan usne teen bottles ke labels padhe: hand sanitiser mein **ethanol** (CH₃CH₂OH), floor disinfectant mein **cresol** (ek phenol derivative), aur rose-scented perfume base mein **anisole** (C₆H₅–O–CH₃, ek ether)! Teeno mein oxygen atom hai — par ek aliphatic carbon se juda hai (**Alcohol**), ek seedha benzene ring se (**Phenol**), aur ek do carbons ke beech bridge bana raha hai (**Ether**)!

---

## Naye Shabd (Key Terms)

| Term | Hinglish Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Alcohol (R–OH)** | Woh compound jismein –OH group aliphatic (sp³) carbon atom se juda ho |
| **Phenol (Ar–OH)** | Woh compound jismein –OH group **seedha aromatic (benzene) ring ke sp² carbon** se juda ho |
| **Ether (R–O–R')** | Woh compound jismein oxygen atom do alkyl ya aryl groups ke beech juda ho (simple/symmetrical jab R = R', mixed/unsymmetrical jab R ≠ R') |
| **Monohydric / Dihydric / Trihydric** | 1, 2, ya 3 –OH groups wale alcohols ya phenols (e.g., ethylene glycol = dihydric, glycerol = trihydric) |
| **Allylic & Benzylic Alcohol** | –OH group sp³ carbon par jo C=C (allylic) ya benzene ring (benzylic) ke theek bagal mein ho |

---

## 1. Classification

1. **Alcohols:**
   - **By number of –OH groups:** Monohydric (CH₃OH), Dihydric (HO–CH₂–CH₂–OH, ethane-1,2-diol), Trihydric (HO–CH₂–CH(OH)–CH₂–OH, propane-1,2,3-triol / glycerol).
   - **By sp³ C–OH carbon:** **1°** (R–CH₂OH), **2°** (R₂CH–OH), **3°** (R₃C–OH), **Allylic** (CH₂=CH–CH₂OH), **Benzylic** (C₆H₅–CH₂OH).
   - **By sp² C–OH carbon:** **Vinylic** (CH₂=CH–OH — unstable, tautomerises to acetaldehyde) and **Phenols** (C₆H₅–OH).
2. **Ethers:**
   - **Simple (Symmetrical):** Both alkyl/aryl groups same (C₂H₅–O–C₂H₅, ethoxyethane).
   - **Mixed (Unsymmetrical):** Both groups different (CH₃–O–C₂H₅, methoxyethane; C₆H₅–O–CH₃, methoxybenzene / anisole).

---

## 2. IUPAC Nomenclature

### (a) Alcohols (Suffix: **-ol**)
- Longest carbon chain containing the –OH carbon choose karo; numbering aise karo ki –OH carbon ko **lowest possible number** mile (halogens aur double/triple bonds se zyada priority!).
- Alkane ka final 'e' hata kar **-ol** lagao (diol/triol mein 'e' rakho: e.g., **ethane-1,2-diol**, **propane-1,2,3-triol**!).
- Examples:
  - CH₃–CH(OH)–CH₃ → **Propan-2-ol** (isopropyl alcohol)
  - (CH₃)₃C–OH → **2-Methylpropan-2-ol** (tert-butyl alcohol)
  - CH₂=CH–CH₂OH → **Prop-2-en-1-ol** (allyl alcohol)

### (b) Phenols (Parent name: **Phenol**)
- Dihydroxybenzenes (must-know common names!):
  - **Catechol:** Benzene-1,2-diol (ortho)
  - **Resorcinol:** Benzene-1,3-diol (meta)
  - **Hydroquinone (Quinol):** Benzene-1,4-diol (para)
- Methylphenols = **Cresols:** o-Cresol = **2-Methylphenol**, m-Cresol = **3-Methylphenol**, p-Cresol = **4-Methylphenol**.

### (c) Ethers (**Alkoxyalkane**)
- **Bade (longer) carbon group ko parent alkane** banao, aur **chhote group + O ko alkoxy prefix** (methoxy, ethoxy, phenoxy)!
- Examples:
  - CH₃–O–CH₂CH₃ → **Methoxyethane**
  - C₆H₅–O–CH₃ → **Methoxybenzene** (**Anisole**)
  - C₆H₅–O–C₂H₅ → **Ethoxybenzene** (**Phenetole**)
  - CH₃–O–CH(CH₃)₂ → **2-Methoxypropane**

## 3. Structures of Functional Groups (Bond Angles & Bond Lengths)

Teeno mein oxygen **sp³ hybridised** hai (2 bond pairs + 2 lone pairs → bent/tetrahedral geometry):

| Property | Methanol (CH₃–OH) | Phenol (C₆H₅–OH) | Methoxymethane (CH₃–O–CH₃) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bond Angle | **C–O–H = 108.9°** | **C–O–H = 109°** | **C–O–C = 111.7°** |
| C–O Bond Length | **142 pm** | **136 pm** (shorter!) | **141 pm** |

### Why These Exact Differences?
1. **Alcohol C–O–H angle (108.9° < 109.5°):** Oxygen par maujood do **lone pairs** ka lone pair–lone pair repulsion bond angle ko tetrahedral 109.5° se thoda kam (108.9°) kar deta hai!
2. **Ether C–O–C angle (111.7° > 109.5°):** Do bulky alkyl (–CH₃) groups ke beech ka **steric repulsion** lone-pair repulsion par bhari padta hai, isliye angle 109.5° se badh kar **111.7°** ho jata hai!
3. **Phenol C–O bond shorter (136 pm vs 142 pm in methanol):** Do kaaran — (i) Oxygen ka lone pair benzene ring ke saath **resonance** karke C–O bond ko **partial double-bond character** deta hai, aur (ii) Phenol ka ring carbon **sp² hybridised** (zyada s-character) hota hai!

## Solved Examples

### Example 1: Writing IUPAC Names of Alcohols, Phenols & Ethers
Write the IUPAC names of:
(a) CH₃–CH(CH₃)–CH(OH)–CH₃
(b) C₆H₅–O–CH₂–CH₂–CH₃
(c) 1,3-Dihydroxybenzene (Resorcinol)

**Solution:**
(a) Longest chain = 4 carbons; number from right so –OH gets C2 → **3-Methylbutan-2-ol**.
(b) Larger group is benzene (6 C), smaller is propoxy (3 C) → **1-Propoxybenzene** (or propoxybenzene).
(c) **Benzene-1,3-diol**.

Jaanch: Lowest locant for –OH (C2) in (a); larger ring is parent in (b); 'e' retained before 'diol' in (c) ✅

---

### Example 2: Comparing Bond Angles in Methanol and Dimethyl Ether
Why is the C–O–C bond angle in methoxymethane (111.7°) larger than the C–O–H bond angle in methanol (108.9°)?

**Solution:**
In methanol, lone pair–lone pair repulsion on the sp³ oxygen compresses the C–O–H angle slightly below the ideal tetrahedral angle (109.5°) to 108.9°. In methoxymethane (CH₃–O–CH₃), the steric repulsion between the **two bulky methyl groups** exceeds the lone-pair repulsion, opening the C–O–C bond angle to **111.7°**.

Jaanch: 111.7° > 109.5° > 108.9° ✅

---

### Example 3: Distinguishing Phenol from Benzylic Alcohol
Is C₆H₅–CH₂–OH a phenol or an alcohol? Give reason.

**Solution:**
It is a **primary benzylic alcohol** (phenylmethanol), **NOT a phenol**, because the –OH group is attached to an **sp³-hybridised side-chain carbon (–CH₂–)** rather than directly to an sp² carbon of the benzene ring.

Jaanch: Phenol requires Ar–OH (direct sp² ring attachment); C₆H₅–CH₂–OH has an intervening CH₂ ✅

## Aam Galtiyan (Common Mistakes)

1. **Ether ke IUPAC naam mein chhote group ko parent alkane bana dena:**
   - *Galat:* CH₃–O–CH₂CH₃ ko ethoxymethane likhna.
   - *Sahi:* Hamesha **bade chain ko parent alkane** banao → **Methoxyethane**!

2. **Diol/Triol ke naam mein alkane ka 'e' hata dena:**
   - *Galat:* propan-1,2,3-triol.
   - *Sahi:* Jab suffix consonant ('d' ya 't') se shuru ho toh 'e' rakhte hain → **propane-1,2,3-triol**!

3. **Benzyl alcohol ko phenol samajh lena:**
   - *Galat:* C₆H₅–CH₂OH ek phenol hai.
   - *Sahi:* Yeh **benzylic alcohol** hai; phenol sirf **C₆H₅–OH** hai!

---

## Quick Recap

- **Alcohols:** sp³ C–OH (1°, 2°, 3°, allylic, benzylic); suffix **-ol**.
- **Phenols:** sp² aromatic C–OH; Catechol (1,2), Resorcinol (1,3), Hydroquinone (1,4); Cresols = methylphenols.
- **Ethers:** **Alkoxyalkane** (smaller group = alkoxy, larger = parent); Anisole = methoxybenzene.
- **Structure:** Methanol C–O–H = **108.9°** (142 pm); Phenol C–O = **136 pm** (resonance + sp² C); Ether C–O–C = **111.7°** (bulky R–R steric repulsion).

## Practice Questions

1. Classify each of the following as 1°, 2°, 3°, allylic, benzylic alcohol, or phenol:
   (a) CH₂=CH–CH₂OH, (b) (CH₃)₃C–OH, (c) p-CH₃–C₆H₄–OH, (d) C₆H₅–CH(OH)–CH₃.
2. Write the IUPAC names of:
   (a) HO–CH₂–CH(OH)–CH₂–OH
   (b) CH₃–CH₂–O–CH(CH₃)₂
   (c) (CH₃)₂CH–CH₂–OH
3. Write the structures and IUPAC names of catechol, resorcinol, and hydroquinone.
4. Explain why the C–O bond length in phenol (136 pm) is shorter than in methanol (142 pm).
5. Why is the C–O–C bond angle in ethers slightly greater than the tetrahedral angle of 109.5°?
6. Give the IUPAC name of: (a) Anisole, (b) Phenetole, (c) o-Cresol.
7. Write all structural isomers of alcohols with molecular formula C₄H₁₀O and give their IUPAC names.
8. Write all structural isomers of ethers with molecular formula C₄H₁₀O and give their IUPAC names.
9. What is the hybridisation of the oxygen atom in alcohols, phenols, and ethers?
10. Why is vinyl alcohol (CH₂=CH–OH) unstable? What does it convert into?

---

### Answers

1. (a) **1° allylic alcohol**, (b) **3° alkyl alcohol**, (c) **phenol** (4-methylphenol), (d) **2° benzylic alcohol**.
2. (a) **Propane-1,2,3-triol** (glycerol)
   (b) **2-Ethoxypropane** (parent = 3C propane, substituent = ethoxy at C2)
   (c) **2-Methylpropan-1-ol**
3. - **Catechol:** Benzene-1,2-diol (two –OH ortho)
   - **Resorcinol:** Benzene-1,3-diol (two –OH meta)
   - **Hydroquinone:** Benzene-1,4-diol (two –OH para)
4. Two reasons: (i) **Resonance** between oxygen's lone pair and the benzene π-system gives the C–O bond **partial double-bond character**; (ii) the benzene carbon is **sp² hybridised** (higher s-character = shorter bond) vs sp³ in methanol.
5. Because steric repulsion between the **two bulky alkyl groups (R and R')** bonded to oxygen pushes the bonds apart beyond 109.5° (to ~111.7°).
6. (a) **Methoxybenzene**, (b) **Ethoxybenzene**, (c) **2-Methylphenol**.
7. Four alcohol isomers of C₄H₁₀O:
   1. CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂OH: **Butan-1-ol** (1°)
   2. CH₃CH₂CH(OH)CH₃: **Butan-2-ol** (2°)
   3. (CH₃)₂CHCH₂OH: **2-Methylpropan-1-ol** (1°)
   4. (CH₃)₃COH: **2-Methylpropan-2-ol** (3°)
8. Three ether isomers of C₄H₁₀O:
   1. CH₃–O–CH₂CH₂CH₃: **1-Methoxypropane**
   2. CH₃–O–CH(CH₃)₂: **2-Methoxypropane**
   3. CH₃CH₂–O–CH₂CH₃: **Ethoxyethane**
   Jaanch: 4 alcohols + 3 ethers = 7 total structural isomers of C₄H₁₀O ✅
9. **sp³ hybridised** in all three (2 bond pairs + 2 lone pairs).
10. Vinyl alcohol is an unstable enol; it rapidly tautomerises (keto–enol tautomerism) into the much more stable aldehyde **ethanal (acetaldehyde, CH₃–CHO)**.
