## 1. Crossed & Intramolecular Cannizzaro Reactions

### (a) Crossed Cannizzaro Reaction (HCHO + ArCHO + conc. NaOH)
When **formaldehyde (HCHO)** reacts with any other non-enolisable aldehyde (e.g., benzaldehyde) in conc. NaOH:
- **HCHO is ALWAYS oxidised to formate (HCOO⁻)**, and the **other aldehyde is ALWAYS reduced to alcohol (Ar–CH₂OH)**!
- Why? Because HCHO is the smallest, most electrophilic aldehyde, so OH⁻ attacks HCHO first to form the hydride donor [H–CH(OH)O⁻], which transfers H⁻ to Ar–CHO!
- **Example:** HCHO + C₆H₅–CHO + conc. NaOH → **HCOONa + C₆H₅–CH₂OH (Benzyl alcohol)**!

### (b) Intramolecular Cannizzaro Reaction
Dialdehydes with no α-H undergo internal hydride shift:
- **Glyoxal (OHC–CHO) + conc. NaOH → HO–CH₂–COONa** (sodium glycolate) —(H⁺)→ **Glycolic acid**!
- **Phenylglyoxal (C₆H₅–CO–CHO) + conc. NaOH → C₆H₅–CH(OH)–COONa** (OH⁻ attacks more electrophilic –CHO, transfers H⁻ to >C=O → **Mandelic acid**!).

## 2. Intramolecular Aldol Condensation (Ring-Forming Magic!)

When a molecule has **two carbonyl groups** separated by 4 or 5 carbons (1,4-, 1,5-, 1,6-, or 1,7-dicarbonyls), dilute NaOH + Δ closes a stable **5-membered or 6-membered α,β-unsaturated cyclic ketone**!
- **1,6-Diketone (Hexane-2,5-dione is 1,4-diketone → 5-ring; Heptane-2,6-dione is 1,5-diketone → 6-ring!):**
  - **Hexane-2,5-dione** (CH₃COCH₂CH₂COCH₃) + dil. NaOH/Δ → **3-Methylcyclopent-2-en-1-one** (5-membered ring — never 3-membered!).
  - **Heptane-2,6-dione** + dil. NaOH/Δ → **3-Methylcyclohex-2-en-1-one** (6-membered ring!).
  - **Golden Rule:** Always choose the enolate that forms a strain-free **5- or 6-membered ring** (never 3- or 4-membered)!

---

## 3. Perkin Condensation & Baeyer–Villiger Oxidation

### (a) Perkin Condensation (Aromatic Aldehyde → α,β-Unsaturated Acid)
**C₆H₅–CHO + (CH₃CO)₂O —(CH₃COONa, 453 K; then H₃O⁺)→ C₆H₅–CH=CH–COOH (Cinnamic acid)** + CH₃COOH.
- Uses the sodium salt of the same acid as the anhydride (acts as base to generate enolate from the anhydride!).

### (b) Baeyer–Villiger Oxidation (Ketone + Peroxyacid → Ester!)
**R–CO–R' + R''CO₃H (e.g., m-CPBA or CF₃CO₃H) → R–CO–O–R'** (an oxygen atom inserts next to C=O!).
- **Which group migrates (gets the oxygen)?** Follow **Migratory Aptitude** (more electron-rich group migrates preferentially!):
  **–H > 3° alkyl > cyclohexyl > 2° alkyl ≈ phenyl > 1° alkyl > –CH₃**!
- **Example:** **C₆H₅–CO–CH₃ (Acetophenone) + m-CPBA → C₆H₅–O–CO–CH₃ (Phenyl acetate)** (Ph migrates over CH₃!).
- **Cyclic ketone → Lactone** (ring expands by +1 O atom: Cyclopentanone → δ-valerolactone!).

## 4. The Ortho-Effect in Substituted Benzoic Acids

Almost **ANY ortho-substituted benzoic acid** (whether EWG like –NO₂, –Cl, –OH OR EDG like –CH₃, –OCH₃!) is a **STRONGER acid than benzoic acid** itself!
- **Cause (SIR = Steric Inhibition of Resonance):** The bulky ortho substituent twists the –COOH group out of the plane of the benzene ring.
  - In neutral benzoic acid, cross-conjugation (+R from the benzene ring into C=O) normally weakens acidity. Twisting **destroys this cross-conjugation**, making the neutral acid less stable!
  - In the twisted benzoate anion (Ar–COO⁻), the two oxygens still resonate equally with each other (unaffected by twisting!), AND hydration is improved → **overall equilibrium shifts strongly to H⁺ release!**
- **Classic Exam Orders (Must Memorise!):**
  - **Toluic acids (–CH₃):** **o-Toluic (3.91) > Benzoic (4.19) > m-Toluic (4.27) > p-Toluic (4.36)**!
  - **Nitrobenzoic acids (–NO₂):** **o-Nitro (2.17) > p-Nitro (3.42) > m-Nitro (3.49) > Benzoic (4.19)**!
  - **Hydroxybenzoic acids (–OH):** **o-Hydroxy / Salicylic (2.98) > m-Hydroxy (4.08) > Benzoic (4.19) > p-Hydroxy (4.58)**!

## Deep Solved Problems

### Problem 1: Predicting Crossed Cannizzaro Products
Predict the products of treating **p-methoxybenzaldehyde** and **formaldehyde** with 50% NaOH.

**Solution:**
Formaldehyde (HCHO) is always more electrophilic and is oxidised to **sodium formate (HCOONa)**, while p-methoxybenzaldehyde is reduced to **p-methoxybenzyl alcohol (p-CH₃O–C₆H₄–CH₂OH)**.

Jaanch: HCHO → HCOO⁻ (oxidised); ArCHO → ArCH₂OH (reduced) ✅

---

### Problem 2: Predicting Intramolecular Aldol Product
Predict the major product when **heptane-2,6-dione** (CH₃–CO–CH₂–CH₂–CH₂–CO–CH₃) is heated with dilute NaOH.

**Solution:**
Two possible enolates:
- At C1 (terminal –CH₃): attacks C6 carbonyl → forms a strain-free **6-membered ring**!
- At C3 (internal –CH₂–): attacks C6 carbonyl → forms a strained 4-membered ring (unfavoured!).
Therefore, C1 enolate attacks C6, followed by dehydration → **3-Methylcyclohex-2-en-1-one**!

Jaanch: 6-membered ring (C1 to C6 = 6 carbons in ring) beats 4-membered ring ✅

---

### Problem 3: Baeyer–Villiger Oxidation of an Unsymmetrical Ketone
Predict the major ester formed when **2-methylcyclopentanone** reacts with m-CPBA (meta-chloroperoxybenzoic acid).

**Solution:**
The two carbons attached to C=O are **C2 (2° carbon bearing –CH₃)** and **C5 (1°/CH₂ ring carbon)**.
By migratory aptitude (2° > 1°), the **more substituted C2** migrates to oxygen → oxygen inserts between C1(=O) and C2(CH–CH₃) → **6-methyltetrahydro-2H-pyran-2-one** (6-membered lactone with –CH₃ adjacent to ring oxygen!).

Jaanch: Migratory aptitude 3° > 2° > 1° → oxygen inserts on the more substituted side ✅

---

### Problem 4: Explaining Why o-Toluic Acid Is Stronger Than p-Toluic Acid
Explain why o-methylbenzoic acid (pKₐ 3.91) is stronger than both benzoic acid (pKₐ 4.19) and p-methylbenzoic acid (pKₐ 4.36).

**Solution:**
In **p-methylbenzoic acid**, the –CH₃ group releases electrons (+I, hyperconjugation) toward –COOH, weakening acidity compared to benzoic acid. In **o-methylbenzoic acid**, the bulky ortho –CH₃ twists the –COOH group out of the benzene ring plane (**Steric Inhibition of Resonance / Ortho-Effect**), preventing electron-donating resonance from the ring into C=O while leaving the benzoate anion's equivalent O–C–O resonance intact — making o-toluic acid stronger than benzoic acid!

Jaanch: pKₐ order: o-toluic (3.91) < benzoic (4.19) < p-toluic (4.36) ✅

---

### Problem 5: Rate-Determining Step of Haloform Reaction
In the base-catalysed bromination (haloform reaction) of acetone (CH₃COCH₃ + Br₂ + OH⁻), the rate is found to be **independent of [Br₂]** (Rate = k [CH₃COCH₃][OH⁻]). Explain why!

**Solution:**
The rate-determining step (Step 1) is the **removal of an α-proton by OH⁻ to form the enolate ion** (CH₃–CO–CH₂⁻). Once formed, the enolate reacts **very rapidly** with Br₂ (fast step, does not appear in rate law!). Hence halogenation is zero-order in [Br₂], and replacing Br₂ with Cl₂ or I₂ gives the exact same reaction rate!

Jaanch: RDS = enolate formation → Rate = k[Acetone][OH⁻] (zero order in halogen!) ✅

## Advanced Practice Questions

1. Predict the product of treating **glyoxal (OHC–CHO)** with concentrated NaOH followed by H₃O⁺.
2. Predict the major product of **intramolecular aldol condensation** of **hexane-2,5-dione** with dilute NaOH (Δ).
3. Write the **Perkin condensation** reaction between benzaldehyde and propanoic anhydride in the presence of sodium propanoate.
4. Predict the major ester formed by **Baeyer–Villiger oxidation** (m-CPBA) of: (a) tert-butyl methyl ketone, (b) p-methoxyacetophenone.
5. Arrange o-hydroxybenzoic acid, m-hydroxybenzoic acid, p-hydroxybenzoic acid, and benzoic acid in decreasing order of acid strength.
6. Why does **2,6-dihydroxybenzoic acid** have an exceptionally low pKₐ (~1.30), even lower than salicylic acid (2.98)?
7. Predict the products of **crossed Cannizzaro reaction** between **2,2-dimethylpropanal** and **formaldehyde** with 50% NaOH.
8. Explain why **2,4,6-trimethylbenzaldehyde** does NOT react with NaHSO₃, whereas **ethanal** reacts rapidly.
9. In base-catalysed iodination of acetone, if CH₃COCH₃ is replaced by deuterated CD₃COCD₃, the rate drops by ~7 times (primary kinetic isotope effect). What does this prove about the RDS?
10. Predict the major product when **phenylglyoxal (C₆H₅–CO–CHO)** is treated with conc. NaOH followed by H⁺.

---

### Solutions

1. Intramolecular Cannizzaro reaction → **HO–CH₂–COOH (glycolic acid / 2-hydroxyethanoic acid)**.
2. C1 enolate attacks C5 carbonyl to form a 5-membered ring (beating a 3-membered ring) → **3-Methylcyclopent-2-en-1-one**.
3. C₆H₅–CHO + (CH₃CH₂CO)₂O —(CH₃CH₂COONa, Δ; H₃O⁺)→ **C₆H₅–CH=C(CH₃)–COOH (α-methylcinnamic acid)**.
   *(Always the α-carbon of the anhydride attacks the aldehyde!)*
4. (a) 3° tert-butyl migrates over CH₃ → **tert-butyl acetate (CH₃–CO–O–C(CH₃)₃)**.
   (b) Electron-rich p-methoxyphenyl migrates over CH₃ → **p-methoxyphenyl acetate (CH₃–CO–O–C₆H₄–OCH₃)**.
   Jaanch: Migratory aptitude 3° > aryl > CH₃ ✅
5. Decreasing acid strength: **o-Hydroxybenzoic acid (2.98) > m-Hydroxybenzoic acid (4.08) > Benzoic acid (4.19) > p-Hydroxybenzoic acid (4.58)**.
   *(o = H-bond + ortho effect; m = −I only; p = +R > −I weakens acidity!)*
6. Because **BOTH** ortho –OH groups form **two simultaneous intramolecular hydrogen bonds** with BOTH oxygens of the –COO⁻ anion, massively stabilising the conjugate base!
7. HCHO is oxidised → **HCOONa**; 2,2-dimethylpropanal is reduced → **(CH₃)₃C–CH₂OH (neopentyl alcohol)**.
8. Two bulky ortho –CH₃ groups severely crowd the –CHO group (**steric hindrance**), blocking attack by the bulky bisulphite nucleophile (HSO₃⁻).
9. Because C–D bonds are stronger than C–H bonds, the 7× rate drop proves that **C–H bond cleavage (α-proton abstraction to form the enolate)** occurs directly in the **rate-determining step**!
10. OH⁻ attacks the more electrophilic –CHO group, followed by intramolecular hydride shift to C=O → **C₆H₅–CH(OH)–COOH (mandelic acid)**.
