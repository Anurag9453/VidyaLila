## Naye Shabd (Key Terms)

| Term | Hinglish Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Nucleophilic Addition** | Carbonyl carbon (>C=O) par nucleophile (Nu⁻) ka attack (sp² planar → sp³ tetrahedral) — aldehydes & ketones ki signature reaction |
| **Clemmensen Reduction** | >C=O → >CH₂ using **Zn–Hg / conc. HCl** (acid-sensitive groups ke liye mat use karo!) |
| **Wolff–Kishner Reduction** | >C=O → >CH₂ using **NH₂–NH₂ (hydrazine) + KOH / ethylene glycol (Δ)** (base-sensitive groups ke liye mat use karo!) |
| **Tollens' Reagent** | Ammoniacal silver nitrate [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ — **SABHI aldehydes** (aliphatic + aromatic) iske saath **silver mirror** dete hain; ketones nahi! |
| **Fehling's Solution** | Alkaline CuSO₄ + Rochelle salt (Cu²⁺) — sirf **aliphatic aldehydes** reddish-brown Cu₂O ppt dete hain (**benzaldehyde NAHI deta!**) |
| **Aldol Condensation** | **α-Hydrogen wale** aldehydes/ketones + dilute NaOH → β-hydroxy aldehyde/ketone (aldol) —(Δ)→ α,β-unsaturated carbonyl! |
| **Cannizzaro Reaction** | **BINA α-Hydrogen wale** aldehydes (HCHO, C₆H₅CHO) + **conc. NaOH (50%)** → disproportionation (1 mol alcohol + 1 mol carboxylate salt!) |

## 1. Physical Properties & Reactivity in Nucleophilic Addition

- **Boiling Point:** Aldehydes & Ketones > Alkanes & Ethers (strong C=O dipole–dipole), par **< Alcohols** (no self H-bonding!).
- **Why are Aldehydes MORE reactive than Ketones towards Nucleophilic Addition? (2 Reasons!)**
  1. **Steric Reason:** Ketone mein carbonyl carbon par **2 bulky alkyl groups** hote hain (zyada steric hindrance), jabki aldehyde mein sirf 1 alkyl aur 1 chhota H hota hai.
  2. **Electronic Reason:** Ketone mein **2 electron-releasing (+I) alkyl groups** carbonyl carbon ka δ⁺ charge kam kar dete hain (kam electrophilic!).
  - Reactivity order: **HCHO > CH₃CHO > CH₃CH₂CHO > CH₃COCH₃ > C₆H₅CHO > C₆H₅COCH₃** (benzaldehyde resonance ki wajah se aliphatic aldehydes se kam reactive hai!).

---

## 2. Important Nucleophilic Addition Reactions

1. **Addition of HCN (base catalyst OH⁻):** Forms **Cyanohydrin** (R–CH(OH)–CN).
2. **Addition of NaHSO₃:** Forms crystalline **Bisulphite adduct** (water-soluble; used to purify/separate aldehydes & methyl ketones! Bulky ketones don't react).
3. **Addition of Alcohols (dry HCl gas):**
   - Aldehyde + 1 eq. ROH → **Hemiacetal**; + 2nd eq. ROH → **Acetal** (gem-dialkoxy).
   - Ketone + ethylene glycol (dry HCl) → **Cyclic Ketal** (used to protect ketones!).
4. **Addition of Ammonia Derivatives (H₂N–Z, weakly acidic pH ~3.5) → C=N–Z + H₂O:**

| Reagent (H₂N–Z) | Product (>C=N–Z) | Special Use |
|---|---|---|
| Hydroxylamine (NH₂–OH) | **Oxime** (>C=N–OH) | Derivative |
| Hydrazine (NH₂–NH₂) | **Hydrazone** (>C=N–NH₂) | Wolff–Kishner step 1 |
| Phenylhydrazine (C₆H₅NHNH₂) | **Phenylhydrazone** | Derivative |
| **2,4-Dinitrophenylhydrazine (2,4-DNP)** | **2,4-DNP derivative** | **Yellow/orange/red ppt — confirmatory test for ALL aldehydes & ketones!** |
| Semicarbazide (NH₂–NH–CO–NH₂) | **Semicarbazone** | Only –NH₂ NOT attached to C=O attacks (other is resonance-locked!) |
| 1° Amine (R–NH₂) | **Imine (Schiff's base)** (>C=N–R) | Synthetic intermediate |

## 3. Reduction & Oxidation (Distinction Tests!)

### (a) Reduction to Hydrocarbons (>C=O → >CH₂):
- **Clemmensen:** >C=O —(Zn–Hg / conc. HCl)→ **>CH₂** + H₂O.
- **Wolff–Kishner:** >C=O —(1. NH₂NH₂, 2. KOH / ethylene glycol, Δ)→ **>CH₂** + N₂↑.

### (b) Oxidation & Golden Distinction Tests (100% Exam Questions!):
- **Tollens' Test ([Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ / OH⁻, warm):**
  R–CHO + 2[Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ + 3OH⁻ → R–COO⁻ + **2Ag↓ (Silver Mirror!)** + 2H₂O + 4NH₃.
  - Positive: **ALL aldehydes** (aliphatic + benzaldehyde!). Negative: Ketones!
- **Fehling's Test (Fehling A = aq. CuSO₄ + Fehling B = alk. Rochelle salt):**
  R–CHO + 2Cu²⁺ + 5OH⁻ → R–COO⁻ + **Cu₂O↓ (Reddish-Brown ppt!)** + 3H₂O.
  - Positive: **Aliphatic aldehydes ONLY!** Negative: **Aromatic aldehydes (benzaldehyde)** and Ketones!
- **Iodoform (Haloform) Test (I₂ + NaOH, warm):**
  For **Methyl Ketones (CH₃–CO–R)** AND **Ethanal (CH₃–CHO)** (only aldehyde!):
  R–CO–CH₃ + 3I₂ + 4NaOH → **CHI₃↓ (Yellow ppt)** + R–COONa + 3NaI + 3H₂O!
  - *(Bonus: Does NOT touch C=C double bonds!)*

## 4. Reactions Due to α-Hydrogen: Aldol vs Cannizzaro (SUPER IMPORTANT!)

α-Hydrogen of aldehydes/ketones is **acidic** because the resulting **enolate ion** is resonance-stabilised (**⁻CH₂–CH=O ↔ CH₂=CH–O⁻**)!

### (a) Aldol Condensation (REQUIRES at least 1 α-Hydrogen + **Dilute NaOH**!)
- **Self-Aldol of Ethanal (2 moles):**
  2CH₃–CHO —(dil. NaOH)→ **CH₃–CH(OH)–CH₂–CHO** (3-Hydroxybutanal — Aldol) —(Δ, −H₂O)→ **CH₃–CH=CH–CHO (But-2-enal)**!
- **Self-Aldol of Propanone:**
  2CH₃–CO–CH₃ —(Ba(OH)₂)→ (CH₃)₂C(OH)–CH₂–CO–CH₃ —(Δ, −H₂O)→ **(CH₃)₂C=CH–CO–CH₃ (4-Methylpent-3-en-2-one)**.
- **Cross-Aldol:** Two different aldehydes/ketones with α-H give a mixture of **4 products** (2 self + 2 cross). If one has **NO α-H** (e.g., benzaldehyde + acetophenone), a single clean cross-aldol product forms!
  C₆H₅–CHO + CH₃–CO–C₆H₅ —(dil. NaOH, 293 K)→ **C₆H₅–CH=CH–CO–C₆H₅ (1,3-Diphenylprop-2-en-1-one / Benzalacetophenone)**!

### (b) Cannizzaro Reaction (ONLY Aldehydes with **NO α-Hydrogen** + **Conc. NaOH (50%)**!)
Self oxidation–reduction (**disproportionation**): 1 molecule oxidises to carboxylic acid salt, 1 molecule reduces to alcohol!
- **2HCHO + conc. KOH → CH₃OH (Methanol) + HCOOK (Potassium formate)**
- **2C₆H₅–CHO + conc. NaOH → C₆H₅–CH₂OH (Benzyl alcohol) + C₆H₅–COONa (Sodium benzoate)**

### (c) Electrophilic Substitution of Benzaldehyde:
–CHO is **meta-directing and deactivating** (−R, −I):
C₆H₅–CHO + conc. HNO₃ / H₂SO₄ (273–283 K) → **m-Nitrobenzaldehyde (3-nitrobenzaldehyde)**.

## Solved Examples

### Example 1: Distinguishing Three Carbonyl Pairs Chemically
Give ONE chemical test to distinguish between:
(a) Propanal and Propanone
(b) Ethanal and Propanal
(c) Benzaldehyde and Propanal

**Solution:**
(a) **Tollens' test (or Fehling's test):** **Propanal** gives a silver mirror (or red-brown Cu₂O ppt); **Propanone** gives NO reaction. (Or Iodoform test: propanone gives yellow CHI₃ ppt; propanal does not!)
(b) **Iodoform test (I₂ + NaOH):** **Ethanal (CH₃CHO)** gives a **yellow precipitate of CHI₃**; **Propanal (CH₃CH₂CHO)** does NOT.
(c) **Fehling's test:** **Propanal** (aliphatic aldehyde) gives a **reddish-brown precipitate of Cu₂O**; **Benzaldehyde** (aromatic aldehyde) gives NO reaction!

Jaanch: Every test gives an unambiguous positive vs negative visual result ✅

---

### Example 2: Classifying Compounds into Aldol vs Cannizzaro
Which of the following will undergo (i) Aldol condensation, (ii) Cannizzaro reaction, (iii) Neither?
Methanal, 2-Methylpentanal, Benzaldehyde, Benzophenone, 2,2-Dimethylbutanal, Cyclohexanone.

**Solution:**
- **Aldol condensation (needs α-H):** **2-Methylpentanal** (1 α-H), **Cyclohexanone** (4 α-H).
- **Cannizzaro reaction (aldehyde with NO α-H):** **Methanal (HCHO)**, **Benzaldehyde (C₆H₅CHO)**, **2,2-Dimethylbutanal ((CH₃)₂C(C₂H₅)–CHO)**.
- **Neither:** **Benzophenone (C₆H₅–CO–C₆H₅)** (ketone with no α-H!).

Jaanch: α-H count checked for all 6 compounds ✅

---

### Example 3: Choosing Between Clemmensen and Wolff–Kishner Reduction
You want to reduce **HO–CH₂–CH₂–CO–CH₃** to **HO–CH₂–CH₂–CH₂–CH₃** without destroying the –OH group. Should you use Clemmensen or Wolff–Kishner reduction?

**Solution:**
Use **Wolff–Kishner reduction (NH₂NH₂ + KOH / ethylene glycol, Δ)**! Clemmensen reduction uses concentrated HCl, which would protonate the acid-sensitive –OH group and convert it into –Cl or cause dehydration to an alkene. Basic Wolff–Kishner leaves –OH untouched!

Jaanch: Acid-sensitive group (–OH, acetal) → Wolff–Kishner; Base-sensitive group (–Br, ester) → Clemmensen ✅

## Aam Galtiyan (Common Mistakes)

1. **Benzaldehyde ko Fehling's test positive bol dena:**
   - *Galat:* Benzaldehyde ek aldehyde hai toh Fehling's test dega.
   - *Sahi:* Benzaldehyde **Tollens' test deta hai**, par **Fehling's test NAHI deta**! (Aromatic aldehydes ko aliphatic aldehydes se alag karne ka yahi best test hai!)

2. **Aldol aur Cannizzaro mein NaOH ki concentration aur α-H condition ulta kar dena:**
   - *Galat:* HCHO dil. NaOH se aldol deta hai.
   - *Sahi:* **Aldol** = **α-H present** + **dilute NaOH**; **Cannizzaro** = **NO α-H** + **concentrated NaOH (50%)**!

3. **Semicarbazide (H₂N–NH–CO–NH₂) ke dono –NH₂ ko barabar reactive maan lena:**
   - *Galat:* C=O ke bagal wala –NH₂ carbonyl par attack karta hai.
   - *Sahi:* C=O ke bagal wale –NH₂ ka lone pair amide resonance mein busy rehta hai → sirf **door wala (hydrazine-end) –NH₂** attack karta hai!

## Quick Recap

- **Nucleophilic Addition Reactivity:** HCHO > RCHO > RCOR' (less steric hindrance + less +I effect).
- **2,4-DNP test:** Positive for ALL aldehydes & ketones (yellow/orange/red ppt).
- **Tollens':** ALL aldehydes (silver mirror). **Fehling's:** Aliphatic aldehydes ONLY (red-brown Cu₂O). **Iodoform:** CH₃CO– & CH₃CHO (yellow CHI₃).
- **>C=O → >CH₂:** Clemmensen (Zn–Hg/HCl) or Wolff–Kishner (NH₂NH₂/KOH/glycol).
- **Aldol:** α-H + dil. NaOH → β-hydroxy carbonyl → α,β-unsaturated carbonyl.
- **Cannizzaro:** NO α-H aldehyde + conc. NaOH → alcohol + carboxylate salt.

### Answers

1. (i) **Steric factor:** Two bulky alkyl groups in ketones hinder nucleophile approach more than one alkyl + one small H in aldehydes. (ii) **Electronic factor:** Two +I alkyl groups in ketones reduce the electrophilicity (δ⁺) of the carbonyl carbon more than one alkyl group in aldehydes.
2. Increasing reactivity: **Butanone < Propanone < Propanal < Ethanal**.
3. The –NH₂ group directly attached to the carbonyl group (–CO–NH₂) has its lone pair delocalised into C=O by **resonance**, making it non-nucleophilic. Therefore, only the terminal –NH₂ (hydrazine end) attacks the carbonyl compound.
4. (a) CH₃–CO–CH₃ —(Zn–Hg / conc. HCl)→ **CH₃–CH₂–CH₃ (propane)** + H₂O.
   (b) C₆H₅–CHO —(1. NH₂NH₂, 2. KOH / ethylene glycol, Δ)→ **C₆H₅–CH₃ (toluene)** + N₂↑.
5. (a) 2CH₃CHO —(dil. NaOH)→ CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CHO —(Δ, −H₂O)→ **CH₃–CH=CH–CHO (but-2-enal)**.
   (b) 2C₆H₅CHO + conc. NaOH → **C₆H₅CH₂OH (benzyl alcohol) + C₆H₅COONa (sodium benzoate)**.
6. (a) **Iodoform test (I₂ + NaOH):** **Acetophenone (C₆H₅COCH₃)** gives yellow CHI₃ ppt; **benzophenone** does not.
   (b) **Iodoform test (I₂ + NaOH):** **Pentan-2-one** (has CH₃CO–) gives yellow CHI₃ ppt; **pentan-3-one** does not.
   (c) **Fehling's test (or Iodoform test):** **Ethanal** gives red-brown Cu₂O ppt (and yellow CHI₃ ppt); **benzaldehyde** gives neither!
7. Because of the strong electron-withdrawing (−I) effect of the adjacent C=O group AND **resonance stabilisation of the conjugate base (enolate ion)** after losing H⁺.
8. C₆H₅–CHO + CH₃–CHO —(dil. NaOH, Δ)→ **C₆H₅–CH=CH–CHO (cinnamaldehyde / 3-phenylprop-2-enal)** + H₂O.
9. **3-Nitrobenzaldehyde (m-nitrobenzaldehyde)** because –CHO is electron-withdrawing (−R, −I) and therefore **meta-directing**.
10. (a) 2CH₃CHO —(dil. NaOH, Δ, Aldol)→ **CH₃–CH=CH–CHO**.
    (b) 2CH₃CHO —(dil. NaOH)→ CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CHO —(NaBH₄)→ **CH₃–CH(OH)–CH₂–CH₂OH (butane-1,3-diol)**.
    (c) CH₃COCH₃ —(NaBH₄)→ CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ —(conc. H₂SO₄, 443 K)→ **CH₃–CH=CH₂ (propene)**.
    Jaanch: All three multi-step routes are standard, high-yield sequences ✅
