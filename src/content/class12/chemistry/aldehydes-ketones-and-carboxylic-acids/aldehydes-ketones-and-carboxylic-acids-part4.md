## Naye Shabd (Key Terms)

| Term | Hinglish Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Hydrogen-Bonded Dimer** | Do carboxylic acid molecules ka apas mein 2 strong H-bonds se judkar ek cyclic pair banana (vapour phase ya benzene mein molar mass double dikhta hai!) |
| **Carboxylate Ion (R–COO⁻)** | Carboxylic acid se H⁺ nikalne par bana conjugate base, jismein negative charge **do equivalent oxygen atoms** par barabar delocalise hota hai |
| **Soda Lime** | NaOH + CaO (3:1 ratio) ka mixture jo sodium carboxylate ko heat karne par **CO₂ nikal kar alkane** banata hai (**Decarboxylation**) |
| **HVZ Reaction (Hell–Volhard–Zelinsky)** | α-Hydrogen wale carboxylic acid + X₂ (Cl₂/Br₂) + small **Red Phosphorus**, phir H₂O → **α-Halocarboxylic acid** |

## 1. Physical Properties of Carboxylic Acids

- **Very High Boiling Points:** Comparable molar mass ke aldehydes, ketones aur **even alcohols** se bhi zyada BP! Kyun? Kyunki carboxylic acids extensive **intermolecular H-bonding** karte hain aur **vapour phase / aprotic solvents (jaise benzene) mein cyclic DIMERS** ke roop mein rehte hain!
- **Solubility:** Lower aliphatic acids (C₁–C₄) paani ke saath H-bonding ki wajah se miscible hain; **Benzoic acid** thande paani mein lagbhag insoluble hai (large hydrophobic benzene ring).

---

## 2. Acidic Nature of Carboxylic Acids (HIGH-YIELD EXAM TOPIC!)

Carboxylic acids active metals (Na), bases (NaOH), aur **weak bases like NaHCO₃** sabhi ke saath react karte hain:
**R–COOH + NaHCO₃ → R–COONa + H₂O + CO₂↑ (Brisk effervescence — confirmatory test for –COOH vs Phenol!)**

### Why Are Carboxylic Acids MUCH Stronger Than Phenols?
- **Carboxylate ion (R–COO⁻)** mein negative charge **do EQUIVALENT electronegative oxygen atoms** par delocalise hota hai (**R–C(=O)–O⁻ ↔ R–C(O⁻)=O**)!
- **Phenoxide ion (C₆H₅–O⁻)** mein negative charge kam electronegative **carbon atoms** par delocalise hota hai (non-equivalent resonance structures)!
- Isliye **Carboxylate ion >> Phenoxide ion** in stability → **R–COOH (pKₐ ~4–5) >> Phenol (pKₐ ~10) >> Alcohol (pKₐ ~16)**!

### Effect of Substituents on Acid Strength:
1. **Electron-Withdrawing Groups (EWG: –F, –Cl, –Br, –CN, –NO₂, –CF₃)** disperse negative charge on –COO⁻ → **INCREASE acidity** (decrease pKₐ)!
   - **Master Order:** **CF₃COOH > CCl₃COOH > CHCl₂COOH > O₂N–CH₂COOH > NC–CH₂COOH > F–CH₂COOH > Cl–CH₂COOH > Br–CH₂COOH > HCOOH > Cl–CH₂CH₂COOH > C₆H₅COOH > CH₃COOH > CH₃CH₂COOH**!
   - *(Notice: **Inductive effect distance ke saath tezi se ghat-ta hai!** 2-Chlorobutanoic acid > 3-Chlorobutanoic acid > 4-Chlorobutanoic acid!)*
2. **Electron-Donating Groups (EDG: –CH₃, –C₂H₅, –OCH₃)** intensify negative charge on –COO⁻ → **DECREASE acidity**!
   - **HCOOH (no +I group) > CH₃COOH > CH₃CH₂COOH > (CH₃)₃CCOOH**.
3. **Benzoic Acid vs Acetic Acid:** **C₆H₅COOH (pKₐ 4.19) > CH₃COOH (pKₐ 4.76)** — kyunki phenyl ring ka **sp² carbon** zyada electronegative (−I) hota hai!
   - Substituted benzoic acids: **p-Nitrobenzoic acid > Benzoic acid > p-Methoxybenzoic acid**!

## 3. Chemical Reactions of Carboxylic Acids

### (a) Cleavage of C–OH Bond (Formation of Acid Derivatives)
1. **Anhydride formation:** 2R–COOH —(P₂O₅ or H₂SO₄, Δ)→ **(R–CO)₂O** + H₂O.
2. **Esterification:** R–COOH + R'–OH —(conc. H₂SO₄ / dry HCl)→ **R–COOR'** + H₂O.
3. **Acid chloride formation:**
   - R–COOH + **SOCl₂** → **R–COCl** + SO₂↑ + HCl↑ (BEST method — gaseous by-products!).
   - R–COOH + PCl₅ → R–COCl + POCl₃ + HCl.
4. **Reaction with NH₃ → Amide (via Ammonium salt):**
   - CH₃COOH + NH₃ ⇌ CH₃COONH₄ —(Δ, −H₂O)→ **CH₃CONH₂ (Ethanamide)**.
   - **Phthalic acid + 2NH₃ → Ammonium phthalate —(Δ)→ Phthalamide —(strong Δ, −NH₃)→ Phthalimide!**

### (b) Reactions Involving the –COOH Group as a Whole
1. **Reduction to 1° Alcohol:** R–COOH —(1. LiAlH₄/ether or B₂H₆, 2. H₃O⁺)→ **R–CH₂OH** *(B₂H₆ esters/nitro/halides ko nahi chhoota; NaBH₄ acids ko reduce NAHI karta!)*.
2. **Decarboxylation (Step-Down by −1 Carbon!):**
   - **Soda Lime (NaOH + CaO, Δ):** **R–COONa + NaOH —(CaO, Δ)→ R–H (Alkane) + Na₂CO₃**!
     *(Example: Sodium propanoate CH₃CH₂COONa → **Ethane CH₃–CH₃**!)*
   - **Kolbe Electrolysis:** 2R–COONa + 2H₂O —(electrolysis)→ **R–R** (at anode) + 2CO₂ + H₂ + 2NaOH.

### (c) Substitution in Hydrocarbon Part: **HVZ Reaction**
**Hell–Volhard–Zelinsky (HVZ) Reaction** (Requires **α-Hydrogen**!):
**R–CH₂–COOH —(1. X₂ / Red P, 2. H₂O)→ R–CH(X)–COOH**   (X = Cl or Br)
- Example: CH₃COOH —(1. Cl₂ / Red P, 2. H₂O)→ **Cl–CH₂–COOH (2-Chloroethanoic acid)**.
- *(Note: Formic acid HCOOH aur Benzoic acid C₆H₅COOH mein α-H nahi hai → NO HVZ reaction!)*

### (d) Ring Substitution in Benzoic Acid:
–COOH is **meta-directing and strongly deactivating** (−R, −I):
- Nitration (conc. HNO₃/H₂SO₄) → **m-Nitrobenzoic acid**.
- Bromination (Br₂ / FeBr₃) → **m-Bromobenzoic acid**.
- **CRITICAL EXAM FACT:** **Benzoic acid does NOT undergo Friedel–Crafts reaction!** Kyun? Do kaaran: (i) –COOH strongly deactivating hai, aur (ii) Lewis acid catalyst **AlCl₃** carboxyl group (–COOH) ke saath bond banakar salt bana leta hai!

## 4. Uses of Carboxylic Acids
- **Methanoic acid (HCOOH):** Rubber, textile, dyeing, leather and electroplating industries.
- **Ethanoic acid (CH₃COOH):** Vinegar (food industry), solvent.
- **Hexanedioic acid (Adipic acid):** Manufacture of **Nylon-6,6**.
- **Sodium benzoate:** Food preservative; esters of benzoic acid in perfumery.
- **Higher fatty acids:** Manufacture of soaps and detergents.

## Solved Examples

### Example 1: Explaining Acidity Order of Chloroacetic Acids
Arrange CH₃COOH, ClCH₂COOH, Cl₂CHCOOH, and Cl₃CCOOH in increasing order of acid strength. Give reason.

**Solution:**
Chlorine is strongly electron-withdrawing (**−I effect**), which disperses the negative charge of the carboxylate anion (–COO⁻) and stabilises it. As the number of Cl atoms increases (0 → 1 → 2 → 3), the −I effect and carboxylate stability increase sharply.
Increasing order: **CH₃COOH < ClCH₂COOH < Cl₂CHCOOH < Cl₃CCOOH**.

Jaanch: pKₐ drops dramatically: 4.76 → 2.87 → 1.26 → 0.65 ✅

---

### Example 2: Comparing Benzoic Acid, p-Nitrobenzoic Acid & p-Methoxybenzoic Acid
Arrange in decreasing order of acid strength: 4-Nitrobenzoic acid, Benzoic acid, 4-Methoxybenzoic acid.

**Solution:**
- **–NO₂** is strongly electron-withdrawing (−R, −I) → stabilises benzoate ion → **strongest acid**.
- **–OCH₃** at para is electron-donating (+R > −I) → destabilises benzoate ion → **weakest acid**.
Decreasing order: **4-Nitrobenzoic acid > Benzoic acid > 4-Methoxybenzoic acid**.

Jaanch: EWG (–NO₂) > H > EDG (–OCH₃) ✅

---

### Example 3: Step-Down Conversion Using Soda Lime Decarboxylation
How will you convert **propanoic acid** into **ethane**?

**Solution:**
Step 1: Neutralise with NaOH → CH₃CH₂COOH + NaOH → CH₃CH₂COONa + H₂O.
Step 2 (Soda lime decarboxylation): CH₃CH₂COONa + NaOH —(CaO, Δ)→ **CH₃–CH₃ (ethane)** + Na₂CO₃.

Jaanch: 3-carbon acid cleanly loses CO₂ (as Na₂CO₃) to give 2-carbon alkane ✅

## Aam Galtiyan (Common Mistakes)

1. **Benzoic acid par Friedel–Crafts alkylation/acylation karwa dena:**
   - *Galat:* C₆H₅COOH + CH₃Cl (AlCl₃) → m-toluic acid.
   - *Sahi:* **Benzoic acid Friedel–Crafts reaction NAHI deta** (–COOH strongly deactivating hai aur AlCl₃ ke saath complex/salt bana leta hai)!

2. **Phenol aur Carboxylic acid ko NaOH se distinguish kar dena:**
   - *Galat:* NaOH dalne par sirf carboxylic acid react karega.
   - *Sahi:* Phenol aur Carboxylic acid **dono NaOH mein ghul jate hain**! Dono ko alag karne ke liye **NaHCO₃** use karo — sirf **Carboxylic acid brisk CO₂ effervescence** deta hai!

3. **HVZ reaction mein Cl ko ring ya β-carbon par laga dena:**
   - *Galat:* CH₃CH₂COOH + Cl₂/Red P → 3-chloropropanoic acid.
   - *Sahi:* HVZ hamesha **α-carbon (C2)** par halogen lagata hai → **2-Chloropropanoic acid**!

---

## Quick Recap

- **BP:** Carboxylic acids > Alcohols (cyclic H-bonded dimers!).
- **Acidity:** R–COOH >> Phenol >> Alcohol (carboxylate has 2 **equivalent** O-resonances!). EWG (–F, –Cl, –NO₂) ↑ acidity; EDG (–CH₃, –OCH₃) ↓ acidity.
- **Test for –COOH:** Brisk CO₂ effervescence with **NaHCO₃**.
- **Key Reactions:** SOCl₂ → RCOCl; NH₃/Δ → amide; LiAlH₄/B₂H₆ → 1° alcohol; **Soda lime (NaOH+CaO, Δ)** → decarboxylation (−1 C); **HVZ (X₂/Red P, H₂O)** → α-haloacid; **No Friedel–Crafts** on benzoic acid!

## Practice Questions

2. Explain why carboxylic acids are stronger acids than phenols.
3. Arrange in increasing order of acid strength:
   (a) CH₃COOH, HCOOH, FCH₂COOH, ClCH₂COOH
   (b) CH₃CH₂CH(Cl)COOH, CH₃CH(Cl)CH₂COOH, ClCH₂CH₂CH₂COOH
4. Give a simple chemical test to distinguish between **benzoic acid** and **phenol**.
5. Write the chemical equation for the **Hell–Volhard–Zelinsky (HVZ)** reaction of propanoic acid with Br₂/Red P.
6. Why does benzoic acid NOT undergo Friedel–Crafts alkylation or acylation?
7. Write the conversion of **phthalic acid** to **phthalimide** using ammonia.
8. Complete the following reactions:
   (a) CH₃COONa + NaOH —(CaO, Δ)→ ?
   (b) C₆H₅COOH + SOCl₂ → ?
   (c) C₆H₅COOH + conc. HNO₃ / conc. H₂SO₄ → ?
9. Why is **C₆H₅COOH** (benzoic acid) a stronger acid than **CH₃COOH** (ethanoic acid)?
10. Convert: (a) Benzoic acid to benzene, (b) Ethanoic acid to 2-chloroethanoic acid, (c) Benzoic acid to benzamide.

### Answers

1. Carboxylic acids form **stronger intermolecular hydrogen bonds** than alcohols, associating into stable **cyclic dimers** (even in vapour phase), requiring more energy to boil.
2. In **carboxylate ion (R–COO⁻)**, the negative charge is delocalised over **two equivalent electronegative oxygen atoms**, whereas in **phenoxide ion (C₆H₅O⁻)** the negative charge delocalises onto less electronegative carbon atoms. Greater conjugate-base stability makes R–COOH far more acidic.
3. (a) **CH₃COOH < HCOOH < ClCH₂COOH < FCH₂COOH** (F has stronger −I than Cl).
   (b) **ClCH₂CH₂CH₂COOH < CH₃CH(Cl)CH₂COOH < CH₃CH₂CH(Cl)COOH** (−I effect weakens with distance from –COOH!).
4. Add aqueous **NaHCO₃** to both: **Benzoic acid** gives **brisk effervescence of CO₂ gas**, whereas **phenol** gives NO effervescence.
5. **CH₃–CH₂–COOH —(1. Br₂ / Red P, 2. H₂O)→ CH₃–CH(Br)–COOH (2-bromopropanoic acid)**.
6. Because (i) the –COOH group is strongly **deactivating** towards electrophilic substitution, and (ii) the Lewis acid catalyst **AlCl₃** reacts with and binds to the –COOH group.
7. Phthalic acid + 2NH₃ → Ammonium phthalate —(Δ)→ Phthalamide —(strong Δ, −NH₃)→ **Phthalimide**.
8. (a) **CH₄ (methane)** + Na₂CO₃ (soda lime decarboxylation)
   (b) **C₆H₅COCl (benzoyl chloride)** + SO₂↑ + HCl↑
   (c) **m-Nitrobenzoic acid (3-nitrobenzoic acid)**
9. Because the carboxyl carbon in benzoic acid is attached to an **sp²-hybridised phenyl carbon** (more electronegative, −I effect), whereas in ethanoic acid it is attached to an electron-releasing (+I) sp³ –CH₃ group.
10. (a) C₆H₅COOH —(NaOH)→ C₆H₅COONa —(NaOH + CaO, Δ)→ **C₆H₆ (benzene)** + Na₂CO₃.
    (b) CH₃COOH —(1. Cl₂/Red P, 2. H₂O — HVZ)→ **ClCH₂COOH**.
    (c) C₆H₅COOH + NH₃ → C₆H₅COONH₄ —(Δ)→ **C₆H₅CONH₂ (benzamide)** + H₂O.
