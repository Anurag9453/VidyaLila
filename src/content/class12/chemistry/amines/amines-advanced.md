Gyan ek physical-organic seminar mein ek paradoxical data point dekh raha tha: **o-Toluidine (2-methylaniline, pK_b = 9.56)** **aniline (pK_b = 9.38)** se KAM basic tha — jabki –CH₃ toh electron-donating group hai! Professor ne muskurakar kaha: "Carboxylic acids mein ortho-effect acidity **badhata** hai, aur anilines mein wahi ortho-effect basicity **ghatata** hai — kyunki ortho group protonated **–NH₃⁺** ke hydration (solvation) ko rok deta hai (**SIP: Steric Inhibition of Protonation**)!"

---

## 1. Pyramidal Inversion (Why Chiral 3° Amines Cannot Be Resolved!)

Consider a tertiary amine with 3 different alkyl groups + 1 lone pair: **R₁R₂R₃N** (e.g., ethylmethylpropylamine). Nitrogen has 4 different "groups" (chiral centre!).
- **Yet it CANNOT be resolved into pure d and l enantiomers at room temperature!**
- **Why?** Because the lone pair rapidly oscillates from one side of the sp³ pyramid to the other through a planar sp² transition state (**Pyramidal / Umbrella Inversion**, energy barrier only ~25 kJ mol⁻¹ → flips **10⁹ times per second** at room temp!), causing instantaneous **racemisation**!
- **Exceptions (CAN be resolved into stable enantiomers!):**
  1. **Quaternary ammonium salts [R₁R₂R₃R₄N]⁺ X⁻** (no lone pair → inversion impossible!).
  2. **Bridgehead amines (like Tröger's base)** where rigid bicyclic rings lock nitrogen from flattening!

---

## 2. Ortho-Effect in Substituted Anilines (SIP — Steric Inhibition of Protonation)

Almost **ANY ortho-substituted aniline** (whether –CH₃, –OCH₃, –Cl, or –NO₂!) is a **WEAKER base than aniline** itself!
- **Cause (SIP):** When o-substituted aniline is protonated to bulky **–NH₃⁺**, the adjacent ortho group sterically crowds –NH₃⁺ AND blocks water molecules from hydrating/solvating –NH₃⁺, destabilising the conjugate acid!
- **Classic Exam Orders (Must Memorise!):**
  - **Toluidines (–CH₃):** **p-Toluidine > m-Toluidine > Aniline > o-Toluidine**!
  - **Anisidines (–OCH₃):** **p-Anisidine > Aniline > o-Anisidine > m-Anisidine**! *(meta –OCH₃ has only −I effect, no +R!)*
  - **Nitroanilines (–NO₂):** **Aniline > m-Nitroaniline > p-Nitroaniline > o-Nitroaniline**! *(o-NO₂ has both −R, strong −I, and SIP!)*
- **SIR Exception in N,N-Dimethylanilines:** **2,6-Dimethyl-N,N-dimethylaniline** is **STRONGER** base than N,N-dimethylaniline! Why? The two ortho –CH₃ groups twist –N(CH₃)₂ out of the ring plane (**SIR**), stopping lone-pair delocalisation into the ring so the lone pair stays **localised on N**!

## 3. Basicity of Guanidine, Amidine, Pyridine vs Pyrrole

1. **Guanidine (H₂N–C(=NH)–NH₂):** One of the **strongest neutral organic bases** (pK_b ≈ 0.4)! Protonation on the =NH nitrogen gives a **guanidinium ion [C(NH₂)₃]⁺** with **3 completely equivalent resonance structures**!
2. **Pyridine vs Pyrrole (Classic Exam Trap!):**
   - **Pyridine (C₅H₅N, pK_b = 8.75):** Nitrogen is sp²; its lone pair sits in an **sp² orbital perpendicular to the aromatic π-cloud** (NOT part of the 6 π-electron sextet!) → **available for protonation (moderately basic!)**.
   - **Pyrrole (C₄H₅N, pK_b ≈ 13.6 — EXTREMELY WEAK BASE!):** Nitrogen's lone pair is **part of the aromatic 6 π-electron sextet**! Protonating N destroys aromaticity → pyrrole refuses to act as a base!
   - **Order:** **Piperidine (aliphatic sp³ N) > Pyridine (aromatic sp² lone pair) >> Aniline > Pyrrole (aromatic sextet lone pair)**!

---

## 4. Hofmann Exhaustive Methylation & Elimination (Anti-Zaitsev Alkene!)

1. **Exhaustive Methylation:** Treat any 1°/2°/3° amine with **excess CH₃I** → forms quaternary ammonium iodide (**R–N⁺(CH₃)₃ I⁻**).
2. **Moist Ag₂O (AgOH):** Converts iodide to quaternary ammonium hydroxide (**R–N⁺(CH₃)₃ OH⁻**).
3. **Thermal E2 Elimination (Δ):** Eliminates (CH₃)₃N + H₂O to give the **LESS SUBSTITUTED ALKENE (Hofmann Product — Opposite to Zaitsev!)**!
   - **Why?** The bulky **–N⁺(CH₃)₃** leaving group sterically hinders OH⁻ from reaching internal 2°/3° β-hydrogens, so OH⁻ plucks the most accessible, **least hindered (most acidic) terminal 1° β-hydrogen**!
   - **Example:** sec-Butyltrimethylammonium hydroxide —(Δ)→ **But-1-ene (95%, Major — Hofmann!)** + But-2-ene (5%) + (CH₃)₃N + H₂O!

## Deep Solved Problems

### Problem 1: Explaining Basicity Order of Toluidines
Arrange aniline, o-toluidine, m-toluidine, and p-toluidine in decreasing order of basic strength in water.

**Solution:**
- **p-Toluidine:** +I and hyperconjugation of –CH₃ donate electron density to –NH₂ → **strongest base**.
- **m-Toluidine:** +I effect only (no hyperconjugation at meta) → stronger than aniline, weaker than para.
- **Aniline:** Reference.
- **o-Toluidine:** **Ortho-effect (SIP)** destabilises the protonated –NH₃⁺ ion by steric crowding and reduced hydration → **weakest base**!
Decreasing order: **p-Toluidine > m-Toluidine > Aniline > o-Toluidine**.

Jaanch: Matches experimental pK_b: p (8.92) < m (9.30) < aniline (9.38) < o (9.56) ✅

---

### Problem 2: Comparing Basicity of Pyridine, Piperidine, Aniline & Pyrrole
Arrange piperidine, pyridine, aniline, and pyrrole in decreasing order of basicity. Explain briefly.

**Solution:**
1. **Piperidine** (saturated 6-ring secondary amine): sp³ N, localised lone pair, +I carbons → **strongest base**.
2. **Pyridine:** sp² N (higher electronegativity than sp³), lone pair NOT in aromatic sextet → **2nd**.
3. **Aniline:** Lone pair delocalised into benzene ring by resonance → **3rd**.
4. **Pyrrole:** Lone pair is essential to the 6 π-electron aromatic sextet; protonation destroys aromaticity → **weakest base**.
Decreasing order: **Piperidine > Pyridine > Aniline > Pyrrole**.

Jaanch: sp³ localised > sp² localised > ring-delocalised > aromatic-sextet lone pair ✅

---

### Problem 3: Predicting Hofmann Elimination Product
Predict the major alkene formed when **ethyl(methyl)(propyl)(sec-butyl)ammonium hydroxide** is heated strongly.

**Solution:**
In Hofmann elimination of quaternary ammonium hydroxides, the **smallest/least hindered alkyl group containing a β-hydrogen** eliminates preferentially to form the **least substituted alkene**!
- Methyl has NO β-H.
- **Ethyl** (2 C, primary β-H) eliminates much faster than propyl or sec-butyl → **Ethene (CH₂=CH₂)** is the major alkene!

Jaanch: Least hindered β-H is on ethyl group → ethene forms fastest ✅

---

### Problem 4: Why 2,6-Dimethyl-N,N-dimethylaniline Is More Basic Than N,N-Dimethylaniline
Explain why adding two ortho-methyl groups to aniline decreases basicity, whereas adding two ortho-methyl groups to N,N-dimethylaniline **increases** basicity!

**Solution:**
In **2,6-dimethyl-N,N-dimethylaniline**, the bulky –N(CH₃)₂ group is sterically forced to **twist 90° out of the plane** of the benzene ring (**Steric Inhibition of Resonance, SIR**). Because the nitrogen lone pair can no longer overlap with the ring π-system, it stays **localised on nitrogen**, dramatically increasing basicity!

Jaanch: SIR prevents lone-pair delocalisation into benzene → stronger base ✅

---

### Problem 5: Counting Moles of CH₃I in Exhaustive Methylation
How many moles of CH₃I are consumed in the exhaustive methylation of 1 mole of: (a) Ethylamine, (b) Diethylamine, (c) Triethylamine?

**Solution:**
To form the quaternary ammonium iodide (R₄N⁺I⁻), nitrogen needs **4 carbon groups** total:
(a) Ethylamine (1° — 1 C on N) needs **3 moles CH₃I** → C₂H₅N⁺(CH₃)₃I⁻.
(b) Diethylamine (2° — 2 C on N) needs **2 moles CH₃I** → (C₂H₅)₂N⁺(CH₃)₂I⁻.
(c) Triethylamine (3° — 3 C on N) needs **1 mole CH₃I** → (C₂H₅)₃N⁺(CH₃)I⁻.

Jaanch: 1° + 3 = 4; 2° + 2 = 4; 3° + 1 = 4 bonds on N⁺ ✅

## Advanced Practice Questions

1. Why can **ethylmethylpropylamine** NOT be resolved into optically active enantiomers at room temperature, whereas **ethylmethylpropylammonium iodide** (with a 4th different group, e.g., benzyl) CAN be resolved?
2. Arrange o-nitroaniline, m-nitroaniline, p-nitroaniline, and aniline in decreasing order of basicity.
3. Explain why **guanidine (H₂N–C(=NH)–NH₂)** is a much stronger base than methylamine.
4. Explain why **pyrrole** is virtually non-basic compared to **pyridine**.
5. Predict the major alkene formed on heating **(CH₃)₃N⁺–CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₃ OH⁻** (Hofmann elimination).
6. Arrange aniline, o-anisidine, m-anisidine, and p-anisidine in decreasing order of basicity.
7. Why is **cyclohexylamine** a stronger base than **aniline**, and **benzylamine** a stronger base than **aniline**?
8. How many moles of CH₃I are required to convert 1 mole of **ethane-1,2-diamine (H₂N–CH₂–CH₂–NH₂)** into its bis-quaternary ammonium salt?
9. In **Hofmann bromamide degradation**, what is the reactive electron-deficient intermediate formed before rearrangement to isocyanate (R–N=C=O)?
10. If optically active **(R)-2-phenylpropanamide** undergoes Hoffmann bromamide degradation, does the resulting 1-phenylethanamine show **retention**, **inversion**, or **racemisation**?

### Solutions

1. **Ethylmethylpropylamine** undergoes rapid **pyramidal (umbrella) inversion** (~25 kJ mol⁻¹ barrier) through a planar sp² transition state, interconverting enantiomers billions of times per second. A **quaternary ammonium salt** has **no lone pair**, so pyramidal inversion is impossible and enantiomers are permanently stable!
2. Decreasing basicity: **Aniline > m-Nitroaniline > p-Nitroaniline > o-Nitroaniline** (o has −R, strong −I, and SIP; p has −R and −I; m has −I only).
3. Protonation of guanidine forms the **guanidinium cation [C(NH₂)₃]⁺**, stabilised by **3 completely equivalent resonance structures** spreading the + charge equally over all 3 nitrogens!
4. Pyrrole's lone pair is part of its **6 π-electron aromatic sextet** (protonation destroys aromaticity), whereas pyridine's lone pair lies in an external **sp² orbital** not involved in aromaticity.
5. Hofmann elimination removes the least hindered terminal β-H → **But-1-ene (CH₂=CH–CH₂–CH₃)** is the major alkene!
6. Decreasing basicity: **p-Anisidine (+R > −I) > Aniline > o-Anisidine (SIP + strong −I) > m-Anisidine (only −I, no +R!)**.
7. Neither cyclohexylamine nor benzylamine allows resonance delocalisation of nitrogen's lone pair into a benzene ring (in benzylamine, the –CH₂– spacer blocks conjugation!), so their lone pairs remain fully available for protonation.
8. Each –NH₂ group (1°) needs 3 moles of CH₃I → 2 × 3 = **6 moles of CH₃I**!
   Jaanch: 2 × (4 − 1) = 6 moles ✅
9. An **acyl nitrene** (R–CO–N:, or concerted bromide loss/migration) which immediately undergoes 1,2-alkyl/aryl migration to an **alkyl/aryl isocyanate (R–N=C=O)**.
10. **100% RETENTION of configuration!** Because the migrating chiral group shifts intramolecularly with its bonding electron pair to nitrogen without ever breaking free as a carbocation or radical!
