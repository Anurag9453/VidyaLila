## Naye Shabd (Key Terms)

| Term | Hinglish Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Ammonolysis (Hofmann)** | Sealed tube (373 K) mein R–X + ethanolic NH₃ → 1°, 2°, 3° amines aur quaternary salt ka **mixture** |
| **Gabriel Phthalimide Synthesis** | Phthalimide + KOH → K-phthalimide + R–X (SN2) → N-alkylphthalimide —(aq. NaOH)→ **100% pure 1° ALIPHATIC amine ONLY!** |
| **Hoffmann Bromamide Degradation** | Amide (R–CONH₂) + **Br₂ + 4NaOH** → **1° amine (R–NH₂) with ONE CARBON LESS (−1 C!)** |

---

## 1. Reduction of Nitro Compounds (Best for Aromatic Amines!)
- **Ar–NO₂ + 3H₂ —(Pd / ethanol)→ Ar–NH₂ + 2H₂O**
- **Ar–NO₂ —(Fe / HCl or Sn / HCl)→ Ar–NH₂**
- **Why is Fe/HCl preferred over Sn/HCl?** Because **FeCl₂** formed during the reaction undergoes hydrolysis to **regenerate HCl**; therefore, only a very small amount of HCl is needed to initiate the reaction!

---

## 2. Ammonolysis of Alkyl Halides (Hofmann Ammonolysis)
- R–X + NH₃ (ethanolic, 373 K, sealed tube) → R–NH₃⁺ X⁻ —(NaOH)→ **R–NH₂ (1°)**
- **Major Limitation:** Bana hua 1° amine khud nucleophile hai aur bache hue R–X se aage react karke **1°, 2°, 3° amines aur quaternary ammonium salt (R₄N⁺X⁻)** ka messy **mixture** bana leta hai!
  - *(Fix: Use large **excess of NH₃** to get 1° amine as major product!)*
- Reactivity order of halides: **R–I > R–Br > R–Cl**.

---

## 3. Reduction of Nitriles (R–CN) and Amides (R–CONH₂)
1. **Reduction of Nitriles (Ascent / Step-Up by +1 Carbon!):**
   **R–C≡N —(H₂/Ni, or LiAlH₄, or Na(Hg)/C₂H₅OH)→ R–CH₂–NH₂** (pure 1° amine with **same carbons as R–CN**, i.e., **+1 carbon** over R–X!).
2. **Reduction of Amides (Same Number of Carbons):**
   **R–CONH₂ —(1. LiAlH₄ / ether, 2. H₂O)→ R–CH₂–NH₂**

---

## 4. Gabriel Phthalimide Synthesis (Pure 1° Aliphatic Amines ONLY!)
- **Step 1:** Phthalimide + ethanolic KOH → **Potassium phthalimide** (strong nucleophile)
- **Step 2 (SN2):** Potassium phthalimide + **R–X** (1° alkyl halide) → **N-Alkylphthalimide**
- **Step 3:** N-Alkylphthalimide + aq. NaOH (hydrolysis) → **R–NH₂ (Pure 1° aliphatic amine!)** + Sodium phthalate.
- **CRITICAL EXAM QUESTION:** **Why CANNOT aromatic primary amines (like Aniline) be prepared by Gabriel phthalimide synthesis?**
  Because **aryl halides (e.g., C₆H₅–Cl / C₆H₅–Br) do NOT undergo SN2 nucleophilic substitution** with the phthalimide anion (due to resonance partial double-bond character of C–X and sp² carbon)!

---

## 5. Hoffmann Bromamide Degradation (Step-Down by −1 Carbon!)
**R–CO–NH₂ + Br₂ + 4NaOH → R–NH₂ + Na₂CO₃ + 2NaBr + 2H₂O**
- **Magic:** Carbonyl group (**–CO–**) gayab ho jata hai (as Na₂CO₃), aur **1° amine** milta hai jismein amide se **1 carbon KAM** hota hai!
- **Works for BOTH aliphatic AND aromatic 1° amines!**
  - Ethanamide (CH₃CONH₂) → **Methanamine (CH₃NH₂)**
  - Propanamide (CH₃CH₂CONH₂) → **Ethanamine (CH₃CH₂NH₂)**
  - Benzamide (C₆H₅CONH₂) → **Aniline (C₆H₅NH₂)**!

## Solved Examples

### Example 1: Step-Up (+1 C) vs Step-Down (−1 C) Conversions
How will you convert **ethanoic acid (CH₃COOH)** into:
(a) **Ethanamine** (same 2 carbons)
(b) **Methanamine** (step-down: 2 C → 1 C)

**Solution:**
First convert CH₃COOH + NH₃ (Δ) → **CH₃CONH₂ (ethanamide)**.
(a) **Same carbons (reduction):** CH₃CONH₂ —(1. LiAlH₄, 2. H₂O)→ **CH₃CH₂NH₂ (ethanamine)**.
(b) **Step-down −1 C (Hoffmann bromamide):** CH₃CONH₂ + Br₂ + 4NaOH → **CH₃NH₂ (methanamine)** + Na₂CO₃ + 2NaBr + 2H₂O.

Jaanch: LiAlH₄ reduces C=O to CH₂ (keeps 2 C); Br₂/NaOH removes C=O (drops to 1 C) ✅

---

### Example 2: Explaining Why Aniline Fails Gabriel Phthalimide Synthesis
Why cannot aniline be prepared by Gabriel phthalimide synthesis? Suggest two good methods to prepare aniline instead.

**Solution:**
Gabriel phthalimide synthesis requires an **SN2 nucleophilic substitution** between the potassium phthalimide anion and an organic halide. **Aryl halides (like chlorobenzene)** do not undergo SN2 substitution because their C–X bond has partial double-bond character (resonance) and an sp² carbon.
**Good methods for aniline:**
1. Reduction of nitrobenzene with **Fe / HCl** (or H₂/Pd).
2. **Hoffmann bromamide degradation** of benzamide (C₆H₅CONH₂ + Br₂ + 4NaOH → C₆H₅NH₂).

Jaanch: Both routes give pure aniline in high yield ✅

---

### Example 3: Step-Up from Alkyl Halide to Amine (+1 Carbon)
Convert **chloroethane (CH₃CH₂Cl)** into **propan-1-amine (CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₂)**.

**Solution:**
- Step 1: CH₃CH₂Cl + KCN (alc.) → CH₃CH₂CN (propanenitrile)
- Step 2: CH₃CH₂CN —(H₂/Ni or LiAlH₄)→ **CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₂ (propan-1-amine)**!

Jaanch: C2 → C3 cleanly via cyanide + reduction ✅

## Aam Galtiyan (Common Mistakes)

1. **Gabriel phthalimide se aniline ya 2° amine bana dena:**
   - *Galat:* Phthalimide + C₆H₅Cl → Aniline.
   - *Sahi:* Gabriel phthalimide **SIRF 1° ALIPHATIC amines** banata hai — na aniline, na 2°/3° amines!

2. **Hoffmann bromamide mein carbon count same rakh dena:**
   - *Galat:* CH₃CH₂CONH₂ + Br₂ + NaOH → CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₂.
   - *Sahi:* Hoffmann bromamide **1 carbon KAM** karta hai → **CH₃CH₂NH₂ (ethanamine)**!

3. **Ammonolysis ko pure 1° amine banane ka best method bol dena:**
   - *Galat:* R–X + NH₃ sirf 1° amine deta hai.
   - *Sahi:* Ammonolysis 1°, 2°, 3° aur R₄N⁺X⁻ ka mixture deta hai; pure 1° aliphatic amine ke liye **Gabriel phthalimide** best hai!

---

## Quick Recap

- **Best for Ar–NH₂:** Ar–NO₂ + **Fe/HCl** (FeCl₂ hydrolyses to regenerate HCl!).
- **Ammonolysis (R–X + NH₃):** Gives mixture (use excess NH₃ for 1° major).
- **Step-up (+1 C):** R–X → R–CN —(LiAlH₄ / H₂-Ni)→ R–CH₂NH₂.
- **Gabriel Phthalimide:** Pure **1° aliphatic amines ONLY** (NO arylamines!).
- **Hoffmann Bromamide (Br₂ + 4NaOH):** R–CONH₂ → **R–NH₂ (−1 carbon!)** — works for both aliphatic & aromatic!

### Answers

1. Because **FeCl₂** formed during the reaction undergoes hydrolysis with water to **regenerate HCl**, so only a small catalytic quantity of HCl is needed to initiate the reduction.
2. **C₆H₅–CONH₂ + Br₂ + 4NaOH → C₆H₅–NH₂ (aniline) + Na₂CO₃ + 2NaBr + 2H₂O**.
3. Gabriel phthalimide gives **100% pure 1° aliphatic amines** free from 2°/3° amine contamination. It **cannot** prepare aniline because aryl halides do not undergo SN2 nucleophilic substitution with the phthalimide anion.
4. Drawback: Produces a **mixture of 1°, 2°, 3° amines and quaternary ammonium salt**. Yield of 1° amine is improved by using a **large excess of ammonia (NH₃)**.
5. (a) **CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₂ (propan-1-amine)**
   (b) **CH₃CH₂NH₂ (ethanamine)** + Na₂CO₃ + 2NaBr + 2H₂O
   (c) **CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₂ (propan-1-amine)**
6. (a) C₆H₆ —(conc. HNO₃/H₂SO₄)→ C₆H₅NO₂ —(Fe/HCl)→ **C₆H₅NH₂**.
   (b) CH₃Cl + KCN → CH₃CN —(H₂/Ni or LiAlH₄)→ **CH₃CH₂NH₂**.
   (c) CH₃CH₂NH₂ —(HNO₂)→ CH₃CH₂OH —(KMnO₄)→ CH₃COOH —(NH₃, Δ)→ CH₃CONH₂ —(Br₂/NaOH)→ **CH₃NH₂**.
7. Because C–I is the longest and weakest bond (lowest bond enthalpy), making I⁻ the best leaving group in SN2 ammonolysis.
8. Phthalimide + alc. KOH → K-phthalimide —(CH₃CH₂Br)→ N-ethylphthalimide —(aq. NaOH)→ **CH₃CH₂NH₂** + sodium phthalate.
9. Hexanenitrile (C₆) —(partial hydrolysis H⁺/H₂O or H₂O₂/OH⁻)→ Hexanamide (CH₃(CH₂)₄CONH₂) —(Br₂ + 4NaOH, Hoffmann)→ **CH₃(CH₂)₄NH₂ (1-aminopentane, C₅)**!
   Jaanch: Partial hydrolysis to amide + Hoffmann bromamide cleanly removes 1 carbon ✅
10. **Gabriel phthalimide synthesis** — because benzyl chloride (C₆H₅–CH₂–Cl) is a reactive 1° benzylic halide (NOT an aryl halide!) that undergoes clean SN2 with K-phthalimide to give pure benzylamine without 2°/3° by-products!
