## Naye Shabd (Key Terms)

| Term | Hinglish Meaning |
|---|---|
| **pK_b Value** | Basic strength ka measure (pK_b = −log K_b): **Jitna chhota pK_b, utna zyada strong base!** |
| **Carbylamine Reaction (Isocyanide Test)** | **1° amine ONLY** (aliphatic ya aromatic) + CHCl₃ + alc. KOH (Δ) → **foul-smelling isocyanide (R–NC)**! (2° & 3° amines NAHI dete!) |
| **Hinsberg's Reagent** | **Benzenesulphonyl chloride (C₆H₅SO₂Cl)** — 1°, 2°, aur 3° amines ko clearly distinguish karne ka golden reagent! |
| **Anilinium Ion (C₆H₅–NH₃⁺)** | Strong acid (H₂SO₄/HNO₃) mein aniline ka protonated form, jo **meta-directing** hota hai! |
| **Zwitterion (Sulphanilic Acid)** | Dipolar ion jismein ek hi molecule ke andar + aur − dono charges hon (**⁺H₃N–C₆H₄–SO₃⁻**) |

## 1. Physical Properties (Boiling Point & Solubility)

- **Boiling Point Order (isomeric amines):** **1° (RNH₂) > 2° (R₂NH) > 3° (R₃N)**!
  - Kyun? 1° amine mein N par **2 H atoms** hain (extensive intermolecular H-bonding); 2° mein sirf **1 H atom**; 3° mein **0 H atoms** (NO intermolecular H-bonding!).
- **Alcohols vs Amines:** Comparable molar mass ke **alcohols ka BP amines se zyada** hota hai (O is more electronegative than N → O–H...O H-bonds are stronger than N–H...N H-bonds!).
- Lower amines water-soluble hain (H-bonding with H₂O); aniline paani mein lagbhag insoluble hai.

---

## 2. Basic Character of Amines (100% EXAM FAVOURITE!)

Amines nitrogen ke **lone pair** ki wajah se **Lewis bases** hain.

### (a) Gas Phase Basicity (Only +I Inductive Effect):
**3° > 2° > 1° > NH₃** (zyada +I alkyl groups → N par zyada electron density → stronger base!).

### (b) Aqueous Phase Basicity (Balance of +I Effect + Solvation/H-Bonding + Steric Hindrance!):
Paani mein protonated cation (RNH₃⁺) jitna zyada H-bonds se **hydrate (solvate)** hoga, utna stable hoga!
- **Solvation stability:** **1° (3 H-bonds) > 2° (2 H-bonds) > 3° (1 H-bond + steric crowding!)**.
- Combining +I effect (favours 3°) and solvation/steric effect (favours 1°), **2° amine ALWAYS wins in water!**

**TWO MUST-MEMORISE AQUEOUS BASICITY ORDERS:**
1. **Ethyl group (R = –C₂H₅):** **(C₂H₅)₂NH (2°) > (C₂H₅)₃N (3°) > C₂H₅NH₂ (1°) > NH₃**   *(Trick: **231** for Ethyl!)*
2. **Methyl group (R = –CH₃):** **(CH₃)₂NH (2°) > CH₃NH₂ (1°) > (CH₃)₃N (3°) > NH₃**   *(Trick: **213** for Methyl!)*

### (c) Why Is Aniline MUCH Weaker Base Than NH₃ and Aliphatic Amines?
- **Aniline (pK_b = 9.38) << NH₃ (pK_b = 4.75) < Aliphatic amines (pK_b ~3.3)!**
- **Reason:** Aniline mein nitrogen ka lone pair benzene ring ke saath **resonance** (5 structures) mein delocalise ho jata hai → protonation ke liye kam available! Protonation ke baad bana **anilinium ion (C₆H₅NH₃⁺)** sirf 2 Kekulé resonance structures rakhta hai (kam stable!).
- **Substituents on Aniline:** EDG (–OCH₃, –CH₃) **increase** basicity; EWG (–NO₂, –SO₃H, –X) **decrease** basicity!
  Order: **p-Toluidine > Aniline > p-Nitroaniline**.
- *(Bonus: **Benzylamine C₆H₅–CH₂–NH₂** mein N ka lone pair ring se resonance nahi karta → benzylamine aniline se kahin zyada basic hai!)*

## 3. Important Chemical Reactions & Distinction Tests

### (a) Acylation (Protecting the –NH₂ Group!)
1° & 2° amines acid chlorides / anhydrides (pyridine base) ke saath **amides** banate hain (3° amines mein N–H nahi hai → NO acylation!):
**C₆H₅–NH₂ + (CH₃CO)₂O —(pyridine)→ C₆H₅–NH–COCH₃ (Acetanilide) + CH₃COOH**

### (b) Carbylamine Reaction (Isocyanide Test — 1° Amines ONLY!)
> **Safety Note:** Isocyanides extremely toxic aur foul-smelling hote hain; yeh test sirf theoretical study ya fume-hood teacher demo hai.
**R–NH₂ + CHCl₃ + 3KOH (alc., Δ) → R–NC (foul-smelling isocyanide!) + 3KCl + 3H₂O**
- Positive: **1° aliphatic AND 1° aromatic amines (aniline)**.
- Negative: **2° and 3° amines!**

### (c) Reaction with Nitrous Acid (HNO₂ = NaNO₂ + HCl at 0–5°C):
- **1° Aliphatic amine (R–NH₂) + HNO₂ →** Unstable R–N₂⁺Cl⁻ → immediately decomposes with **N₂ gas evolution** to give **Alcohol (R–OH)**!
- **1° Aromatic amine (C₆H₅–NH₂) + HNO₂ (0–5°C) →** Stable **Benzene diazonium chloride (C₆H₅–N₂⁺Cl⁻)** (resonance stabilised, NO immediate N₂ gas!).

### (d) Hinsberg's Test (Distinguishing 1°, 2°, and 3° Amines!)
**Reagent:** **Benzenesulphonyl chloride (C₆H₅SO₂Cl)** + aqueous KOH:

| Amine Type | Reaction with C₆H₅SO₂Cl | Solubility in Aqueous KOH |
|---|---|---|
| **1° Amine (R–NH₂)** | Forms **N-alkylbenzenesulphonamide** (C₆H₅SO₂–NH–R) | **SOLUBLE in KOH!** (strongly acidic H on N attached to –SO₂–) |
| **2° Amine (R₂NH)** | Forms **N,N-dialkylbenzenesulphonamide** (C₆H₅SO₂–NR₂) | **INSOLUBLE in KOH!** (NO acidic H on N!) |
| **3° Amine (R₃N)** | **NO reaction** with C₆H₅SO₂Cl | Insoluble (dissolves in dil. HCl!) |

### (e) Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution (EAS) of Aniline (EXAM GOLDMINE!)
–NH₂ group ring ko **behad zyada activate** karta hai (+R):
1. **Bromination:**
   - **Aniline + Br₂ / H₂O (room temp) → 2,4,6-Tribromoaniline (white precipitate!)** (all 3 o,o,p positions react!).
   - **How to get ONLY p-Bromoaniline (monobromination)?** **Protect –NH₂ by acetylation!**
     Aniline —((CH₃CO)₂O / pyridine)→ **Acetanilide** —(Br₂ / CH₃COOH)→ p-Bromoacetanilide —(H⁺/H₂O or OH⁻)→ **p-Bromoaniline**!
     *(Why does acetylation work? Nitrogen's lone pair resonates with the –COCH₃ carbonyl group, reducing its donation into the benzene ring!)*
2. **Nitration (SHOCKING 47% META PRODUCT!):**
   - Direct nitration of aniline with conc. HNO₃ + conc. H₂SO₄ (288 K) gives: **51% para + 47% META + 2% ortho!**
   - **Why 47% meta?!** Because in strongly acidic medium, aniline gets protonated to **Anilinium ion (C₆H₅–NH₃⁺)**, which is **meta-directing**!
   - **How to get clean p-Nitroaniline?** Protect as **acetanilide** → nitrate (conc. HNO₃/H₂SO₄) → hydrolyse → **p-Nitroaniline**!
3. **Sulphonation:**
   Aniline + conc. H₂SO₄ → Anilinium hydrogensulphate —(453–473 K)→ **Sulphanilic acid (p-aminobenzenesulphonic acid)**, which exists as a **zwitterion (⁺H₃N–C₆H₄–SO₃⁻)**!
4. **NO Friedel–Crafts Reaction!**
   Aniline **does NOT undergo Friedel–Crafts** alkylation/acylation because basic aniline forms a **salt with Lewis acid AlCl₃** (C₆H₅–NH₂⁺–AlCl₃⁻), putting a positive charge on N which **strongly deactivates** the ring!

## Solved Examples

### Example 1: Arranging Amines in Increasing Order of Basic Strength
Arrange the following in **decreasing order of pK_b** (i.e., **increasing order of basic strength**!) in aqueous solution:
C₂H₅NH₂, C₆H₅NHCH₃, (C₂H₅)₂NH, and C₆H₅NH₂.

**Solution:**
- Aromatic amines are far weaker bases than aliphatic amines due to lone-pair resonance with benzene.
- Between C₆H₅NH₂ and C₆H₅NHCH₃, the +I methyl group makes **C₆H₅NHCH₃ slightly more basic** than C₆H₅NH₂.
- Between aliphatic ethylamines, **2° (C₂H₅)₂NH > 1° C₂H₅NH₂** ("231" rule!).
- Increasing basic strength (decreasing pK_b):
  **C₆H₅NH₂ < C₆H₅NHCH₃ < C₂H₅NH₂ < (C₂H₅)₂NH**.

Jaanch: Highest pK_b (weakest base) = C₆H₅NH₂ (9.38); Lowest pK_b (strongest base) = (C₂H₅)₂NH (3.00) ✅

---

### Example 2: Chemical Distinction Using Hinsberg & Carbylamine Tests
Give ONE simple chemical test to distinguish between:
(a) Methylamine (1°) and Dimethylamine (2°)
(b) Ethylamine (1° aliphatic) and Aniline (1° aromatic)

**Solution:**
(a) **Carbylamine test (CHCl₃ + alc. KOH, Δ):** **Methylamine (1°)** gives a foul-smelling isocyanide; **Dimethylamine (2°)** gives NO reaction. (Or Hinsberg test: 1° gives KOH-soluble sulphonamide; 2° gives KOH-insoluble sulphonamide.)
(b) **Azo-dye test (NaNO₂ + HCl at 0–5°C, then β-naphthol in NaOH):** **Aniline** forms a brilliant **orange-red azo dye**; **Ethylamine** evolves N₂ gas bubbles forming ethanol (no dye!).

Jaanch: Both pairs distinguished by unmistakable visual/chemical tests ✅

---

### Example 3: Explaining Why Acetylation Controls Bromination of Aniline
Why does acetanilide give mainly p-bromoacetanilide with Br₂/CH₃COOH, whereas aniline gives 2,4,6-tribromoaniline with Br₂/H₂O?

**Solution:**
In acetanilide (C₆H₅–NH–CO–CH₃), the nitrogen lone pair is delocalised onto the electronegative oxygen of the **acetyl (C=O)** group by resonance (**–NH–C(=O)CH₃ ↔ –N⁺H=C(O⁻)CH₃**). As a result, less electron density is donated into the benzene ring, **moderating the activating power** of nitrogen so clean monobromination occurs at the less hindered para position!

Jaanch: Cross-conjugation with C=O reduces +R activation of the ring ✅

## Aam Galtiyan (Common Mistakes)

1. **Methyl aur Ethyl aqueous basicity orders mix kar dena:**
   - *Galat:* Methyl mein bhi 2° > 3° > 1° likh dena.
   - *Sahi:* **Ethyl = 231** (2° > 3° > 1° > NH₃); **Methyl = 213** (2° > 1° > 3° > NH₃)!

2. **pK_b aur Basic Strength ko same direction mein arrange kar dena:**
   - *Galat:* Stronger base ka pK_b zyada hota hai.
   - *Sahi:* **Smaller pK_b = STRONGER base!** (Sawal dhyan se padho: pK_b poocha hai ya basic strength!)

3. **Aniline ke nitration ko sirf o/p likh dena:**
   - *Galat:* Aniline + conc. HNO₃/H₂SO₄ → sirf o- aur p-nitroaniline.
   - *Sahi:* Acidic medium mein anilinium ion (–NH₃⁺) banne se **47% meta-nitroaniline** banta hai!

---

## Quick Recap

- **BP:** 1° > 2° > 3° (more N–H bonds = more H-bonding); Alcohols > Amines.
- **Basicity:** Gas: 3° > 2° > 1° > NH₃. Aqueous: **Ethyl = 231**, **Methyl = 213**. Aniline << NH₃ (lone pair in ring resonance).
- **Tests:** **Carbylamine** (CHCl₃/KOH) = 1° ONLY; **Hinsberg** (C₆H₅SO₂Cl) = 1° (KOH-soluble), 2° (KOH-insoluble), 3° (no rxn).
- **Aniline EAS:** Br₂/H₂O → 2,4,6-tribromoaniline; protect with (CH₃CO)₂O for monobromo/p-nitro; Direct nitration → **47% meta**; Sulphonation → zwitterionic sulphanilic acid; **NO Friedel–Crafts** (salt with AlCl₃!).

### Answers

1. 1° amines (R–NH₂) have two N–H bonds capable of forming extensive **intermolecular hydrogen bonds**, whereas 3° amines (R₃N) have no N–H bonds and cannot self-hydrogen bond, resulting in much lower boiling points.
2. In the **gas phase**, only the +I inductive effect operates → 3° > 2° > 1° > NH₃. In **aqueous solution**, stability of the conjugate acid depends on **+I effect, hydration (H-bonding with H₂O: 1° > 2° > 3°), and steric hindrance**. For small methyl groups, hydration favours 1° over 3°, giving **2° > 1° > 3° > NH₃** ("213").
3. In aniline, nitrogen's lone pair is **delocalised into the benzene ring by resonance**, making it much less available to accept a proton. In cyclohexylamine/ethylamine, the lone pair is localised on N and enhanced by the +I alkyl group.
4. Increasing basic strength: **p-Nitroaniline < Aniline < p-Toluidine** (–NO₂ withdraws electrons, –CH₃ donates electrons).
5. **C₆H₅–NH₂ + CHCl₃ + 3KOH (alc., Δ) → C₆H₅–NC (phenyl isocyanide, foul smell) + 3KCl + 3H₂O**. Only **1° amines** (both aliphatic and aromatic) respond.
6. React with **C₆H₅SO₂Cl**:
   - **1° amine** → C₆H₅SO₂NHR, **soluble in aq. KOH** (acidic N–H).
   - **2° amine** → C₆H₅SO₂NR₂, **insoluble in aq. KOH** (no N–H).
   - **3° amine** → **no reaction** with C₆H₅SO₂Cl.
7. In strongly acidic nitrating mixture, aniline is protonated to the **anilinium ion (C₆H₅–NH₃⁺)**, which is **meta-directing**, producing 47% m-nitroaniline. To get pure **p-nitroaniline**, first **acetylate** aniline with (CH₃CO)₂O/pyridine to acetanilide, nitrate with conc. HNO₃/H₂SO₄, and then hydrolyse (H⁺/H₂O)!
8. Because aniline is a Lewis base that forms a **salt with the Lewis acid catalyst AlCl₃** (C₆H₅–NH₂⁺–AlCl₃⁻), imparting a positive charge on nitrogen that strongly deactivates the benzene ring.
9. **⁺H₃N–C₆H₄–SO₃⁻** (para positions on benzene ring).
10. (a) **Carbylamine test (CHCl₃ + alc. KOH):** **Aniline (1°)** gives foul-smelling phenyl isocyanide; **N-methylaniline (2°)** gives NO reaction.
    (b) **Nitrous acid (NaNO₂ + HCl, 0–5°C) + β-naphthol:** **Aniline** forms a brilliant **orange-red azo dye**; **benzylamine** (1° aliphatic-like) evolves N₂ gas forming benzyl alcohol (no dye!).
