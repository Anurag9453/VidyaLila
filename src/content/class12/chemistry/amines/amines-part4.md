# Diazonium Salts: Preparation, Reactions and Importance in Synthesis

> **Pichle topic mein:** Humne [Physical Properties, Basic Character and Chemical Reactions of Amines](amines-part3.md) padha. Ab hum aromatic chemistry ke "Master Key" — **Benzene Diazonium Chloride (C₆H₅–N₂⁺Cl⁻)** — ki preparation, stability, replacement reactions, aur **Azo-Dye Coupling** padhenge!

---

Gourav apne uncle ke textile dyeing studio mein gaya tha jahan safed cotton kapde ko chamakdar **narangi (orange)** aur **peele (yellow)** rangon mein ranga ja raha tha! Uncle ne bataya: "Yeh **Azo Dyes** hain! Ice-cold **benzene diazonium chloride** mein phenol milao toh orange dye banta hai, aur aniline milao toh yellow dye!" Gourav ne dekha ki ek hi diazonium salt se duniya bhar ke rang aur aromatic compounds banaye ja sakte hain!

> **Safety Note:** Dry solid diazonium salts heat ya shock se explosive hote hain; inhe hamesha ice-cold aqueous solution (0–5°C) mein banakar turant use kiya jata hai (strictly theoretical / industrial study).

---

## Naye Shabd (Key Terms)

| Term | Hinglish Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Diazotisation** | Ice-cold temperature (**273–278 K / 0–5°C**) par 1° aromatic amine + NaNO₂ + 2HCl → **Arene diazonium chloride (Ar–N₂⁺Cl⁻)** |
| **Sandmeyer Reaction** | Ar–N₂⁺Cl⁻ + **Cu₂Cl₂/HCl**, **Cu₂Br₂/HBr**, ya **CuCN/KCN** → Ar–Cl, Ar–Br, ya Ar–CN + N₂↑ |
| **Gattermann Reaction** | Ar–N₂⁺Cl⁻ + **Cu powder + HCl/HBr** → Ar–Cl ya Ar–Br + N₂↑ (Sandmeyer ki yield zyada achhi hoti hai!) |
| **Azo Coupling Reaction** | Electrophilic substitution jismein Ar–N₂⁺ (weak electrophile) phenol ya aniline ke para position par judkar **–N=N–** bridge wala coloured **Azo Dye** banata hai |

---

## 1. Preparation and Stability of Diazonium Salts

**C₆H₅–NH₂ + NaNO₂ + 2HCl —(273–278 K)→ C₆H₅–N₂⁺Cl⁻ + NaCl + 2H₂O**

- **Why are aromatic diazonium salts (Ar–N₂⁺Cl⁻) stable at 0–5°C while aliphatic diazonium salts (R–N₂⁺Cl⁻) decompose immediately?**
  Because in **Ar–N₂⁺**, the positive charge on the diazonium group is **delocalised over the benzene ring by resonance**! Aliphatic R–N₂⁺ has no resonance stabilisation and immediately loses stable N₂ gas to form carbocation/alcohol.

---

## 2. Reactions Involving Displacement of Nitrogen (N₂ Leaves!)

N₂ ek behad stable neutral molecule (behtareen leaving group!) hai — isliye C₆H₅–N₂⁺Cl⁻ aasani se N₂ chhod kar **9 alag-alag aromatic compounds** bana deta hai!

| Reagent Added to C₆H₅–N₂⁺Cl⁻ | Product Formed | Named Reaction / Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Cu₂Cl₂ / HCl** | **C₆H₅–Cl** (Chlorobenzene) + N₂ | **Sandmeyer** |
| **Cu₂Br₂ / HBr** | **C₆H₅–Br** (Bromobenzene) + N₂ | **Sandmeyer** |
| **CuCN / KCN** | **C₆H₅–CN** (Benzonitrile) + N₂ | **Sandmeyer** (adds +1 C!) |
| **Cu powder / HCl (or HBr)** | **C₆H₅–Cl** (or C₆H₅–Br) + N₂ | **Gattermann** |
| **KI (warm)** | **C₆H₅–I** (Iodobenzene) + N₂ | Simple shaking/warming! |
| **HBF₄, then Δ** | **C₆H₅–F** (Fluorobenzene) + N₂ + BF₃ | **Balz–Schiemann** |
| **HBF₄, then NaNO₂ / Cu, Δ** | **C₆H₅–NO₂** (Nitrobenzene) + N₂ | Replaces –NH₂ by –NO₂! |
| **H₃PO₂ + H₂O** (or **CH₃CH₂OH**) | **C₆H₆** (Benzene) + N₂ (+ H₃PO₃ or CH₃CHO) | **Deamination (Removes –NH₂!)** |
| **Warm H₂O (283 K)** | **C₆H₅–OH** (Phenol) + N₂ + HCl | Hydrolysis |

---

## 3. Reactions Involving Retention of Diazo Group (Azo Coupling!)

Benzene diazonium ion (C₆H₅–N≡N⁺) ek **weak electrophile** hai, isliye yeh sirf **strongly activated rings (Phenol aur Aniline)** ke **para position** par attack karta hai (**N₂ nahi nikalta!**):

1. **Coupling with Phenol (Weakly Alkaline medium, pH 9–10):**
   C₆H₅–N₂⁺Cl⁻ + C₆H₅–OH —(OH⁻, pH 9–10, 273–278 K)→ **p-Hydroxyazobenzene** (**Orange Dye!**) + Cl⁻ + H₂O
   *(Why alkaline? Base converts phenol into hyper-active **phenoxide ion C₆H₅O⁻**!)*
2. **Coupling with Aniline (Weakly Acidic medium, pH 4–5):**
   C₆H₅–N₂⁺Cl⁻ + C₆H₅–NH₂ —(H⁺, pH 4–5, 273–278 K)→ **p-Aminoazobenzene** (**Yellow Dye!**) + Cl⁻ + H₂O
   *(Note: Too much acid would convert aniline to inactive C₆H₅NH₃⁺; mildly acidic pH 4–5 keeps free aniline reactive!)*

## 4. Importance of Diazonium Salts in Aromatic Synthesis

Direct substitution se jo compounds benzene par banana **impossible** hai (jaise **Fluorobenzene, Iodobenzene, Benzonitrile, 1,3,5-Tribromobenzene, m-Bromophenol**), woh diazonium salts ke zariye **behad aasani** se ban jate hain!

---

## Solved Examples

### Example 1: Master Synthesis — Aniline to 1,3,5-Tribromobenzene!
How will you convert **aniline** into **1,3,5-tribromobenzene** (note: direct bromination of benzene NEVER gives 1,3,5-isomer because –Br is o,p-directing!)?

**Solution:**
- **Step 1 (Use –NH₂ to put 3 Br at 2,4,6):**
  C₆H₅–NH₂ + 3Br₂ (H₂O) → **2,4,6-Tribromoaniline** (white ppt)
- **Step 2 (Diazotisation at 0–5°C):**
  2,4,6-Tribromoaniline + NaNO₂ / HCl (273–278 K) → 2,4,6-Tribromobenzenediazonium chloride
- **Step 3 (Remove –N₂⁺ completely with H₃PO₂ + H₂O or C₂H₅OH!):**
  2,4,6-Tribromobenzenediazonium chloride + **H₃PO₂ + H₂O** → **1,3,5-Tribromobenzene** + N₂↑ + H₃PO₃ + HCl!

Jaanch: –NH₂ did its directing job, then H₃PO₂ cleanly erased it leaving 1,3,5-tribromobenzene ✅

---

### Example 2: Aniline to Benzoic Acid (Via Sandmeyer Cyanide)
How will you convert **aniline** into **benzoic acid** in three steps?

**Solution:**
- Step 1: C₆H₅NH₂ + NaNO₂/HCl (0–5°C) → C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻
- Step 2 (Sandmeyer): C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ + CuCN/KCN → C₆H₅CN (benzonitrile)
- Step 3 (Hydrolysis): C₆H₅CN + H₃O⁺ (Δ) → **C₆H₅COOH (benzoic acid)**!

Jaanch: –NH₂ → –N₂⁺ → –CN → –COOH cleanly adds the carboxyl carbon ✅

---

### Example 3: Explaining Why Coupling with Phenol Needs Alkaline pH
Why is coupling of benzene diazonium chloride with phenol carried out in mildly alkaline medium (pH 9–10) rather than strongly acidic medium?

**Solution:**
Benzene diazonium ion (C₆H₅N₂⁺) is a **weak electrophile**. In mildly alkaline medium (pH 9–10), phenol is deprotonated to the **phenoxide ion (C₆H₅O⁻)**, which is far more electron-rich and strongly activates the para position toward electrophilic attack! (In strongly alkaline medium, C₆H₅N₂⁺ would convert into unreactive diazohydroxide/diazotate; in acidic medium, neutral phenol is not reactive enough.)

Jaanch: pH 9–10 maximises [C₆H₅O⁻] while keeping C₆H₅N₂⁺ active ✅

## Aam Galtiyan (Common Mistakes)

1. **H₃PO₂ (hypophosphorous acid) ki jagah H₃PO₄ likh dena:**
   - *Galat:* C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ + H₃PO₄ → Benzene.
   - *Sahi:* **H₃PO₂ + H₂O** (ya **CH₃CH₂OH**) reducing agent hai jo C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ ko **Benzene (C₆H₆)** mein reduce karta hai!

2. **Phenol aur Aniline ke coupling colours mix kar dena:**
   - *Galat:* Phenol se yellow aur aniline se orange dye banta hai.
   - *Sahi:* **Phenol** → **Orange dye** (p-hydroxyazobenzene, pH 9–10); **Aniline** → **Yellow dye** (p-aminoazobenzene, pH 4–5)!

3. **Sandmeyer aur Gattermann ke catalysts mix kar dena:**
   - *Galat:* Gattermann mein Cu₂Cl₂ use hota hai.
   - *Sahi:* **Sandmeyer** = **Cu(I) salt** (Cu₂Cl₂ / Cu₂Br₂ / CuCN); **Gattermann** = **Cu metal powder** + HCl/HBr!

## Quick Recap

- **Diazotisation:** Ar–NH₂ + NaNO₂ + 2HCl (273–278 K) → Ar–N₂⁺Cl⁻ (stabilised by ring resonance).
- **Replacements:** Cu₂Cl₂ (Cl), Cu₂Br₂ (Br), CuCN (CN), KI (I), HBF₄/Δ (F), HBF₄ + NaNO₂/Cu (NO₂), **H₃PO₂/H₂O or C₂H₅OH (H → Benzene)**, warm H₂O (OH → Phenol).
- **Azo Coupling:** + Phenol (pH 9–10) → **p-Hydroxyazobenzene (Orange)**; + Aniline (pH 4–5) → **p-Aminoazobenzene (Yellow)**.

7. Convert **nitrobenzene** into **phenol** via diazonium salt.
8. Convert **p-toluidine** into **p-bromotoluene** via a diazonium salt.
9. What is the role of ethanol (CH₃CH₂OH) when warmed with benzene diazonium chloride? What does ethanol itself get oxidised into?

### Answers

1. **C₆H₅NH₂ + NaNO₂ + 2HCl —(273–278 K)→ C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ + NaCl + 2H₂O**. Above 278 K, the diazonium salt decomposes with water to form **phenol** and N₂ gas.
2. Because in aromatic diazonium ions (Ar–N₂⁺), the positive charge is **delocalised over the benzene ring by resonance**; aliphatic R–N₂⁺ lacks resonance and immediately loses N₂.
3. - **Sandmeyer** uses **Cu(I) halide** (Cu₂Cl₂/HCl or Cu₂Br₂/HBr): C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ + Cu₂Cl₂/HCl → C₆H₅Cl + N₂↑ (higher yield).
   - **Gattermann** uses **Cu powder + HCl/HBr**: C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ + Cu/HCl → C₆H₅Cl + N₂↑.
4. (a) + HBF₄, then heat → **C₆H₅F** (Balz–Schiemann).
   (b) + KI (warm) → **C₆H₅I**.
   (c) + H₃PO₂ + H₂O (or C₂H₅OH) → **C₆H₆**.
   (d) + HBF₄ → C₆H₅N₂⁺BF₄⁻ —(NaNO₂ / Cu, Δ)→ **C₆H₅NO₂**.
5. (a) + Phenol (OH⁻, pH 9–10) → **p-Hydroxyazobenzene** (**Orange dye**).
   (b) + Aniline (H⁺, pH 4–5) → **p-Aminoazobenzene** (**Yellow dye**).
6. Aniline —(Br₂/H₂O)→ 2,4,6-Tribromoaniline —(NaNO₂/HCl, 0–5°C)→ 2,4,6-Tribromobenzenediazonium chloride —(H₃PO₂/H₂O)→ **1,3,5-Tribromobenzene**.
7. C₆H₅NO₂ —(Fe/HCl)→ C₆H₅NH₂ —(NaNO₂/HCl, 0–5°C)→ C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ —(warm H₂O)→ **C₆H₅OH (phenol)**.
8. p-CH₃–C₆H₄–NH₂ —(NaNO₂/HCl, 0–5°C)→ p-CH₃–C₆H₄–N₂⁺Cl⁻ —(Cu₂Br₂/HBr, Sandmeyer)→ **p-CH₃–C₆H₄–Br (p-bromotoluene)**.
9. Ethanol acts as a **reducing agent** converting C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ into **benzene (C₆H₆)**; ethanol itself gets oxidised to **ethanal (CH₃CHO)**: C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ + CH₃CH₂OH → C₆H₆ + CH₃CHO + N₂↑ + HCl.
10. C₆H₅NH₂ —(NaNO₂/HCl, 0–5°C)→ C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ —(CuCN/KCN)→ **C₆H₅CN** —(H₂/Ni or LiAlH₄)→ **C₆H₅CH₂NH₂ (benzylamine)**.
    Jaanch: Clean 3-step conversion adding +1 carbon via Sandmeyer cyanide ✅
