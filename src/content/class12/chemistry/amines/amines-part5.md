## Naye Shabd (Master Conversion & Test Snapshot)

| Goal / Test | Key Reagent / Result |
|---|---|
| Step-up amine (+1 C) | R–X → **KCN** → R–CN → **LiAlH₄ / H₂-Ni** → R–CH₂NH₂ |
| Step-down amine (−1 C) | R–CONH₂ + **Br₂ + 4NaOH** → R–NH₂ (**Hoffmann bromamide**) |
| Pure 1° aliphatic amine | **Gabriel phthalimide** (NO aniline!) |
| Distinguish 1° / 2° / 3° amines | **Hinsberg (C₆H₅SO₂Cl)**: 1° KOH-soluble, 2° KOH-insoluble, 3° no rxn |
| Distinguish 1° vs 2°/3° | **Carbylamine (CHCl₃ + alc. KOH)**: 1° ONLY gives foul R–NC |
| Distinguish Ar–NH₂ vs R–NH₂ | **Azo-dye test** (NaNO₂/HCl 0–5°C + β-naphthol): Ar–NH₂ → orange-red dye! |

## Mixed Solved Examples

### Example 1: Four Classic Multi-Step Conversions
Carry out the following conversions:
(a) Ethanoic acid → Methanamine (step-down: 2 C → 1 C)
(b) Methanamine → Ethanamine (step-up: 1 C → 2 C)
(c) Aniline → p-Bromoaniline
(d) Benzamide → Toluene

**Solution:**
(a) **Ethanoic acid → Methanamine:**
CH₃COOH —(NH₃, Δ)→ CH₃CONH₂ —(Br₂ + 4NaOH, Hoffmann)→ **CH₃NH₂**.

(b) **Methanamine → Ethanamine:**
CH₃NH₂ —(HNO₂)→ CH₃OH —(SOCl₂)→ CH₃Cl —(KCN)→ CH₃CN —(LiAlH₄ or H₂/Ni)→ **CH₃CH₂NH₂**.

(c) **Aniline → p-Bromoaniline:**
C₆H₅NH₂ —((CH₃CO)₂O / pyridine)→ C₆H₅NHCOCH₃ —(Br₂ / CH₃COOH)→ p-Br–C₆H₄–NHCOCH₃ —(H⁺/H₂O)→ **p-Br–C₆H₄–NH₂**.

(d) **Benzamide → Toluene:**
C₆H₅CONH₂ —(Br₂ + 4NaOH, Hoffmann)→ C₆H₅NH₂ —(NaNO₂/HCl, 0–5°C)→ C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ —(H₃PO₂/H₂O)→ C₆H₆ —(CH₃Cl / anhyd. AlCl₃)→ **C₆H₅CH₃ (toluene)**.

Jaanch: Every conversion respects directing effects, step-up/step-down rules, and protection steps ✅

### Example 2: Unknown Organic Compound Deduction (A, B, C)
An aromatic compound **A** on treatment with aqueous ammonia and heating forms compound **B**, which on heating with Br₂ and KOH forms compound **C** of molecular formula **C₆H₇N**. Write the structures and IUPAC names of A, B, and C.

**Solution:**
- **C** (C₆H₇N) formed from Hoffmann bromamide degradation of aromatic amide **B** must be **Aniline (C₆H₅–NH₂)**!
- Since Hoffmann removes 1 carbonyl carbon (–CO–), **B = Benzamide (C₆H₅–CONH₂)**!
- Since **B** is formed by heating **A** with aqueous NH₃, **A = Benzoic acid (C₆H₅–COOH)**!

Jaanch: C₆H₅COOH (A) → C₆H₅CONH₂ (B) → C₆H₅NH₂ (C, C₆H₇N) ✅

## Aam Galtiyan (Mixed-Conversion Traps)

1. **Nitrobenzene se m-Bromophenol banane mein pehle –NO₂ ko –NH₂ mein reduce kar dena:**
   - *Galat:* C₆H₅NO₂ → C₆H₅NH₂ → Br₂ (yeh 2,4,6-tribromoaniline dega, meta nahi!).
   - *Sahi:* Pehle **meta-directing –NO₂** rehte hue **Br₂/FeBr₃** lagao → m-bromonitrobenzene, phir reduce + diazotise + warm H₂O → **m-bromophenol**!

2. **Step-up (RNH₂ → RCH₂NH₂) mein seedha CH₃Cl daal dena:**
   - *Galat:* CH₃NH₂ + CH₃Cl → CH₃CH₂NH₂.
   - *Sahi:* CH₃NH₂ + CH₃Cl **2° amine (CH₃)₂NH** banata hai (carbon chain nahi badhata)! Chain badhane ke liye **HNO₂ → R–OH → R–Cl → KCN → reduction** follow karo!

## Quick Recap

- **Aqueous Basicity:** **Ethyl = 231** ((C₂H₅)₂NH > (C₂H₅)₃N > C₂H₅NH₂ > NH₃); **Methyl = 213** ((CH₃)₂NH > CH₃NH₂ > (CH₃)₃N > NH₃); **Aniline << NH₃**.
- **Master Aromatic Synthesis:** Use **–NO₂** for meta-direction, **–NHCOCH₃** for clean para-direction, **–NH₂** for 2,4,6-trisubstitution, and **–N₂⁺Cl⁻ + H₃PO₂** to erase nitrogen completely!

## Practice Questions

1. Convert: (a) Nitrobenzene to benzoic acid, (b) Benzene to m-bromophenol, (c) Aniline to 2,4,6-tribromofluorobenzene.
2. Convert: (a) Propanamide to ethanamine, (b) Ethanamine to propan-1-amine, (c) Chlorobenzene to p-chloroaniline.
3. Arrange in increasing order of basic strength in aqueous solution:
   (a) C₆H₅NH₂, C₆H₅N(CH₃)₂, (C₂H₅)₂NH, CH₃NH₂
   (b) (CH₃)₃N, (CH₃)₂NH, CH₃NH₂, NH₃
4. Give ONE chemical test to distinguish between:
   (a) Dimethylamine and Trimethylamine
   (b) Aniline and Ethylamine
   (c) Ethylamine and Diethylamine
5. Complete the reaction sequence:
   C₆H₅NO₂ —(Fe/HCl)→ **A** —(NaNO₂ + HCl, 273 K)→ **B** —(H₃PO₂ + H₂O)→ **C**.
6. Explain why pK_b of aniline is more than that of methylamine.
7. Why is an aromatic amine like aniline NOT prepared by Gabriel phthalimide synthesis?
8. Account for: (a) Ethylamine is soluble in water whereas aniline is not. (b) Aniline does not undergo Friedel–Crafts reaction.
9. Write the products of coupling benzene diazonium chloride with: (a) Phenol (pH 9–10), (b) Aniline (pH 4–5).
10. An aromatic compound **X** (C₇H₇NO) on treatment with Br₂/NaOH gives **Y** (C₆H₇N), which gives a positive carbylamine test and forms an orange dye on diazotisation followed by coupling with phenol. Identify X and Y.

---

### Answers

1. (a) C₆H₅NO₂ —(Fe/HCl)→ C₆H₅NH₂ —(NaNO₂/HCl, 0–5°C)→ C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ —(CuCN/KCN)→ C₆H₅CN —(H₃O⁺)→ **C₆H₅COOH**.
   (b) C₆H₆ —(HNO₃/H₂SO₄)→ C₆H₅NO₂ —(Br₂/FeBr₃)→ m-Br–C₆H₄–NO₂ —(Fe/HCl)→ m-Br–C₆H₄–NH₂ —(NaNO₂/HCl, 0–5°C; warm H₂O)→ **m-Bromophenol**.
   (c) Aniline —(Br₂/H₂O)→ 2,4,6-Tribromoaniline —(NaNO₂/HCl, 0–5°C)→ Diazonium salt —(HBF₄, Δ)→ **2,4,6-Tribromofluorobenzene**.
2. (a) CH₃CH₂CONH₂ —(Br₂ + 4NaOH, Hoffmann)→ **CH₃CH₂NH₂**.
   (b) CH₃CH₂NH₂ —(HNO₂)→ CH₃CH₂OH —(SOCl₂)→ CH₃CH₂Cl —(KCN)→ CH₃CH₂CN —(LiAlH₄)→ **CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₂**.
   (c) C₆H₅Cl —(conc. HNO₃/H₂SO₄)→ p-Cl–C₆H₄–NO₂ (major) —(Fe/HCl)→ **p-Chloroaniline**.
3. (a) **C₆H₅NH₂ < C₆H₅N(CH₃)₂ < CH₃NH₂ < (C₂H₅)₂NH**.
   (b) **NH₃ < (CH₃)₃N < CH₃NH₂ < (CH₃)₂NH** ("213" rule!).
4. (a) **Hinsberg test (C₆H₅SO₂Cl):** Dimethylamine (2°) forms KOH-insoluble sulphonamide; Trimethylamine (3°) does not react.
   (b) **Azo-dye test (NaNO₂/HCl 0–5°C + β-naphthol):** Aniline → brilliant orange-red dye; Ethylamine → N₂ gas bubbles, no dye.
   (c) **Carbylamine test (CHCl₃ + alc. KOH):** Ethylamine (1°) → foul isocyanide smell; Diethylamine (2°) → no reaction.
5. **A = C₆H₅NH₂ (aniline)**; **B = C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ (benzene diazonium chloride)**; **C = C₆H₆ (benzene)**.
6. In aniline, nitrogen's lone pair is delocalised into the benzene ring by resonance (weaker base → **higher pK_b**). In methylamine, the +I methyl group increases electron density on N (stronger base → **lower pK_b**).
7. Because aryl halides do not undergo SN2 nucleophilic substitution with the potassium phthalimide anion.
8. (a) Ethylamine forms strong H-bonds with water; aniline has a large hydrophobic benzene ring that prevents water solubility.
   (b) Basic aniline forms a salt with Lewis acid AlCl₃ (C₆H₅–NH₂⁺–AlCl₃⁻), which strongly deactivates the ring.
9. (a) **p-Hydroxyazobenzene** (orange dye). (b) **p-Aminoazobenzene** (yellow dye).
10. **X = Benzamide (C₆H₅CONH₂, C₇H₇NO)**; **Y = Aniline (C₆H₅NH₂, C₆H₇N)**.
    Jaanch: C₇H₇NO − CO = C₆H₇N; aniline is 1° (carbylamine+) and aromatic (azo-dye+) ✅
