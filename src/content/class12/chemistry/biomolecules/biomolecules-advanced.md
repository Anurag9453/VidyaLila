Khushi ek biochemistry research workshop mein ek polarimeter dekh rahi thi: jab pure **α-D-glucose** ko paani mein ghola gaya, toh uski specific rotation **+112°** thi — par kuch ghanton mein woh ghat kar **+52.5°** par ruk gayi! Jab pure **β-D-glucose (+19°)** ko paani mein ghola gaya, toh uski rotation badh kar **EXACT +52.5°** par ruk gayi! Iska naam hai **Mutarotation** — aur sirf ek simple linear equation se hum equilibrium par α aur β forms ka exact percentage nikal sakte hain!

## 1. Mutarotation & Equilibrium Anomer Percentages

**Mutarotation:** Freshly dissolved pure α- ya β-anomer ke specific optical rotation ka spontaneous change hokar ek constant equilibrium value par pahunchna (via tiny ~0.02% open-chain aldehyde intermediate!).
- **α-D-Glucose (+112°)  ⇌  Open chain (~0.02%)  ⇌  β-D-Glucose (+19°)**
- Equilibrium specific rotation = **+52.5°**!
- **Condition:** Mutarotation sirf **reducing sugars** (free hemiacetal –OH) dikhati hain! **Sucrose** aur **methyl glucoside** (acetal) mutarotation **NAHI** dikhate!

### Calculating Exact Equilibrium Percentages:
Let mole fraction of α-anomer = x, and β-anomer = (1 − x):
112 x + 19 (1 − x) = 52.5
93 x = 52.5 − 19 = 33.5 → **x = 33.5 / 93 = 0.36 (36% α-D-glucose)** and **1 − x = 64% β-D-glucose**!
*(Why is β-D-glucose (64%) more abundant than α-D-glucose (36%)? Because in the chair conformation of β-D-glucose, ALL five bulky substituents — including C1–OH — sit in sterically strain-free **equatorial** positions!)*

## 2. Epimers & Osazone Formation (Why Glucose = Mannose = Fructose!)

- **Epimers:** Diastereomers jo sirf **EK chiral carbon** par configuration mein alag hote hain:
  - **D-Glucose and D-Mannose** are **C2 epimers**.
  - **D-Glucose and D-Galactose** are **C4 epimers**.
  - **α-D-Glucose and β-D-Glucose** are **C1 epimers** (called **Anomers**!).
- **Osazone Formation (3 moles of Phenylhydrazine, C₆H₅NHNH₂):**
  - Excess phenylhydrazine reacts ONLY at **C1 and C2** (consuming **3 moles** of C₆H₅NHNH₂: 1 mole oxidises C2–OH to C2=O producing NH₃ + C₆H₅NH₂, and 2 moles form the bis-phenylhydrazone!).
  - **GOLDEN EXAM FACT:** Because **D-Glucose, D-Mannose, and D-Fructose** have **IDENTICAL configurations at C3, C4, and C5**, all three form the **EXACT SAME yellow crystalline Osazone (Glucosazone)**! (D-Galactose differs at C4 → forms a DIFFERENT osazone!)

## 3. Isoelectric Point (pI) of Amino Acids

**Isoelectric Point (pI):** Woh exact **pH** jis par amino acid ka **net electric charge = 0** hota hai (100% zwitterion form → electric field mein kisi bhi electrode ki taraf migrate NAHI karta, aur water solubility minimum hoti hai!).

### Three Exact Formulas for pI:
1. **Neutral Amino Acids** (1 –COOH with pKₐ₁, 1 –NH₃⁺ with pKₐ₂):
   **pI = (pKₐ₁ + pKₐ₂) / 2**   (typically pI ≈ **5.5–6.5**)
2. **Acidic Amino Acids** (e.g., Aspartic acid, Glutamic acid — has extra side-chain –COOH with pKₐ_R):
   Average the **TWO LOWEST (acidic) pKₐ values**:
   **pI = (pKₐ₁ + pKₐ_R) / 2**   (typically pI ≈ **2.8–3.2**)
3. **Basic Amino Acids** (e.g., Lysine, Arginine — has extra side-chain –NH₃⁺ with pKₐ_R):
   Average the **TWO HIGHEST (basic) pKₐ values**:
   **pI = (pKₐ₂ + pKₐ_R) / 2**   (typically pI ≈ **9.7–10.8**)

### Electrophoresis Rule (Migration in Electric Field):
- If **pH < pI** (acidic relative to pI): Amino acid is positively charged (**cation**) → migrates to **Cathode (−)**!
- If **pH > pI** (basic relative to pI): Amino acid is negatively charged (**anion**) → migrates to **Anode (+)**!
- If **pH = pI**: Neutral zwitterion → **stays stationary**!

## 4. N-Terminal Analysis (Sanger's Reagent) & Lobry de Bruyn–van Ekenstein Rearrangement

1. **Sanger's Reagent (2,4-Dinitrofluorobenzene, DNFB):** Tags the free **N-terminal amino acid** of a polypeptide via SNAr! After total acid hydrolysis, the yellow DNP-derivative identifies the exact N-terminal residue!
2. **Why Does Fructose (a Ketone!) Reduce Fehling's & Tollens' Reagents?**
   In mildly alkaline medium (Fehling's / Tollens'), **D-fructose** undergoes **Lobry de Bruyn–van Ekenstein rearrangement** via an **enediol intermediate** to equilibrate into **D-glucose and D-mannose** (aldehydes!), which immediately reduce Cu²⁺ / Ag⁺!

## Deep Solved Problems

### Problem 1: Calculating pI of Glutamic Acid and Lysine
Calculate the isoelectric point (pI) of:
(a) **Glutamic acid** (acidic): pKₐ₁(α-COOH) = 2.19, pKₐ_R(side-chain COOH) = 4.25, pKₐ₂(α-NH₃⁺) = 9.67.
(b) **Lysine** (basic): pKₐ₁(α-COOH) = 2.18, pKₐ₂(α-NH₃⁺) = 8.95, pKₐ_R(side-chain NH₃⁺) = 10.53.

**Solution:**
(a) Glutamic acid is acidic → average the two lowest pKₐ values:
pI = (2.19 + 4.25) / 2 = 6.44 / 2 = **3.22**.
(b) Lysine is basic → average the two highest pKₐ values:
pI = (8.95 + 10.53) / 2 = 19.48 / 2 = **9.74**.

Jaanch: Acidic amino acid has low pI (3.22 < 7); basic amino acid has high pI (9.74 > 7) ✅

---

### Problem 2: Electrophoretic Separation of a Mixture at pH 6.0
A mixture of **Alanine (pI = 6.0)**, **Aspartic acid (pI = 2.8)**, and **Lysine (pI = 9.7)** is subjected to electrophoresis at **pH 6.0**. Which direction does each amino acid move?

**Solution:**
Compare buffer pH (6.0) with each pI:
- **Alanine (pI = 6.0 = pH):** Net charge = 0 → **does NOT move** (stays at origin).
- **Aspartic acid (pH 6.0 > pI 2.8):** Exists as **anion (−)** → migrates toward **Anode (+)**.
- **Lysine (pH 6.0 < pI 9.7):** Exists as **cation (+)** → migrates toward **Cathode (−)**.

Jaanch: pH > pI → anion → anode (+); pH < pI → cation → cathode (−) ✅

### Problem 3: Equilibrium Composition of a Sugar Showing Mutarotation
A freshly prepared α-anomer of a sugar has [α] = +150° and its β-anomer has [α] = +50°. At equilibrium, the specific rotation settles at +80°. Calculate the percentage of α and β forms at equilibrium.

**Solution:**
Let fraction of α = x, β = 1 − x:
150 x + 50 (1 − x) = 80
100 x + 50 = 80 → 100 x = 30 → **x = 0.30 (30% α-anomer)** and **1 − x = 70% β-anomer**.

Jaanch: 0.30(150) + 0.70(50) = 45 + 35 = +80° ✅

---

### Problem 4: Why Fructose Gives Positive Tollens' Test Despite Being a Ketone
Fructose is a ketohexose (no –CHO group), yet it reduces both Tollens' reagent and Fehling's solution. Explain!

**Solution:**
Both Tollens' and Fehling's reagents are **alkaline**. In alkaline medium, fructose tautomerises via a **1,2-enediol intermediate** (**Lobry de Bruyn–van Ekenstein rearrangement**) into an equilibrium mixture containing **D-glucose and D-mannose**, both of which are aldehydes and rapidly reduce [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ and Cu²⁺!

Jaanch: Base-catalysed enediol isomerisation converts α-hydroxyketone into aldose ✅

---

### Problem 5: Identifying Tripeptide Sequence Using Sanger's Reagent
A tripeptide gives Gly, Ala, and Phe on complete hydrolysis. Treatment with **Sanger's reagent (DNFB)** followed by hydrolysis yields **DNP–Ala**. Partial hydrolysis gives the dipeptide **Phe–Gly**. Deduce the exact sequence of the tripeptide!

**Solution:**
- Sanger's reagent tags the **N-terminal** residue → **Ala** is at the N-terminus (**Ala–...**).
- Partial hydrolysis fragment **Phe–Gly** shows Phe is directly bonded to Gly (with Gly at C-terminus).
- Complete sequence: **Ala–Phe–Gly**!

Jaanch: N-terminus = Ala, fragment = Phe–Gly, total 3 residues = Ala–Phe–Gly ✅

## Advanced Practice Questions

1. Calculate the isoelectric point (pI) of **Glycine** given pKₐ₁ = 2.34 and pKₐ₂ = 9.60.
2. Calculate the pI of **Aspartic acid** given pKₐ₁ = 1.88, pKₐ_R = 3.65, and pKₐ₂ = 9.60.
3. At pH 2.0, what is the net charge on Glycine (pI = 5.97), and toward which electrode will it migrate?
4. Why do **D-glucose, D-mannose, and D-fructose** yield the exact same osazone with excess phenylhydrazine, while **D-galactose** yields a different osazone?
5. How many moles of phenylhydrazine (C₆H₅NHNH₂) are consumed per mole of glucose during complete osazone formation?
6. Why does **methyl α-D-glucopyranoside** NOT exhibit mutarotation and NOT reduce Fehling's solution?
7. Why is **β-D-glucopyranose (64%)** more stable than **α-D-glucopyranose (36%)** in aqueous solution?
8. Define **C2-epimers** and **C4-epimers** with one example pair each.
9. A double-stranded DNA fragment has **500 base pairs**, of which **180 are A–T pairs**. Calculate the total number of hydrogen bonds holding the two strands together.
10. What is **Sanger's reagent**? Which end (N-terminal or C-terminal) of a polypeptide chain does it identify?

---

### Solutions

1. Neutral amino acid: pI = (2.34 + 9.60) / 2 = 11.94 / 2 = **5.97**.
   Jaanch: 2.34 + 9.60 = 11.94; 11.94 / 2 = 5.97 ✅
2. Acidic amino acid (average two lowest pKₐ): pI = (1.88 + 3.65) / 2 = 5.53 / 2 = **2.765 ≈ 2.77**.
   Jaanch: 5.53 / 2 = 2.765 ✅
3. Since pH (2.0) < pI (5.97), Glycine is fully protonated as **⁺H₃N–CH₂–COOH** (**+1 charge**) and migrates toward the **Cathode (−)**.
4. Osazone formation destroys stereochemistry at C1 and C2 only. **D-Glucose, D-mannose, and D-fructose** have identical configurations at **C3, C4, and C5**, so they form the same osazone. **D-Galactose** differs at **C4**, yielding a different osazone!
5. **3 moles** of phenylhydrazine per mole of glucose (1 mole oxidises C2–OH to C2=O; 2 moles form the bis-hydrazone at C1 and C2).
6. Because methyl α-D-glucopyranoside is a cyclic **acetal** (not a hemiacetal!), so it cannot open in neutral/alkaline water to form a free –CHO group — hence no mutarotation and non-reducing!
7. In the chair conformation of **β-D-glucopyranose**, ALL five bulky groups (four –OH groups and –CH₂OH) occupy strain-free **equatorial** positions (zero 1,3-diaxial repulsion), whereas α-D-glucopyranose has an axial C1–OH!
8. **C2-epimers:** D-Glucose & D-Mannose (differ only at C2). **C4-epimers:** D-Glucose & D-Galactose (differ only at C4).
9. Total base pairs = 500; A–T pairs = 180 → G–C pairs = 500 − 180 = **320**.
   Total H-bonds = 180(2) + 320(3) = 360 + 960 = **1320 hydrogen bonds**!
   Jaanch: 360 + 960 = 1320 ✅
10. **2,4-Dinitrofluorobenzene (DNFB)**; it identifies the **N-terminal** amino acid of a polypeptide chain.
