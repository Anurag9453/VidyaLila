## Naye Shabd (Key Terms)

| Term | Hinglish Meaning |
|---|---|
| **α-Amino Acid** | Woh organic compound jismein –NH₂ aur –COOH dono ek hi α-carbon par jude hon: R–CH(NH₂)–COOH |
| **Essential Amino Acids** | Woh 10 amino acids jo hamara sharir khud nahi bana sakta aur humein **diet (khaane)** se lene padte hain (jaise Valine, Leucine, Lysine) |
| **Zwitterion** | Amino acid ka neutral dipolar ion form (**⁺H₃N–CH(R)–COO⁻**) jismein + aur − dono charges ek saath maujood hon |
| **Peptide Linkage (–CO–NH–)** | Ek amino acid ke –COOH aur doosre ke –NH₂ ke beech paani (H₂O) nikalne se bana amide bond |
| **Fibrous vs Globular Proteins** | **Fibrous** = lambe thread-like, water-insoluble (keratin, myosin); **Globular** = spherical folded, water-soluble (insulin, albumin) |
| **Denaturation** | Temperature ya pH badalne se protein ke **H-bonds tootna**, jisse uski **2°, 3°, aur 4° structures** aur biological activity nasht ho jati hai (par **1° structure intact** rehti hai!) |

## 1. Amino Acids — Classification, Optical Activity & Zwitterion

Proteins ke hydrolysis se **20 naturally occurring α-amino acids** milte hain (sabhi natural amino acids **L-configuration** mein hote hain!).

### (a) Classification of Amino Acids:
1. **By Acidic / Basic / Neutral Nature:**
   - **Neutral:** Equal –NH₂ and –COOH (1 each) — e.g., **Glycine, Alanine, Valine**.
   - **Acidic:** More –COOH than –NH₂ — e.g., **Aspartic acid, Glutamic acid**.
   - **Basic:** More –NH₂ than –COOH — e.g., **Lysine, Arginine, Histidine**.
2. **By Dietary Need:**
   - **Non-essential (10):** Body can synthesise them (e.g., Glycine, Alanine, Glutamic acid).
   - **Essential (10):** Must be obtained from diet (e.g., Valine, Leucine, Isoleucine, Lysine, Phenylalanine, Tryptophan).

### (b) Optical Activity Exception (100% Exam Question!):
Sabhi natural α-amino acids **optically active (chiral)** hote hain — **SIWAYE GLYCINE (H₂N–CH₂–COOH) ke!**
Kyun? Kyunki glycine ke α-carbon par **do identical H-atoms** jude hain (achiral / no asymmetric carbon)!

### (c) Zwitterion & Amphoteric Behaviour:
Aqueous solution aur solid state mein –COOH apna proton (H⁺) –NH₂ ko de deta hai → **Zwitterion (dipolar ion)**:
**R–CH(NH₂)–COOH  ⇌  R–CH(⁺NH₃)–COO⁻**
- **Why do amino acids have high melting points and behave like salts?** Because they exist as strongly attracted electrostatic **zwitterions**!
- **Amphoteric nature:**
  - Acidic medium (low pH) mein: COO⁻ proton lekar **cation** (⁺H₃N–CH(R)–COOH) ban jata hai.
  - Basic medium (high pH) mein: ⁺NH₃ proton dekar **anion** (H₂N–CH(R)–COO⁻) ban jata hai.

## 2. Peptide Bond & Polypeptides

Jab do amino acids (e.g., Glycine + Alanine) judte hain, toh ek ka –COOH aur doosre ka –NH₂ milkar H₂O nikalte hain aur **Peptide Bond (–CO–NH–)** banta hai!
- **2 amino acids → Dipeptide** (**1** peptide bond) — e.g., Glycylalanine (Gly–Ala).
- **3 amino acids → Tripeptide** (**2** peptide bonds).
- **n amino acids → Polypeptide** (**n − 1** peptide bonds!).
- **Protein:** Polypeptide with **> 100 amino acid residues** and molecular mass **> 10,000 u** (Exception: **Insulin** mein sirf **51 amino acids** hain, phir bhi use protein kehte hain!).

---

## 3. Classification & Four Levels of Protein Structure

### (a) Fibrous vs Globular Proteins:
| Feature | Fibrous Proteins | Globular Proteins |
|---|---|---|
| Shape | Parallel polypeptide chains forming **fibre-like / thread-like** structure | Polypeptide chains coil into **spherical / globular** shape |
| Bonds holding chains | Hydrogen bonds and **disulphide (–S–S–)** bonds | H-bonds, disulphide, electrostatic & van der Waals |
| Water solubility | **INSOLUBLE** in water | **SOLUBLE** in water |
| Examples | **Keratin** (hair, wool, silk), **Myosin** (muscles) | **Insulin**, **Albumins** (egg white), **Haemoglobin** |

### (b) Four Structural Levels of Proteins:
1. **Primary (1°) Structure:** Polypeptide chain mein **amino acids ka exact linear sequence** (peptide bonds se juda). Ek bhi amino acid badalne se alag protein ban jata hai (jaise sickle-cell anaemia!).
2. **Secondary (2°) Structure:** Polypeptide backbone ka regular local folding (**intramolecular / intermolecular H-bonds** between C=O and N–H of peptide bonds):
   - **α-Helix:** Right-handed screw/spiral stabilised by **intramolecular H-bonds** between C=O of one turn and N–H of adjacent turn (e.g., keratin in hair/wool).
   - **β-Pleated Sheet:** Stretched chains lying side-by-side held by **intermolecular H-bonds** (e.g., silk fibroin).
3. **Tertiary (3°) Structure:** 2° structure ka overall **3D folding** (gives fibrous or globular shape). Stabilised by **4 forces:** Hydrogen bonds, **Disulphide linkages (–S–S–)**, van der Waals forces, and **Electrostatic (salt-bridge)** attractions.
4. **Quaternary (4°) Structure:** Do ya zyada polypeptide chains (**subunits**) ka ek saath spatial arrangement (e.g., Haemoglobin has 4 subunits!).

## 4. Denaturation of Proteins

Jab native protein ko **physical change (temperature/heat)** ya **chemical change (pH/acid/base)** diya jata hai:
- Hydrogen bonds aur non-covalent forces disturb hokar **globules uncoil** aur **helices unfold** ho jati hain!
- Protein apni **biological activity kho deta hai** (**Denaturation**)!
- **CRITICAL EXAM FACT:** Denaturation mein **2°, 3°, aur 4° structures destroy ho jati hain**, lekin **1° (PRIMARY) STRUCTURE (peptide bonds / amino-acid sequence) BILKUL INTACT REHTI HAI!**
- **Common Examples:** (i) **Coagulation of egg white on boiling**, (ii) **Curdling of milk** (bacteria producing lactic acid lowers pH!).

## Solved Examples

### Example 1: Counting Peptide Bonds and Possible Tripeptides
(a) How many peptide bonds are present in a **tetrapeptide**?
(b) How many different **tripeptides** can be formed using three different amino acids Gly, Ala, and Val (using each once)?

**Solution:**
(a) An n-peptide has **(n − 1)** peptide bonds. For a tetrapeptide (n = 4): 4 − 1 = **3 peptide bonds**.
(b) With 3 distinct amino acids used once each, total arrangements = 3! = 3 × 2 × 1 = **6 tripeptides** (Gly–Ala–Val, Gly–Val–Ala, Ala–Gly–Val, Ala–Val–Gly, Val–Gly–Ala, Val–Ala–Gly).

Jaanch: 4 residues linked linearly require 3 links; 3! = 6 distinct sequences ✅

---

### Example 2: Explaining Water Solubility of Amino Acids
Why do amino acids behave like crystalline salts (high melting points, soluble in water) rather than simple amines or carboxylic acids?

**Solution:**
In the solid state and aqueous solution, amino acids undergo internal proton transfer from –COOH to –NH₂ to form dipolar **zwitterions (⁺H₃N–CH(R)–COO⁻)**. Strong electrostatic (ionic) attractions between oppositely charged ends give high melting points and high water solubility (strong ion–dipole hydration), just like inorganic salts!

Jaanch: Dipolar ionic lattice = salt-like high MP and water solubility ✅

---

### Example 3: What Happens to Water During Boiling of an Egg?
Where does the water present in a raw egg go after boiling the egg?

**Solution:**
During boiling, the globular proteins in egg white undergo **denaturation** (unfolding and coagulation into a 3D network). The water molecules present in the egg get **absorbed / trapped inside this coagulated protein network through hydrogen bonding**!

Jaanch: No water evaporates through the sealed shell — it is H-bonded into the denatured protein gel ✅

## Aam Galtiyan (Common Mistakes)

1. **Sabhi natural amino acids ko optically active bol dena:**
   - *Galat:* Saare 20 α-amino acids optically active hain.
   - *Sahi:* **Glycine (H₂N–CH₂–COOH)** achiral aur **optically INACTIVE** hai! Baaki 19 optically active hain.

2. **Denaturation mein primary structure ko bhi tootna bol dena:**
   - *Galat:* Ubalne par protein ke peptide bonds toot jate hain.
   - *Sahi:* Denaturation mein **sirf 2°, 3°, aur 4° structures** toot-ti hain; **1° structure (covalent peptide bonds) intact** rehti hai!

3. **Tripeptide mein 3 peptide bonds gin lena:**
   - *Galat:* Tripeptide = 3 peptide bonds.
   - *Sahi:* Tripeptide = **3 amino acids** + **2 peptide bonds** (hamesha n − 1)!

## Quick Recap

- **Amino Acids:** 20 α-amino acids (all L-config; all chiral EXCEPT **Glycine**); exist as amphoteric **zwitterions** (⁺H₃N–CHR–COO⁻); 10 essential (from diet).
- **Peptide bond:** –CO–NH– (n residues → n − 1 peptide bonds).
- **Fibrous** (keratin, myosin — insoluble) vs **Globular** (insulin, albumin — soluble).
- **Structures:** 1° (sequence), 2° (α-helix & β-pleated sheet via H-bonds), 3° (3D folding via H-bond, –S–S–, electrostatic, vdW), 4° (subunits).
- **Denaturation:** Heat/pH destroys 2°, 3°, 4° & biological activity; **1° stays intact** (e.g., boiling egg, curdling milk).

---

### Answers

1. **Essential amino acids** cannot be synthesised in the body and must be obtained from diet (e.g., valine, leucine, lysine). **Non-essential amino acids** are synthesised by the body (e.g., glycine, alanine).
2. **Glycine (H₂N–CH₂–COOH)** — because its α-carbon is bonded to two identical hydrogen atoms (not chiral).
3. A **zwitterion** is a neutral dipolar ion containing both positive and negative charges on the same molecule: **CH₃–CH(⁺NH₃)–COO⁻**. It is amphoteric because –COO⁻ accepts H⁺ in acid (forming a cation) while –⁺NH₃ donates H⁺ in base (forming an anion).
4. A **peptide linkage (–CO–NH–)** is an amide bond formed between the –COOH of one amino acid and the –NH₂ of another with elimination of H₂O. A pentapeptide (5 amino acids) has 5 − 1 = **4 peptide bonds**.
5. **Fibrous:** Thread-like parallel chains, **insoluble** in water (e.g., keratin, myosin). **Globular:** Folded spherical chains, **soluble** in water (e.g., insulin, albumin).
6. **Secondary (2°):** Hydrogen bonds between C=O and N–H of the peptide backbone. **Tertiary (3°):** Hydrogen bonds, **disulphide linkages (–S–S–)**, van der Waals forces, and electrostatic attractions.
7. **α-Helix** is a right-handed spiral stabilised by **intramolecular H-bonds** along the same chain (e.g., wool/hair keratin). **β-Pleated sheet** consists of stretched chains held side-by-side by **intermolecular H-bonds** (e.g., silk).
8. **Denaturation** is the loss of biological activity and 3D folding of a native protein due to heat or pH change. **2°, 3°, and 4° structures** are destroyed, while the **primary (1°) structure remains completely intact**.
9. (i) **Coagulation of egg white** on boiling; (ii) **Curdling of milk** by lactic acid.
10. Because amino acids exist as **zwitterions** held together by strong electrostatic (ionic) forces in the solid state, whereas haloacids are neutral covalent molecules held only by weaker H-bonds/dipole forces.
