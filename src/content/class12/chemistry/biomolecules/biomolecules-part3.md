# Enzymes, Vitamins and Nucleic Acids (DNA and RNA)

Ab hum biological catalysts (**Enzymes**), micronutrients (**Vitamins** aur unki deficiency diseases), aur zindagi ka genetic blueprint (**Nucleic Acids: DNA aur RNA**) padhenge!

Doctor ne muskura kar bataya: "**Vitamin C paani mein ghul (water-soluble)** jata hai aur urine ke saath roz sharir se bahar nikal jata hai — isliye store nahi hota! Jabki **Vitamins A, D, E, K fat-soluble** hain aur liver mein store rehte hain!"

## Naye Shabd (Key Terms)

| Term | Hinglish Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Enzymes** | Globular proteins jo living organisms mein **biocatalysts** ka kaam karte hain (highly specific, activation energy Eₐ kam karte hain!) |
| **Vitamins** | Essential organic micronutrients jo diet mein chhoti maatra mein chahiye hote hain (inki kami se **deficiency diseases** hoti hain) |
| **Nucleoside** | **Nitrogenous Base + Pentose Sugar** (C1' par juda) — **NO phosphate group!** |
| **Nucleotide** | **Nitrogenous Base + Pentose Sugar + Phosphate Group** (C5' par juda) — nucleic acid ka monomer! |
| **Phosphodiester Linkage** | Do nucleotides ke beech C5' aur C3' carbons ko jodne wala phosphate bridge |
| **Complementary Base Pairing** | DNA double helix mein hydrogen bonds se fixed pairing: **A = T** (2 H-bonds) aur **G ≡ C** (3 H-bonds)! |

## 1. Enzymes (Biological Catalysts)

- Lagbhag sabhi enzymes **globular proteins** hote hain.
- **Extraordinary specificity:** Har enzyme sirf ek particular reaction ya substrate par kaam karta hai (ending in **-ase**):
  - **Maltase:** Maltose → 2 Glucose
  - **Invertase:** Sucrose → Glucose + Fructose
  - **Oxidoreductases:** Oxidation–reduction reactions catalysing enzymes
- **Mechanism:** Enzymes activation energy (Eₐ) ko drastically kam kar dete hain (e.g., sucrose hydrolysis ki Eₐ acid ke saath 6.22 kJ mol⁻¹ se enzyme sucrase ke saath sirf 2.15 kJ mol⁻¹ reh jati hai!).

## 2. Vitamins — Classification & Deficiency Diseases (100% Exam Table!)

### (a) Classification by Solubility:
1. **Fat-Soluble Vitamins:** **Vitamins A, D, E, and K** — oils/fats mein soluble, **liver aur adipose (fat) tissues mein store** hote hain!
2. **Water-Soluble Vitamins:** **B-Group Vitamins (B₁, B₂, B₆, B₁₂) and Vitamin C** — paani mein soluble, **urine ke through roz excrete** ho jate hain, isliye regular diet mein chahiye!
   - **CRITICAL EXCEPTION:** **Vitamin B₁₂** water-soluble hone ke bawajood **sharir (liver) mein store ho sakta hai**!

### (b) Must-Memorise Vitamin Deficiency Table:
| Vitamin | Chemical Name | Key Sources | Deficiency Disease |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vitamin A** | Retinol | Fish liver oil, carrots, butter, milk | **Xerophthalmia** (hardening of cornea), **Night blindness** |
| **Vitamin B₁** | Thiamine | Yeast, milk, green vegetables, cereals | **Beri-beri** (loss of appetite, retarded growth) |
| **Vitamin B₂** | Riboflavin | Milk, egg white, liver, kidney | **Cheilosis** (fissuring at corners of mouth & lips), digestive disorders |
| **Vitamin B₆** | Pyridoxine | Yeast, milk, egg yolk, cereals, grams | **Convulsions** |
| **Vitamin B₁₂** | Cyanocobalamin (contains **Co!**) | Meat, fish, egg, curd | **Pernicious anaemia** (RBC deficient in haemoglobin) |
| **Vitamin C** | Ascorbic acid | Citrus fruits (lemon, orange), amla, green leafy veg | **Scurvy** (bleeding gums) |
| **Vitamin D** | Calciferol | Exposure to sunlight, fish, egg yolk | **Rickets** (bone deformities in children), **Osteomalacia** (soft bones in adults) |
| **Vitamin E** | Tocopherol | Vegetable oils (wheat germ oil, sunflower oil) | Increased fragility of RBCs, **muscular weakness** |
| **Vitamin K** | Phylloquinone | Green leafy vegetables | **Increased blood clotting time** |

## 3. Nucleic Acids (DNA and RNA)

Chromosomes ke andar maujood **Nucleic Acids** (polynucleotides) genetic information (heredity) ko ek peedhi se doosri peedhi tak pahunchate hain!

### Three Chemical Components of Nucleic Acids:
1. **Pentose Sugar:**
   - **DNA:** **β-D-2-Deoxyribose** (C2 par –H, oxygen missing!)
   - **RNA:** **β-D-Ribose** (C2 par –OH)
2. **Nitrogenous Bases (Purines & Pyrimidines):**
   - **Purines (2-ring):** **Adenine (A)** and **Guanine (G)** — BOTH in DNA & RNA.
   - **Pyrimidines (1-ring):** **Cytosine (C)** (in both), **Thymine (T)** (ONLY in DNA!), **Uracil (U)** (ONLY in RNA!).
3. **Phosphoric Acid (H₃PO₄):** Forms **3'→5' phosphodiester linkage** joining adjacent nucleotides!

### Nucleoside vs Nucleotide:
- **Nucleoside** = Sugar + Base (at C1')
- **Nucleotide** = Sugar + Base (at C1') + Phosphate (at C5')

### DNA vs RNA Complete Comparison:
| Feature | DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid) | RNA (Ribonucleic Acid) |
|---|---|---|
| Pentose Sugar | **β-D-2-Deoxyribose** | **β-D-Ribose** |
| Nitrogenous Bases | A, G, C, **T (Thymine)** | A, G, C, **U (Uracil)** |
| Structure | **Double-stranded α-helix** (Watson & Crick, 1953) | **Single-stranded** (occasionally folds back on itself) |
| Base Pairing | **A = T** (2 H-bonds); **G ≡ C** (3 H-bonds) | No permanent double helix (3 types: **mRNA, rRNA, tRNA**) |
| Main Biological Function | **Heredity** (genetic blueprint, self-replication) | **Protein synthesis** |

*(Brief note on **Hormones:** Intercellular chemical messengers secreted by endocrine glands directly into blood — e.g., **Insulin** (peptide hormone lowering blood glucose), **Glucagon** (raises blood glucose), **Adrenaline** (amine hormone, fight-or-flight), **Thyroxine** (iodinated tyrosine derivative; deficiency causes goitre), **Estrogen/Testosterone** (steroid hormones).)*

## Solved Examples

### Example 1: Writing the Complementary DNA Strand
If one strand of a DNA molecule has the base sequence **5'–A–T–G–C–C–T–A–G–3'**, write the sequence of its complementary DNA strand.

**Solution:**
Using Watson–Crick complementary base-pairing rules (**A ↔ T** and **G ↔ C**):
- Given strand:          5'–A–T–G–C–C–T–A–G–3'
- Complementary strand: **3'–T–A–C–G–G–A–T–C–5'**

Jaanch: Every A pairs with T (4 pairs) and every G pairs with C (4 pairs); strands are antiparallel (5'→3' vs 3'→5') ✅

---

### Example 2: Why Cannot Vitamin C Be Stored in Our Body?
Explain why Vitamin C must be supplied regularly in our diet, whereas Vitamin A does not need daily replenishment.

**Solution:**
**Vitamin C (ascorbic acid)** is **water-soluble**, so excess Vitamin C is readily dissolved in water and excreted in **urine** — it cannot be stored in the body. **Vitamin A** is **fat-soluble** and is stored in the **liver and adipose (fat) tissues** for long-term use.

Jaanch: Water-soluble (B, C except B₁₂) = excreted; Fat-soluble (A, D, E, K) = stored ✅

---

### Example 3: Distinguishing Between Nucleoside and Nucleotide
What is the structural difference between a nucleoside and a nucleotide? Name the linkage that joins two nucleotides in DNA.

**Solution:**
- **Nucleoside** = Pentose sugar + Nitrogenous base (linked at C1').
- **Nucleotide** = Pentose sugar + Nitrogenous base (at C1') + **Phosphate group** (at C5').
- Two nucleotides are joined by a **phosphodiester linkage** between the **5' carbon** of one sugar and the **3' carbon** of the next sugar.

Jaanch: Nucleotide = Nucleoside + Phosphate; backbone = 3'–5' phosphodiester ✅

## Aam Galtiyan (Common Mistakes)

1. **Sabhi water-soluble vitamins ko non-storable bol dena:**
   - *Galat:* Koi bhi water-soluble vitamin body mein store nahi hota.
   - *Sahi:* **Vitamin B₁₂** water-soluble hote hue bhi **liver mein store** hota hai!

2. **DNA aur RNA ke unique pyrimidine bases ulta kar dena:**
   - *Galat:* DNA mein Uracil aur RNA mein Thymine hota hai.
   - *Sahi:* **DNA = Thymine (T)**; **RNA = Uracil (U)**! (A, G, C dono mein common hain.)

3. **A–T aur G–C ke Hydrogen bonds ka number mix kar dena:**
   - *Galat:* A–T mein 3 aur G–C mein 2 H-bonds hote hain.
   - *Sahi:* **A = T (2 H-bonds)** aur **G ≡ C (3 H-bonds)**!

## Quick Recap

- **Enzymes:** Specific globular protein biocatalysts (lower Eₐ).
- **Vitamins:** Fat-soluble (**A, D, E, K** — stored); Water-soluble (**B, C** — excreted, EXCEPT **B₁₂** stored!). Deficiencies: A (night blindness), B₁ (beri-beri), B₁₂ (pernicious anaemia), C (scurvy), D (rickets), K (slow clotting).
- **Nucleic Acids:** Nucleoside = Base + Sugar; Nucleotide = Base + Sugar + Phosphate; linked by **3'–5' phosphodiester bond**.
- **DNA vs RNA:** DNA = 2-deoxyribose, A=T & G≡C, double helix (heredity); RNA = ribose, A/U/G/C, single strand (mRNA, rRNA, tRNA — protein synthesis).

## Practice Questions

1. What are enzymes? How do they increase the rate of biochemical reactions?
2. Classify vitamins based on their solubility. Why can Vitamin B₁₂ be stored in our body despite being water-soluble?
3. Name the deficiency diseases caused by lack of: (a) Vitamin A, (b) Vitamin B₁, (c) Vitamin C, (d) Vitamin D, (e) Vitamin K.
4. Distinguish between a **nucleoside** and a **nucleotide**.
5. What are the hydrolysis products of: (a) DNA, (b) RNA?
6. State three major structural differences between DNA and RNA.
7. Name the two types of nitrogenous bases (purines and pyrimidines) and list which ones belong to each class.
8. Write the complementary DNA strand for the sequence: **5'–G–G–A–T–C–A–T–C–3'**. How many hydrogen bonds hold this 8-base-pair segment together?
9. What are the three types of RNA and what is the primary biological function of RNA?
10. Name the hormone responsible for regulating blood glucose level and the trace element whose deficiency causes goitre.

---

### Answers

1. **Enzymes** are highly specific globular proteins that act as biological catalysts in living organisms. They speed up reactions by providing an alternate pathway with a much **lower activation energy (Eₐ)**.
2. **Fat-soluble (A, D, E, K)** and **Water-soluble (B-complex, C)**. Although Vitamin B₁₂ is water-soluble, the human liver has specific binding proteins that store several years' supply of B₁₂!
3. (a) **Night blindness / Xerophthalmia**; (b) **Beri-beri**; (c) **Scurvy**; (d) **Rickets / Osteomalacia**; (e) **Increased blood clotting time**.
4. **Nucleoside** = Pentose sugar + Nitrogenous base. **Nucleotide** = Pentose sugar + Nitrogenous base + Phosphoric acid (phosphate group).
5. (a) **DNA:** β-D-2-deoxyribose + phosphoric acid + A, G, C, **T**.
   (b) **RNA:** β-D-ribose + phosphoric acid + A, G, C, **U**.
6. (i) Sugar: DNA has 2-deoxyribose, RNA has ribose. (ii) Base: DNA has Thymine (T), RNA has Uracil (U). (iii) Strands: DNA is double-stranded helix, RNA is single-stranded.
7. **Purines (2-ring):** Adenine (A), Guanine (G). **Pyrimidines (1-ring):** Cytosine (C), Thymine (T), Uracil (U).
8. Complementary strand: **3'–C–C–T–A–G–T–A–G–5'**.
   Count pairs: 4 A–T pairs (4 × 2 = 8 H-bonds) + 4 G–C pairs (4 × 3 = 12 H-bonds) = **20 hydrogen bonds**!
   Jaanch: 4(2) + 4(3) = 8 + 12 = 20 H-bonds ✅
9. Three types: **mRNA** (messenger RNA), **rRNA** (ribosomal RNA), **tRNA** (transfer RNA). Primary function: **Protein synthesis**.
10. **Insulin** (lowers blood glucose) and **Glucagon** (raises blood glucose); **Iodine** deficiency causes goitre (low thyroxine).
