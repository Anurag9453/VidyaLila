## Mixed Solved Examples

### Example 1: Chargaff's Base-Pairing Percentage Problem
In a double-stranded DNA sample, **Adenine (A)** makes up **22%** of the total nitrogenous bases. Calculate the percentages of **Thymine (T), Guanine (G), and Cytosine (C)**.

**Solution:**
In double-stranded DNA, A always pairs with T (%A = %T), and G always pairs with C (%G = %C):
- **%T = %A = 22%**.
- Remaining percentage for G + C = 100% − (22% + 22%) = 100% − 44% = **56%**.
- Since %G = %C → **%G = %C = 56% / 2 = 28%**.

Jaanch: 22% (A) + 22% (T) + 28% (G) + 28% (C) = 100%; A/T = 1 and G/C = 1 ✅

---

### Example 2: Distinguishing Sucrose vs Glucose, and Fibrous vs Globular Proteins
(a) How will you distinguish chemically between aqueous solutions of **glucose** and **sucrose**?
(b) Classify **keratin** and **insulin** as fibrous or globular proteins and state their water solubility.

**Solution:**
(a) Warm both with **Fehling's solution** (or Tollens' reagent): **Glucose** (reducing sugar) gives a **reddish-brown precipitate of Cu₂O** (or silver mirror), whereas **Sucrose** (non-reducing sugar, C1–C2 linkage) gives **NO reaction**.
(b) **Keratin** = **fibrous protein** (**insoluble** in water); **Insulin** = **globular protein** (**soluble** in water).

Jaanch: Reducing vs non-reducing test cleanly separates glucose and sucrose ✅

### Example 3: Cross-Topic Reasoning (Denaturation, Glycine & Vitamin B₁₂)
Give concise chemical reasons for each:
(a) Boiling an egg turns it solid, yet its nutritional amino-acid sequence is unchanged.
(b) Glycine does not rotate plane-polarised light, whereas all other 19 natural amino acids do.
(c) Vitamin B₁₂ deficiency leads to pernicious anaemia, yet unlike Vitamin C, B₁₂ is stored in the body.

**Solution:**
(a) Boiling causes **denaturation**, which disrupts H-bonds and unfolds **2° and 3° structures** (coagulating the protein), while covalent peptide bonds (**1° structure / amino-acid sequence**) remain completely intact.
(b) Glycine (H₂N–CH₂–COOH) has two identical H atoms on its α-carbon (**achiral**), so it is optically inactive.
(c) Vitamin B₁₂ (cyanocobalamin, a cobalt complex) is water-soluble yet is uniquely stored in the **liver** via specific binding proteins.

Jaanch: All three connect directly to structural chemistry of biomolecules ✅

## Aam Galtiyan (Mixed-Problem Traps)

1. **DNA mein A + G = 50% rule ko single-stranded RNA par laga dena:**
   - *Galat:* RNA mein bhi %A = %U aur %G = %C hota hai.
   - *Sahi:* **%A = %T aur %G = %C (Chargaff's rule)** sirf **double-stranded DNA** par lagta hai, single-stranded RNA par nahi!

2. **Glycosidic, Peptide aur Phosphodiester linkages ko aapas mein mix kar dena:**
   - *Galat:* Proteins mein glycosidic linkage hoti hai.
   - *Sahi:* **Carbohydrates = Glycosidic**, **Proteins = Peptide (–CONH–)**, **Nucleic Acids = Phosphodiester**!

---

## Quick Recap

- **Linkages trio:** Carbohydrates = **Glycosidic**; Proteins = **Peptide**; Nucleic Acids = **3'–5' Phosphodiester**.
- **Exceptions trio:** Non-reducing disaccharide = **Sucrose**; Optically inactive α-amino acid = **Glycine**; Storable water-soluble vitamin = **Vitamin B₁₂**.
- **DNA math:** %A = %T, %G = %C, %(A + G) = %(T + C) = 50%; A=T (2 H-bonds), G≡C (3 H-bonds).

## Practice Questions

1. Name the linkage present in: (a) Starch, (b) Proteins, (c) DNA.
2. In a double-stranded DNA molecule, **Guanine (G)** accounts for **32%** of the total bases. Calculate the percentages of C, A, and T.
3. Why is sucrose called a non-reducing sugar, whereas lactose is a reducing sugar?
4. Name the monomers obtained on complete hydrolysis of: (a) Maltose, (b) Cellulose, (c) Insulin, (d) RNA.
5. How many peptide bonds and how many water molecules are eliminated in forming a **decapeptide** (10 amino acids)?
6. What happens to the primary, secondary, and tertiary structures of a protein during denaturation?
7. Match each vitamin with its deficiency disease: Vitamin A, Vitamin B₁, Vitamin C, Vitamin D, Vitamin K.
8. Explain why the two strands of DNA are **complementary** rather than identical.
9. What is the difference between **amylose** and **cellulose** with respect to monomer unit and linkage?
10. Why does glucose pentaacetate NOT react with hydroxylamine (NH₂OH)? What does this prove?

---

### Answers

1. (a) **α-1,4 & α-1,6 Glycosidic linkage**; (b) **Peptide linkage (–CO–NH–)**; (c) **3'–5' Phosphodiester linkage**.
2. %C = %G = **32%**. Remaining A + T = 100% − 64% = 36% → **%A = %T = 18%**.
   Jaanch: 32 + 32 + 18 + 18 = 100% ✅
3. In sucrose, both anomeric carbons (C1 of glucose and C2 of fructose) are locked in the glycosidic bond (no free hemiacetal). In lactose (β-1,4 linkage), C1 of the glucose unit remains free to open and reduce Fehling's/Tollens' reagents.
4. (a) **α-D-Glucose** only; (b) **β-D-Glucose** only; (c) **α-Amino acids**; (d) **β-D-Ribose + phosphoric acid + A, G, C, U**.
5. For n = 10 amino acids: **n − 1 = 9 peptide bonds** formed, and **9 molecules of H₂O** eliminated!
   Jaanch: Each peptide bond releases 1 H₂O → 9 bonds = 9 H₂O ✅
6. **2° and 3° structures** are destroyed (H-bonds break, helices/globules unfold), whereas the **1° structure** (peptide bonds / amino-acid sequence) remains completely intact.
7. Vitamin A → **Night blindness / Xerophthalmia**; Vitamin B₁ → **Beri-beri**; Vitamin C → **Scurvy**; Vitamin D → **Rickets**; Vitamin K → **Delayed blood clotting**.
8. Because hydrogen bonding is strictly specific (**A pairs ONLY with T**, and **G pairs ONLY with C**), so the base sequence of one strand automatically dictates the opposite complementary sequence of the second strand (enabling faithful DNA replication!).
9. **Amylose** is a linear polymer of **α-D-glucose** (α-1,4 linkage); **Cellulose** is a linear polymer of **β-D-glucose** (β-1,4 linkage).
10. Because in cyclic glucose pentaacetate, the C1 anomeric –OH is acetylated and locked in the pyranose ring, so it cannot open to form a free –CHO group — proving the **cyclic hemiacetal structure of glucose**!
