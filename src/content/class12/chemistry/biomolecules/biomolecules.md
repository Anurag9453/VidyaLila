## Naye Shabd (Key Terms)

| Term | Hinglish Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Carbohydrates** | Optically active **polyhydroxy aldehydes** ya **polyhydroxy ketones**, ya aise compounds jo hydrolysis par aisi units dein |
| **Monosaccharides** | Sabse simple carbohydrates jo aur chhote units mein hydrolyse nahi ho sakte (jaise Glucose, Fructose, Ribose) |
| **Reducing Sugars** | Woh sugars jo **Fehling's solution aur Tollens' reagent** ko reduce kar sakein (inmein free –CHO ya >C=O hemiacetal group hota hai: sabhi monosaccharides + maltose + lactose!) |
| **Non-Reducing Sugar** | Woh sugar jismein dono anomeric carbons glycosidic bond mein bandh jate hain → Fehling's/Tollens' **NAHI** deti (e.g., **Sucrose**!) |
| **Anomers** | Cyclic sugars ka woh pair (α aur β) jo sirf **anomeric carbon (C1 in glucose, C2 in fructose)** par –OH ki position mein alag hote hain |
| **Glycosidic Linkage** | Do monosaccharide units ko oxygen atom ke through jodne wala ether-like oxide linkage (–C–O–C–) |
| **Invert Sugar** | Sucrose (dextrorotatory, +66.5°) ke hydrolysis se bana glucose (+52.5°) + fructose (−92.4°) ka equimolar mixture, jiska net rotation **laevorotatory (−)** ho jata hai! |

## 1. Classification of Carbohydrates

1. **Monosaccharides:** Hydrolyse nahi hote (~20 known; e.g., Glucose — aldohexose, Fructose — ketohexose, Ribose — aldopentose).
2. **Oligosaccharides:** Hydrolysis par 2 se 10 monosaccharide units dete hain.
   - **Disaccharides (2 units):**
     - **Sucrose** → α-D-Glucose + β-D-Fructose (**Non-reducing!**)
     - **Maltose** → α-D-Glucose + α-D-Glucose (Reducing)
     - **Lactose (Milk sugar)** → β-D-Galactose + β-D-Glucose (Reducing)
3. **Polysaccharides:** Hydrolysis par bahut saari monosaccharide units dete hain (non-sugars, not sweet): **Starch, Cellulose, Glycogen**.

## 2. Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆ — An Aldohexose)

### Preparation:
- **From Sucrose (Lab):** C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁ + H₂O —(H⁺)→ C₆H₁₂O₆ (Glucose) + C₆H₁₂O₆ (Fructose).
- **From Starch (Commercial):** (C₆H₁₀O₅)ₙ + nH₂O —(H⁺, 393 K, 2–3 atm)→ n C₆H₁₂O₆ (Glucose).

### 5 Classic Structural Proofs of Open-Chain Glucose:
1. **Prolonged heating with HI:** Gives **n-hexane** → proves all **6 carbons are in a straight chain**!
2. **With NH₂OH (forms oxime) & HCN (forms cyanohydrin):** Proves presence of a **carbonyl group (>C=O)**.
3. **Mild oxidation with Bromine water (Br₂/H₂O):** Gives **Gluconic acid** (6-carbon monocarboxylic acid) → proves carbonyl is an **aldehyde (–CHO)**!
4. **Acetylation with acetic anhydride ((CH₃CO)₂O):** Gives **Glucose pentaacetate** → proves presence of **5 –OH groups** on 5 different carbons!
5. **Strong oxidation with conc. HNO₃:** Both glucose and gluconic acid give **Saccharic acid** (dicarboxylic acid) → proves presence of **1 primary alcohol (–CH₂OH)** group!

### Cyclic (Pyranose) Structure of Glucose — Why Open Chain Is Not Enough!
Despite having –CHO, glucose:
- Does **NOT** give **Schiff's test** or react with **NaHSO₃**.
- **Glucose pentaacetate** does **NOT** react with **NH₂OH** (proving no free –CHO group!).
- Exists in two crystalline forms: **α-D-(+)-Glucose** (MP 419 K) and **β-D-(+)-Glucose** (MP 423 K)!

**Explanation:** C5 ka –OH group C1 ke –CHO par intramolecular attack karke **6-membered cyclic hemiacetal (Pyranose ring)** bana leta hai!
- **D- vs L-configuration:** Lowest chiral carbon (**C5**) par –OH **right side** mein hai → **D-glucose** (yeh (+) ya (−) optical rotation se alag hai!).
- **α- vs β-anomers (at C1):** Haworth ring mein C1 par –OH **neeche** (right in Fischer) = **α-D-Glucose**; C1 par –OH **upar** (left in Fischer) = **β-D-Glucose**!

---

## 3. Fructose (C₆H₁₂O₆ — A Ketohexose)

- Natural fructose is **D-(−)-Fructose** (laevorotatory!).
- Has a **ketone group at C2** and 2 primary –OH groups (C1, C6).
- Forms a **5-membered cyclic hemiketal (Furanose ring)** between C5–OH and C2=O → **α-D-(−)-Fructofuranose** and **β-D-(−)-Fructofuranose**.

## 4. Disaccharides & Polysaccharides (Linkages Table — 100% Exam Question!)

| Carbohydrate | Monomer Units | Glycosidic Linkage | Reducing? | Special Fact |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sucrose** | α-D-Glucose + β-D-Fructose | **C1 (α-Glc) – C2 (β-Fru)** | **NO (Non-reducing!)** | Both reducing carbons locked! Hydrolysis gives **invert sugar** |
| **Maltose** | α-D-Glucose + α-D-Glucose | **C1 – C4 (α-1,4)** | **YES** | Free C1 on second glucose |
| **Lactose** | β-D-Galactose + β-D-Glucose | **C1 – C4 (β-1,4)** | **YES** | Milk sugar; free C1 on glucose |
| **Starch** | α-D-Glucose polymer | **Amylose** (15–20%, water-soluble, linear **α-1,4**); **Amylopectin** (80–85%, insoluble, branched **α-1,4 + α-1,6**) | NO | Plant energy storage |
| **Cellulose** | β-D-Glucose polymer | Straight chain **β-1,4** | NO | Plant cell walls; humans cannot digest β-1,4! |
| **Glycogen** | α-D-Glucose polymer | Highly branched **α-1,4 + α-1,6** (like amylopectin, more branched!) | NO | **"Animal starch"** (stored in liver, muscles, brain) |

## Solved Examples

### Example 1: Explaining Why Sucrose Is a Non-Reducing Sugar
Why does sucrose fail to reduce Fehling's solution and Tollens' reagent, whereas maltose and lactose are reducing sugars?

**Solution:**
In **sucrose**, the glycosidic linkage connects the **anomeric C1 (aldehyde carbon) of α-D-glucose** directly to the **anomeric C2 (ketone carbon) of β-D-fructose**. Because BOTH reducing carbonyl carbons are tied up in the glycosidic bond, neither ring can open to form a free –CHO or >C=O group! In **maltose** and **lactose**, the linkage is C1–C4, leaving the C1 of the second glucose unit **free** to open and reduce Fehling's/Tollens' reagents.

Jaanch: C1–C2 linkage locks both anomeric carbons → non-reducing ✅

---

### Example 2: Explaining Inversion of Cane Sugar
Sucrose is dextrorotatory (+66.5°), yet its acid-hydrolysed mixture is laevorotatory. Explain why!

**Solution:**
Hydrolysis of sucrose produces an equimolar (1:1) mixture of **D-(+)-glucose** (specific rotation = **+52.5°**) and **D-(−)-fructose** (specific rotation = **−92.4°**).
Because the laevorotation of fructose (−92.4°) is larger in magnitude than the dextrorotation of glucose (+52.5°), the net optical rotation becomes **negative**: (+52.5 − 92.4)/2 = **−19.95° (laevorotatory)**! This sign flip (+ to −) is called **inversion of cane sugar**, and the product is **invert sugar**.

Jaanch: +52.5° + (−92.4°) = −39.9° < 0 (net laevorotatory!) ✅

---

### Example 3: What Happens When Glucose Reacts with HI, Br₂/H₂O, and HNO₃?
Write the products obtained when D-glucose is treated separately with: (a) HI (Δ), (b) Br₂ water, (c) conc. HNO₃.

**Solution:**
(a) **n-Hexane** (CH₃(CH₂)₄CH₃) — proves straight 6-carbon chain.
(b) **Gluconic acid** (HOOC–(CHOH)₄–CH₂OH) — mild oxidation of –CHO to –COOH.
(c) **Saccharic acid** (HOOC–(CHOH)₄–COOH) — strong oxidation of both –CHO and –CH₂OH.

Jaanch: Each reagent specifically tests a different structural feature of glucose ✅

## Aam Galtiyan (Common Mistakes)

1. **'D' in D-Glucose ko Dextrorotatory (+) samajh lena:**
   - *Galat:* D-fructose dextrorotatory hai kyunki aage 'D' likha hai.
   - *Sahi:* **D/L** sirf lowest chiral carbon (C5) par –OH ki **spatial configuration** batata hai (D-glyceraldehyde se compare karke); **(+)/(−)** optical rotation ki direction batata hai! **D-(−)-Fructose** D-config mein hote hue bhi **laevorotatory (−)** hai!

2. **Amylose aur Amylopectin ke solubility aur branching ulta kar dena:**
   - *Galat:* Amylose branched aur insoluble hai.
   - *Sahi:* **Amylose** = **linear (α-1,4), water-SOLUBLE (15–20%)**; **Amylopectin** = **branched (α-1,4 + α-1,6), water-INSOLUBLE (80–85%)**!

3. **Cellulose ko α-D-glucose ka polymer bol dena:**
   - *Galat:* Starch aur cellulose dono α-D-glucose se bane hain.
   - *Sahi:* **Starch & Glycogen** = **α-D-glucose**; **Cellulose** = **β-D-glucose (β-1,4 linkage)**!

## Quick Recap

- **Glucose proofs:** HI → n-hexane; NH₂OH/HCN → >C=O; Br₂/H₂O → gluconic acid (–CHO); Ac₂O → pentaacetate (5 –OH); conc. HNO₃ → saccharic acid (1° –OH).
- **Cyclic glucose:** 6-ring pyranose (α/β anomers at C1); fails Schiff's & NaHSO₃; pentaacetate fails NH₂OH.
- **Disaccharides:** Sucrose (α-Glc C1 – β-Fru C2, **non-reducing**, invert sugar); Maltose (α-1,4 Glc–Glc); Lactose (β-1,4 Gal–Glc).
- **Polysaccharides:** Starch (amylose linear α-1,4 + amylopectin branched α-1,4/α-1,6); Cellulose (β-1,4); Glycogen (animal starch, highly branched α-1,4/α-1,6).

### Answers

1. **Monosaccharides** cannot be hydrolysed further (e.g., glucose). **Oligosaccharides** yield 2–10 monosaccharide units on hydrolysis (e.g., sucrose). **Polysaccharides** yield a large number of monosaccharide units on hydrolysis (e.g., starch).
2. **Reducing sugars** reduce Fehling's and Tollens' reagents due to a free aldehydic/ketonic hemiacetal group (e.g., glucose, maltose). **Non-reducing sugars** have their reducing groups locked in a glycosidic bond (e.g., **sucrose**).
3. (a) HI (Δ) → **n-hexane** (proves 6-C straight chain).
   (b) Br₂/H₂O → **gluconic acid** (proves –CHO group).
   (c) conc. HNO₃ → **saccharic acid** (proves 1° –CH₂OH group).
4. (i) Glucose does not give Schiff's test or form a NaHSO₃ addition product despite having –CHO. (ii) Glucose pentaacetate does not react with NH₂OH (proving absence of free –CHO).
5. **Anomers** are cyclic stereoisomers differing only in the configuration of the –OH group at the **anomeric carbon (C1 in glucose)**. In α-D-glucose, C1–OH is below the Haworth ring (right in Fischer); in β-D-glucose, C1–OH is above the ring (left in Fischer).
6. **Invert sugar** is the equimolar mixture of D-(+)-glucose (+52.5°) and D-(−)-fructose (−92.4°) obtained by hydrolysing dextrorotatory sucrose (+66.5°). Because fructose's laevorotation (−92.4°) outweighs glucose's dextrorotation (+52.5°), the net rotation flips from (+) to (−).
7. (a) **Sucrose:** α-D-glucose + β-D-fructose (**C1–C2** linkage).
   (b) **Maltose:** Two α-D-glucose units (**α-1,4** linkage).
   (c) **Lactose:** β-D-galactose + β-D-glucose (**β-1,4** linkage).
8. **Amylose** is water-soluble (15–20% of starch) and has a **linear** chain of α-D-glucose (α-1,4). **Amylopectin** is water-insoluble (80–85% of starch) and has a **branched** chain (α-1,4 with α-1,6 branches).
9. **Starch** is a polymer of **α-D-glucose** (α-1,4 and α-1,6 linkages), whereas **cellulose** is a straight-chain polymer of **β-D-glucose** (β-1,4 linkages).
10. Because its structure is similar to amylopectin (highly branched α-D-glucose polymer) and it serves as the primary carbohydrate energy reserve in animals; stored in **liver, muscles, and brain**.
