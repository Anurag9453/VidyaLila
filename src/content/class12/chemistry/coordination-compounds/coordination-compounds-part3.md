# Bonding in Coordination Compounds: VBT and CFT

> **Pichle topic mein:** Humne [Isomerism in Coordination Compounds](coordination-compounds-part2.md) mein structural, geometrical aur optical isomers padhe. Ab sabse interesting sawal: [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻ **square planar aur diamagnetic** kyun hai, jabki [NiCl₄]²⁻ **tetrahedral aur paramagnetic** kyun hai? Iska jawab do powerful theories deti hain — **Valence Bond Theory (VBT)** aur **Crystal Field Theory (CFT)**!

---

Sunny apne school trip par mineral museum gaya tha. Guide ne do keemti patthar dikhaye: laal **Ruby** (Al₂O₃ mein thoda Cr³⁺) aur hara **Emerald** (beryl mineral mein wahi Cr³⁺!). Sunny ne poocha, "Dono mein wahi Cr³⁺ ion hai, phir ruby laal aur emerald hara kyun hai?" Guide ne bataya: "Cr³⁺ ke aas-paas ke ligands badal gaye — ligands ki taqat badalne se Cr³⁺ ke d-orbitals ki **splitting energy (Δₒ)** badal jati hai, aur isliye absorb hone wala rang badal jata hai!"

Aaj hum VBT aur CFT dono ko bilkul clean steps mein master karenge!

---

## Naye Shabd (Key Terms)

| Term | Hinglish Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Inner Orbital Complex (Low-Spin)** | Octahedral complex jo andar ke **(n−1)d** orbitals use kare (**d²sp³** hybridisation), aam taur par strong-field ligands ke saath |
| **Outer Orbital Complex (High-Spin)** | Octahedral complex jo bahar ke **nd** orbitals use kare (**sp³d²** hybridisation), aam taur par weak-field ligands ke saath |
| **Crystal Field Splitting (Δₒ / Δₜ)** | Ligands ke electrostatic repulsion ki wajah se 5 degenerate d-orbitals ka lower aur higher energy sets mein tootna |
| **t₂g and e_g Orbitals** | Octahedral field mein 3 lower-energy orbitals (**t₂g**: d_xy, d_yz, d_zx) aur 2 higher-energy orbitals (**e_g**: d_(x²−y²), d_z²) |
| **Spectrochemical Series** | Ligands ko unki crystal field splitting taqat (field strength) ke increasing order mein arrange karne wali experimental series |
| **Pairing Energy (P)** | Ek hi orbital mein do electrons ko pair karne (repulsion overcome karne) ke liye zaroori energy |

---

## 1. Valence Bond Theory (VBT) — Hybridisation & Magnetism

VBT ke mutabik, central metal ion apni coordination number ke barabar **empty hybrid orbitals** (s, p, d mix karke) banata hai, jo ligands ke lone pairs accept karke coordinate bonds banate hain.

| Coordination Number | Hybridisation | Geometry | Classic Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | **sp³** | Tetrahedral | [NiCl₄]²⁻, [Ni(CO)₄], [Zn(NH₃)₄]²⁺ |
| 4 | **dsp²** | Square Planar | [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻, [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] |
| 5 | **sp³d** | Trigonal Bipyramidal | [Fe(CO)₅] |
| 6 | **d²sp³** (Inner) | Octahedral | [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺, [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ |
| 6 | **sp³d²** (Outer) | Octahedral | [CoF₆]³⁻, [FeF₆]³⁻, [Fe(H₂O)₆]²⁺ |

### Four Classic VBT Comparisons (Exam Favourites!):
1. **[Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ vs [CoF₆]³⁻ (both have Co³⁺ = 3d⁶):**
   - **[Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺:** NH₃ strong-field ligand hai → 3d⁶ electrons pair ho jate hain → do 3d orbitals khali → **d²sp³** (inner orbital, **diamagnetic**, n = 0, μ = 0).
   - **[CoF₆]³⁻:** F⁻ weak-field ligand hai → pairing nahi hoti → outer 4d orbitals use hote hain → **sp³d²** (outer orbital, **paramagnetic**, n = 4, μ = 4.90 BM).
2. **[Ni(CN)₄]²⁻ vs [NiCl₄]²⁻ (both have Ni²⁺ = 3d⁸):**
   - **[Ni(CN)₄]²⁻:** CN⁻ strong-field ligand hai → 3d⁸ electrons pair ho jate hain → ek 3d orbital khali → **dsp²** (**square planar**, **diamagnetic**, n = 0).
   - **[NiCl₄]²⁻:** Cl⁻ weak-field ligand hai → pairing nahi hoti → **sp³** (**tetrahedral**, **paramagnetic**, n = 2, μ = 2.83 BM).
3. **[Ni(CO)₄] (Ni⁰ = 3d⁸ 4s²):** CO strong ligand hai → 4s² electrons 3d mein push hokar **3d¹⁰** bana lete hain → empty 4s + three 4p → **sp³** (**tetrahedral**, **diamagnetic**, n = 0)!

### Limitations of VBT:
- Yeh magnetic data ko quantitively explain nahi karta.
- Yeh coordination compounds ke **colours** aur spectral absorption ko bilkul explain nahi karta.
- Yeh strong-field vs weak-field ligands mein fark nahi batata aur square planar vs tetrahedral ka exact thermodynamic reason nahi deta.

## 2. Crystal Field Theory (CFT)

CFT metal-ligand bond ko **purely electrostatic** maanti hai (ligands = point charges ya dipoles). Free metal ion mein paanchon d-orbitals ki energy barabar (**degenerate**) hoti hai. Ligands paas aane par repulsion hota hai — jo orbitals ligands ke raaste mein aate hain unki energy badh jati hai!

### (a) Splitting in Octahedral Field (Δₒ)
Octahedron mein 6 ligands **x, y, z axes ke along** aate hain:
- **e_g orbitals** (d_(x²−y²) aur d_z²) axes ke along hain → zyada repulsion → energy **+0.6 Δₒ (+3/5 Δₒ)** upar jati hai!
- **t₂g orbitals** (d_xy, d_yz, d_zx) axes ke beech mein hain → kam repulsion → energy **−0.4 Δₒ (−2/5 Δₒ)** neeche jati hai!

```
Octahedral Crystal Field Splitting (Δₒ)

             ____  ____   e_g  (+0.6 Δₒ)
                 ^
                 │  Δₒ
  _ _ _ _ _      │        barycentre (average energy = 0)
  5 free d       v
           ____ ____ ____ t₂g  (-0.4 Δₒ)
```

### (b) Spectrochemical Series (Weak → Strong Field Ligands)
**I⁻ < Br⁻ < SCN⁻ < Cl⁻ < S²⁻ < F⁻ < OH⁻ < C₂O₄²⁻ < H₂O < NCS⁻ < EDTA⁴⁻ < NH₃ < en < CN⁻ < CO**
*(Trick: Halides weak; O-donors medium-weak; N-donors medium-strong; C-donors CN⁻, CO strongest!)*

### (c) Filling d⁴ to d⁷ Electrons: Δₒ vs Pairing Energy (P)
d¹, d², d³ mein electrons hamesha t₂g mein single bharte hain. **4th electron (d⁴)** ke paas 2 choices hain:
1. **Weak-field ligand (Δₒ < P):** Electron upar e_g mein jump kar jata hai → **High-Spin complex**!
   - Example (d⁴): **t₂g³ e_g¹** (4 unpaired e⁻).
2. **Strong-field ligand (Δₒ > P):** Electron neeche t₂g mein hi pair kar leta hai → **Low-Spin complex**!
   - Example (d⁴): **t₂g⁴ e_g⁰** (2 unpaired e⁻).

### (d) Splitting in Tetrahedral Field (Δₜ)
Tetrahedron mein 4 ligands axes ke **beech** se aate hain — isliye splitting **exact ULTI** ho jati hai!
- **t₂** (d_xy, d_yz, d_zx) upar (**+0.4 Δₜ**); **e** (d_(x²−y²), d_z²) neeche (**−0.6 Δₜ**). (No 'g' subscript kyunki tetrahedron mein centre of symmetry nahi hota!)
- **Relation:** **Δₜ = (4/9) Δₒ** (kyunki sirf 4 ligands hain aur koi bhi orbital directly ligands ki taraf point nahi karta).
- Kyunki Δₜ bahut chhota hota hai (hamesha Δₜ < P), **tetrahedral complexes lagbhag hamesha HIGH-SPIN** hote hain (low-spin tetrahedral rare hain)!

## 3. Colour in Coordination Compounds (CFT Explanation)

Example: **[Ti(H₂O)₆]³⁺** (Ti³⁺ = 3d¹, configuration **t₂g¹ e_g⁰**):
- Jab white light solution par padti hai, yeh **blue-green light** (~500 nm) absorb karke ek electron ko t₂g se e_g mein excite karta hai (**t₂g¹ e_g⁰ → t₂g⁰ e_g¹**).
- Bachi hui transmitted light ka complementary colour **purple (violet)** dikhta hai!
- Agar **[Ti(H₂O)₆]Cl₃** ko heat karke saara H₂O nikal diya jaye (anhydrous TiCl₃), toh ligand hatne se splitting khatam ho jati hai aur compound **colourless/white** ho jata hai!
- Isi tarah anhydrous CuSO₄ **white** hai, par hydrated CuSO₄·5H₂O (jismein [Cu(H₂O)₄]²⁺ hai) **blue** hota hai!

### Limitation of CFT:
CFT ligands ko sirf point charges maanta hai, isliye anionic ligands (jaise OH⁻, F⁻) ko neutral ligands (jaise CO, NH₃) se stronger predict karta — jabki reality mein **CO aur NH₃ strong-field** hain (covalent π-bonding ko CFT ignore karta hai).

## Solved Examples

### Example 1: Predicting Hybridisation, Geometry & Magnetism (VBT)
Using VBT, predict the hybridisation, geometry, and spin-only magnetic moment of:
(a) [Fe(CN)₆]³⁻   (b) [Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺   (Fe: Z = 26)

**Solution:**
In both complexes, Fe is in +3 state: Fe³⁺ = [Ar] 3d⁵.
(a) **[Fe(CN)₆]³⁻:** CN⁻ is a strong-field ligand → forces pairing in 3d → two pairs + 1 unpaired e⁻ (leaving 2 empty 3d orbitals).
Hybridisation: **d²sp³** (inner orbital, low-spin, **octahedral**).
Unpaired electrons n = 1 → μ = √[1(3)] = **1.73 BM** (weakly paramagnetic).

(b) **[Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺:** H₂O is a weak-field ligand → no pairing → all 5 electrons remain unpaired in 3d; outer 4d orbitals used.
Hybridisation: **sp³d²** (outer orbital, high-spin, **octahedral**).
Unpaired electrons n = 5 → μ = √[5(7)] = **5.92 BM** (strongly paramagnetic).

Jaanch: Both have Fe³⁺ (3d⁵); CN⁻ gives n = 1 (1.73 BM), H₂O gives n = 5 (5.92 BM) ✅

---

### Example 2: Writing CFT Electronic Configuration
Write the CFT electronic configuration of a **d⁵** metal ion in an octahedral complex when:
(a) Δₒ < P (weak-field ligand)
(b) Δₒ > P (strong-field ligand)

**Solution:**
(a) When Δₒ < P (high-spin): Electrons fill singly across all 5 orbitals before pairing → **t₂g³ e_g²** (5 unpaired electrons).
(b) When Δₒ > P (low-spin): Electrons pair in lower t₂g before entering e_g → **t₂g⁵ e_g⁰** (1 unpaired electron).

Jaanch: Total d-electrons = 3 + 2 = 5 in (a), and 5 + 0 = 5 in (b) ✅

---

### Example 3: Why [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ and [Fe(H₂O)₆]²⁺ Have Different Colours
Both [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ and [Fe(H₂O)₆]²⁺ contain Fe²⁺ (3d⁶), yet they show different colours in dilute solutions. Why?

**Solution:**
According to the spectrochemical series, **CN⁻ is a much stronger field ligand than H₂O**, so the crystal field splitting energy **Δₒ** is much larger in [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ than in [Fe(H₂O)₆]²⁺. Since absorbed light energy ΔE = hc/λ = Δₒ differs, the two complexes absorb different wavelengths of visible light and therefore transmit different complementary colours.

Jaanch: Larger Δₒ → shorter absorbed wavelength λ → different complementary colour ✅

## Aam Galtiyan (Common Mistakes)

1. **[Ni(CO)₄] ko square planar (dsp²) bol dena:**
   - *Galat:* CO strong ligand hai toh [Ni(CO)₄] bhi [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻ ki tarah square planar hoga.
   - *Sahi:* [Ni(CO)₄] mein **Ni⁰ (3d⁸ 4s²)** hai! 4s ke 2 electrons 3d mein jakar **3d¹⁰** full kar dete hain — koi 3d orbital khali nahi bachta! Isliye hybridisation **sp³ (tetrahedral, diamagnetic)** hoti hai!

2. **Tetrahedral splitting mein t₂g aur e_g likh dena:**
   - *Galat:* Tetrahedral complex mein t₂g upar aur e_g neeche hota hai.
   - *Sahi:* Tetrahedron mein centre of symmetry nahi hota, isliye **'g' subscript nahi lagate** — sirf **t₂ (upar)** aur **e (neeche)** likhte hain!

3. **Δₜ = (9/4) Δₒ likh dena:**
   - *Galat:* Δₜ, Δₒ se bada hota hai.
   - *Sahi:* **Δₜ = (4/9) Δₒ** — tetrahedral splitting octahedral splitting ke aadhe se bhi kam hoti hai!

## Quick Recap

- **VBT:** Inner orbital = d²sp³ (strong ligand, low spin); Outer orbital = sp³d² (weak ligand, high spin). CN = 4: [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻ = dsp² (square planar, μ = 0); [NiCl₄]²⁻ = sp³ (tetrahedral, μ = 2.83 BM); [Ni(CO)₄] = sp³ (tetrahedral, μ = 0).
- **CFT Octahedral:** t₂g (−0.4 Δₒ) lower, e_g (+0.6 Δₒ) higher. Δₒ > P → low-spin; Δₒ < P → high-spin.
- **CFT Tetrahedral:** e (−0.6 Δₜ) lower, t₂ (+0.4 Δₜ) higher; **Δₜ = (4/9) Δₒ** (always high-spin).
- **Colour:** d–d transition across Δₒ; anhydrous salts lose ligands → lose splitting → become colourless.

## Practice Questions

1. Using VBT, explain why [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻ is square planar and diamagnetic whereas [NiCl₄]²⁻ is tetrahedral and paramagnetic.
2. Predict the hybridisation, geometry, and number of unpaired electrons in [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ and [CoF₆]³⁻.
3. Explain why [Ni(CO)₄] is tetrahedral and diamagnetic.
4. Draw the crystal field splitting diagram for an octahedral complex and label t₂g, e_g, and Δₒ.
5. Write the CFT electronic configuration for a d⁶ ion in an octahedral field when: (a) Δₒ > P, (b) Δₒ < P.
6. What is the spectrochemical series? Arrange Cl⁻, H₂O, CN⁻, NH₃, and F⁻ in increasing order of crystal field strength.
7. State the mathematical relationship between Δₜ and Δₒ. Why are low-spin tetrahedral complexes rarely formed?
8. Explain why [Ti(H₂O)₆]³⁺ is purple in aqueous solution, whereas anhydrous TiCl₃ (or [Sc(H₂O)₆]³⁺) is colourless.
9. State two major limitations of Valence Bond Theory (VBT) and one limitation of Crystal Field Theory (CFT).
10. [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ is diamagnetic, while [Fe(H₂O)₆]²⁺ is strongly paramagnetic with μ = 4.90 BM. Explain using CFT.

---

### Answers

1. In both, Ni²⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁸. Strong-field **CN⁻** pairs the two unpaired 3d electrons, freeing one 3d orbital for **dsp²** hybridisation (**square planar**, n = 0, diamagnetic). Weak-field **Cl⁻** does not pair them, so Ni²⁺ uses 4s and 4p for **sp³** hybridisation (**tetrahedral**, n = 2, paramagnetic, μ = 2.83 BM).
2. Co³⁺ = 3d⁶.
   - **[Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺:** NH₃ strong-field → pairing → **d²sp³**, octahedral, **0** unpaired e⁻ (diamagnetic).
   - **[CoF₆]³⁻:** F⁻ weak-field → no pairing → **sp³d²**, octahedral, **4** unpaired e⁻ (paramagnetic, μ = 4.90 BM).
3. In [Ni(CO)₄], Ni is in 0 oxidation state (3d⁸ 4s²). Strong-field CO pushes the two 4s electrons into 3d to give **3d¹⁰** (0 unpaired e⁻, diamagnetic). The empty 4s and three 4p orbitals hybridise to **sp³ (tetrahedral)**.
4. In an octahedral field, the 5 degenerate d-orbitals split into 3 lower-energy **t₂g** orbitals (d_xy, d_yz, d_zx at −0.4 Δₒ) and 2 higher-energy **e_g** orbitals (d_(x²−y²), d_z² at +0.6 Δₒ), separated by energy gap **Δₒ**.
5. (a) Δₒ > P (strong field, low spin): **t₂g⁶ e_g⁰** (0 unpaired e⁻).
   (b) Δₒ < P (weak field, high spin): **t₂g⁴ e_g²** (4 unpaired e⁻).
   Jaanch: 6 + 0 = 6 and 4 + 2 = 6 ✅
6. The spectrochemical series arranges ligands in increasing order of their ability to split d-orbitals (Δₒ). Order: **Cl⁻ < F⁻ < H₂O < NH₃ < CN⁻**.
7. **Δₜ = (4/9) Δₒ**. Because Δₜ is much smaller than the pairing energy P (Δₜ < P), pairing is energetically unfavourable, so tetrahedral complexes are almost always high-spin.
8. [Ti(H₂O)₆]³⁺ has Ti³⁺ (3d¹, t₂g¹ e_g⁰); it absorbs blue-green light for the t₂g → e_g transition and transmits purple light. Without ligands (anhydrous) there is no d-orbital splitting, and in [Sc(H₂O)₆]³⁺ (3d⁰) there are no d-electrons for transition — so both are colourless.
9. **VBT limitations:** Cannot explain colours/spectra of complexes; cannot distinguish weak vs strong ligands quantitatively. **CFT limitation:** Treats ligands purely as point charges, ignoring covalent (π) character (cannot explain why neutral CO/NH₃ are stronger than anionic F⁻/OH⁻).
10. Both contain Fe²⁺ (3d⁶). With strong-field CN⁻ (Δₒ > P), configuration is **t₂g⁶ e_g⁰** (0 unpaired e⁻ → diamagnetic). With weak-field H₂O (Δₒ < P), configuration is **t₂g⁴ e_g²** (4 unpaired e⁻ → μ = √[4(6)] = √24 = **4.90 BM**).
    Jaanch: √24 = 4.899 ≈ 4.90 BM ✅
