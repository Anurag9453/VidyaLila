## Naye Shabd (Key Terms)

| Term | Hinglish Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Metal Carbonyls** | Homoleptic coordination compounds jinmein sirf carbon monoxide (CO) ligands zero-valent transition metals se jude hote hain (jaise [Ni(CO)₄], [Fe(CO)₅]) |

## 1. Bonding in Metal Carbonyls (Synergic Bonding)

> **Safety Note:** Metal carbonyls (jaise Ni(CO)₄) aur carbon monoxide (CO) behad zehreele (highly toxic) hote hain; inka study sirf industrial processes aur theory mein hota hai.

### Common Metal Carbonyl Structures:
- **[Ni(CO)₄]:** Tetrahedral (Ni = 0, sp³)
- **[Fe(CO)₅]:** Trigonal bipyramidal (Fe = 0, dsp³ / sp³d)
- **[Cr(CO)₆]:** Octahedral (Cr = 0, d²sp³)
- **[Mn₂(CO)₁₀]:** Do square-pyramidal Mn(CO)₅ units ek **Mn–Mn single bond** se judi hui (no bridging CO!)
- **[Co₂(CO)₈]:** Ek **Co–Co bond** + **2 bridging CO** groups + 6 terminal CO groups!

### How Synergic Bonding Works (σ + π Back-Bonding):
Metal–Carbon (M–C) bond mein **double-bond character** hota hai, jo do steps mein banta hai:
1. **M ← C σ-bond:** CO ke carbon par maujood lone pair metal ke vacant hybrid orbital mein donate hota hai.
2. **M → C π-bond (Back-bonding):** Metal ke **filled d-orbital** se ek electron pair CO ke **vacant antibonding π* orbital** mein wapas donate hota hai!

```
Synergic Bonding in M—C≡O

        σ-donation (lone pair)
      <------------------------
    M                           C ≡ O
      ------------------------>
      π back-donation (filled d → vacant π* of CO)
```

**Nateeja (Result):**
- Dono bonds ek-doosre ko mazboot banate hain (**synergic effect**)!
- **M–C bond mazboot aur chhota** ho jata hai (bond order > 1).
- **C–O bond thoda kamzor aur lamba** ho jata hai (kyunki electrons CO ke *antibonding* π* orbital mein gaye!).

## 2. Stability of Coordination Compounds & Chelate Effect

Solution mein metal ion M aur n ligands L ke milne ka overall equilibrium:
M + nL ⇌ MLₙ

**Overall Stability Constant:** **βₙ = [MLₙ] / ([M][L]ⁿ) = K₁ × K₂ × K₃ × ... × Kₙ**
- Jahan K₁, K₂, ..., Kₙ **stepwise stability constants** hain.
- **Instability (dissociation) constant = 1 / βₙ.**
- Jitni badi **log βₙ** value, utna zyada thermodynamically stable complex!

### Factors Increasing Complex Stability:
1. **Higher charge and smaller size of central metal ion** (high charge/radius ratio → stronger attraction for ligands; e.g., Fe³⁺ complexes are more stable than Fe²⁺ complexes).
2. **Stronger Lewis base ligand** (jaise CN⁻, NH₃, en).
3. **Chelate Effect:** Bidentate/polydentate ligands (en, ox, EDTA⁴⁻) 5- ya 6-membered rings banate hain jo monodentate complexes (jaise NH₃, H₂O) se kahin zyada stable hote hain (kyunki ring banne par solution mein zyada free solvent molecules release hote hain → **entropy ΔS badhti hai**!).

## 3. Importance and Applications of Coordination Compounds

1. **Biological Systems:**
   - **Chlorophyll** (photosynthesis pigment): **Magnesium (Mg²⁺)** complex.
   - **Haemoglobin** (oxygen carrier in blood): **Iron (Fe²⁺)** complex.
   - **Vitamin B₁₂** (cyanocobalamin, anti-pernicious anaemia factor): **Cobalt (Co)** complex.
   - **Carboxypeptidase A & Carbonic anhydrase** (enzymes): **Zinc (Zn²⁺)** complexes.
2. **Medicinal Chemistry (Chelation Therapy & Anti-Cancer):**
   - **Cisplatin** (cis-[Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂]): Effectively inhibits tumour growth in cancer chemotherapy.
   - **Ca-EDTA (Calcium disodium EDTA):** **Lead (Pb²⁺) poisoning** ka ilaj (Pb²⁺ EDTA ke saath soluble complex banakar urine se bahar nikal jata hai!).
   - **D-Penicillamine & Desferrioxamine B:** Body mein excess **Copper (Cu)** aur **Iron (Fe)** hatane ke liye (chelation therapy).
3. **Analytical Chemistry & Water Hardness:**
   - **EDTA titration:** Hard water mein **Ca²⁺ aur Mg²⁺** ki concentration napne ke liye (Ca²⁺ aur Mg²⁺ EDTA⁴⁻ ke saath stable 1:1 complexes banate hain).
   - **Qualitative analysis:** **Dimethylglyoxime (DMG)** se Ni²⁺ ka rosy-red precipitate; **α-nitroso-β-naphthol** se Co²⁺; **cupron** se Cu²⁺.
4. **Metallurgy (Extraction of Noble Metals — Industrial Process):**
   - **Gold (Au) aur Silver (Ag)** ko ore se dilute NaCN/KCN + air se soluble complex **[Au(CN)₂]⁻** aur **[Ag(CN)₂]⁻** banakar extract kiya jata hai, phir Zn dust se precipitate karte hain!
   - **Mond Process** (Ni purification): Impure Ni + 4CO (330–350 K) → volatile [Ni(CO)₄], jise 450–470 K par decompose karke pure Ni milta hai.
5. **Black-and-White Photography:**
   - Film par bacha hua unexposed **AgBr** **hypo solution (Na₂S₂O₃)** mein soluble complex **[Ag(S₂O₃)₂]³⁻** banakar dhul jata hai!
   - AgBr + 2Na₂S₂O₃ → Na₃[Ag(S₂O₃)₂] + NaBr.
6. **Industrial Catalysis:**
   - **Wilkinson's catalyst** [(Ph₃P)₃RhCl]: Hydrogenation of alkenes.
   - **Ziegler–Natta catalyst** (TiCl₄ + Al(C₂H₅)₃): Polymerisation of ethene to high-density polythene.
7. **Electroplating:**
   - Smooth, uniform silver/gold plating ke liye simple AgNO₃ ki jagah **[Ag(CN)₂]⁻** aur **[Au(CN)₂]⁻** baths use hote hain (slow, controlled release of metal ions).

## Solved Examples

### Example 1: Calculating Overall Stability Constant β₄
For the formation of [Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺ in water, the four stepwise stability constants are:
log K₁ = 4.0, log K₂ = 3.2, log K₃ = 2.7, log K₄ = 2.0.
Calculate log β₄ and β₄.

**Solution:**
Since β₄ = K₁ × K₂ × K₃ × K₄:
log β₄ = log K₁ + log K₂ + log K₃ + log K₄
= 4.0 + 3.2 + 2.7 + 2.0 = **11.9**.
Therefore, **β₄ = 10¹¹·⁹ = 7.94 × 10¹¹**.

Jaanch: 4.0 + 3.2 = 7.2; 7.2 + 2.7 = 9.9; 9.9 + 2.0 = 11.9 → very large β₄ means [Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺ is very stable ✅

---

### Example 2: Explaining Chelate Stability
Why is [Ni(en)₃]²⁺ (log β = 18.6) far more stable than [Ni(NH₃)₆]²⁺ (log β = 8.6), even though both have six Ni–N bonds?

**Solution:**
In [Ni(en)₃]²⁺, each bidentate ethane-1,2-diamine (en) ligand forms a stable **5-membered chelate ring** with Ni²⁺ (3 chelate rings total). When 3 en molecules displace 6 H₂O molecules from [Ni(H₂O)₆]²⁺, the total number of free particles in solution increases from 4 to 7, producing a large **positive entropy change (ΔS > 0)** and more negative ΔG° = ΔH° − TΔS° (**chelate effect**).

Jaanch: [Ni(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 3en → [Ni(en)₃]²⁺ + 6H₂O: 4 reactant particles → 7 product particles (ΔS > 0) ✅

---

### Example 3: Matching Real-Life Applications to Complexes
Match each application with the corresponding metal/complex:
(a) Removal of excess lead from the human body
(b) Fixing of photographic film
(c) Hydrogenation of alkenes

**Solution:**
(a) **Ca-EDTA** (forms water-soluble [Pb(EDTA)]²⁻ excreted in urine)
(b) **Sodium thiosulphate (Na₂S₂O₃)** forming soluble **[Ag(S₂O₃)₂]³⁻**
(c) **Wilkinson's catalyst**, **[(Ph₃P)₃RhCl]**

Jaanch: Each application matches its exact industrial/medical complex ✅

## Aam Galtiyan (Common Mistakes)

1. **Synergic back-bonding se C–O bond ko mazboot bata dena:**
   - *Galat:* Back-bonding se M–C aur C–O dono bonds mazboot ho jate hain.
   - *Sahi:* Back-bonding CO ke **antibonding π*** orbital mein electrons bhejti hai — isliye **M–C bond mazboot** hota hai, par **C–O bond kamzor (lamba)** ho jata hai!

2. **Chlorophyll aur Haemoglobin ke metals mix kar dena:**
   - *Galat:* Chlorophyll mein Iron hota hai.
   - *Sahi:* **Chlorophyll = Mg²⁺**, **Haemoglobin = Fe²⁺**, **Vitamin B₁₂ = Co**!

3. **Stepwise K values ko add karke β nikal lena:**
   - *Galat:* β₄ = K₁ + K₂ + K₃ + K₄.
   - *Sahi:* **β₄ = K₁ × K₂ × K₃ × K₄** (multiply hote hain!); **log β₄** mein log K values **add** hoti hain!

## Quick Recap

- **Metal Carbonyls:** Homoleptic CO complexes ([Ni(CO)₄] tetrahedral, [Fe(CO)₅] TBP, [Cr(CO)₆] octahedral, [Mn₂(CO)₁₀] has Mn–Mn bond, [Co₂(CO)₈] has Co–Co + 2 bridging CO).
- **Synergic bonding:** C → M σ-bond + M → C π* back-bond (strengthens M–C, weakens C–O).
- **Stability:** βₙ = K₁ × K₂ × ... × Kₙ; **Chelate effect** gives extra stability via positive ΔS.
- **Applications:** Chlorophyll (Mg), Haemoglobin (Fe), Vitamin B₁₂ (Co), Cisplatin (cancer), EDTA (hard water Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ & Pb poisoning), [Ag(S₂O₃)₂]³⁻ (photography), [(Ph₃P)₃RhCl] (Wilkinson's catalyst).

---

### Answers

1. In metal carbonyls, CO donates a lone pair from carbon to a vacant metal orbital (M ← C **σ-bond**), and the metal donates a pair of electrons from a filled d-orbital back into the vacant **π* antibonding orbital** of CO (M → C **π-bond**). Both mutually reinforce each other (**synergic bonding**).
2. Back-bonding increases M–C bond order (making **M–C shorter and stronger**) while populating CO's antibonding π* orbital (making **C–O longer and weaker**).
3. **[Fe(CO)₅]:** Trigonal bipyramidal; **[Cr(CO)₆]:** Octahedral; **[Mn₂(CO)₁₀]:** Two square-pyramidal Mn(CO)₅ units linked by a direct Mn–Mn single bond (no bridging CO).
4. Overall stability constant β₄ is the equilibrium constant for M + 4L ⇌ ML₄. Relation: **β₄ = K₁ × K₂ × K₃ × K₄** (and log β₄ = log K₁ + log K₂ + log K₃ + log K₄).
5. The extra thermodynamic stability of complexes formed by polydentate (chelate) ligands compared to analogous monodentate ligands (largely driven by entropy increase). Example: **[Ni(en)₃]²⁺** is much more stable than **[Ni(NH₃)₆]²⁺**.
6. (a) **Mg²⁺ (Magnesium)**, (b) **Fe²⁺ (Iron)**, (c) **Co (Cobalt)**.
7. (a) **Water hardness:** Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ ions are titrated against Na₂EDTA because they form stable 1:1 complexes with EDTA⁴⁻. (b) **Lead poisoning:** Ca-EDTA exchanges Ca²⁺ for Pb²⁺ in the body, forming soluble [Pb(EDTA)]²⁻ which is safely excreted in urine.
8. **AgBr + 2Na₂S₂O₃ → Na₃[Ag(S₂O₃)₂] + NaBr** (soluble complex washes away unexposed AgBr).
9. Wilkinson's catalyst is **[(Ph₃P)₃RhCl]** (chloridotris(triphenylphosphine)rhodium(I)); used for the **selective catalytic hydrogenation of alkenes**.
10. Instability constant = 1 / βₙ. When βₙ is very large, 1/βₙ is tiny.
    Instability constant = 1 / (2.0 × 10¹²) = **5.0 × 10⁻¹³**.
    Jaanch: (2.0 × 10¹²) × (5.0 × 10⁻¹³) = 10 × 10⁻¹ = 1 ✅
