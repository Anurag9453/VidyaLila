## Naye Shabd (Revision Snapshot)

Is wrap-up topic mein hum saare tools ko ek saath lagayenge:
- **Werner:** AgCl moles = ionisable Cl⁻ outside [ ... ].
- **IUPAC:** Alphabetical ligands → metal (−ate if anionic) → Roman numeral OS.
- **Isomerism:** Structural (4 types) + Geometrical (cis/trans, fac/mer; never tetrahedral) + Optical (chiral: [M(AA)₃], cis-[M(AA)₂b₂]).
- **VBT & CFT:** Strong ligand (CN⁻, CO, en, NH₃) → Δₒ > P → low-spin/inner orbital; Weak ligand (F⁻, Cl⁻, H₂O) → Δₒ < P → high-spin/outer orbital; Δₜ = (4/9) Δₒ.

## Mixed Solved Examples

### Example 1: Complete Analysis of a Mystery Complex
A coordination compound has formula CrCl₃·5H₂O·(something) — specifically, a green hydrated chloride of chromium has empirical formula CrCl₃·6H₂O. When 1 mole is treated with excess AgNO₃, exactly 1 mole of AgCl precipitates.
(a) Write its structural formula and IUPAC name.
(b) State its coordination number and oxidation state of Cr.
(c) Predict the number of geometrical isomers it can form.
(d) Calculate its spin-only magnetic moment. (Cr: Z = 24)

**Solution:**
(a) 1 mole AgCl → 1 Cl⁻ outside [ ... ]; 2 Cl⁻ and 4 H₂O inside [ ... ] (for CN = 6), with 2 lattice H₂O outside.
Formula: **[Cr(H₂O)₄Cl₂]Cl·2H₂O**.
IUPAC name: **tetraaquadichloridochromium(III) chloride dihydrate**.
(b) Oxidation state of Cr: x + 4(0) + 2(−1) = +1 → **+3**; **CN = 6**.
(c) [Cr(H₂O)₄Cl₂]⁺ is of type Ma₄b₂ (octahedral) → **2 geometrical isomers** (cis and trans).
(d) Cr³⁺ = [Ar] 3d³ → t₂g³ e_g⁰ → **3 unpaired electrons** (independent of ligand strength for d³!).
μ = √[3(3 + 2)] = √15 = **3.87 BM**.

Jaanch: Charge balance: [+3 − 2] = +1 balanced by 1 Cl⁻; 4 + 2 = 6 H₂O total; 2 + 1 = 3 Cl total; μ = √15 = 3.87 BM ✅

### Example 2: Comparing Three Nickel Complexes Together
Compare **[Ni(CN)₄]²⁻**, **[NiCl₄]²⁻**, and **[Ni(CO)₄]** with respect to:
(a) oxidation state of Ni, (b) hybridisation and geometry, (c) magnetic moment (BM).

**Solution:**
| Complex | Ni OS | Config (in complex) | Hybridisation & Shape | Unpaired e⁻ | μ (BM) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻ | +2 | 3d⁸ (paired) | **dsp²** (Square planar) | 0 | 0.00 (Diamagnetic) |
| [NiCl₄]²⁻ | +2 | 3d⁸ (unpaired) | **sp³** (Tetrahedral) | 2 | 2.83 (Paramagnetic) |
| [Ni(CO)₄] | 0 | 3d¹⁰ (4s pushed to 3d) | **sp³** (Tetrahedral) | 0 | 0.00 (Diamagnetic) |

Jaanch: Ni (Z=28) = [Ar] 3d⁸4s² → Ni²⁺ = 3d⁸, Ni⁰ = 3d¹⁰ in CO field; √[2(4)] = 2.83 BM ✅

### Example 3: Joining IUPAC, CFT & Stereoisomerism
For the complex **tris(ethane-1,2-diamine)cobalt(III) chloride**:
(a) Write its chemical formula.
(b) Write its CFT electronic configuration (en is a strong-field ligand).
(c) Predict its magnetic nature and type of stereoisomerism shown.

**Solution:**
(a) Formula: **[Co(en)₃]Cl₃** (Co = +3, en = 0, balanced by 3 Cl⁻).
(b) Co³⁺ = 3d⁶; strong-field en (Δₒ > P) → low-spin **t₂g⁶ e_g⁰**.
(c) 0 unpaired electrons → **diamagnetic** (μ = 0). Being of type [M(AA)₃], it shows **optical isomerism** (d and l enantiomers; 2 stereoisomers, no geometrical isomerism).

Jaanch: CN = 3 × 2 = 6 (octahedral); 6 electrons paired in t₂g → diamagnetic ✅

## Aam Galtiyan (Mixed-Problem Traps)

1. **d³ ion (jaise Cr³⁺) ke liye strong vs weak ligand mein magnetic moment badal dena:**
   - *Galat:* [Cr(CN)₆]³⁻ low-spin hai toh n = 1 hoga.
   - *Sahi:* **d¹, d², d³, d⁸, d⁹, d¹⁰** ke liye octahedral field mein **strong aur weak dono ligands same number of unpaired electrons** dete hain! Sirf **d⁴, d⁵, d⁶, d⁷** mein high-spin vs low-spin fark padta hai!

2. **Formula se IUPAC likhte waqt hydrate water ko andar rakh dena:**
   - *Galat:* [Cr(H₂O)₅Cl]Cl₂·H₂O ko hexaaquachloridochromium(III) chloride likhna.
   - *Sahi:* Sirf square bracket ke andar wale 5 H₂O ligands hain (**pentaaqua**); bahar wala ·H₂O end mein **monohydrate** likha jata hai!

---

## Quick Recap

- **High-spin vs low-spin matters ONLY for d⁴, d⁵, d⁶, d⁷** in octahedral complexes.
- **Nickel trio:** [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻ (dsp², sq planar, μ=0), [NiCl₄]²⁻ (sp³, tetrahedral, μ=2.83), [Ni(CO)₄] (sp³, tetrahedral, μ=0).
- **Iron pair:** [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ (d²sp³, μ=0) vs [Fe(H₂O)₆]²⁺ (sp³d², μ=4.90); [Fe(CN)₆]³⁻ (d²sp³, μ=1.73) vs [Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺ (sp³d², μ=5.92).

## Practice Questions

1. Write the IUPAC name, oxidation state, and coordination number of K₃[Fe(CN)₆].
2. Predict the hybridisation, geometry, and spin-only magnetic moment of [MnBr₄]²⁻ (Mn: Z = 25; Br⁻ is weak-field).
3. Which of the following complexes shows BOTH geometrical and optical isomerism: [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺, [Co(NH₃)₄Cl₂]⁺, [Co(en)₂Cl₂]⁺, [Co(en)₃]³⁺?
4. Why is [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ an inner-orbital complex whereas [Ni(NH₃)₆]²⁺ is an outer-orbital complex?
5. Give the chemical formula and IUPAC name of the linkage isomer of [Co(NH₃)₅(SCN)]Cl₂.
6. Arrange [CoF₆]³⁻, [Co(H₂O)₆]³⁺, and [Co(CN)₆]³⁻ in increasing order of crystal field splitting energy Δₒ.
7. Explain why [Cr(NH₃)₆]³⁺ is paramagnetic while [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻ is diamagnetic.
8. A complex Co(NH₃)₅BrSO₄ exists in two isomeric forms A and B. Isomer A gives a white precipitate with BaCl₂, while Isomer B gives a pale yellow precipitate with AgNO₃. Write the formulas and IUPAC names of A and B.
9. Why does the C–O bond length in [Fe(CO)₅] increase slightly compared to free CO gas?
10. Name the coordination compound or metal complex used in: (a) cancer chemotherapy, (b) removal of hardness of water, (c) extraction of gold from its ore.

---

### Answers

1. IUPAC name: **potassium hexacyanidoferrate(III)**. Oxidation state of Fe = **+3**; **CN = 6**.
2. Mn²⁺ = [Ar] 3d⁵. With weak-field Br⁻ (CN = 4), hybridisation is **sp³ (tetrahedral)** with **5 unpaired electrons**.
   μ = √[5(5 + 2)] = √35 = **5.92 BM**.
   Jaanch: Tetrahedral always high-spin → n = 5 → √35 = 5.92 BM ✅
3. **[Co(en)₂Cl₂]⁺** shows BOTH geometrical (cis and trans) and optical (d-cis and l-cis) isomerism!
4. Co³⁺ = 3d⁶: with strong NH₃, all 6 electrons pair in three 3d orbitals, leaving two empty 3d orbitals for **d²sp³ (inner orbital)**. Ni²⁺ = 3d⁸: even after pairing, 8 electrons occupy at least four 3d orbitals, leaving only ONE 3d orbital free — not enough for d²sp³! So Ni²⁺ must use outer 4d orbitals (**sp³d², outer orbital**).
5. Linkage isomer: **[Co(NH₃)₅(NCS)]Cl₂**.
   IUPAC name: **pentaammineisothiocyanato-N-cobalt(III) chloride** (or pentaamminethiocyanato-N-cobalt(III) chloride).
6. By spectrochemical series (F⁻ < H₂O < CN⁻):
   **[CoF₆]³⁻ < [Co(H₂O)₆]³⁺ < [Co(CN)₆]³⁻**.
7. [Cr(NH₃)₆]³⁺ has Cr³⁺ (3d³, t₂g³ e_g⁰) with **3 unpaired electrons** (μ = 3.87 BM, paramagnetic). [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻ has Ni²⁺ (3d⁸) paired by strong CN⁻ into four 3d orbitals (dsp², **0 unpaired electrons**, diamagnetic).
8. - **Isomer A** (gives BaSO₄ ppt → SO₄²⁻ outside): **[Co(NH₃)₅Br]SO₄** — pentaamminebromidocobalt(III) sulphate.
   - **Isomer B** (gives AgBr ppt → Br⁻ outside): **[Co(NH₃)₅(SO₄)]Br** — pentaamminesulphatocobalt(III) bromide.
9. Due to **synergic π back-bonding** from filled Fe d-orbitals into the **vacant antibonding π* orbitals** of CO, the C–O bond order decreases slightly, making the C–O bond longer than in free CO.
10. (a) **Cisplatin** (cis-[Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂]), (b) **EDTA** (Na₂EDTA), (c) **Cyanide complex [Au(CN)₂]⁻** (using NaCN/KCN + air).

## Chapter Ka Saar (Coordination Compounds Wrap-up)

| Topic | Key Concept / Formula | Must-Remember Fact |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Werner & IUPAC | Primary = OS; Secondary = CN | Alphabetical ligands → metal (−ate if anionic) → Roman OS; en/ox = bidentate, EDTA = hexadentate |
| 2. Isomerism | Structural (4) + Geometrical + Optical | No cis/trans in tetrahedral; Ma₃b₃ → fac/mer; [M(AA)₃] & cis-[M(AA)₂b₂] optically active |
| 3. VBT & CFT | Δₒ (t₂g −0.4, e_g +0.6); Δₜ = (4/9) Δₒ | Strong ligand (CN⁻, CO) → low spin; Weak ligand (F⁻, Cl⁻) → high spin; [Ni(CO)₄] = sp³ (μ=0) |
| 4. Carbonyls & Stability | Synergic σ + π*; βₙ = K₁×K₂×...×Kₙ | Back-bonding strengthens M–C, weakens C–O; Chelate effect boosts stability (ΔS > 0) |
| 5. Applications | Bio, medicine, metallurgy, photography | Chlorophyll (Mg), Haemoglobin (Fe), B₁₂ (Co), Cisplatin, EDTA, [Ag(S₂O₃)₂]³⁻, Wilkinson's catalyst |
