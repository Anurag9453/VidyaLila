## Naye Shabd (Key Terms)

| Term | Hinglish Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Double Salt** | Do simple salts ka addition compound jo paani mein poori tarah apne individual ions mein toot jata hai (jaise Mohr's salt, Carnallite, Potash alum) |
| **Coordination Compound (Complex)** | Woh compound jismein central metal atom/ion fixed number of ligands se coordinate bonds dwara juda hota hai aur paani mein apni pehchan (identity) banaye rakhta hai |
| **Central Metal Atom/Ion** | Coordination entity ke beech ka metal (usually d-block) jo ligands se electron pairs accept karta hai (Lewis acid) |
| **Ligand** | Woh neutral molecule ya anion jo central metal ko ek ya zyada lone pairs donate karta hai (Lewis base) |
| **Denticity** | Ek ligand mein kitne donor atoms ek saath central metal se bond bana sakte hain |
| **Chelate Ligand** | Di- ya polydentate ligand jo central metal ke saath ek stable ring structure (chelate ring) banata hai |
| **Coordination Number (CN)** | Central metal atom/ion se directly jude hue **ligand donor atoms (sigma bonds)** ki total sankhya |
| **Coordination Sphere** | Square brackets [ ... ] ke andar likha gaya central metal aur uske directly bonded ligands; bahar wale ions **counter ions** kehlate hain |

## 1. Werner's Theory of Coordination Compounds (1893)

Alfred Werner (Nobel Prize 1913) ne CoCl₃ aur NH₃ ke compounds par AgNO₃ precipitation experiment karke **do tarah ki valencies** batayin:

| Feature | Primary Valency | Secondary Valency |
|---|---|---|
| Modern meaning | **Oxidation State** of central metal | **Coordination Number (CN)** of central metal |
| Nature | Ionisable (broken in water) | Non-ionisable (fixed inside [ ... ]) |
| Satisfied by | Negative ions (anions) only | Neutral molecules, anions, or rarely cations (ligands) |
| Directionality | Non-directional | Directional — gives definite **geometry** (octahedral, tetrahedral, square planar) |

### Classic Werner Table (1 mol compound + excess AgNO₃):
| Old Formula | Modern Werner Formula | Moles of AgCl Precipitated | Total Ions in Water |
|---|---|---|---|
| CoCl₃·6NH₃ | [Co(NH₃)₆]Cl₃ | 3 mol | 4 (1 complex + 3 Cl⁻) |
| CoCl₃·5NH₃ | [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]Cl₂ | 2 mol | 3 (1 complex + 2 Cl⁻) |
| CoCl₃·4NH₃ | [Co(NH₃)₄Cl₂]Cl | 1 mol | 2 (1 complex + 1 Cl⁻) |
| CoCl₃·3NH₃ | [Co(NH₃)₃Cl₃] | 0 mol | 0 (non-electrolyte!) |

## 2. Types of Ligands & Key Terms

1. **Unidentate (Monodentate — 1 donor atom):** Cl⁻, Br⁻, OH⁻, CN⁻, NH₃, H₂O, CO, NO.
2. **Didentate (Bidentate — 2 donor atoms, forms chelate ring):**
   - **en** = ethane-1,2-diamine (H₂N–CH₂–CH₂–NH₂) — neutral (charge 0), 2 N donors.
   - **ox** = oxalate ion (C₂O₄²⁻) — charge −2, 2 O donors.
3. **Polydentate / Hexadentate:**
   - **EDTA⁴⁻** (ethylenediaminetetraacetate ion) — **6 donor atoms** (2 N + 4 O), forms 5 interlocking chelate rings!
4. **Ambidentate Ligand:** Unidentate ligand jiske paas **2 alag donor atoms** hote hain par ek samay par sirf **1** hi bond banata hai:
   - **NO₂⁻:** Nitrito-N (via N) ya Nitrito-O (–ONO⁻, via O)
   - **SCN⁻:** Thiocyanato-S (via S) ya Thiocyanato-N / Isothiocyanato (–NCS⁻, via N)
   - **CN⁻:** Cyano (via C) ya Isocyano (–NC⁻, via N)
5. **Homoleptic vs Heteroleptic Complexes:**
   - **Homoleptic:** Sirf ek hi tarah ke ligands — jaise [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺, [Ni(CO)₄].
   - **Heteroleptic:** Ek se zyada tarah ke ligands — jaise [Co(NH₃)₄Cl₂]⁺.

## 3. IUPAC Nomenclature of Coordination Compounds

### Step-by-Step Rules (Formula → Name):
1. **Cation pehle, Anion baad mein** (simple salt ki tarah: NaCl = sodium chloride).
2. **Coordination sphere ke andar:** Pehle sabhi **ligands alphabetical order** mein (prefixes di-, tri-, tetra- ko alphabetical order mein ignore karte hain!), uske baad **central metal**, phir uski **oxidation state Roman numerals** mein parentheses mein — bina space ke ek saath!
3. **Ligand names:**
   - **Anionic ligands end in -o:** Cl⁻ (chlorido), Br⁻ (bromido), CN⁻ (cyanido), OH⁻ (hydroxido), C₂O₄²⁻ (oxalato), SO₄²⁻ (sulphato), NO₂⁻ (nitrito-N), ONO⁻ (nitrito-O), CO₃²⁻ (carbonato).
   - **Neutral ligands (special names):** H₂O (**aqua**), NH₃ (**ammine** — double 'm'!), CO (**carbonyl**), NO (**nitrosyl**); baaki neutral molecules apna regular naam rakhte hain (e.g., ethane-1,2-diamine).
4. **Numerical prefixes:**
   - Simple ligands: di-, tri-, tetra-, penta-, hexa-.
   - Complex ligands (jinke naam mein pehle se di/tri/tetra aata ho, jaise ethane-1,2-diamine): **bis-, tris-, tetrakis-** use karo aur ligand naam parentheses mein rakho!
5. **Central metal ending:**
   - Agar complex **cationic ya neutral** hai → metal ka normal naam (cobalt, iron, copper, platinum).
   - Agar complex **anionic** hai → metal ke naam ke end mein **-ate** lagao! (Fe → **ferrate**, Cu → **cuprate**, Ag → **argentate**, Au → **aurate**, Pb → **plumbate**, Sn → **stannate**, Co → **cobaltate**, Cr → **chromate**, Ni → **nickelate**, Pt → **platinate**).
6. **Counter ion** ka number (di/tri) naam mein nahi likhte! Jaise K₄[Fe(CN)₆] = potassium hexacyanidoferrate(II) (NOT tetrapotassium!).

## Solved Examples

### Example 1: Oxidation State and Coordination Number
Find the oxidation state and coordination number of the central metal in:
(a) [Co(en)₂Cl₂]⁺   (b) K₃[Fe(C₂O₄)₃]

**Solution:**
(a) **Oxidation state:** Let Co = x. en is neutral (0), each Cl⁻ = −1.
x + 2(0) + 2(−1) = +1 → x − 2 = +1 → **x = +3**.
**Coordination number:** Each en is bidentate (2 donors), each Cl⁻ is monodentate (1 donor).
CN = 2(2) + 2(1) = 4 + 2 = **6**.

(b) **Oxidation state:** Complex anion is [Fe(C₂O₄)₃]³⁻. Each C₂O₄²⁻ = −2.
x + 3(−2) = −3 → x − 6 = −3 → **x = +3**.
**Coordination number:** Each C₂O₄²⁻ is bidentate (2 donors) → CN = 3 × 2 = **6**.

Jaanch: Both are octahedral complexes (CN = 6); charge sums: +3 + 0 − 2 = +1 and +3 − 6 = −3 ✅

---

### Example 2: Writing IUPAC Names from Formulas
Write the IUPAC names of:
(a) [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl(NO₂)]
(b) K₃[Al(C₂O₄)₃]
(c) [CoCl₂(en)₂]Cl

**Solution:**
(a) Neutral complex; Pt oxidation state: x + 2(0) + (−1) + (−1) = 0 → +2 (II).
Ligands alphabetically: ammine, chlorido, nitrito-N.
Name: **diamminechloridonitrito-N-platinum(II)**.

(b) Anionic complex [Al(C₂O₄)₃]³⁻; Al = +3 (III) → aluminate.
Name: **potassium trioxalatoaluminate(III)**.

(c) Cationic complex [CoCl₂(en)₂]⁺; Co = +3 (III); ligands alphabetically: chlorido before ethane-1,2-diamine; use bis for en.
Name: **dichloridobis(ethane-1,2-diamine)cobalt(III) chloride**.

Jaanch: Alphabetical order checked: a < c < n in (a), c < e in (c); anionic ending -ate only in (b) ✅

---

### Example 3: Writing Formulas from IUPAC Names
Write the formulas for:
(a) tetraammineaquachloridocobalt(III) chloride
(b) potassium tetracyanidonickelate(II)

**Solution:**
(a) Metal = Co(+3), ligands = 4 NH₃ (0), 1 H₂O (0), 1 Cl⁻ (−1).
Net charge on complex = +3 + 0 + 0 − 1 = +2 → needs 2 Cl⁻ counter ions.
Formula: **[Co(NH₃)₄(H₂O)Cl]Cl₂**.

(b) Metal = Ni(+2), ligands = 4 CN⁻ (−4).
Net charge on complex = +2 − 4 = −2 → needs 2 K⁺ counter ions.
Formula: **K₂[Ni(CN)₄]**.

Jaanch: Total charges: [Co(+3) + Cl(−1)] = +2 balanced by 2Cl⁻; [Ni(+2) + 4CN⁻] = −2 balanced by 2K⁺ ✅

## Aam Galtiyan (Common Mistakes)

1. **Bidentate ligand ke liye coordination number ghalat ginna:**
   - *Galat:* [Fe(C₂O₄)₃]³⁻ mein 3 ligands dikh rahe hain toh CN = 3 bol dena.
   - *Sahi:* C₂O₄²⁻ aur en **bidentate** (2 donor atoms each) hain! Isliye 3 × 2 = **CN = 6** hoga!

2. **Ammine mein single 'm' likhna ya counter ion mein di/tri lagana:**
   - *Galat:* tetrapotassium hexacyanidoferrate(II) ya tetraamine.
   - *Sahi:* NH₃ ke liye **ammine (double m)** hota hai, aur counter ion ka number nahi likhte (**potassium** hexacyanidoferrate(II)).

3. **Cationic complex mein bhi metal par -ate laga dena:**
   - *Galat:* [Co(NH₃)₆]Cl₃ ko hexaamminecobaltate(III) chloride likhna.
   - *Sahi:* **-ate sirf anionic complex** mein lagta hai! Cationic/neutral mein simple **cobalt(III)** likho.

## Quick Recap

- **Double salt** dissociates completely in water; **coordination compound** retains its complex ion [ ... ] in solution.
- **Werner's theory:** Primary valency = oxidation state (ionisable); Secondary valency = coordination number (non-ionisable, directional).
- **Key ligands:** en (bidentate, 0), ox/C₂O₄²⁻ (bidentate, −2), EDTA⁴⁻ (hexadentate, −4), NO₂⁻/SCN⁻ (ambidentate).
- **IUPAC order:** Cation first, then anion; inside [ ... ] ligands alphabetically → metal (with -ate if anionic) → Roman numeral oxidation state.

## Practice Questions

1. Distinguish between a double salt and a coordination compound with one example each.
2. According to Werner's theory, how many moles of AgCl will precipitate when 1 mole of [Pt(NH₃)₃Cl₃]Cl is treated with excess AgNO₃?
3. What is an ambidentate ligand? Give two examples with their donor atoms.
4. Determine the oxidation state and coordination number of the central metal in: (a) [Cr(H₂O)₄Cl₂]⁺, (b) [Co(en)₃]³⁺, (c) K₂[Zn(OH)₄].
5. Classify [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ and [Co(NH₃)₅(CO₃)]Cl as homoleptic or heteroleptic complexes.
6. Write the IUPAC names of:
   (a) [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]Cl₂
   (b) K₄[Fe(CN)₆]
   (c) [Ni(CO)₄]
7. Write the IUPAC names of:
   (a) [Cr(en)₃]Cl₃
   (b) [Ag(NH₃)₂][Ag(CN)₂]
8. Write the chemical formulas of:
   (a) hexaammineplatinum(IV) chloride
   (b) potassium trioxalatochromate(III)
   (c) pentaamminenitrito-O-cobalt(III) ion
9. Why is EDTA⁴⁻ called a hexadentate ligand? How many chelate rings does it form with a Ca²⁺ ion?
10. A coordination compound has the empirical formula CrCl₃·6H₂O. When 1 mole of it is treated with excess AgNO₃, 2 moles of AgCl precipitate. Write its structural formula and IUPAC name.

---

### Answers

1. A **double salt** completely dissociates into simple ions in water (e.g., Mohr's salt FeSO₄·(NH₄)₂SO₄·6H₂O gives Fe²⁺, NH₄⁺, SO₄²⁻ tests). A **coordination compound** retains its complex ion intact in water (e.g., K₄[Fe(CN)₆] gives K⁺ and [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻, not free Fe²⁺).
2. Only the 1 Cl⁻ outside the square bracket is ionisable → **1 mole of AgCl** precipitates.
3. A monodentate ligand that has **two different donor atoms** capable of coordinating to the metal, one at a time. Examples: **NO₂⁻** (donor N or O) and **SCN⁻** (donor S or N).
4. (a) Cr: x + 0 − 2 = +1 → **OS = +3**, **CN = 6**.
   (b) Co: x + 0 = +3 → **OS = +3**, **CN = 3 × 2 = 6**.
   (c) Zn in [Zn(OH)₄]²⁻: x − 4 = −2 → **OS = +2**, **CN = 4**.
   Jaanch: All oxidation states match common stable states of Cr(+3), Co(+3), Zn(+2) ✅
5. [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ is **homoleptic** (only CN⁻ ligands); [Co(NH₃)₅(CO₃)]Cl is **heteroleptic** (both NH₃ and CO₃²⁻ ligands).
6. (a) **pentaamminechloridocobalt(III) chloride**
   (b) **potassium hexacyanidoferrate(II)**
   (c) **tetracarbonylnickel(0)**
7. (a) **tris(ethane-1,2-diamine)chromium(III) chloride**
   (b) **diamminesilver(I) dicyanidoargentate(I)**
8. (a) **[Pt(NH₃)₆]Cl₄** (Pt +4 balanced by 4 Cl⁻)
   (b) **K₃[Cr(C₂O₄)₃]** (Cr +3, 3 ox = −6 → −3 complex balanced by 3 K⁺)
   (c) **[Co(NH₃)₅(ONO)]²⁺** (Co +3, ONO = −1 → +2)
   Jaanch: Charge balance verified for all three formulas ✅
9. EDTA⁴⁻ has **6 donor atoms** (2 nitrogen atoms and 4 carboxylate oxygen atoms) that can simultaneously coordinate to one metal ion, forming **5 chelate rings** around Ca²⁺.
10. 2 moles of AgCl mean 2 ionisable Cl⁻ outside the bracket; 1 Cl⁻ and 5 H₂O are inside to give CN = 6, with 1 H₂O of crystallisation outside:
    Formula: **[Cr(H₂O)₅Cl]Cl₂·H₂O**
    IUPAC name: **pentaaquachloridochromium(III) chloride monohydrate**.
