## Naye Shabd (New Words)

- **Electrolysis:** External DC electrical energy dekar kisi non-spontaneous redox reaction ko karwana (Electrolytic cell mein **Anode = Positive (+)**, **Cathode = Negative (−)** — galvanic cell ke signs ka ulta! Par **Oxidation hamesha Anode** par aur **Reduction hamesha Cathode** par hota hai!).
- **Electrochemical Equivalent (Z):** 1 Coulomb charge pass karne par electrode par deposit hone wale substance ka mass: **Z = M / (n F)**.
- **Overpotential (Overvoltage):** Kuch reactions (jaise O₂ gas banna) kinetically slow hone ke karan unhe chalane ke liye theoretical E° se kuch extra voltage lagta hai — ise overpotential kehte hain!

## 1. Faraday's Two Laws of Electrolysis

### First Law:
Electrode par deposit ya liberate hone wale substance ka mass **w**, pass kiye gaye total electric charge **Q = I t** ke directly proportional hota hai:

**w = Z Q = Z I t = [ M / (n F) ] × I t**

*(1 Faraday = **96,500 C** = 1 mole electrons — yeh hamesha **1 gram-equivalent (= M / n)** substance deposit karta hai!)*
- Ag⁺ + 1e⁻ → Ag: **1 F** deposits **1 mol Ag (108 g)**.
- Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu: **2 F** deposits **1 mol Cu (63.5 g)** (1 F deposits 31.75 g).
- Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al: **3 F** deposits **1 mol Al (27 g)** (1 F deposits 9 g).

### Second Law:
Jab **same quantity of electricity (Q)** alag-alag electrolytic cells (series mein jude!) se pass ki jaati hai, to deposit hone wale masses unke **Equivalent Weights (E = M / n)** ke directly proportional hote hain:

**w₁ / w₂ = E₁ / E₂ = (M₁ / n₁) / (M₂ / n₂)**

## 2. Products of Electrolysis (4 Classic Cases — Exam Favourites!)

- **Cathode (Reduction):** Jiska **E° (reduction) ZYADA** hoga, woh pehle reduce hoga!
- **Anode (Oxidation):** Jiska **E° (reduction) KAM** hoga, woh pehle oxidise hoga (exception: **Cl⁻ vs H₂O** mein overpotential ke karan **Cl₂ gas** banti hai!).

| Electrolyte (Inert Pt electrodes) | Cathode Product (Reduction) | Anode Product (Oxidation) |
|---|---|---|
| **Molten NaCl** | **Na(l)** metal | **Cl₂(g)** |
| **Aqueous NaCl (Brine)** | **H₂(g)** (H₂O beats Na⁺!) | **Cl₂(g)** (due to overpotential of O₂!) |
| **Dilute H₂SO₄** | **H₂(g)** | **O₂(g)** |
| **Conc. H₂SO₄** | **H₂(g)** | **S₂O₈²⁻** (Peroxodisulphate!) |
| **Aqueous CuSO₄ (Pt electrodes)** | **Cu(s)** | **O₂(g)** |
| **Aqueous CuSO₄ (Cu electrodes)** | **Cu(s)** deposits | **Cu(s) dissolves** as Cu²⁺! |

## Solved Examples

### Example 1: Mass of Copper Deposited (Faraday's First Law)
A solution of **CuSO₄** is electrolysed for **10 minutes** with a current of **1.5 A**. What is the mass of copper deposited at the cathode? (Molar mass of Cu = **63.5 g/mol**, F = **96,500 C/mol**.)

**Hal (Solution):**
- t = 10 min = **600 s** ⟹ Total charge **Q = I t = 1.5 × 600 = 900 C**.
- Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu (**n = 2**):
  **w = (M × Q) / (n F) = (63.5 × 900) / (2 × 96500) = 57,150 / 193,000 ≈ 0.296 g**.
- **Jaanch:** 2 F (193,000 C) deposits 63.5 g ⟹ 900 C deposits (900/193000) × 63.5 ≈ 0.296 g ✅.

### Example 2: Faraday's Second Law (AgNO₃ & CuSO₄ in Series)
Two electrolytic cells containing **AgNO₃** (Ag = 108) and **CuSO₄** (Cu = 63.5) are connected in **series**. If **1.08 g of Ag** is deposited in the first cell, what mass of **Cu** is deposited in the second cell?

**Hal (Solution):**
- Equivalent weights: E_Ag = 108 / 1 = **108**; E_Cu = 63.5 / 2 = **31.75**.
- **w_Cu / w_Ag = E_Cu / E_Ag** ⟹ w_Cu = 1.08 × (31.75 / 108) = 0.01 × 31.75 = **0.3175 g**!
- **Jaanch:** 1.08 g Ag = 0.01 mol e⁻ ⟹ 0.005 mol Cu = 0.005 × 63.5 = 0.3175 g ✅.

## Aam Galtiyan (Common Mistakes)

1. **Aqueous NaCl ke electrolysis mein Cathode par Na metal aur Anode par O₂ bol dena:**
   - *Sahi:* Aqueous NaCl mein **Cathode par H₂(g)** (Na⁺ ka E° = −2.71 V bahut kam hai!) aur **Anode par Cl₂(g)** (O₂ ke overpotential ke karan!) milta hai!
2. **Faraday's law mein time ko minutes mein hi rakh dena:**
   - *Sahi:* Q = I × t mein time **hamesha SECONDS** mein rakho!
3. **1 mol Al³⁺ → Al ke liye 1 F charge bol dena:**
   - *Sahi:* Al³⁺ + **3e⁻** → Al ⟹ **3 Faradays (3 × 96,500 C)** chahiye!

|---|---|---|
| Faraday's 1st Law | **w = (M / nF) × I t** | 1 F = 96,500 C deposits 1 gram-equivalent |
| Faraday's 2nd Law | **w₁ / w₂ = E₁ / E₂** | Same Q passed through cells in series |
| Aqueous NaCl Electrolysis | Cathode: **H₂(g)**; Anode: **Cl₂(g)** | Solution becomes alkaline (NaOH)! |

## Practice Questions

1. State Faraday's first and second laws of electrolysis.
2. How much charge in terms of Faradays (and Coulombs) is required to reduce (a) 1 mole of Al³⁺ to Al, and (b) 1 mole of MnO₄⁻ to Mn²⁺?
3. How many Coulombs of electricity are required to deposit 40 g of Calcium from molten CaCl₂ (atomic mass of Ca = 40 g/mol, F = 96,500 C/mol)?
4. A steady current of 2.0 A is passed through an AgNO₃ solution for 16 minutes and 5 seconds (965 s). Calculate the mass of silver deposited at the cathode (Ag = 108 g/mol, F = 96,500 C/mol).
5. Why is **Cl₂ gas** evolved at the anode instead of O₂ gas during the electrolysis of aqueous NaCl solution, even though E°(O₂/H₂O) = +1.23 V is lower than E°(Cl₂/Cl⁻) = +1.36 V?
6. Predict the products of electrolysis of (a) aqueous CuSO₄ using **inert Platinum** electrodes, and (b) aqueous CuSO₄ using **active Copper** electrodes.
7. Two cells containing ZnSO₄ (Zn = 65.4) and AgNO₃ (Ag = 108) are connected in series. If 2.16 g of Ag is deposited, calculate the mass of Zn deposited.
8. How long (in seconds) must a current of 5.0 A be passed through molten NaCl to produce 2.3 g of Sodium metal (Na = 23 g/mol, F = 96,500 C/mol)?
9. State whether True or False: In an electrolytic cell, reduction still takes place at the cathode and oxidation at the anode, just like in a galvanic cell.
10. What volume of H₂ gas at STP (22.4 L/mol) is liberated at the cathode when 1 Faraday (96,500 C) of charge is passed through acidified water?

### Answers

1. **First Law:** Deposited mass **w ∝ Q (= It)**. **Second Law:** Same Q pass karne par alag-alag cells mein deposited masses unke **equivalent weights (E = M/n)** ke proportional hote hain.

2. (a) Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al ⟹ **3 F = 3 × 96500 = 289,500 C**.
   (b) MnO₄⁻ (+7) + 5e⁻ → Mn²⁺ (+2) ⟹ **5 F = 5 × 96500 = 482,500 C**.
   Jaanch: Oxidation state drop 7 − 2 = 5 electrons ✅.

3. 40 g Ca = 1 mol Ca; Ca²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Ca ⟹ **2 F = 2 × 96,500 = 193,000 C**.
   Jaanch: n = 2 ⟹ 2 F ✅.

4. Q = I t = 2.0 × 965 = **1930 C** = 1930 / 96500 = **0.02 F**.
   Ag⁺ + 1e⁻ → Ag ⟹ w = 0.02 × 108 = **2.16 g**.
   Jaanch: 1930 / 96500 = 1/50 = 0.02; 0.02 × 108 = 2.16 g ✅.

5. Water se O₂ gas banne ki reaction kinetically slow hoti hai aur use extra voltage (**Overpotential / Overvoltage**) chahiye hota hai — isliye anode par Cl⁻ pehle oxidise hokar **Cl₂(g)** banata hai!

6. (a) **Inert Pt electrodes:** Cathode: **Cu(s)**; Anode: **O₂(g)**.
   (b) **Active Cu electrodes:** Cathode: **Cu(s)** deposits; Anode: **Cu(s) dissolves** as Cu²⁺ (used in electrorefining of copper!).

7. 2.16 g Ag = 2.16 / 108 = 0.02 mol e⁻.
   Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Zn ⟹ moles of Zn = 0.02 / 2 = 0.01 mol ⟹ **w_Zn = 0.01 × 65.4 = 0.654 g**.
   Jaanch: w_Zn / 2.16 = 32.7 / 108 ⟹ 0.654 g ✅.

8. 2.3 g Na = 0.10 mol Na (n = 1) ⟹ Q = 0.10 × 96,500 = **9650 C**.
   t = Q / I = 9650 / 5.0 = **1930 seconds** (≈ 32.2 min).
   Jaanch: 5 × 1930 = 9650 C ✅.

9. **True ✅.** Signs (+/−) badal jaate hain, par **Anode = Oxidation** aur **Cathode = Reduction** har cell mein 100% pakka rehta hai!

10. 2 H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂(g): 2 F gives 1 mol H₂ (22.4 L) ⟹ **1 F gives 0.5 mol H₂ = 11.2 L at STP**!
    Jaanch: 22.4 / 2 = 11.2 L ✅.
