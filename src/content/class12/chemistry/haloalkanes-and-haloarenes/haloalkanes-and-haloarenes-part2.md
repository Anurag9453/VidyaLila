# Methods of Preparation of Haloalkanes and Haloarenes

> **Pichle topic mein:** Humne [Classification, Nomenclature and Nature of C–X Bond](haloalkanes-and-haloarenes.md) padha. **Class 11 bridge:** Alkenes par HX addition (Markovnikov rule) aur benzene ka electrophilic substitution hum [Hydrocarbons](../../../class11/chemistry/hydrocarbons/hydrocarbons.md) mein padh chuke hain — ab hum haloalkanes aur haloarenes banane ke sabhi best industrial aur lab methods seekhenge!

---

Uma ek chemical manufacturing video dekh rahi thi jahan ethanol se chloroethane banane ke teen tareeke dikhaye gaye: PCl₅ ke saath, PCl₃ ke saath, aur **SOCl₂ (thionyl chloride)** ke saath. Factory engineer ne kaha, "Hum hamesha **SOCl₂** choose karte hain!" Uma ne socha, "Kyun?" Engineer ne dikhaya: SOCl₂ ke saath jo by-products bante hain — SO₂ aur HCl — woh dono **gases** hain jo upar ud jati hain, aur neeche bilkul **100% pure chloroethane** bachta hai!

> **Safety Note:** Thionyl chloride (SOCl₂), phosphorus halides, bromine (Br₂), aur diazonium salts corrosive/toxic hote hain; yeh reactions sirf fume-hood industrial/research setups aur theory mein padhi jati hain.

---

## Naye Shabd (Key Terms)

| Term | Hinglish Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Lucas Reagent** | Concentrated HCl + anhydrous ZnCl₂ ka mixture jo alcohols ko alkyl chlorides mein badalta hai (aur 1°/2°/3° alcohols identify karta hai) |
| **Darzen's Method** | Alcohol + SOCl₂ (thionyl chloride) → R–Cl + SO₂(↑) + HCl(↑) — chloroalkanes banane ka sabse clean tareeka |
| **Finkelstein Reaction** | Dry acetone mein R–Cl ya R–Br + NaI → R–I + NaX(↓) — **alkyl iodides** banane ka halogen-exchange method |
| **Swarts Reaction** | R–Br ya R–Cl + metallic fluoride (AgF, Hg₂F₂, CoF₂, SbF₃) → R–F — **alkyl fluorides** banane ka best method |
| **Sandmeyer Reaction** | Benzene diazonium chloride (ArN₂⁺Cl⁻) + Cu₂Cl₂/HCl ya Cu₂Br₂/HBr → chlorobenzene / bromobenzene + N₂(↑) |

## 1. Preparation of Haloalkanes from Alcohols (R–OH → R–X)

Yeh haloalkanes banane ka sabse best aur common tareeka hai!
1. **With HX (Groove's Process):**
   - R–OH + HCl (anhydrous ZnCl₂) → R–Cl + H₂O
   - Alcohol reactivity order: **3° > 2° > 1°** (3° alcohols sirf conc. HCl ke saath room temp par turant react kar lete hain, ZnCl₂ ki zaroorat bhi nahi!).
   - R–OH + NaBr + H₂SO₄ → R–Br + NaHSO₄ + H₂O.
   - *(Note: HI banane ke liye KI + H₂SO₄ use **NAHI** karte! Kyunki H₂SO₄ oxidising agent hai aur HI ko I₂ mein oxidise kar deta hai — isliye non-oxidising **H₃PO₄** (95%) + KI use karte hain!)*
2. **With Phosphorus Halides:**
   - 3R–OH + PCl₃ → 3R–Cl + H₃PO₃
   - R–OH + PCl₅ → R–Cl + POCl₃ + HCl
   - R–OH + Red P / X₂ (X₂ = Br₂, I₂) → R–X (PBr₃/PI₃ in situ banta hai).
3. **With Thionyl Chloride (SOCl₂ — BEST Method!):**
   - **R–OH + SOCl₂ → R–Cl + SO₂(g)↑ + HCl(g)↑**
   - Best kyun? Kyunki dono by-products (SO₂ aur HCl) escapable gases hain, jisse pure R–Cl milta hai!
- **Important Note:** Alcohols wala koi bhi method **phenols (Ar–OH)** par kaam **NAHI** karta! Kyunki phenol mein C–O bond resonance ki wajah se partial double bond hota hai aur tootna bahut mushkil hota hai!

## 2. Preparation from Hydrocarbons

1. **Free-Radical Halogenation of Alkanes:**
   CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₃ + Cl₂ (UV light / heat) → CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂Cl + CH₃CH₂CH(Cl)CH₃ (mixture of isomeric mono- and poly-haloalkanes — isliye lab mein pure halide ke liye achha method nahi hai).
2. **From Alkenes (Class 11 Hydrocarbons link):**
   - **Addition of HX (Markovnikov rule):** CH₃–CH=CH₂ + HBr → CH₃–CH(Br)–CH₃ (major).
   - **Anti-Markovnikov (Peroxide / Kharasch effect — ONLY for HBr!):** CH₃–CH=CH₂ + HBr (peroxide) → CH₃–CH₂–CH₂–Br (major).
   - **Addition of Br₂ in CCl₄ (Test for unsaturation):** CH₂=CH₂ + Br₂ (CCl₄) → Br–CH₂–CH₂–Br (vic-dibromide; reddish-brown colour disappears!).

## 3. Halogen Exchange Reactions (For R–I and R–F!)

### (a) Finkelstein Reaction (For Alkyl Iodides, R–I):
**R–X + NaI —(dry acetone)→ R–I + NaX↓**   (X = Cl or Br)
- **Dry acetone kyun?** Kyunki NaI acetone mein soluble hai, par **NaCl aur NaBr dry acetone mein INSOLUBLE** hote hain aur precipitate hokar baith jate hain! Le Chatelier's principle se reaction forward direction mein complete ho jati hai!

### (b) Swarts Reaction (For Alkyl Fluorides, R–F):
Direct fluorination explosive hoti hai, isliye alkyl bromide/chloride ko metallic fluoride (**AgF, Hg₂F₂, CoF₂, ya SbF₃**) ke saath heat karte hain:
**CH₃–Br + AgF → CH₃–F + AgBr↓**

## 4. Preparation of Haloarenes

### (a) Electrophilic Halogenation of Arenes (Dark, Lewis Acid Catalyst):
Toluene + Cl₂ —(Fe / FeCl₃, dark)→ **o-Chlorotoluene + p-Chlorotoluene** (para major; ortho aur para ke melting points mein kaafi fark hone se easily separate ho jate hain).
- **Direct iodination reversible hai** (Ar–H + I₂ ⇌ Ar–I + HI) kyunki HI strong reducing agent hai → isliye oxidising agent (**HNO₃ ya HIO₃**) dalte hain jo HI ko I₂ mein oxidise kar deta hai!
- **Direct fluorination** bahut violent hai → is method se fluorobenzene nahi banate.

### (b) From Amines via Diazonium Salt (Sandmeyer & Related Reactions — BEST Method!):
1. **Diazotisation (0–5°C / 273–278 K):**
   Aniline (C₆H₅–NH₂) + NaNO₂ + 2HCl —(273–278 K)→ **C₆H₅–N₂⁺Cl⁻** (Benzene diazonium chloride) + NaCl + 2H₂O
2. **Sandmeyer Reaction (for Cl and Br):**
   - C₆H₅–N₂⁺Cl⁻ + Cu₂Cl₂ / HCl → **C₆H₅–Cl** + N₂↑
   - C₆H₅–N₂⁺Cl⁻ + Cu₂Br₂ / HBr → **C₆H₅–Br** + N₂↑
3. **For Iodobenzene (no Cu catalyst needed!):**
   - C₆H₅–N₂⁺Cl⁻ + KI (warm) → **C₆H₅–I** + KCl + N₂↑
4. **Balz–Schiemann Reaction (for Fluorobenzene):**
   - C₆H₅–N₂⁺Cl⁻ + HBF₄ → C₆H₅–N₂⁺BF₄⁻ —(heat)→ **C₆H₅–F** + BF₃ + N₂↑

## Solved Examples

### Example 1: Why H₂SO₄ Is Not Used with KI to Convert Alcohols to R–I
Why is sulphuric acid (H₂SO₄) not used during the reaction of alcohols with KI? What acid is used instead?

**Solution:**
Concentrated H₂SO₄ is a strong oxidising agent. It oxidises the HI formed in situ (from KI + H₂SO₄) into molecular iodine (**I₂**), preventing the formation of the desired alkyl iodide (R–I):
2KI + 2H₂SO₄ → 2KHSO₄ + 2HI; then 2HI + H₂SO₄ → I₂ + SO₂ + 2H₂O.
Instead, a **non-oxidising acid like phosphoric acid (95% H₃PO₄)** is used with KI.

Jaanch: H₃PO₄ generates HI without oxidising I⁻ to I₂ ✅

---

### Example 2: Multi-Step Conversion Using Named Reactions
How will you convert:
(a) Propene to 1-iodopropane
(b) Aniline to bromobenzene

**Solution:**
(a) Step 1 (Anti-Markovnikov addition): CH₃–CH=CH₂ + HBr —(peroxide)→ CH₃–CH₂–CH₂–Br (1-bromopropane)
Step 2 (Finkelstein reaction): CH₃–CH₂–CH₂–Br + NaI —(dry acetone)→ **CH₃–CH₂–CH₂–I** + NaBr↓

(b) Step 1 (Diazotisation): C₆H₅–NH₂ + NaNO₂ + HCl —(273–278 K)→ C₆H₅–N₂⁺Cl⁻
Step 2 (Sandmeyer reaction): C₆H₅–N₂⁺Cl⁻ + Cu₂Br₂ / HBr → **C₆H₅–Br** + N₂↑

Jaanch: Peroxide puts Br on terminal C1, Finkelstein swaps Br for I cleanly; Sandmeyer replaces N₂⁺ cleanly with Br ✅

---

### Example 3: Identifying Why Phenol Does Not React with SOCl₂/HCl
Why cannot chlorobenzene be prepared by reacting phenol (C₆H₅–OH) with HCl/ZnCl₂ or SOCl₂?

**Solution:**
In phenol, the lone pair on the oxygen atom delocalises into the benzene ring by **resonance**, giving the **C–O bond partial double-bond character**. This makes the C–O bond much stronger and shorter than the single C–O bond in aliphatic alcohols, so it cannot be broken by HCl/ZnCl₂ or SOCl₂.

Jaanch: Partial double bond in Ar–OH resists nucleophilic C–O cleavage ✅

## Aam Galtiyan (Common Mistakes)

1. **Free-radical chlorination aur electrophilic chlorination ke conditions mix kar dena:**
   - *Galat:* Toluene + Cl₂ + UV light → o/p-chlorotoluene.
   - *Sahi:* **UV light / heat** se side-chain par free-radical chlorination hoti hai (**benzyl chloride**, C₆H₅–CH₂Cl)! Ring par substitution ke liye **Fe/FeCl₃ + dark** chahiye!

2. **Finkelstein aur Swarts reactions ke naam ulta kar dena:**
   - *Galat:* Swarts reaction se R–I banta hai.
   - *Sahi:* **Finkelstein** (NaI/dry acetone) → **R–I (Iodide)**; **Swarts** (AgF/Hg₂F₂) → **R–F (Fluoride)**!

3. **Diazotisation ka temperature bhool jana:**
   - *Galat:* Aniline + NaNO₂ + HCl ko room temperature (25°C) par karwana.
   - *Sahi:* **273–278 K (0–5°C)** mandatory hai! Zyada garam karne par diazonium salt paani se react karke **phenol** bana leta hai!

## Quick Recap

- **Best for R–Cl from R–OH:** SOCl₂ (Darzen's method — gaseous SO₂ & HCl escape).
- **For R–I from R–OH:** KI + 95% H₃PO₄ (never H₂SO₄!).
- **Halogen exchange:** Finkelstein (NaI/dry acetone → R–I); Swarts (AgF/Hg₂F₂ → R–F).
- **Haloarenes:** Ar–H + X₂ (Fe/FeCl₃, dark; add HNO₃/HIO₃ for I₂); Best route: Aniline → C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ (0–5°C) → Sandmeyer (Cu₂Cl₂/Cu₂Br₂) or KI (Ar–I).

### Answers

1. Because the by-products formed with SOCl₂ are **SO₂ and HCl**, which are both **gases** that escape easily, leaving behind pure alkyl chloride without tedious purification.
2. **R–X + NaI —(dry acetone)→ R–I + NaX↓** (X = Cl, Br). Dry acetone dissolves NaI but precipitates out NaCl/NaBr, driving the equilibrium forward by Le Chatelier's principle.
3. **CH₃–CH₂–Br + AgF → CH₃–CH₂–F + AgBr↓** (or using Hg₂F₂ / SbF₃).
4. Direct iodination of benzene (C₆H₆ + I₂ ⇌ C₆H₅I + HI) is **reversible** because HI is a strong reducing agent that reduces C₆H₅I back to benzene. Adding HNO₃ or HIO₃ oxidises HI to I₂, shifting the equilibrium forward.
5. (a) **CH₃–CH₂–CH₂–Br** (1-bromopropane, anti-Markovnikov)
   (b) **C₆H₅–I + KCl + N₂↑** (iodobenzene)
   (c) **CH₃CH₂Cl + POCl₃ + HCl**
6. Step 1: C₆H₅–NH₂ + NaNO₂ + 2HCl —(273–278 K)→ C₆H₅–N₂⁺Cl⁻ + NaCl + 2H₂O
   Step 2: C₆H₅–N₂⁺Cl⁻ + Cu₂Cl₂ / HCl → **C₆H₅–Cl** + N₂↑
7. Because resonance in phenol gives the C–O bond partial double-bond character, making it too strong to break with HCl/ZnCl₂.
8. (a) Dark + FeCl₃ (electrophilic ring substitution): **o-Chlorotoluene + p-Chlorotoluene**.
   (b) Sunlight / UV (free-radical side-chain substitution): **Benzyl chloride (C₆H₅–CH₂Cl)** (and further chlorination to C₆H₅CHCl₂, C₆H₅CCl₃).
9. (a) C₂H₅OH + SOCl₂ → C₂H₅Cl —(AgF, Swarts)→ **C₂H₅F**.
   (b) CH₃CH₂CH=CH₂ + HBr —(peroxide)→ CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂Br —(NaI/dry acetone, Finkelstein)→ **CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂I**.
   Jaanch: Both 2-step routes give pure target halides in high yield ✅
10. Because free-radical chlorination produces a complex **mixture of isomeric monochlorides** (1-chlorobutane + 2-chlorobutane) as well as **polychlorinated products**, which are difficult to separate.
