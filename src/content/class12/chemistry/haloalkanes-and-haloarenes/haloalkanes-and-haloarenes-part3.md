# Physical Properties, SN1, SN2 and Elimination Reactions

> **Pichle topic mein:** Humne [Methods of Preparation of Haloalkanes and Haloarenes](haloalkanes-and-haloarenes-part2.md) padhe. Ab dekhenge ki haloalkanes ki physical properties (boiling point, melting point, solubility) kaisi hoti hain, aur woh nucleophiles aur bases ke saath **SN2, SN1, Elimination (Zaitsev rule), Grignard aur Wurtz** reactions kaise dete hain!

---

Uttam ek kinetics graph dekh raha tha jismein do bromobutanes ko paani/OH⁻ ke saath react karwaya gaya: **1-bromobutane** (1°) ka rate OH⁻ double karne par double ho gaya, jabki **2-bromo-2-methylpropane** (3°) ka rate OH⁻ double karne par **bilkul nahi badla!** Uttam ne hairan hokar poocha, "Dono mein C–Br bond toot raha hai, phir ek ka rate OH⁻ par depend kar raha hai aur doosre ka nahi?"

Yahi hai Organic Chemistry ke do sabse mashhoor mechanisms ka antar: **SN2 (ek-step backside attack)** vs **SN1 (do-step carbocation pathway)**!

---

## Naye Shabd (Key Terms)

| Term | Hinglish Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Nucleophilic Substitution (SN)** | Woh reaction jismein ek stronger nucleophile (Nu⁻) haloalkane se halide leaving group (X⁻) ko replace kar deta hai |
| **SN2 Mechanism** | Bimolecular (2nd order), single-step backside attack with **Walden inversion** (Rate = k[R–X][Nu⁻]) |
| **SN1 Mechanism** | Unimolecular (1st order), two-step mechanism via a planar **carbocation** intermediate with **racemisation** (Rate = k[R–X]) |
| **Ambident Nucleophile** | Woh nucleophile jiske paas do alag nucleophilic attacking atoms hon (jaise CN⁻: C ya N; NO₂⁻: O ya N) |
| **Zaitsev (Saytzeff) Rule** | Dehydrohalogenation (β-elimination) mein woh alkene **major product** hota hai jismein C=C double bond par **sabse zyada alkyl groups** (zyada substituted alkene) jude hon |
| **Grignard Reagent (R–Mg–X)** | Organometallic reagent jo R–X + Mg (dry ether) se banta hai; carbon par δ⁻ charge (strong base & nucleophile!) |

## 1. Physical Properties of Haloalkanes & Haloarenes

1. **Boiling Points (BP):**
   - For the same alkyl group: **R–I > R–Br > R–Cl > R–F** (halogen ka mass aur size badhne se van der Waals forces badhti hain).
   - For isomeric haloalkanes: **Branching badhne par BP GHAT-TA hai!** Kyunki branching se molecule spherical ho jata hai → surface area kam → weaker van der Waals forces!
     Example: **1-Bromobutane (375 K) > 2-Bromobutane (364 K) > 2-Bromo-2-methylpropane (346 K)**.
2. **Melting Points of Dichlorobenzenes (Symmetry Effect!):**
   - **para-Dichlorobenzene** ka melting point ortho aur meta isomers se **bahut zyada** (323 K vs 256 K, 249 K) hota hai! Kyunki para isomer **symmetrical** hai aur crystal lattice mein tightly pack ho jata hai!
3. **Solubility:** Polar hone ke bawajood haloalkanes **paani mein almost insoluble** hote hain! Kyunki yeh paani ke strong intermolecular H-bonds ko todne ke liye zaroori energy release nahi kar paate (organic solvents mein soluble hain).

## 2. Nucleophilic Substitution Reactions (SN2 vs SN1)

### Key Ambident Nucleophile Reactions (Exam Favourites!):
- **R–X + KCN (ionic) → R–CN** (Alkyl cyanide / nitrile — C–C bond stronger!).
- **R–X + AgCN (covalent) → R–NC** (Alkyl isocyanide — Ag–C covalent hone se N ka lone pair attack karta hai!).
- **R–X + KNO₂ (ionic) → R–O–N=O** (Alkyl nitrite — O⁻ attack karta hai!).
- **R–X + AgNO₂ (covalent) → R–NO₂** (Nitroalkane — Ag–O covalent hone se N ka lone pair attack karta hai!).

### SN2 vs SN1 Complete Comparison:

| Feature | SN2 (Substitution Nucleophilic Bimolecular) | SN1 (Substitution Nucleophilic Unimolecular) |
|---|---|---|
| Steps | **1 step** (concerted, via pentavalent transition state) | **2 steps** (Step 1 slow: carbocation forms; Step 2 fast: Nu⁻ attacks) |
| Rate Law & Order | Rate = k [R–X][Nu⁻] (**2nd order**) | Rate = k [R–X] (**1st order**, independent of [Nu⁻]) |
| Reactivity Order | **CH₃X > 1° > 2° > 3°** (least steric hindrance wins!) | **3° > 2° > 1° > CH₃X** (most stable carbocation wins! Allylic & benzylic also very fast!) |
| Stereochemistry | **100% Inversion of configuration** (Walden inversion — umbrella flip!) | **Racemisation** (planar carbocation par front + back attack → d + l mixture) |
| Leaving group order | R–I > R–Br > R–Cl > R–F | R–I > R–Br > R–Cl > R–F |
| Favoured by | Strong nucleophile, polar **aprotic** solvent (acetone, DMSO) | Weak nucleophile, polar **protic** solvent (H₂O, ethanol) |

## 3. Elimination Reactions (Dehydrohalogenation — Zaitsev Rule)

Jab β-hydrogen wale haloalkane ko **alcoholic KOH** ke saath heat kiya jata hai, toh β-carbon se H aur α-carbon se X nikal kar **alkene** banta hai!
- **Example:** 2-Bromopentane + alc. KOH (Δ) → **Pent-2-ene (81%, Major — Zaitsev)** + Pent-1-ene (19%, Minor).
- **Substitution vs Elimination:**
  - **Aqueous KOH** → **Substitution** (alcohol banta hai).
  - **Alcoholic KOH** (stronger base RO⁻) + heat → **Elimination** (alkene banta hai, especially 3° halides mein!).

---

## 4. Reactions with Metals (Grignard Reagent & Wurtz Reaction)

> **Safety Note:** Metallic sodium (Na) paani ke saath violently react karta hai, aur dry ether highly flammable hai — yeh reactions sirf theory/controlled industrial lab mein ki jati hain.

1. **Grignard Reagent (Victor Grignard, Nobel Prize 1912):**
   **R–X + Mg —(dry ether)→ R–Mg–X**
   - Carbon par **δ⁻** charge hota hai (extremely strong base!).
   - **Dry ether kyun zaroori hai?** Kyunki paani (ya alcohol/amine) ki ek boond bhi Grignard reagent ko turant **alkane** mein badal deti hai!
     **R–Mg–X + H₂O → R–H + Mg(OH)X**
2. **Wurtz Reaction (Doubling the Carbon Chain!):**
   **2R–X + 2Na —(dry ether)→ R–R + 2NaX**
   - Symmetrical even-carbon alkanes (jaise CH₃Br → CH₃–CH₃) banane ke liye best hai.

## Solved Examples

### Example 1: Predicting Faster SN2 and SN1 Reactants
From each pair, predict which compound reacts faster and why:
(a) **SN2:** CH₃–CH₂–CH₂–CH₂–Br vs (CH₃)₃C–Br
(b) **SN1:** (CH₃)₃C–Cl vs CH₃–CH₂–Cl
(c) **SN1:** C₆H₅–CH₂–Cl vs CH₃–CH₂–Cl

**Solution:**
(a) **CH₃–CH₂–CH₂–CH₂–Br (1°)** reacts much faster by **SN2** because it has minimal steric hindrance for backside attack, whereas (CH₃)₃C–Br (3°) is heavily crowded.
(b) **(CH₃)₃C–Cl (3°)** reacts much faster by **SN1** because it forms a stable **3° carbocation** (stabilised by +I effect and 9 hyperconjugation H-atoms), whereas 1° carbocation is very unstable.
(c) **C₆H₅–CH₂–Cl (benzyl chloride)** reacts much faster by **SN1** because the benzyl carbocation (C₆H₅–CH₂⁺) is heavily stabilised by **resonance** over the benzene ring!

Jaanch: SN2 = least steric crowding; SN1 = most stable carbocation (resonance > 3° > 2° > 1°) ✅

---

### Example 2: Explaining KCN vs AgCN Products
Haloalkanes react with KCN to form alkyl cyanides as the main product, while with AgCN they form isocyanides as the chief product. Explain.

**Solution:**
- **KCN is predominantly ionic**, releasing free cyanide ions (:C≡N:⁻) in solution. Both C and N can donate electrons (ambident nucleophile), but attack occurs predominantly through **carbon** because the **C–C bond** formed in R–CN is stronger than the C–N bond.
- **AgCN is predominantly covalent**, so the carbon atom is bound to Ag and unavailable for attack. Therefore, the **nitrogen atom** donates its lone pair to form **alkyl isocyanide (R–NC)**.

Jaanch: KCN (ionic) → R–CN; AgCN (covalent) → R–NC ✅

---

### Example 3: Applying Zaitsev Rule
Predict the major alkene formed when 2-bromo-2-methylbutane is heated with alcoholic KOH.

**Solution:**
CH₃–C(Br)(CH₃)–CH₂–CH₃ has two types of β-hydrogens (on C1 and C3).
- Loss of H from C3 gives **2-methylbut-2-ene** (CH₃)₂C=CH–CH₃ (**trisubstituted**, 3 alkyl groups on C=C) → **Major product (Zaitsev)**.
- Loss of H from C1 gives 2-methylbut-1-ene CH₂=C(CH₃)–CH₂–CH₃ (disubstituted) → minor product.

Jaanch: 2-methylbut-2-ene has 9 α-hydrogens (more hyperconjugation = more stable alkene) ✅

## Aam Galtiyan (Common Mistakes)

1. **Aqueous KOH aur Alcoholic KOH ke products mix kar dena:**
   - *Galat:* CH₃CH₂Br + alc. KOH → CH₃CH₂OH.
   - *Sahi:* **Aqueous KOH** → substitution (**CH₃CH₂OH**); **Alcoholic KOH** → elimination (**CH₂=CH₂**)!

2. **Benzyl chloride ko 1° dekh kar SN1 mein slow bol dena:**
   - *Galat:* C₆H₅–CH₂–Cl primary (1°) hai toh SN1 slow hoga.
   - *Sahi:* **Allylic aur benzylic halides** resonance-stabilised carbocation banate hain, isliye woh **SN1 mein bahut fast** hote hain (aur kam crowding ki wajah se SN2 mein bhi fast)!

3. **SN1 mein 100% inversion likh dena:**
   - *Galat:* SN1 mein configuration invert ho jati hai.
   - *Sahi:* **SN2 = Inversion** (backside attack); **SN1 = Racemisation** (planar carbocation par dono sides se attack)!

## Quick Recap

- **BP:** R–I > R–Br > R–Cl > R–F; branching ↓ BP; p-dichlorobenzene has highest MP (symmetry).
- **Ambident:** KCN → R–CN, AgCN → R–NC; KNO₂ → R–ONO, AgNO₂ → R–NO₂.
- **SN2:** 1 step, 2nd order, CH₃X > 1° > 2° > 3°, inversion.
- **SN1:** 2 steps, carbocation, 1st order, 3° > 2° > 1° (plus allylic/benzylic), racemisation.
- **Elimination:** alc. KOH + Δ → Zaitsev (most substituted alkene).
- **Metals:** Grignard (R–Mg–X in dry ether, avoid moisture!); Wurtz (2R–X + 2Na → R–R).

## Practice Questions

1. Arrange 1-bromobutane, 2-bromobutane, and 2-bromo-2-methylpropane in increasing order of boiling point. Give reason.
2. Why does para-dichlorobenzene have a higher melting point than its ortho and meta isomers?
3. Although haloalkanes are polar, why are they nearly insoluble in water?
4. State four points of difference between SN1 and SN2 mechanisms.
5. Which compound in each pair will react faster by SN2 mechanism:
   (a) CH₃–Br or CH₃–I, (b) 1-chloropentane or 2-chloropentane?
6. Which compound in each pair will react faster by SN1 mechanism:
   (a) (CH₃)₃C–Br or (CH₃)₂CH–Br, (b) C₆H₅–CH(Cl)–C₆H₅ or C₆H₅–CH₂–Cl?
7. What happens when bromoethane is treated with: (a) aqueous KOH, (b) alcoholic KOH, (c) AgNO₂?
8. Explain why Grignard reagents must be prepared under strictly anhydrous (moisture-free) conditions.
9. State Zaitsev's rule and predict the major product of treating 2-chlorobutane with alcoholic KOH.
10. Write the Wurtz reaction to prepare n-butane from bromoethane.

---

### Answers

1. Increasing BP: **2-bromo-2-methylpropane < 2-bromobutane < 1-bromobutane**. As branching increases, the molecule becomes more spherical with less surface area, weakening intermolecular van der Waals forces.
2. **para-Dichlorobenzene** is more symmetrical than ortho and meta isomers, allowing its molecules to pack much more closely in the crystal lattice, requiring more heat energy to melt.
3. Because the energy released when haloalkane molecules form new attractions with water is less than the energy required to break existing strong **hydrogen bonds** between water molecules.
4. (i) SN2 is 1-step (2nd order, Rate = k[RX][Nu⁻]); SN1 is 2-step via carbocation (1st order, Rate = k[RX]). (ii) Reactivity: SN2 is 1° > 2° > 3°; SN1 is 3° > 2° > 1°. (iii) Stereochemistry: SN2 gives inversion; SN1 gives racemisation. (iv) Solvent: SN2 favoured by polar aprotic; SN1 by polar protic.
5. (a) **CH₃–I** (I⁻ is a better leaving group due to weaker C–I bond).
   (b) **1-chloropentane** (1°, less sterically hindered than 2°).
6. (a) **(CH₃)₃C–Br** (forms more stable 3° carbocation).
   (b) **C₆H₅–CH(Cl)–C₆H₅** (forms diphenylmethyl carbocation stabilised by resonance over TWO benzene rings vs one in benzyl chloride!).
7. (a) **CH₃CH₂OH** (ethanol, substitution).
   (b) **CH₂=CH₂** (ethene, β-elimination).
   (c) **CH₃CH₂NO₂** (nitroethane, N-attack from covalent AgNO₂).
8. Because Grignard reagents (R–Mg–X) are extremely strong bases that immediately react with even traces of water (or any proton donor) to form hydrocarbons: **R–Mg–X + H₂O → R–H + Mg(OH)X**.
9. **Zaitsev's rule:** In dehydrohalogenation, the preferred (major) alkene is the one with the greater number of alkyl groups attached to the doubly bonded carbon atoms. From 2-chlorobutane + alc. KOH, major product is **but-2-ene** (CH₃–CH=CH–CH₃).
10. **2CH₃–CH₂–Br + 2Na —(dry ether)→ CH₃–CH₂–CH₂–CH₃ (n-butane) + 2NaBr**.
