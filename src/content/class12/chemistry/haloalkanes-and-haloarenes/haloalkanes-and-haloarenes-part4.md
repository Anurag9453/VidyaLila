## Naye Shabd (Key Terms)

| Term | Hinglish Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Plane-Polarised Light (PPL)** | Aisi light jiski waves sirf ek hi plane mein vibrate karti hain (Nicol prism se guzarne par milti hai) |
| **Optical Activity** | Kisi compound ka PPL ke plane ko clockwise (**dextrorotatory, d / +**) ya anticlockwise (**laevorotatory, l / −**) rotate karne ka gun |
| **Chiral (Asymmetric) Carbon** | Woh sp³ carbon atom jisse **4 bilkul alag atoms ya groups** jude hon (e.g., CH₃–CH(Br)–C₂H₅ mein C2) |
| **Enantiomers** | Non-superimposable mirror-image stereoisomers jinke MP, BP, solubility same hote hain par optical rotation opposite (+ aur −) hota hai |
| **Racemic Mixture (dl / ±)** | Do enantiomers ka exact **50:50 equimolar mixture**, jo external compensation ki wajah se **optically inactive (0° rotation)** hota hai |
| **Retention vs Inversion** | Reaction ke baad spatial arrangement wahi rehna = **Retention**; bilkul ulta ho jana = **Inversion**; 50:50 mix ban jana = **Racemisation** |

## 1. Stereochemistry: Chirality & SN1/SN2 Outcomes

- **Condition for optical activity:** Molecule **chiral** (asymmetric — no plane of symmetry, non-superimposable mirror image) hona chahiye.
- Example: **2-Bromobutane** (CH₃–CH(Br)–CH₂CH₃) mein C2 par 4 alag groups (–H, –Br, –CH₃, –C₂H₅) hain → **chiral (optically active)**!
- **Propan-2-ol** (CH₃–CH(OH)–CH₃) mein C2 par do same –CH₃ groups hain → **achiral (optically inactive)**!
- **SN2 on optically active halide:** Backside attack causes **100% inversion** (e.g., (−)-2-bromooctane + OH⁻ → (+)-octan-2-ol).
- **SN1 on optically active halide:** Planar sp² carbocation allows equal front & back attack → **racemisation** (± mixture, optically inactive!).

## 2. Reactions of Haloarenes (Why Are They So Unreactive to SN?)

### Why Haloarenes Resist Nucleophilic Substitution (4 Reasons!):
1. **Resonance Effect:** Halogen ka lone pair benzene ring mein delocalise hota hai → **C–X bond ko partial double-bond character** milta hai (tootna bahut mushkil!).
2. **sp² Hybridisation of Carbon:** Ring carbon sp² (33.3% s-character) hai vs haloalkane ka sp³ (25% s-character) → sp² carbon electrons ko zyada tight pakadta hai → **shorter, stronger C–X bond** (169 pm vs 177 pm).
3. **Instability of Phenyl Cation (C₆H₅⁺):** Resonance se stabilise nahi hota → SN1 impossible!
4. **Electronic Repulsion:** Electron-rich benzene ring aur electron-rich nucleophile (Nu⁻) aapas mein repel karte hain!

### How Haloarenes CAN Undergo Nucleophilic Substitution (Dow's Process & –NO₂ Effect):
- **Chlorobenzene → Phenol (Dow's Process):** Extreme conditions chahiye:
  C₆H₅–Cl + NaOH —(623 K, 300 atm)→ C₆H₅–ONa —(H⁺)→ **C₆H₅–OH (Phenol)**.
- **Effect of Electron-Withdrawing Groups (–NO₂) at ortho & para positions:**
  –NO₂ group ring se electron density khinchta hai aur carbanion intermediate ko resonance-stabilise karta hai, isliye reaction bahut **mild conditions** mein ho jati hai!
  - 4-Nitrochlorobenzene + NaOH: **443 K**
  - 2,4-Dinitrochlorobenzene + NaOH: **368 K**
  - **2,4,6-Trinitrochlorobenzene** + warm H₂O: **Warm water hi kaafi hai** → **Picric acid**!
  - *(Note: **meta-NO₂** ka koi resonance effect nahi hota, isliye meta-nitrochlorobenzene ki reactivity lagbhag nahi badhti!)*

### Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution (EAS) of Haloarenes:
- Halogens **ortho- and para-directing** hain (+R resonance effect o/p par electron density badhata hai), par **overall deactivating** hain (strong −I inductive effect ring se electrons khinchta hai)!
- **Chlorobenzene ke 4 classic EAS reactions (para product is MAJOR due to less steric hindrance):**
  1. **Halogenation:** + Cl₂ (anhyd. FeCl₃) → **1,4-Dichlorobenzene (major)** + 1,2-dichlorobenzene.
  2. **Nitration:** + conc. HNO₃ / conc. H₂SO₄ → **1-Chloro-4-nitrobenzene (major)** + 1-chloro-2-nitrobenzene.
  3. **Sulphonation:** + conc. H₂SO₄ (Δ) → **4-Chlorobenzenesulphonic acid (major)** + 2-isomer.
  4. **Friedel–Crafts Alkylation / Acylation:** + CH₃Cl (anhyd. AlCl₃) → **1-Chloro-4-methylbenzene (major)**; + CH₃COCl (anhyd. AlCl₃) → **4-Chloroacetophenone (major)**.

### Reactions with Metals (Wurtz–Fittig & Fittig):
- **Wurtz–Fittig Reaction (Aryl halide + Alkyl halide + 2Na / dry ether):**
  C₆H₅–Br + CH₃–Br + 2Na —(dry ether)→ **C₆H₅–CH₃ (Toluene)** + 2NaBr.
- **Fittig Reaction (2 Aryl halides + 2Na / dry ether):**
  2C₆H₅–Cl + 2Na —(dry ether)→ **C₆H₅–C₆H₅ (Diphenyl / Biphenyl)** + 2NaCl.

## 3. Polyhalogen Compounds (Uses & Environmental Hazards)

> **Safety Note:** CHCl₃, CCl₄, aur DDT toxic/hazardous chemicals hain; inke sirf theoretical properties, safe storage rules aur environmental impacts padhne hain.

| Compound | Formula | Key Uses & Important Exam Facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Dichloromethane** | CH₂Cl₂ | Paint remover, solvent, aerosol propellant; harms central nervous system |
| **Chloroform** | CHCl₃ | Solvent; slowly oxidised by **air + sunlight** to poisonous **Phosgene gas (COCl₂)**: 2CHCl₃ + O₂ —(light)→ 2COCl₂ + 2HCl. **Stored in completely filled, dark brown bottles** (no air, no light!) |
| **Iodoform** | CHI₃ | Yellow solid; antiseptic (due to **liberation of free I₂**, NOT CHI₃ itself!) |
| **Carbon tetrachloride** | CCl₄ | Non-flammable solvent & refrigerant feedstock; causes liver cancer & **ozone depletion** |
| **Freons (CFCs)** | CCl₂F₂ (Freon-12) | Refrigerants & aerosol propellants; reach stratosphere and generate Cl• free radicals that **destroy the ozone (O₃) layer**! |
| **DDT** | p,p'-Dichlorodiphenyltrichloroethane | Powerful insecticide; **non-biodegradable**, accumulates in fatty tissues of animals (biomagnification) → banned in many countries |

## Solved Examples

### Example 1: Identifying Chiral vs Achiral Molecules
Which of the following compounds are chiral (optically active)?
(a) 2-Chlorobutane   (b) 1-Chlorobutane   (c) 2-Bromo-2-methylpropane   (d) 2,3-Dichloropentane

**Solution:**
- (a) CH₃–CH*(Cl)–CH₂CH₃: C2 has 4 different groups (H, Cl, CH₃, C₂H₅) → **Chiral (optically active)**.
- (b) CH₃–CH₂–CH₂–CH₂Cl: No carbon has 4 different groups → **Achiral**.
- (c) (CH₃)₃C–Br: C2 has three identical CH₃ groups → **Achiral**.
- (d) CH₃–CH*(Cl)–CH*(Cl)–CH₂CH₃: Both C2 and C3 are chiral centres and the molecule has no plane of symmetry → **Chiral (optically active)**.

Jaanch: Only molecules with at least one asymmetric carbon and no symmetry plane are chiral ✅

---

### Example 2: Why Chloroform Is Stored in Dark Filled Bottles
Why is chloroform (CHCl₃) stored in dark-coloured bottles completely filled to the brim? Write the relevant chemical equation.

**Solution:**
In the presence of **light and atmospheric oxygen (air)**, chloroform slowly oxidises to an extremely poisonous gas called **carbonyl chloride (phosgene, COCl₂)**:
**2CHCl₃ + O₂ —(light)→ 2COCl₂ (Phosgene) + 2HCl**
Dark bottles block sunlight, and filling to the brim leaves no air space above the liquid, preventing phosgene formation. (Often 1% ethanol is also added to convert any trace phosgene into harmless diethyl carbonate!)

Jaanch: Atoms balanced: 2C, 2H, 6Cl, 2O on both sides ✅

---

### Example 3: Explaining Why Chlorine Is o,p-Directing Yet Deactivating
In chlorobenzene, the –Cl group is deactivating for electrophilic substitution, yet it directs incoming electrophiles to ortho and para positions. Why?

**Solution:**
- **Reactivity (controlled by stronger −I effect):** Chlorine is strongly electronegative and withdraws electrons from the ring via the inductive effect (−I > +R), destabilising the carbocation intermediate and slowing down substitution (**deactivating**).
- **Orientation (controlled by +R resonance effect):** Chlorine's lone pair delocalises into the ring, placing extra negative charge specifically at the **ortho and para** positions, so incoming electrophiles attack preferentially at o/p positions (**o,p-directing**).

Jaanch: Reactivity = −I wins; Orientation = +R wins ✅

## Aam Galtiyan (Common Mistakes)

1. **meta-Nitrochlorobenzene ko bhi NaOH ke saath fast react karwa dena:**
   - *Galat:* Kisi bhi position par –NO₂ lagane se chlorobenzene ka hydrolysis aasan ho jata hai.
   - *Sahi:* Sirf **ortho aur para –NO₂** carbanion ka negative charge resonance se stabilise karte hain! **meta-NO₂** par negative charge kabhi nahi aata, isliye koi khaas asar nahi hota!

2. **Wurtz, Wurtz–Fittig aur Fittig reactions mix kar dena:**
   - *Galat:* 2C₆H₅Cl + 2Na ko Wurtz–Fittig bol dena.
   - *Sahi:* **Wurtz** = alkyl + alkyl; **Wurtz–Fittig** = aryl + alkyl (gives alkylbenzene); **Fittig** = aryl + aryl (gives diphenyl)!

3. **Iodoform ki antiseptic property khud CHI₃ ki wajah se bol dena:**
   - *Galat:* CHI₃ khud bacteria maarta hai.
   - *Sahi:* Antiseptic action **free iodine (I₂)** ke release hone ki wajah se hota hai, CHI₃ ke khud ke kaaran nahi!

## Quick Recap

- **Chirality:** 4 different groups on sp³ C, non-superimposable mirror images (enantiomers). 50:50 dl mix = racemic (optically inactive).
- **Haloarenes resist SN:** Resonance (partial C=X), sp² carbon, unstable phenyl cation, electronic repulsion. Activated by **o/p –NO₂** groups.
- **Haloarene EAS:** o,p-directing (+R), deactivating (−I); para is major.
- **Metal reactions:** Wurtz–Fittig (Ar–X + R–X → Ar–R); Fittig (2Ar–X → Ar–Ar).
- **Polyhalogens:** CHCl₃ → phosgene COCl₂ (dark filled bottles); CHI₃ (antiseptic via free I₂); Freons/CCl₄ (ozone depletion); DDT (non-biodegradable).

---

### Answers

1. (a) **Chirality:** Property of a molecule being non-superimposable on its mirror image. (b) **Enantiomers:** Non-superimposable mirror-image stereoisomers rotating PPL equally in opposite directions. (c) **Racemic mixture:** Equimolar (50:50) mixture of d and l enantiomers, optically inactive due to external compensation.
2. Chiral compounds: **2-bromobutane** (C2 has H, Br, CH₃, C₂H₅) and **2-chloropentane** (C2 has H, Cl, CH₃, C₃H₇). (2-bromopropane and 1-bromo-2-methylpropane have two identical CH₃ groups → achiral.)
3. (i) **Resonance** imparts partial double-bond character to C–X; (ii) **sp² carbon** holds C–X electrons more tightly (shorter, stronger bond); (iii) **instability of phenyl cation** prevents SN1; (iv) repulsion between electron-rich ring and nucleophile.
4. During nucleophilic attack on chlorobenzene, the negative charge in the intermediate carbanion delocalises strictly onto the **ortho and para** carbons. An –NO₂ group at o/p withdraws this negative charge by resonance, strongly stabilising the intermediate; at meta, no resonance stabilisation occurs.
5. (a) **Wurtz–Fittig:** C₆H₅–Br + CH₃–Br + 2Na —(dry ether)→ **C₆H₅–CH₃ (toluene)** + 2NaBr.
   (b) **Fittig:** 2C₆H₅–Cl + 2Na —(dry ether)→ **C₆H₅–C₆H₅ (diphenyl)** + 2NaCl.
6. (a) **4-Chloroacetophenone (major)** + 2-chloroacetophenone.
   (b) **1-Chloro-4-nitrobenzene (major)** + 1-chloro-2-nitrobenzene.
7. Reactivity is governed by the stronger **−I effect** (deactivating), while orientation is governed by the **+R resonance effect** which increases electron density at ortho and para positions (**o,p-directing**).
8. To prevent slow oxidation by air and sunlight into the highly toxic gas **phosgene (COCl₂)**: 2CHCl₃ + O₂ —(light)→ 2COCl₂ + 2HCl.
9. Because on contact with wounds/skin, iodoform slowly **liberates free iodine (I₂)**, which is the actual antiseptic agent.
10. **Freon-12 (CCl₂F₂):** Initiates Cl• radical chain reactions in the stratosphere that **deplete the ozone (O₃) layer**. **DDT:** **Non-biodegradable**, accumulates in animal fatty tissues (biomagnification) causing severe ecological toxicity.
