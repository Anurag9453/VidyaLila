---

Ajit apne doston ke saath organic chemistry ka "Conversion Relay" khel raha tha. Rule simple tha: start molecule se target molecule tak kam se kam steps mein pahunchna! Sawal aaya: "Propene se Propan-1-ol banao!" Ajit ne do steps mein game jeet liya: Step 1 — HBr + peroxide (anti-Markovnikov) se 1-bromopropane; Step 2 — aqueous KOH (SN2) se Propan-1-ol! Yahi hai organic synthesis ka maza — ek reaction ka product agle ka reactant ban jata hai!

---

## Naye Shabd (Conversion Toolkit)

| Goal | Best Reagent / Named Reaction |
|---|---|
| R–OH → R–Cl (cleanest) | **SOCl₂** (Darzen's method) |
| R–Cl / R–Br → R–I | **NaI / dry acetone** (Finkelstein) |
| R–Cl / R–Br → R–F | **AgF / Hg₂F₂** (Swarts) |
| Step-up chain by +1 Carbon | **KCN** (R–X → R–CN) |
| Double the carbon chain | **Na / dry ether** (Wurtz / Fittig / Wurtz–Fittig) |
| Alkene (Zaitsev) from R–X | **Alcoholic KOH + Δ** |
| Alcohol from R–X | **Aqueous KOH** |
| Aniline → Ar–Cl / Ar–Br / Ar–I | **NaNO₂ + HCl (0–5°C)** → **Cu₂Cl₂ / Cu₂Br₂ / KI** |

## Mixed Solved Examples

### Example 1: Classic Multi-Step Organic Conversions
Carry out the following conversions (in at most 2–3 steps):
(a) 1-Bromopropane → 2-Bromopropane
(b) Ethanol → But-1-yne
(c) Chlorobenzene → p-Nitrophenol

**Solution:**
(a) **1-Bromopropane → 2-Bromopropane:**
- Step 1 (Elimination): CH₃–CH₂–CH₂–Br + alc. KOH (Δ) → CH₃–CH=CH₂ (propene)
- Step 2 (Markovnikov addition): CH₃–CH=CH₂ + HBr → **CH₃–CH(Br)–CH₃ (2-bromopropane)**

(b) **Ethanol → But-1-yne:**
- Step 1: CH₃CH₂OH + SOCl₂ → CH₃CH₂Cl (chloroethane)
- Step 2 (SN2 with sodium acetylide): CH₃CH₂Cl + Na⁺ ⁻C≡CH → **CH₃–CH₂–C≡CH (but-1-yne)** + NaCl

(c) **Chlorobenzene → p-Nitrophenol:**
- Step 1 (Nitration): C₆H₅–Cl + conc. HNO₃ / conc. H₂SO₄ → p-O₂N–C₆H₄–Cl (major)
- Step 2 (Hydrolysis — mild because of p-NO₂!): p-O₂N–C₆H₄–Cl + NaOH (443 K), then H⁺ → **p-O₂N–C₆H₄–OH (p-nitrophenol)**

Jaanch: Every step uses high-yield standard reagents with correct regioselectivity ✅

---

### Example 2: Chemical Distinction Test (Alkyl vs Aryl Halide)
How will you chemically distinguish between **benzyl chloride (C₆H₅–CH₂–Cl)** and **chlorobenzene (C₆H₅–Cl)** using a simple test?

**Solution:**
Warm both compounds separately with **aqueous KOH**, acidify with dilute HNO₃, and add **AgNO₃ solution**:
- **Benzyl chloride (C₆H₅–CH₂–Cl):** Undergoes rapid nucleophilic substitution (resonance-stabilised benzyl carbocation) releasing Cl⁻ ions → gives an immediate **white precipitate of AgCl**.
- **Chlorobenzene (C₆H₅–Cl):** C–Cl bond has partial double-bond character (resonance) and does not hydrolyse under mild conditions → **NO precipitate** with AgNO₃!

Jaanch: Clear visible white AgCl ppt vs no ppt distinguishes benzylic from aryl halide ✅

### Example 3: Identifying Unknown Compound A, B, C
An optically active alkyl halide **A** (C₄H₉Br) reacts with aqueous KOH to form an alcohol **B** with inversion of configuration. When **A** is treated with Mg in dry ether and then water, it gives n-butane (**C**). Identify A, B, and C.

**Solution:**
- C₄H₉Br giving n-butane via Grignard + H₂O must have an unbranched 4-carbon chain (either 1-bromobutane or 2-bromobutane).
- Since **A** is **optically active** (chiral), it must be **2-bromobutane (CH₃–CH(Br)–CH₂CH₃)**!
- SN2 with aqueous KOH (inversion) gives **B = butan-2-ol (CH₃–CH(OH)–CH₂CH₃)**.
- Mg/dry ether → CH₃–CH(MgBr)–CH₂CH₃ —(H₂O)→ **C = n-butane (CH₃–CH₂–CH₂–CH₃)**.

Jaanch: 2-bromobutane is the ONLY chiral isomer of C₄H₉Br; C₄H₉Br → C₄H₉OH → C₄H₁₀ ✅

## Aam Galtiyan (Mixed-Conversion Traps)

1. **Direct haloalkane mein carbon chain +1 badhane ke liye Wurtz laga dena:**
   - *Galat:* CH₃Br se CH₃CH₂CN ya odd-carbon alkane banane mein Wurtz use karna.
   - *Sahi:* Wurtz hamesha **even/double** carbon chain deta hai (mixed Wurtz se 3 alkanes ka messy mixture banta hai!). Chain +1 badhane ke liye **KCN** ya **Grignard** use karo!

2. **2-Bromopropane se 1-Bromopropane banate waqt peroxide bhool jana:**
   - *Galat:* Propene + HBr → 1-bromopropane.
   - *Sahi:* Bina peroxide ke Markovnikov product (2-bromopropane) banega! 1-bromopropane ke liye **HBr + peroxide** likhna zaroori hai!

---

## Quick Recap

- **Shifting halogen position:** 1-halo → (alc. KOH) → alkene → (HBr) → 2-halo; reverse uses **HBr + peroxide**!
- **Distinguishing R–Cl / ArCH₂Cl vs Ar–Cl:** Warm aq. KOH + dil. HNO₃ + AgNO₃ → white AgCl ppt ONLY for alkyl/benzylic/allylic chlorides, NOT aryl/vinylic chlorides!
- **Ring activation trick:** Nitrate chlorobenzene FIRST, then hydrolyse with NaOH at 443 K (much easier than Dow's 623 K!).

## Practice Questions

1. Convert: (a) Propene to propan-1-ol, (b) Bromoethane to propanenitrile (CH₃CH₂CN).
2. Convert: (a) Aniline to chlorobenzene, (b) Chlorobenzene to biphenyl (diphenyl).
3. Convert: (a) 2-Chloropropane to 1-propanol, (b) Benzene to 4-bromonitrobenzene.
4. How will you distinguish chemically between: (a) Chloroethane and chlorobenzene, (b) Vinyl chloride (CH₂=CHCl) and allyl chloride (CH₂=CH–CH₂Cl)?
5. Arrange the following in increasing order of reactivity towards SN1:
   C₆H₅–CH₂–Br, C₆H₅–CH(C₆H₅)–Br, CH₃–CH₂–Br, (CH₃)₃C–Br.
6. Arrange the following in increasing order of reactivity towards SN2:
   1-Bromobutane, 2-Bromobutane, 2-Bromo-2-methylpropane, Bromomethane.
7. An alkyl halide **X** (C₃H₇Br) on treatment with alcoholic KOH gives an alkene **Y** (C₃H₆). **Y** reacts with HBr to give an isomer **Z** of **X**. When **X** is treated with Na in dry ether, it gives n-hexane. Identify X, Y, and Z.
8. Explain why p-nitrochlorobenzene undergoes hydrolysis with aqueous NaOH much more easily than chlorobenzene.
9. Predict the major product when 1-bromo-1-methylcyclohexane is heated with alcoholic KOH.
10. What happens when methyl bromide is treated with: (a) KCN, (b) AgCN, (c) NaI in dry acetone, (d) AgF?

---

### Answers

1. (a) CH₃–CH=CH₂ + HBr (peroxide) → CH₃CH₂CH₂Br —(aq. KOH)→ **CH₃CH₂CH₂OH**.
   (b) CH₃CH₂Br + KCN (alc.) → **CH₃CH₂CN** + KBr.
2. (a) C₆H₅NH₂ + NaNO₂/HCl (0–5°C) → C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ —(Cu₂Cl₂/HCl)→ **C₆H₅Cl**.
   (b) 2C₆H₅Cl + 2Na —(dry ether, Fittig)→ **C₆H₅–C₆H₅** + 2NaCl.
3. (a) CH₃CH(Cl)CH₃ —(alc. KOH, Δ)→ CH₃CH=CH₂ —(HBr, peroxide)→ CH₃CH₂CH₂Br —(aq. KOH)→ **CH₃CH₂CH₂OH**.
   (b) Benzene + Br₂ (FeBr₃) → Bromobenzene —(conc. HNO₃/H₂SO₄)→ **4-Bromonitrobenzene** (major; brominate FIRST because –Br is o,p-directing whereas –NO₂ is m-directing!).
   Jaanch: Order of steps in (b) is critical — nitrating first would give meta isomer! ✅
4. Warm with aq. KOH, acidify with dil. HNO₃, add AgNO₃:
   (a) **Chloroethane** gives white AgCl ppt; **chlorobenzene** gives NO ppt.
   (b) **Allyl chloride** (sp³ C–Cl, resonance-stabilised allyl cation) gives white AgCl ppt; **vinyl chloride** (sp² C–Cl, resonance partial double bond) gives NO ppt.
5. Increasing SN1 reactivity (carbocation stability):
   **CH₃–CH₂–Br (1°) < C₆H₅–CH₂–Br (1° benzylic) < (CH₃)₃C–Br (3°) < C₆H₅–CH(C₆H₅)–Br (diphenylmethyl)**.
   *(Note: Both benzyl and 3° are very fast; diphenylmethyl with 2 rings is fastest!)*
6. Increasing SN2 reactivity (least steric hindrance fastest):
   **2-Bromo-2-methylpropane (3°) < 2-Bromobutane (2°) < 1-Bromobutane (1°) < Bromomethane (CH₃Br)**.
7. Wurtz reaction of **X** (C₃H₇Br) gives **n-hexane** (straight 6-carbon chain) → **X = 1-bromopropane (CH₃CH₂CH₂Br)**.
   X + alc. KOH → **Y = propene (CH₃–CH=CH₂)**.
   Y + HBr (Markovnikov) → **Z = 2-bromopropane (CH₃–CH(Br)–CH₃)** (isomer of X!).
   Jaanch: 2 × 1-bromopropane + 2Na → n-hexane; 2-bromopropane would give 2,3-dimethylbutane ✅
8. Because the electron-withdrawing **–NO₂ group at the para position** stabilises the negatively charged carbanion intermediate by resonance, lowering the activation energy for nucleophilic substitution.
9. By Zaitsev's rule, elimination yields the more substituted endocyclic alkene: **1-methylcyclohexene** (trisubstituted C=C) as the major product.
10. (a) **CH₃CN** (ethanenitrile / methyl cyanide)
    (b) **CH₃NC** (methyl isocyanide)
    (c) **CH₃I** (iodomethane, Finkelstein)
    (d) **CH₃F** (fluoromethane, Swarts)

## Chapter Ka Saar (Haloalkanes and Haloarenes Wrap-up)

| Topic | Key Reactions / Rules | Must-Remember Fact |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Classification & C–X Bond | sp³ (alkyl, allylic, benzylic) vs sp² (vinylic, aryl) | Dipole moment: **CH₃Cl > CH₃F** > CH₃Br > CH₃I; Bond strength: C–F > C–Cl > C–Br > C–I |
| 2. Preparation | SOCl₂ (Darzen), NaI/acetone (Finkelstein), AgF (Swarts), Sandmeyer | SOCl₂ gives gaseous SO₂+HCl; KI needs H₃PO₄ (not H₂SO₄); Diazotisation at 0–5°C |
| 3. SN2, SN1 & Elimination | SN2 (1° > 2° > 3°, inversion) vs SN1 (3° > 2° > 1°, racemisation) | KCN→RCN, AgCN→RNC; alc. KOH → Zaitsev alkene; Grignard needs dry ether |
| 4. Chirality & Haloarenes | Chiral C (4 diff groups); Haloarene EAS = o,p-directing, deactivating | Haloarenes resist SN (resonance + sp² C); o/p –NO₂ activates SN; Wurtz–Fittig & Fittig |
| 5. Polyhalogens | CHCl₃, CHI₃, CCl₄, Freons, DDT | CHCl₃ + O₂/light → phosgene COCl₂ (dark filled bottles); Freons destroy ozone |
