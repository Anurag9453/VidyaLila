# Classification, Nomenclature and Nature of the C–X Bond

---

Tulasi ne ghar ke medical box aur kitchen mein dekha ki halogen-containing organic compounds har jagah hain: typhoid ke ilaj mein use hone wala **chloramphenicol**, malaria ki dawai **chloroquine**, surgery ka anaesthetic **halothane**, aur non-stick pans ki coating **Teflon**! Jab hydrocarbon mein ek ya zyada hydrogen atoms ko halogen (F, Cl, Br, I) se replace kar diya jata hai, toh molecule ki reactivity aur properties bilkul badal jati hain!

---

## Naye Shabd (Key Terms)

| Term | Hinglish Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Haloalkane (Alkyl Halide, R–X)** | Aliphatic hydrocarbon ka halogen derivative jismein X atom sp³-hybridised carbon se juda hota hai |
| **Haloarene (Aryl Halide, Ar–X)** | Aromatic ring (benzene) ka halogen derivative jismein X atom seedha ring ke sp²-hybridised carbon se juda hota hai |
| **Allylic Halide** | Woh halide jismein X atom aise sp³ carbon se juda ho jo C=C double bond ke **theek bagal** mein ho (CH₂=CH–CH₂–X) |
| **Benzylic Halide** | Woh halide jismein X atom aise sp³ carbon se juda ho jo benzene ring se directly juda ho (C₆H₅–CH₂–X) |
| **Vinylic Halide** | Woh halide jismein X atom seedha C=C double bond ke **sp² carbon** se juda ho (CH₂=CH–X) |
| **Geminal (gem-) Dihalide** | Do halogen atoms **ek hi carbon** par jude hon (e.g., CH₃–CHCl₂, ethylidene chloride) |
| **Vicinal (vic-) Dihalide** | Do halogen atoms **bagal-bagal (adjacent) carbons** par jude hon (e.g., Cl–CH₂–CH₂–Cl, ethylene dichloride) |

## 1. Classification of Haloalkanes and Haloarenes

### (a) On the Basis of Number of Halogen Atoms
- **Monohalo:** CH₃–Cl, C₆H₅–Br (1 halogen)
- **Dihalo:** CH₂Cl–CH₂Cl (vicinal), CH₃–CHCl₂ (geminal) (2 halogens)
- **Trihalo / Polyhalo:** CHCl₃ (chloroform), CHI₃ (iodoform), CCl₄ (3 or more halogens)

### (b) Compounds Containing sp³ C–X Bond
1. **Alkyl Halides (1°, 2°, 3°):**
   - **Primary (1°):** X us carbon par jisse max 1 aur carbon juda ho (CH₃–CH₂–Br).
   - **Secondary (2°):** X us carbon par jisse 2 carbons jude hon (CH₃–CH(Br)–CH₃).
   - **Tertiary (3°):** X us carbon par jisse 3 carbons jude hon ((CH₃)₃C–Br).
2. **Allylic Halides:** X bonded to sp³ carbon adjacent to C=C (CH₂=CH–CH₂–Cl).
3. **Benzylic Halides:** X bonded to sp³ carbon attached to aromatic ring (C₆H₅–CH₂–Cl, benzyl chloride).

### (c) Compounds Containing sp² C–X Bond
1. **Vinylic Halides:** X directly bonded to C=C sp² carbon (CH₂=CH–Cl, vinyl chloride).
2. **Aryl Halides (Haloarenes):** X directly bonded to benzene ring sp² carbon (C₆H₅–Cl, chlorobenzene).

## 2. IUPAC Nomenclature

- Halogens ko hamesha **prefix** ke roop mein likhte hain: **fluoro-, chloro-, bromo-, iodo-** (no special suffix!).
- Longest carbon chain choose karo aur numbering udhar se karo jahan substituents ko **lowest locant set** mile.
- Agar double/triple bond hai, toh **double/triple bond ko halogen se zyada priority** milti hai!
- Multiple halogens ko **alphabetical order** mein likho (bromo before chloro before fluoro before iodo).
- **Important Common vs IUPAC names:**
  - CH₂Cl₂: Methylene chloride → **Dichloromethane**
  - CHCl₃: Chloroform → **Trichloromethane**
  - CH₃–CHCl₂: Ethylidene chloride (gem) → **1,1-Dichloroethane**
  - Cl–CH₂–CH₂–Cl: Ethylene dichloride (vic) → **1,2-Dichloroethane**
  - (CH₃)₃C–CH₂–Br: Neopentyl bromide → **1-Bromo-2,2-dimethylpropane**
  - C₆H₅–CH₂–Cl: Benzyl chloride → **Chlorophenylmethane**

## 3. Nature of the C–X Bond

Halogen (X) carbon se zyada **electronegative** hota hai, isliye C–X bond **polar covalent** hota hai:
**C^(δ+) — X^(δ−)**

Jaise-jaise group 17 mein neeche jate hain (**F → Cl → Br → I**), halogen atom ka size badhta hai:

| Bond | Bond Length (pm) | Bond Enthalpy (kJ mol⁻¹) | Dipole Moment of CH₃–X (Debye) |
|---|---|---|---|
| C–F | 139 (shortest) | 452 (strongest) | 1.847 |
| **C–Cl** | 178 | 351 | **1.860 (Highest!)** |
| C–Br | 193 | 293 | 1.830 |
| C–I | 214 (longest) | 234 (weakest) | 1.636 |

### Two Crucial Trends to Remember:
1. **Reactivity order (C–X bond cleavage):** **R–I > R–Br > R–Cl > R–F** (C–I sabse lamba aur kamzor bond hai, isliye sabse aasani se toot-ta hai!).
2. **Dipole moment exception (CH₃Cl > CH₃F):** Halanki F, Cl se zyada electronegative hai, par **C–F bond length bahut chhoti (139 pm)** hoti hai aur C–Cl bond length kaafi badi (178 pm). Kyunki dipole moment μ = q × d (charge × distance), C–Cl ka product q × d thoda zyada nikalta hai (**1.860 D > 1.847 D**)!

## Solved Examples

### Example 1: Classifying Halides
Classify each of the following as alkyl (1°/2°/3°), allylic, benzylic, vinylic, or aryl halide:
(a) CH₃–CH=CH–CH₂–Br
(b) C₆H₅–CH(Cl)–CH₃
(c) CH₂=CH–Br
(d) p-CH₃–C₆H₄–Cl

**Solution:**
(a) Br is on an sp³ carbon immediately adjacent to C=C → **Allylic halide (1°)**.
(b) Cl is on an sp³ carbon directly attached to the benzene ring → **Benzylic halide (2°)**.
(c) Br is directly attached to an sp² carbon of C=C → **Vinylic halide**.
(d) Cl is directly attached to an sp² carbon of the benzene ring → **Aryl halide (haloarene)**.

Jaanch: Hybridisation of C–X carbon: sp³ in (a) & (b); sp² in (c) & (d) ✅

---

### Example 2: Writing IUPAC Names
Write the IUPAC names of:
(a) CH₃–CH(Cl)–CH=CH₂
(b) (CH₃)₂C(Br)–CH₂–CH₃
(c) Cl–CH₂–C≡C–CH₂–Br

**Solution:**
(a) Double bond gets priority over Cl → number from right: C1=C2–C3(Cl)–C4 → **3-Chlorobut-1-ene**.
(b) Longest chain = 4 carbons (butane); Br and methyl at C2 → **2-Bromo-2-methylbutane**.
(c) Symmetrical triple bond at C2; tie broken alphabetically (bromo before chloro) → number from right → **1-Bromo-4-chlorobut-2-yne**.

Jaanch: Lowest locant for multiple bond in (a) (C1), lowest substituent locant in (b) (2,2), alphabetical tie-breaker in (c) (1-bromo) ✅

---

### Example 3: Explaining Dipole Moment Order
Arrange CH₃F, CH₃Cl, CH₃Br, and CH₃I in decreasing order of dipole moment. Why is CH₃Cl higher than CH₃F?

**Solution:**
Decreasing order: **CH₃Cl (1.86 D) > CH₃F (1.85 D) > CH₃Br (1.83 D) > CH₃I (1.64 D)**.
Although fluorine is more electronegative than chlorine, dipole moment is the product of charge and bond distance (μ = q × d). The much shorter C–F bond length (139 pm) compared to C–Cl (178 pm) outweighs fluorine's higher electronegativity, making μ(CH₃Cl) slightly greater than μ(CH₃F).

Jaanch: C–Cl length (178 pm) is 28% longer than C–F (139 pm) ✅

## Aam Galtiyan (Common Mistakes)

1. **Benzyl chloride ko aryl halide bol dena:**
   - *Galat:* C₆H₅–CH₂–Cl mein benzene ring hai toh yeh aryl halide hai.
   - *Sahi:* Cl benzene ring par nahi, side-chain ke **sp³ –CH₂– carbon** par juda hai → yeh **benzylic halide** hai! Aryl halide sirf **C₆H₅–Cl** (chlorobenzene) hai.

2. **Double bond wale haloalkene mein halogen ko priority de dena:**
   - *Galat:* CH₂=CH–CH₂–Br ko 1-bromoprop-2-ene likhna.
   - *Sahi:* C=C double bond ko hamesha halogen se upar priority milti hai → **3-Bromoprop-1-ene**!

3. **Dipole moment mein CH₃F ko sabse aage rakh dena:**
   - *Galat:* F sabse electronegative hai toh CH₃F ka μ sabse zyada hoga.
   - *Sahi:* **CH₃Cl > CH₃F > CH₃Br > CH₃I** (bond length factor!).

## Quick Recap

- **sp³ C–X:** Alkyl (1°, 2°, 3°), Allylic (C=C–C–X), Benzylic (Ar–C–X).
- **sp² C–X:** Vinylic (C=C–X), Aryl (Ar–X).
- **Dihalides:** gem-dihalide (same C), vic-dihalide (adjacent C).
- **C–X Bond:** Polar (C^δ+–X^δ−). Bond length: C–I > C–Br > C–Cl > C–F. Bond strength: C–F > C–Cl > C–Br > C–I. Dipole moment: **CH₃Cl > CH₃F > CH₃Br > CH₃I**.

## Practice Questions

1. Distinguish between allylic halides and vinylic halides with one example each.
2. What is the difference between a gem-dihalide and a vic-dihalide? Give the IUPAC name of ethylidene chloride and ethylene dichloride.
3. Classify the following as 1°, 2°, 3°, allylic, benzylic, or aryl halides:
   (a) (CH₃)₃C–Cl, (b) C₆H₅–Br, (c) CH₂=CH–CH(Cl)–CH₃, (d) C₆H₅–CH₂–I.
4. Write the IUPAC names of:
   (a) CH₃–CH₂–CH(Br)–CH₂–CH₃
   (b) CH₂=CH–CH₂–Cl
   (c) (CH₃)₃C–CH₂–Br
5. Write the structures of:
   (a) 2-Chloro-3-methylpentane
   (b) 1,4-Dibromobut-2-ene
   (c) 1-Bromo-4-sec-butyl-2-methylbenzene
6. Why is the C–X bond in haloalkanes polar in nature?
7. Arrange C–F, C–Cl, C–Br, and C–I bonds in increasing order of bond enthalpy. Which alkyl halide reacts fastest when C–X bond breaks?
8. Explain why chloromethane (CH₃Cl) has a higher dipole moment than fluoromethane (CH₃F).
9. Why is chlorobenzene's C–Cl bond shorter (169 pm) than that of chloromethane (178 pm)?
10. Write all structural isomers of molecular formula C₄H₉Br and give their IUPAC names.

---

### Answers

1. In **allylic halides**, halogen is bonded to an **sp³ carbon** adjacent to a C=C double bond (e.g., CH₂=CH–CH₂–Cl). In **vinylic halides**, halogen is directly bonded to an **sp² carbon** of the C=C double bond (e.g., CH₂=CH–Cl).
2. **gem-Dihalide** has both halogens on the **same carbon**; **vic-dihalide** has halogens on **adjacent carbons**.
   - Ethylidene chloride (CH₃–CHCl₂) = **1,1-Dichloroethane**.
   - Ethylene dichloride (Cl–CH₂–CH₂–Cl) = **1,2-Dichloroethane**.
3. (a) **3° alkyl halide**, (b) **aryl halide**, (c) **2° allylic halide**, (d) **1° benzylic halide**.
4. (a) **3-Bromopentane**
   (b) **3-Chloroprop-1-ene**
   (c) **1-Bromo-2,2-dimethylpropane**
5. (a) CH₃–CH(Cl)–CH(CH₃)–CH₂–CH₃
   (b) Br–CH₂–CH=CH–CH₂–Br
   (c) Benzene ring with –Br at C1, –CH₃ at C2, and –CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₃ at C4.
6. Because halogens are more electronegative than carbon, they pull the shared bonding electron pair toward themselves, creating partial negative charge (δ⁻) on X and partial positive charge (δ⁺) on C.
7. Increasing bond enthalpy: **C–I < C–Br < C–Cl < C–F**. **Alkyl iodides (R–I)** react fastest because C–I has the lowest bond enthalpy (weakest bond).
8. Because dipole moment μ = q × d, and the C–Cl bond (178 pm) is significantly longer than C–F (139 pm), so the larger distance factor in CH₃Cl outweighs fluorine's higher electronegativity.
9. Two reasons: (i) Carbon in chlorobenzene is **sp² hybridised** (higher s-character = shorter bond) vs sp³ in CH₃Cl; (ii) **resonance** delocalises Cl's lone pair into the benzene ring, giving the C–Cl bond partial double-bond character.
10. Four structural isomers of C₄H₉Br:
    1. CH₃–CH₂–CH₂–CH₂–Br: **1-Bromobutane** (1°)
    2. CH₃–CH₂–CH(Br)–CH₃: **2-Bromobutane** (2°)
    3. (CH₃)₂CH–CH₂–Br: **1-Bromo-2-methylpropane** (1°)
    4. (CH₃)₃C–Br: **2-Bromo-2-methylpropane** (3°)
    Jaanch: All 4 isomers have formula C₄H₉Br (4 carbons, 9 hydrogens, 1 bromine) ✅
