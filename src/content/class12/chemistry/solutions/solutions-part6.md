## Decision Map: Kaunsa Formula Kab Lagayein?

| Sawaal mein kya dikhe? | Kaunsa tool chuno? | Key Formula |
|---|---|---|
| Density d (g/mL) + Molarity M → Molality m | Exact M ↔ m bridge | **m = (1000 M) / (1000 d − M × M_B)** |
| Gas dissolved in liquid at pressure p | Henry's Law | **p = K_H x** (higher K_H → lower solubility!) |
| Two volatile liquids mixed | Raoult's Law + Deviations | P = p_A° x_A + p_B° x_B; y_A = p_A / P_total |
| Non-volatile solute (check electrolyte!) | Colligative + van't Hoff factor i | RLVP = i x_B; ΔT_b = i K_b m; ΔT_f = i K_f m; π = i C R T |
| Partial dissociation or association | α from factor i | Dissoc: α = (i−1)/(n−1); Assoc: α = (1−i)/(1−1/n) |

## Mixed Solved Examples (Sab Kuch Mila Kar!)

### Example 1: Vapour-Phase Composition in an Ideal Solution
Two volatile liquids A (**p_A° = 200 mm Hg**) and B (**p_B° = 500 mm Hg**) are mixed in **equimolar** amounts (x_A = 0.5, x_B = 0.5). Find (a) total vapour pressure **P_total**, and (b) mole fraction of B in the **vapour phase (y_B)**.

**Hal (Solution):**
- (a) p_A = 200 × 0.5 = **100 mm Hg**; p_B = 500 × 0.5 = **250 mm Hg**.
  **P_total = 100 + 250 = 350 mm Hg**.
- (b) Dalton's law se vapour phase mein mole fraction:
  **y_B = p_B / P_total = 250 / 350 = 5 / 7 ≈ 0.714** (aur y_A = 2/7 ≈ 0.286).
  *(Note: Vapour phase hamesha **zyada volatile** component — B — mein rich hoti hai!)*
- **Jaanch:** 0.714 + 0.286 = 1.000 ✅.

### Example 2: Isotonic Comparison — Electrolyte vs Non-Electrolyte
What molarity of an aqueous **BaCl₂** solution (assuming 100% dissociation, **i = 3**) is **isotonic** with a **0.30 M** aqueous **glucose** solution (i = 1) at the same temperature?

**Hal (Solution):**
- Isotonic ⟹ π₁ = π₂ ⟹ **i₁ C₁ = i₂ C₂** (R T kat gaya!).
- 3 × C(BaCl₂) = 1 × 0.30 ⟹ **C(BaCl₂) = 0.30 / 3 = 0.10 M**.
- **Jaanch:** 3 × 0.10 = 0.30 M effective particle concentration = glucose ✅.

### Example 3: Antifreeze Mass Needed for −9.3 °C
What mass of ethylene glycol (**C₂H₆O₂**, molar mass **62 g/mol**, non-electrolyte) must be added to **1.0 kg of water** to lower its freezing point to **−9.3 °C**? (**K_f = 1.86 K kg/mol**.)

**Hal (Solution):**
- ΔT_f = 9.3 K = K_f × m ⟹ **m = 9.3 / 1.86 = 5.0 mol/kg**.
- 1.0 kg water mein 5.0 moles glycol chahiye ⟹ **Mass = 5.0 × 62 = 310 g**.
- **Jaanch:** 1.86 × 5 = 9.3 K; 5 × 62 = 310 g ✅.

## Aam Galtiyan (Synthesis Mistakes)

1. **Isotonic electrolyte solutions mein 'i' bhool kar C₁ = C₂ laga dena:**
   - *Sahi:* Hamesha **i₁ C₁ = i₂ C₂** use karo!
2. **Vapour-phase mole fraction y_A ko liquid-phase x_A ke barabar maan lena:**
   - *Sahi:* Sirf **azeotropes** mein y_A = x_A hota hai! Ideal solution mein **y_A = p_A / P_total = (p_A° x_A) / P_total**!
3. **Freezing point −9.3 °C ko ΔT_f = −9.3 K rakh kar negative mass nikaal dena:**
   - *Sahi:* **ΔT_f = T_f° − T_f = 0 − (−9.3) = +9.3 K** (hamesha positive magnitude!).

## Practice Questions (Mixed Chapter Challenge)

1. Calculate the molality of a 2.0 M aqueous NaCl solution whose density is 1.117 g/mL (molar mass of NaCl = 58.5 g/mol).
2. At 298 K, Henry's law constant for a gas in water is 50 kbar. Find the mole fraction of the gas dissolved at 2.0 bar pressure.
3. Liquids A (p_A° = 300 mm Hg) and B (p_B° = 600 mm Hg) form an ideal solution with x_A = 0.4 and x_B = 0.6. Find (a) P_total, and (b) mole fraction of A in the vapour phase (y_A).
4. Identify whether the following mixtures show **+ve** or **−ve** deviation from Raoult's law: (a) Ethanol + Water, (b) HNO₃ + Water, (c) Acetone + CS₂, (d) Chloroform + Acetone.
5. What molarity of Na₂SO₄ (complete dissociation, i = 3) is isotonic with 0.45 M urea at the same temperature?
6. Calculate the boiling point of a solution containing 11.1 g of CaCl₂ (molar mass 111 g/mol, complete dissociation) in 500 g of water (K_b = 0.52 K kg/mol).
7. A 0.20 m aqueous solution of a weak electrolyte AB is 25% dissociated. Calculate its freezing point (K_f = 1.86 K kg/mol).
8. Find the osmotic pressure (in atm) of a 0.10 M NaCl solution (complete dissociation) at 300 K (R = 0.0821 L atm / (mol K)).
9. State whether True or False: In an ideal solution of two volatile liquids, the vapour phase is always richer in the more volatile component.
10. A 2.0 g sample of a non-electrolyte dissolved in 50 g of benzene (K_f = 5.12 K kg/mol) lowers the freezing point by 0.40 K. Calculate the molar mass of the solute.

### Answers

1. 1 L solution mass = 1117 g; solute mass = 2.0 × 58.5 = 117 g ⟹ solvent mass = 1117 − 117 = **1000 g = 1.0 kg**.
   **m = 2.0 / 1.0 = 2.0 mol/kg**!
   Jaanch: 1117 − 117 = 1000 g = 1 kg ✅.

2. x = p / K_H = 2.0 / 50,000 = **4.0 × 10⁻⁵**.
   Jaanch: 2 / 50000 = 4 × 10⁻⁵ ✅.

3. (a) p_A = 300(0.4) = 120 mm Hg; p_B = 600(0.6) = 360 mm Hg ⟹ **P_total = 480 mm Hg**.
   (b) **y_A = 120 / 480 = 0.25** (and y_B = 0.75).
   Jaanch: 120 + 360 = 480; 120/480 = 1/4 = 0.25 ✅.

4. (a) **+ve**, (b) **−ve**, (c) **+ve**, (d) **−ve**.

5. 3 × C = 1 × 0.45 ⟹ **C = 0.15 M**.
   Jaanch: 3 × 0.15 = 0.45 M ✅.

6. n(CaCl₂) = 11.1 / 111 = 0.10 mol in 0.50 kg water ⟹ m = 0.20 mol/kg; i = 3.
   ΔT_b = 3 × 0.52 × 0.20 = **0.312 K** ⟹ **T_b = 100.312 °C**.
   Jaanch: 3 × 0.2 = 0.6; 0.6 × 0.52 = 0.312 K ✅.

7. n = 2, α = 0.25 ⟹ i = 1 + (2 − 1)(0.25) = 1.25.
   ΔT_f = 1.25 × 1.86 × 0.20 = 0.25 × 1.86 = **0.465 K** ⟹ **T_f = −0.465 °C**.
   Jaanch: 1.25 × 0.20 = 0.25; 1.86 / 4 = 0.465 K ✅.

8. i = 2: π = 2 × 0.10 × 0.0821 × 300 = **4.926 atm**.
   Jaanch: 2 × 2.463 = 4.926 atm ✅.

9. **True ✅.** Zyada p° wale component ka partial pressure p_i = p_i° x_i zyada hota hai, isliye vapour phase usi mein rich hoti hai!

10. M_B = (K_f × w_B × 1000) / (ΔT_f × W_A) = (5.12 × 2.0 × 1000) / (0.40 × 50) = 10,240 / 20 = **512 g/mol**.
    Jaanch: 0.40 × 50 = 20; 10240 / 20 = 512 g/mol ✅.

## Chapter Ka Saar: Solutions

| Topic | Mukhya Formula / Niyam | Yaad Rakhne Ki Khaas Baat |
|---|---|---|
| Concentration Units | M = n/V(L); m = n/W_A(kg); x_A + x_B = 1 | **M** is temp-dependent; **m, x, %w/w, ppm** are temp-independent |
| Solubility & Henry's Law | p = K_H x | Higher K_H = Lower solubility; T↑ → gas solubility↓ |
| Raoult's Law & Azeotropes | P = p_A° x_A + p_B° x_B; y_A = p_A/P | +ve dev → min-boiling azeotrope; −ve dev → max-boiling azeotrope |
| 4 Colligative Properties | RLVP = i x_B; ΔT_b = i K_b m; ΔT_f = i K_f m; π = i C R T | Osmotic pressure best for proteins; RO uses P > π |
| van't Hoff Factor (i) | i = M_norm / M_obs; Dissoc: i = 1+(n−1)α; Assoc: i = 1−α(1−1/n) | Dissociation → i > 1; Association → i < 1 |
