## Naye Shabd (New Words)

- **Binary Solution:** Do components — **Solvent** (jo zyada maatra mein ho aur physical state tay kare) aur **Solute** (jo kam maatra mein ghula ho) — ka homogeneous mixture.
- **Parts per Million (ppm):** Bahut kam maatra (trace amounts, jaise paani mein fluoride ya hawa mein pollutants) naapne ki unit: **ppm = (Mass of solute / Total mass of solution) × 10⁶**.
- **Mole Fraction (x):** Kisi component ke moles aur solution ke total moles ka ratio: **x_A = n_A / (n_A + n_B)** (hamesha **x_A + x_B = 1**, unitless!).
- **Molarity (M):** Per **1 litre of solution** mein ghule solute ke moles: **M = n_B / V(L)** (temperature badalne par badalti hai!).
- **Molality (m):** Per **1 kg of solvent** mein ghule solute ke moles: **m = n_B / W_solvent(kg)** (**temperature-independent**!).

## 1. Types of Solutions (9 Types — Quick Table)

| Type | Solute | Solvent | Everyday Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gaseous | Gas / Liquid / Solid | Gas | Air (O₂ in N₂) / Chloroform vapour in N₂ / Camphor in N₂ |
| Liquid | Gas / Liquid / Solid | Liquid | O₂ or CO₂ in water / Ethanol in water / Glucose in water |
| Solid | Gas / Liquid / Solid | Solid | H₂ in Palladium / Amalgam (Hg in Na) / **Brass (Zn in Cu)** |

## 2. Expressing Concentration — Master Formulas

1. **Mass Percentage (% w/w):** (Mass of solute / Total mass of solution) × 100
2. **Parts per Million (ppm):** (Mass of solute / Total mass of solution) × 10⁶
3. **Mole Fraction (x_B):** n_B / (n_A + n_B)  *(Always **x_A + x_B = 1**!)*
4. **Molarity (M):** (w_B / M_B) × (1000 / V_mL)  *(Unit: mol/L or M)*
5. **Molality (m):** (w_B / M_B) × (1000 / W_A in g)  *(Unit: mol/kg or m)*

### Temperature Dependence (Exam Favourite!):
- **Temperature-DEPENDENT:** **Molarity (M)** aur **% v/v** (kyunki inmein **Volume** aata hai, aur garmi se liquid expand hota hai!).
- **Temperature-INDEPENDENT:** **Molality (m)**, **Mole Fraction (x)**, **Mass % (w/w)**, **ppm** (kyunki inmein sirf **Mass** aata hai!).

## Solved Examples

### Example 1: Mole Fraction of Ethylene Glycol in Water
Calculate the mole fraction of ethylene glycol (**C₂H₆O₂**, molar mass **62 g/mol**) in an aqueous solution containing **20% of C₂H₆O₂ by mass**.

**Hal (Solution):**
- 100 g solution mein: **w_glycol = 20 g**, **w_water = 80 g** (molar mass of H₂O = 18 g/mol).
- Moles of glycol: n_B = 20 / 62 ≈ **0.3226 mol**.
- Moles of water: n_A = 80 / 18 ≈ **4.4444 mol**.
- Total moles = 0.3226 + 4.4444 = **4.767 mol**.
- **x_glycol = 0.3226 / 4.767 ≈ 0.068** (aur x_water = 1 − 0.068 = **0.932**).
- **Jaanch:** 0.068 + 0.932 = 1.000 ✅.

### Example 2: Molarity vs Molality of NaOH Solution
**4.0 g of NaOH** (molar mass **40 g/mol**) is dissolved in water to make **250 mL** of solution (density of solution = **1.04 g/mL**). Calculate (a) Molarity **M**, and (b) Molality **m**.

**Hal (Solution):**
- Moles of NaOH: **n_B = 4.0 / 40 = 0.10 mol**.
- (a) Volume V = 250 mL = 0.25 L ⟹ **M = 0.10 / 0.25 = 0.40 mol/L (0.40 M)**.
- (b) Total mass of solution = 250 mL × 1.04 g/mL = **260 g**.
  Mass of solvent (water) = 260 − 4.0 = **256 g = 0.256 kg**.
  **m = 0.10 / 0.256 ≈ 0.391 mol/kg (0.391 m)**.
- **Jaanch:** 0.10 / 0.25 = 0.40 M ✅; 260 − 4 = 256 g solvent ⟹ 0.10 / 0.256 ≈ 0.391 m ✅.

## Aam Galtiyan (Common Mistakes)

1. **Molality mein solvent ke mass ki jagah POORE SOLUTION ka mass rakh dena:**
   - *Galat:* Example 2 mein m = 0.10 / 0.260 likh dena!
   - *Sahi:* Molality ke denominator mein sirf **SOLVENT ka mass (W_solution − w_solute = 256 g)** aata hai!
2. **Molarity ko temperature-independent bol dena:**
   - *Galat:* Molarity temperature se nahi badalti.
   - *Sahi:* Molarity mein **Volume (L)** hota hai jo garmi se badhta hai ⟹ **Molarity temperature-dependent** hai, **Molality temperature-independent** hai!
3. **Mole fraction ke saath unit likh dena:**
   - *Sahi:* Mole fraction (mol / mol) **unitless** hota hai aur sabhi components ke mole fractions ka sum hamesha **1** hota hai!

## Quick Recap

| Unit | Formula | Temp Dependent? |
|---|---|---|
| Mass % (w/w) | (w_B / W_sol) × 100 | No |
| ppm | (w_B / W_sol) × 10⁶ | No |
| Mole fraction (x_B) | n_B / (n_A + n_B) | No |
| Molarity (M) | n_B / V_sol(L) | **YES** (uses volume!) |
| Molality (m) | n_B / W_solvent(kg) | **No** (preferred in thermodynamics/colligative!) |

## Practice Questions

1. Give one example each of (a) a solid-in-solid solution, and (b) a gas-in-solid solution.
2. Why is **molality (m)** preferred over **molarity (M)** in experiments where temperature varies?
3. Calculate the mass percentage of benzene (C₆H₆) when 22 g of benzene is dissolved in 122 g of carbon tetrachloride (CCl₄).
4. A 500 g sample of seawater contains 3.0 × 10⁻³ g of dissolved oxygen (O₂). Express the concentration of O₂ in parts per million (ppm).
5. Calculate the molarity of a solution containing 5.0 g of NaOH (molar mass 40 g/mol) in 500 mL of solution.
6. Calculate the molality of 2.5 g of ethanoic acid (CH₃COOH, molar mass 60 g/mol) dissolved in 75 g of benzene.
7. A solution contains 9.0 g of glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆, molar mass 180 g/mol) dissolved in 36 g of water (molar mass 18 g/mol). Find the mole fraction of glucose and water.
8. State whether True or False: The sum of mole fractions of all components in any solution is always equal to 1.
9. What is the molarity of pure water (density = 1.0 g/mL, molar mass = 18 g/mol)?
10. A 10% (w/w) aqueous glucose solution has a density of 1.2 g/mL. Calculate its (a) molality, and (b) molarity (molar mass of glucose = 180 g/mol).

### Answers

1. (a) **Brass** (Zinc in Copper) ya **22-carat Gold** (Copper in Gold); (b) **Hydrogen gas in Palladium (Pd)**.

2. Molality sirf **mass** par depend karti hai (jo temperature se nahi badalta), jabki molarity **volume** par depend karti hai jo temperature badhne par expand hota hai!

3. Total mass = 22 + 122 = 144 g.
   Mass % of benzene = (22 / 144) × 100 ≈ **15.28%**.
   Jaanch: 22 / 144 ≈ 0.1528 ✅.

4. ppm = (3.0 × 10⁻³ / 500) × 10⁶ = (3.0 × 10³) / 500 = **6.0 ppm**.
   Jaanch: 3000 / 500 = 6.0 ppm ✅.

5. n = 5.0 / 40 = 0.125 mol; V = 0.50 L:
   **M = 0.125 / 0.50 = 0.25 M**.
   Jaanch: 0.25 × 0.50 = 0.125 mol ✅.

6. n = 2.5 / 60 = 1/24 mol; W_solvent = 75 g = 0.075 kg:
   **m = (1/24) / 0.075 = 1 / 1.8 ≈ 0.556 mol/kg**.
   Jaanch: 24 × 0.075 = 1.8; 1/1.8 = 5/9 ≈ 0.556 ✅.

7. n_glucose = 9.0 / 180 = 0.05 mol; n_water = 36 / 18 = 2.0 mol; total = 2.05 mol.
   **x_glucose = 0.05 / 2.05 = 1/41 ≈ 0.0244**; **x_water = 1 − 0.0244 = 0.9756**.
   Jaanch: 0.0244 + 0.9756 = 1.000 ✅.

8. **True ✅.** x₁ + x₂ + ... = (n₁ + n₂ + ...) / n_total = 1.

9. 1 L (1000 mL) pure water = 1000 g ⟹ n = 1000 / 18 ≈ **55.56 mol** ⟹ **M = 55.56 M**.
   Jaanch: 1000 / 18 = 55.555... ✅.

10. 100 g solution mein: 10 g glucose (n = 10/180 = 1/18 mol), 90 g water (0.090 kg).
    (a) **m = (1/18) / 0.090 = 1 / 1.62 ≈ 0.617 mol/kg**.
    (b) Volume of 100 g solution = 100 / 1.2 = 83.33 mL = 0.08333 L:
        **M = (1/18) / (1/12) = 12 / 18 = 2/3 ≈ 0.667 mol/L**.
    Jaanch: 100 / 1.2 mL = 1/12 L; (1/18)/(1/12) = 2/3 = 0.667 M ✅.
