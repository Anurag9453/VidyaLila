# Oxidation States, Colour, Magnetic Properties and Catalytic Behaviour

> **Pichle topic mein:** Humne [Position, Electronic Configuration and General Properties](the-d-and-f-block-elements.md) mein 3d series ki configurations aur metallic properties padhi. Ab hum transition elements ki 5 sabse mashhoor chemical properties — **variable oxidation states, electrode potentials, colour, magnetism, aur catalysis/complexes/alloys** — ka kaaran aur calculation seekhenge.

Sahdev ne poocha, "Namak (NaCl) aur choona (CaCO₃) toh hamesha safed hote hain, phir in d-block metals ke compounds itne rang-birange kyun hote hain?"

## Naye Shabd (Key Terms)

| Term | Hinglish Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Variable Oxidation States** | Ek hi element ka alag-alag compounds mein ek se zyada oxidation numbers dikhana (jaise Mn: +2 se +7) |
| **d–d Transition** | Visible light absorb karke electron ka lower-energy d-orbital se higher-energy d-orbital mein jump karna (colour ka main kaaran) |
| **Paramagnetic** | Unpaired electrons ki wajah se magnetic field ki taraf attract hone wala substance |
| **Diamagnetic** | Sabhi electrons paired hone ki wajah se magnetic field se halka repel hone wala substance |
| **Spin-Only Magnetic Moment (μ)** | Unpaired electrons (n) se aane wala magnetic moment: μ = √[n(n + 2)] BM (Bohr Magneton) |
| **Interstitial Compounds** | Transition metal crystal lattice ke chhote khali spaces (voids) mein H, C, ya N atoms ke phansne se bane hard compounds (jaise TiC, Fe₃H) |

## 1. Variable Oxidation States

Transition metals bahut saare oxidation states kyun dikhate hain?
Kyunki **(n−1)d aur ns orbitals ke beech energy difference bahut kam** hota hai — dono ke electrons bonding mein participate kar sakte hain!

### 3d Series Oxidation States Trends:
- **Sc (+3 only):** 3d¹4s² → teeno electrons lose karke noble gas [Ar] banata hai.
- **Zn (+2 only):** 3d¹⁰4s² → sirf 4s² lose karta hai.
- **Maximum oxidation state:** Series ke beech mein **Manganese (Mn)** sabse zyada oxidation states dikhata hai: **+2, +3, +4, +5, +6, +7** (jaise MnO₄⁻ mein +7!).
- **Highest oxidation states sirf Fluorine (F) aur Oxygen (O) ke saath** milti hain (high electronegativity aur small size):
  - Oxygen double bond (multiple bonds) bana sakta hai, isliye O fluorine se bhi higher states stabilise karta hai! Jaise Mn ka highest fluoride **MnF₄** (+4) hai, par highest oxide **Mn₂O₇** (+7) hai!
- **Trend down a group (opposite to p-block!):** d-block mein group mein neeche jaane par **higher oxidation states zyada stable** hoti hain! Jaise Mo(VI) aur W(VI) Cr(VI) se zyada stable hain (Cr₂O₇²⁻ strong oxidising agent hai, par MoO₃/WO₃ nahi).

## 2. Standard Electrode Potentials: E°(M²⁺/M) and E°(M³⁺/M²⁺)

### (a) E°(M²⁺/M) Trend Across 3d Series
M(s) → M²⁺(aq) + 2e⁻ ke liye total energy = Δ_sub H° + IE₁ + IE₂ + Δ_hyd H°.
- Lagbhag sabhi 3d metals ka E°(M²⁺/M) **negative** hota hai (acid se H₂ displace kar sakte hain), **SIWAYE Copper (Cu) ke!**
- **Why is E°(Cu²⁺/Cu) = +0.34 V (positive)?**
  Copper ki **high enthalpy of atomisation (Δ_sub H°)** aur **high IE₁ + IE₂** ko uski **hydration enthalpy (Δ_hyd H°)** compensate nahi kar pati! Isliye Cu dilute HCl/H₂SO₄ se H₂ gas nahi nikalta (sirf oxidising acids jaise HNO₃ aur hot conc. H₂SO₄ ke saath react karta hai).
- **Mn (−1.18 V) aur Zn (−0.76 V)** ka E° ummeed se zyada negative hota hai kyunki Mn²⁺ (3d⁵) aur Zn²⁺ (3d¹⁰) extra stable hain; **Ni (−0.25 V)** ki high negative hydration enthalpy use zyada negative banati hai.

### (b) E°(M³⁺/M²⁺) Trend
- **Cr²⁺ is a strong reducing agent** (E° Cr³⁺/Cr²⁺ = −0.41 V): Cr²⁺ (3d⁴) → Cr³⁺ (3d³, half-filled t₂g³ in octahedral aqua complex — bahut stable!).
- **Mn³⁺ is a strong oxidising agent** (E° Mn³⁺/Mn²⁺ = +1.57 V): Mn³⁺ (3d⁴) → Mn²⁺ (3d⁵, half-filled stable!).
- **Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ (+0.77 V)** moderate hai kyunki Fe³⁺ (3d⁵) stable hai.

## 3. Magnetic Properties (Spin-Only Magnetic Moment)

3d series ke ions mein orbital contribution lagbhag quench (cancel) ho jata hai, isliye magnetic moment sirf unpaired electrons (n) par depend karta hai:

**μ = √[ n (n + 2) ]  BM** (Bohr Magneton)

| Unpaired e⁻ (n) | Example Ions | μ = √[n(n+2)] (BM) | Magnetic Nature |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Sc³⁺ (d⁰), Ti⁴⁺ (d⁰), Cu⁺ (d¹⁰), Zn²⁺ (d¹⁰) | 0.00 | Diamagnetic |
| 1 | Ti³⁺ (d¹), Cu²⁺ (d⁹) | √3 = 1.73 | Paramagnetic |
| 2 | Ti²⁺ (d²), V³⁺ (d²), Ni²⁺ (d⁸) | √8 = 2.83 | Paramagnetic |
| 3 | V²⁺ (d³), Cr³⁺ (d³), Co²⁺ (d⁷) | √15 = 3.87 | Paramagnetic |
| 4 | Cr²⁺ (d⁴), Fe²⁺ (d⁶) | √24 = 4.90 | Paramagnetic |
| 5 | Mn²⁺ (d⁵), Fe³⁺ (d⁵) | √35 = 5.92 | Paramagnetic (Max!) |

Shortcut trick: Answer hamesha **n.8 ya n.9** ke aas-paas hota hai! (n = 1 → 1.73, n = 2 → 2.83, n = 3 → 3.87, n = 4 → 4.90, n = 5 → 5.92).

## 4. Formation of Coloured Ions

- Jab transition metal ion ke paas **1 se 9 d-electrons (d¹–d⁹)** hote hain, tab ligands ki presence mein 5 degenerate d-orbitals do energy sets (lower aur higher) mein split ho jate hain.
- Visible light ka ek particular wavelength absorb karke electron lower d-orbital se higher d-orbital mein jump karta hai (**d–d transition**).
- Jo light absorb nahi hoti, uska **complementary colour** humein dikhai deta hai!
  - Example: Cu²⁺(aq) (3d⁹) red/orange absorb karta hai → **blue** dikhta hai!
  - Ti³⁺(aq) (3d¹) → **purple**; Fe²⁺(aq) (3d⁶) → **pale green**; Ni²⁺(aq) (3d⁸) → **green**; Mn²⁺(aq) (3d⁵) → **pale pink**.
- **Colourless ions (no d–d transition possible):** **d⁰** (Sc³⁺, Ti⁴⁺) aur **d¹⁰** (Cu⁺, Zn²⁺) — inmein ya toh koi d-electron nahi hai ya saare orbitals full hain!
- *(Special exception: MnO₄⁻ (Mn⁷⁺, d⁰) aur Cr₂O₇²⁻ (Cr⁶⁺, d⁰) phir bhi deep purple/orange hote hain — **Charge Transfer** ki wajah se, d–d transition se nahi!)*

## 5. Catalytic Behaviour, Complex Formation, Interstitial Compounds & Alloys

1. **Catalytic Properties:** Transition metals (jaise Fe in Haber process, V₂O₅ in Contact process, Ni in hydrogenation of oils) behtareen catalysts kyun hain?
   - **Variable oxidation states** dikha sakte hain (unstable intermediates banakar Eₐ kam karte hain).
   - **Large surface area** aur vacant d-orbitals reactants ko adsorb karke unki local concentration badhate hain.
   - Example: Fe³⁺ catalyses 2I⁻ + S₂O₈²⁻ → I₂ + 2SO₄²⁻ via Fe³⁺ → Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ cycle!
2. **Complex Formation:** Small ionic size, high nuclear charge (high charge/radius ratio), aur **vacant d-orbitals** ki wajah se ligands se lone pairs accept karke coordination complexes banate hain (jaise [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻, [Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺).
3. **Interstitial Compounds:** Small non-metal atoms (H, C, N, B) metal lattice ke voids mein fit ho jate hain (e.g., TiC, Mn₄N, Fe₃H).
   - Properties: High melting point (pure metal se bhi zyada), extreme hardness, retain metallic conductivity, chemically inert, non-stoichiometric.
4. **Alloy Formation:** Similar atomic radii (within ~15%) hone ke kaaran ek metal ke atoms doosre metal ke lattice mein aasani se replace ho jate hain (solid solutions). Examples: **Brass** (Cu + Zn), **Bronze** (Cu + Sn), **Stainless steel** (Fe + Cr + Ni + C).

## Solved Examples

### Example 1: Calculating Spin-Only Magnetic Moment
Calculate the spin-only magnetic moment of: (a) Fe²⁺ (Z = 26), (b) Cr³⁺ (Z = 24), (c) Zn²⁺ (Z = 30).

**Solution:**
(a) Fe²⁺ = [Ar] 3d⁶ → 4 unpaired electrons (n = 4).
μ = √[4(4 + 2)] = √24 = **4.90 BM**.

(b) Cr³⁺ = [Ar] 3d³ → 3 unpaired electrons (n = 3).
μ = √[3(3 + 2)] = √15 = **3.87 BM**.

(c) Zn²⁺ = [Ar] 3d¹⁰ → 0 unpaired electrons (n = 0).
μ = √[0(2)] = **0.00 BM** (diamagnetic).

Jaanch: 4.90² = 24.01 ≈ 24; 3.87² = 14.98 ≈ 15 ✅

---

### Example 2: Identifying the Ion from Magnetic Moment
A divalent transition metal ion M²⁺ of the 3d series has a spin-only magnetic moment of 5.92 BM. Identify the ion and write its electronic configuration.

**Solution:**
√[n(n + 2)] = 5.92 → n(n + 2) = 5.92² = 35.05 ≈ 35.
Solving n² + 2n − 35 = 0 → (n + 7)(n − 5) = 0 → **n = 5** unpaired electrons.
In the 3d series, the divalent ion with 5 unpaired electrons (3d⁵) is **Mn²⁺ (Z = 25)**, with configuration **[Ar] 3d⁵**.

Jaanch: Mn (Z = 25) = [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s² → Mn²⁺ = [Ar] 3d⁵ → n = 5 → √35 = 5.916 ≈ 5.92 BM ✅

---

### Example 3: Explaining Colourless vs Coloured Ions
Predict which of the following ions will be coloured in aqueous solution: Ti⁴⁺, V³⁺, Cu⁺, Cu²⁺, Mn²⁺. Give reasons.

**Solution:**
- **Coloured (incomplete d-orbital, d¹–d⁹, d–d transition possible):**
  - V³⁺ ([Ar] 3d²) — 2 unpaired e⁻ → green
  - Cu²⁺ ([Ar] 3d⁹) — 1 unpaired e⁻ → blue
  - Mn²⁺ ([Ar] 3d⁵) — 5 unpaired e⁻ → pale pink
- **Colourless (d⁰ or d¹⁰, no d–d transition):**
  - Ti⁴⁺ ([Ar] 3d⁰) — 0 d-electrons
  - Cu⁺ ([Ar] 3d¹⁰) — completely filled 3d orbital

Jaanch: Coloured set = {V³⁺, Cu²⁺, Mn²⁺}; Colourless set = {Ti⁴⁺, Cu⁺} ✅

## Aam Galtiyan (Common Mistakes)

1. **d⁶ mein 6 unpaired electrons gin lena:**
   - *Galat:* Fe²⁺ (3d⁶) ke liye n = 6 rakh kar μ = √48 nikalna.
   - *Sahi:* d-subshell mein sirf **5 orbitals** hote hain! Hund's rule se 5 electrons single bharenge, 6th electron pair banayega → **4 unpaired electrons** (n = 4, μ = 4.90 BM)!

2. **Cr²⁺ aur Mn³⁺ ke reducing/oxidising nature ko ulta kar dena:**
   - *Galat:* Dono d⁴ hain toh dono same tarah behave karenge.
   - *Sahi:* **Cr²⁺** reducing agent hai (d⁴ → stable half-filled t₂g³ in Cr³⁺), jabki **Mn³⁺** oxidising agent hai (d⁴ → stable half-filled d⁵ in Mn²⁺)!

3. **Copper ko acid se H₂ gas nikalne wala metal bol dena:**
   - *Galat:* Cu + 2HCl → CuCl₂ + H₂.
   - *Sahi:* E°(Cu²⁺/Cu) = +0.34 V (positive!) hai, isliye Cu dilute HCl se H₂ displace **nahi** kar sakta!

## Quick Recap

- **Variable oxidation states:** Small energy gap between (n−1)d and ns; Mn shows max (+2 to +7); O stabilises higher states better than F (multiple bonds).
- **E° trends:** Cu has positive E°(Cu²⁺/Cu) = +0.34 V; Cr²⁺ is reducing (d⁴ → d³); Mn³⁺ is oxidising (d⁴ → d⁵).
- **Magnetic moment:** μ = √[n(n+2)] BM (max 5.92 BM for d⁵: Mn²⁺, Fe³⁺).
- **Colour:** d–d transitions in d¹–d⁹ ions; d⁰ (Sc³⁺, Ti⁴⁺) and d¹⁰ (Cu⁺, Zn²⁺) are colourless.
- **Catalysts, complexes, interstitial compounds & alloys:** Powered by variable oxidation states, vacant d-orbitals, and similar atomic sizes.

## Practice Questions

1. Why do transition elements exhibit variable oxidation states? Which element in the 3d series shows the largest number of oxidation states?
2. Why is the highest oxidation state of a metal exhibited in its oxide or fluoride only? Why does oxygen stabilise higher states better than fluorine?
3. Explain why E°(Cu²⁺/Cu) is positive (+0.34 V), whereas other 3d metals have negative E°(M²⁺/M) values.
4. Explain why Cr²⁺ is a strong reducing agent while Mn³⁺ is a strong oxidising agent, even though both have d⁴ configuration.
5. Calculate the spin-only magnetic moment of: (a) Co²⁺ (Z = 27), (b) Ni²⁺ (Z = 28), (c) Ti³⁺ (Z = 22).
6. Which of the following ions are colourless in aqueous solution: Sc³⁺, Fe³⁺, Zn²⁺, Ni²⁺, Cu⁺?
7. Give two reasons why transition metals and their compounds act as good catalysts.
8. What are interstitial compounds? Write any three characteristic properties of these compounds.
9. Why do transition metals readily form alloys with one another? Name the constituents of brass and bronze.
10. A 3d-series trivalent ion M³⁺ has a spin-only magnetic moment of 2.83 BM. Identify the metal M and write the electronic configuration of M³⁺.

---

### Answers

1. Because the energy difference between (n−1)d and ns orbitals is very small, allowing electrons from both subshells to participate in bonding. **Manganese (Mn)** shows the largest number of oxidation states in the 3d series (+2 to +7).
2. Oxygen and fluorine have very high electronegativity and small atomic size, enabling them to oxidise metals to their highest states. Oxygen stabilises higher oxidation states even better than fluorine because oxygen can form **multiple (double) bonds** with metals (e.g., Mn₂O₇ has Mn in +7, whereas MnF₄ only reaches +4).
3. The sum of enthalpy of atomisation (Δ_sub H°) and ionisation enthalpies (IE₁ + IE₂) of copper is very high and is not compensated by the negative hydration enthalpy (Δ_hyd H°) of Cu²⁺, making E°(Cu²⁺/Cu) positive (+0.34 V).
4. Cr²⁺ (d⁴) oxidises to Cr³⁺ (d³), which has a stable half-filled **t₂g³** level in aqueous octahedral complex — making Cr²⁺ a strong reducing agent. Mn³⁺ (d⁴) reduces to Mn²⁺ (d⁵), which has an extra-stable half-filled **3d⁵** configuration — making Mn³⁺ a strong oxidising agent.
5. (a) Co²⁺ = [Ar] 3d⁷ → n = 3 unpaired e⁻ → μ = √[3(5)] = √15 = **3.87 BM**.
   (b) Ni²⁺ = [Ar] 3d⁸ → n = 2 unpaired e⁻ → μ = √[2(4)] = √8 = **2.83 BM**.
   (c) Ti³⁺ = [Ar] 3d¹ → n = 1 unpaired e⁻ → μ = √[1(3)] = √3 = **1.73 BM**.
   Jaanch: 3.87² ≈ 15, 2.83² ≈ 8, 1.73² ≈ 3 ✅
6. Colourless ions: **Sc³⁺ (3d⁰), Zn²⁺ (3d¹⁰), and Cu⁺ (3d¹⁰)** — no d–d transition possible. (Fe³⁺ is d⁵ and Ni²⁺ is d⁸, both coloured.)
7. (i) Ability to adopt **multiple oxidation states** and form unstable intermediates that lower activation energy. (ii) Availability of **vacant d-orbitals and large surface area** to adsorb reactant molecules.
8. Interstitial compounds are formed when small non-metal atoms (H, C, N, B) get trapped inside the interstitial voids of transition metal crystal lattices. Properties: (i) very high melting points, (ii) very hard, (iii) retain metallic conductivity and are chemically inert.
9. Transition metals have **very similar atomic radii** (within ~15%), so atoms of one metal can easily substitute into the crystal lattice of another. **Brass:** Cu + Zn; **Bronze:** Cu + Sn.
10. μ = 2.83 BM → n(n + 2) = 8 → **n = 2** unpaired electrons.
    Possible 3d configs for M³⁺ with n = 2: 3d² (V³⁺, Z = 23) or 3d⁸ (unlikely as common +3 ion in 3d). Thus **M = Vanadium (V, Z = 23)** and **V³⁺ = [Ar] 3d²**.
    Jaanch: V (23) = [Ar] 3d³4s² → V³⁺ = [Ar] 3d² → n = 2 → √8 = 2.83 BM ✅
