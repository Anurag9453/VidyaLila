# Preparation and Properties of Potassium Dichromate and Potassium Permanganate

> **Pichle topic mein:** Humne [Oxidation States, Colour, Magnetic Properties and Catalytic Behaviour](the-d-and-f-block-elements-part2.md) mein dekha ki Cr (+6) aur Mn (+7) apni highest oxidation states mein powerful oxidising agents hote hain. Ab hum inke do sabse important industrial aur analytical compounds — **K₂Cr₂O₇** aur **KMnO₄** — ki preparation, structures aur oxidising reactions padhenge.

---

Aaj hum in dono jaadui colour changes aur unke exact balanced redox equations ka raaz samjhenge!

| Term | Hinglish Meaning |

| **Chromate Ion (CrO₄²⁻)** | Yellow tetrahedral ion jo alkaline (basic) medium mein stable hota hai |
| **Dichromate Ion (Cr₂O₇²⁻)** | Orange ion (do tetrahedra ek O-atom se jude) jo acidic medium mein stable hota hai |

## 1. Potassium Dichromate (K₂Cr₂O₇)

> **Safety Note:** Potassium dichromate (Cr(VI)) toxic aur strong oxidiser hai; iski preparation aur reactions sirf industrial setup ya supervised teacher demonstration mein hoti hain.

### Preparation from Chromite Ore (FeCr₂O₄) — 3 Steps:
1. **Roasting with Na₂CO₃ in excess air (forms yellow Sodium Chromate):**
   4FeCr₂O₄ + 8Na₂CO₃ + 7O₂ → 8Na₂CrO₄ + 2Fe₂O₃ + 8CO₂
   *(Na₂CrO₄ paani mein soluble hai, Fe₂O₃ insoluble hai → filter karke alag kar lete hain.)*
2. **Acidification with H₂SO₄ (forms orange Sodium Dichromate):**
   2Na₂CrO₄ + 2H⁺ → Na₂Cr₂O₇ + 2Na⁺ + H₂O
   *(Na₂SO₄·10H₂O crystallise hokar bahar nikal jata hai.)*
3. **Reaction with KCl (forms less soluble Potassium Dichromate crystals):**
   Na₂Cr₂O₇ + 2KCl → **K₂Cr₂O₇** + 2NaCl
   *(K₂Cr₂O₇ thande paani mein Na₂Cr₂O₇ se kam soluble hai, isliye orange crystals alag ho jate hain!)*

### Chromate–Dichromate pH Equilibrium (Interconvertible!)
- **In basic medium (OH⁻):** Orange Cr₂O₇²⁻ → Yellow CrO₄²⁻
  Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 2OH⁻ ⇌ 2CrO₄²⁻ + H₂O
- **In acidic medium (H⁺):** Yellow CrO₄²⁻ → Orange Cr₂O₇²⁻
  2CrO₄²⁻ + 2H⁺ ⇌ Cr₂O₇²⁻ + H₂O

### Structures of CrO₄²⁻ and Cr₂O₇²⁻:
- **CrO₄²⁻:** Tetrahedral; sabhi 4 Cr–O bonds equal (resonance).
- **Cr₂O₇²⁻:** Do tetrahedra ek common oxygen bridge (Cr–O–Cr) share karte hain; **Cr–O–Cr bond angle = 126°**. 6 terminal Cr–O bonds equal (163 pm) aur 2 bridged Cr–O bonds lambe (179 pm) hote hain.

### Oxidising Action of K₂Cr₂O₇ in Acidic Medium:
Half-reaction (n-factor = 6, kyunki do Cr atoms +6 se +3 jate hain):
**Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ + 6e⁻ → 2Cr³⁺ (green) + 7H₂O** (E° = +1.33 V)

Important oxidations in acidic medium:
1. **Iodide to Iodine:** Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 6I⁻ + 14H⁺ → 2Cr³⁺ + 3I₂ + 7H₂O
2. **Ferrous (Fe²⁺) to Ferric (Fe³⁺):** Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 6Fe²⁺ + 14H⁺ → 2Cr³⁺ + 6Fe³⁺ + 7H₂O
3. **Hydrogen sulphide (H₂S) to Sulphur:** Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 3H₂S + 8H⁺ → 2Cr³⁺ + 3S + 7H₂O
4. **Stannous (Sn²⁺) to Stannic (Sn⁴⁺):** Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 3Sn²⁺ + 14H⁺ → 2Cr³⁺ + 3Sn⁴⁺ + 7H₂O

## 2. Potassium Permanganate (KMnO₄)

### Preparation from Pyrolusite Ore (MnO₂) — 2 Steps:
1. **Fusion with KOH and oxidising agent (O₂ or KNO₃) → dark green K₂MnO₄:**
   2MnO₂ + 4KOH + O₂ → 2K₂MnO₄ (dark green) + 2H₂O
2. **Disproportionation in neutral/acidic medium (or electrolytic oxidation) → purple KMnO₄:**
   3MnO₄²⁻ + 4H⁺ → **2MnO₄⁻** (purple) + MnO₂ + 2H₂O
   *(Commercial method: Alkaline oxidative fusion of MnO₂ followed by electrolytic oxidation of MnO₄²⁻ to MnO₄⁻!)*
- **Lab method (from Mn²⁺ salt using peroxodisulphate S₂O₈²⁻):**
  2Mn²⁺ + 5S₂O₈²⁻ + 8H₂O → 2MnO₄⁻ + 10SO₄²⁻ + 16H⁺

### Structures and Magnetism of MnO₄²⁻ vs MnO₄⁻:
- Dono **tetrahedral** hain!
- **MnO₄²⁻ (Manganate, Mn = +6, 3d¹):** Dark green, **paramagnetic** (1 unpaired electron).
- **MnO₄⁻ (Permanganate, Mn = +7, 3d⁰):** Deep purple, **diamagnetic** (0 unpaired electrons). Colour comes from **ligand-to-metal charge transfer** (O²⁻ → Mn⁷⁺).
- **Effect of heating KMnO₄ (513 K):** 2KMnO₄ → K₂MnO₄ + MnO₂ + O₂.

### Oxidising Action of KMnO₄ in Different Media (SUPER IMPORTANT!)

| Medium | Half-Reaction | Mn Oxidation Change | n-factor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Acidic** | MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5e⁻ → Mn²⁺ (colourless) + 4H₂O | +7 → +2 | **5** |
| **Neutral / Faintly Alkaline** | MnO₄⁻ + 2H₂O + 3e⁻ → MnO₂ (brown ppt) + 4OH⁻ | +7 → +4 | **3** |
| **Strongly Alkaline** | MnO₄⁻ + e⁻ → MnO₄²⁻ (green) | +7 → +6 | **1** |

#### Key Reactions in Acidic Medium (with dil. H₂SO₄):
*(Note: HCl is NOT used to acidify KMnO₄ because KMnO₄ oxidises Cl⁻ to toxic Cl₂ gas!)*
1. **Oxalate (C₂O₄²⁻) to CO₂:** 2MnO₄⁻ + 5C₂O₄²⁻ + 16H⁺ → 2Mn²⁺ + 10CO₂ + 8H₂O
2. **Fe²⁺ to Fe³⁺:** MnO₄⁻ + 5Fe²⁺ + 8H⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 5Fe³⁺ + 4H₂O
3. **Iodide (I⁻) to I₂:** 2MnO₄⁻ + 10I⁻ + 16H⁺ → 2Mn²⁺ + 5I₂ + 8H₂O
4. **Nitrite (NO₂⁻) to Nitrate (NO₃⁻):** 2MnO₄⁻ + 5NO₂⁻ + 6H⁺ → 2Mn²⁺ + 5NO₃⁻ + 3H₂O
5. **Sulphite (SO₃²⁻) to Sulphate (SO₄²⁻):** 2MnO₄⁻ + 5SO₃²⁻ + 6H⁺ → 2Mn²⁺ + 5SO₄²⁻ + 3H₂O

#### Key Reactions in Neutral / Faintly Alkaline Medium:
1. **Iodide (I⁻) to Iodate (IO₃⁻)!** (Dhyan do: acidic mein I₂ banta hai, alkaline mein IO₃⁻!)
   2MnO₄⁻ + H₂O + I⁻ → 2MnO₂ + 2OH⁻ + IO₃⁻
2. **Thiosulphate (S₂O₃²⁻) to Sulphate (SO₄²⁻):**
   8MnO₄⁻ + 3S₂O₃²⁻ + H₂O → 8MnO₂ + 6SO₄²⁻ + 2OH⁻
3. **Manganous salt (Mn²⁺) to MnO₂ (catalysed by ZnSO₄/ZnO):**
   2MnO₄⁻ + 3Mn²⁺ + 2H₂O → 5MnO₂ + 4H⁺

## Solved Examples

### Example 1: Stoichiometry of Acidic KMnO₄ with Oxalate
How many moles of KMnO₄ are required to completely oxidise 1.0 mole of ferrous oxalate (FeC₂O₄) in acidic medium?

**Solution:**
In FeC₂O₄, BOTH Fe²⁺ and C₂O₄²⁻ are oxidised!
- Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ + 1e⁻ (1 electron lost)
- C₂O₄²⁻ → 2CO₂ + 2e⁻ (2 electrons lost)
Total electrons lost per mole of FeC₂O₄ = 1 + 2 = **3 moles of e⁻**.
In acidic medium, 1 mole of MnO₄⁻ gains **5 moles of e⁻** (Mn⁷⁺ → Mn²⁺).
By electron balance:
Moles of KMnO₄ × 5 = 1.0 × 3 → Moles of KMnO₄ = **3/5 = 0.60 mol**.

Jaanch: 3 MnO₄⁻ gain 15 e⁻; 5 FeC₂O₄ lose 5 × 3 = 15 e⁻ → ratio 3:5 = 0.60 mol KMnO₄ per mol FeC₂O₄ ✅

---

### Example 2: Balancing Dichromate–Fe²⁺ Redox Reaction
How many moles of Fe²⁺ can be oxidised by 0.25 mol of K₂Cr₂O₇ in acidic medium?

**Solution:**
Balanced equation: Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 6Fe²⁺ + 14H⁺ → 2Cr³⁺ + 6Fe³⁺ + 7H₂O.
1 mol Cr₂O₇²⁻ oxidises 6 mol Fe²⁺.
So 0.25 mol K₂Cr₂O₇ oxidises 0.25 × 6 = **1.50 mol Fe²⁺**.

Jaanch: Electrons gained by 0.25 mol Cr₂O₇²⁻ = 0.25 × 6 = 1.50 mol e⁻; each Fe²⁺ loses 1 e⁻ → 1.50 mol Fe²⁺ ✅

---

### Example 3: Effect of pH on Dichromate Solution
What happens when (a) NaOH is added to an orange solution of K₂Cr₂O₇, and (b) H₂SO₄ is subsequently added to the resulting solution? Write balanced ionic equations.

**Solution:**
(a) On adding NaOH (basic medium), orange dichromate converts to **yellow chromate**:
Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 2OH⁻ → 2CrO₄²⁻ + H₂O
(b) On adding H₂SO₄ (acidic medium), yellow chromate converts back to **orange dichromate**:
2CrO₄²⁻ + 2H⁺ → Cr₂O₇²⁻ + H₂O

Jaanch: Cr stays in +6 oxidation state in both ions (non-redox acid-base equilibrium!) ✅

## Aam Galtiyan (Common Mistakes)

1. **KMnO₄ titration mein HCl use kar lena:**
   - *Galat:* Acidic medium ke liye dilute HCl daal dena.
   - *Sahi:* KMnO₄ HCl ko Cl₂ gas mein oxidise kar deta hai, jisse galat reading aati hai! Hamesha **dilute H₂SO₄** use hota hai.

2. **Alkaline medium mein I⁻ ka product I₂ likh dena:**
   - *Galat:* Neutral/alkaline KMnO₄ + I⁻ → I₂.
   - *Sahi:* **Acidic** KMnO₄ + I⁻ → **I₂**, par **neutral/faintly alkaline** KMnO₄ + I⁻ → **IO₃⁻ (iodate)**!

3. **Cr₂O₇²⁻ ka n-factor 3 maan lena:**
   - *Galat:* Cr +6 se +3 gaya toh n-factor = 3.
   - *Sahi:* Ek Cr₂O₇²⁻ ion mein **2 Cr atoms** hain! Isliye total electrons = 2 × 3 = **6** (n-factor = 6)!

## Quick Recap

- **K₂Cr₂O₇:** Prepared from chromite ore (FeCr₂O₄ → Na₂CrO₄ → Na₂Cr₂O₇ → K₂Cr₂O₇). Orange in acid (Cr₂O₇²⁻, Cr–O–Cr = 126°), yellow in base (CrO₄²⁻). Acidic n-factor = **6**.
- **KMnO₄:** Prepared from pyrolusite (MnO₂ → K₂MnO₄ → KMnO₄). MnO₄²⁻ = green, paramagnetic (d¹); MnO₄⁻ = purple, diamagnetic (d⁰).
- **KMnO₄ n-factors:** Acidic = **5** (→ Mn²⁺); Neutral/faintly alkaline = **3** (→ MnO₂); Strongly alkaline = **1** (→ MnO₄²⁻).
- **Acidic I⁻ → I₂**, but **Neutral/alkaline I⁻ → IO₃⁻**!

### Answers

1. Step 1: 4FeCr₂O₄ + 8Na₂CO₃ + 7O₂ → 8Na₂CrO₄ + 2Fe₂O₃ + 8CO₂
   Step 2: 2Na₂CrO₄ + 2H⁺ → Na₂Cr₂O₇ + 2Na⁺ + H₂O
   Step 3: Na₂Cr₂O₇ + 2KCl → K₂Cr₂O₇ + 2NaCl
2. Increasing pH (adding OH⁻) shifts the equilibrium from orange dichromate to **yellow chromate**: Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 2OH⁻ ⇌ 2CrO₄²⁻ + H₂O.
3. Cr₂O₇²⁻ consists of two tetrahedral CrO₄ units sharing one corner oxygen atom (Cr–O–Cr bridge) with a **Cr–O–Cr bond angle of 126°**. The six terminal Cr–O bonds are equal (163 pm) and shorter than the two bridged Cr–O bonds (179 pm).
4. Step 1: 2MnO₂ + 4KOH + O₂ → 2K₂MnO₄ + 2H₂O
   Step 2: 3MnO₄²⁻ + 4H⁺ → 2MnO₄⁻ + MnO₂ + 2H₂O (or electrolytic oxidation of MnO₄²⁻ to MnO₄⁻).
5. - **MnO₄²⁻:** Mn = +6 (3d¹), dark green, tetrahedral, **paramagnetic** (1 unpaired e⁻).
   - **MnO₄⁻:** Mn = +7 (3d⁰), deep purple, tetrahedral, **diamagnetic** (0 unpaired e⁻).
6. Because KMnO₄ is a strong oxidising agent that oxidises Cl⁻ ions from HCl into chlorine gas (Cl₂), consuming extra KMnO₄ and ruining the titration accuracy.
7. (a) 2MnO₄⁻ + 5C₂O₄²⁻ + 16H⁺ → 2Mn²⁺ + 10CO₂ + 8H₂O
   (b) 2MnO₄⁻ + 10I⁻ + 16H⁺ → 2Mn²⁺ + 5I₂ + 8H₂O
8. 2MnO₄⁻ + H₂O + I⁻ → 2MnO₂ + 2OH⁻ + IO₃⁻
9. Balanced equation: Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 3Sn²⁺ + 14H⁺ → 2Cr³⁺ + 3Sn⁴⁺ + 7H₂O.
   3 moles of Sn²⁺ lose 6 moles of electrons, which exactly match 1 mole of Cr₂O₇²⁻ (n-factor = 6). Answer: **1.0 mole of K₂Cr₂O₇**.
   Jaanch: 3 × 2e⁻ = 6e⁻ = 1 × 6e⁻ ✅
10. On heating to 513 K, KMnO₄ decomposes to potassium manganate, manganese dioxide, and oxygen gas:
    **2KMnO₄ → K₂MnO₄ + MnO₂ + O₂**.
