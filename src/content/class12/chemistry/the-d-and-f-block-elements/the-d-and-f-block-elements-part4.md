# The f-Block: Lanthanoids and Actinoids

> **Pichle topic mein:** Humne [Potassium Dichromate and Potassium Permanganate](the-d-and-f-block-elements-part3.md) ke saath d-block ka study poora kiya. Ab hum periodic table ke neeche alag se rakhi gayi do rows — **f-Block (Inner Transition Elements: Lanthanoids aur Actinoids)** — ke baare mein padhenge.

Technician ne ek toote hue smartphone speaker se chhota sa chamakdar magnet nikala jo iron ke bade magnet se bhi 10 guna zyada takatwar tha! ## Naye Shabd (Key Terms)

| Term | Hinglish Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Inner Transition Elements (f-Block)** | Woh elements jinka last electron ante-penultimate **(n−2)f** subshell mein bharta hai |
| **Lanthanoids (4f series)** | Cerium (Ce, Z = 58) se Lutetium (Lu, Z = 71) tak ke 14 elements (Lanthanum La = 57 ko bhi saath study kiya jata hai) |
| **Actinoids (5f series)** | Thorium (Th, Z = 90) se Lawrencium (Lr, Z = 103) tak ke 14 radioactive elements (Actinium Ac = 89 ke baad) |
| **Lanthanoid Contraction** | La se Lu tak atomic aur ionic radii mein hone wali steady (lagatar) kami, jo 4f electrons ki poor shielding ki wajah se hoti hai |
| **Actinoid Contraction** | Ac se Lr tak hone wali size ki kami (5f electrons ki shielding 4f se bhi kharab hone ki wajah se yeh contraction aur zyada tez hoti hai) |
| **Mischmetall** | Lanthanoid metals (~95%) + Iron (~5%) + traces of S, C, Ca, Al ka alloy (lighter flints aur Mg alloys mein use hota hai) |

## 1. The Lanthanoids (4f Series: Ce, Z = 58 to Lu, Z = 71)

- **General outer electronic configuration:** **[Xe] 4f¹⁻¹⁴ 5d⁰⁻¹ 6s²**
- Sabhi lanthanoids mein 6s² common hai; 5d¹ sirf **La (57), Ce (58), Gd (64, 4f⁷5d¹6s²), aur Lu (71, 4f¹⁴5d¹6s²)** mein hota hai (half-filled f⁷ aur fully-filled f¹⁴ stability ke liye!).
- **Common Oxidation State:** **+3** sabhi lanthanoids ki predominant aur sabse stable state hai.
- **Important Exceptional Oxidation States (+2 and +4):**
  - **Ce⁴⁺ (4f⁰, noble gas [Xe] configuration):** Stable hai, par solution mein +3 ki taraf lautna chahta hai → **strong oxidising agent** (E° Ce⁴⁺/Ce³⁺ = +1.74 V; volumetric analysis mein use hota hai).
  - **Eu²⁺ (4f⁷, half-filled) aur Yb²⁺ (4f¹⁴, fully-filled):** Stable hain, par +3 mein oxidize hone ki tendency rakhte hain → **strong reducing agents**!
  - **Tb⁴⁺ (4f⁷, half-filled):** Strong oxidising agent.

### Lanthanoid Contraction — Cause & Consequences (MOST IMPORTANT!)
**Cause:** Jaise-jaise hum La (Z = 57) se Lu (Z = 71) tak jate hain, nuclear charge +1 badhta jata hai aur naya electron **4f subshell** mein bharta hai. 4f orbitals ka shape diffuse hone ki wajah se unka **shielding (screening) effect bahut poor** hota hai! Isliye effective nuclear charge badhta hai aur outer electrons andar khinch jate hain → **atomic aur ionic (M³⁺) radii lagatar ghat-te hain** (La³⁺ = 106 pm se Lu³⁺ = 85 pm).

**Four Major Consequences of Lanthanoid Contraction:**
1. **4d aur 5d series ke radii lagbhag barabar (Twin pairs):** Jaise **Zr (160 pm) ≈ Hf (159 pm)**, Nb ≈ Ta, Mo ≈ W. Isliye Zr aur Hf ko chemical methods se alag karna bahut mushkil hota hai!
2. **Basicity of hydroxides decreases from La(OH)₃ to Lu(OH)₃:** M³⁺ ion chhota hota jata hai → covalent character badhta hai (Fajans' rule) → **La(OH)₃ sabse zyada basic** aur **Lu(OH)₃ sabse kam basic** hai!
3. **Difficulty in separation of lanthanoids:** Sabhi Ln³⁺ ions ke size aur properties lagbhag ek jaise hone ke kaaran unhe ion-exchange chromatography se alag karna padta hai.
4. **High density of 5d elements:** Mass badhta hai par volume nahi badhta → Os, Ir, Pt bahut dense hote hain.

### General Properties & Uses of Lanthanoids:
- Silvery-white soft metals, tarnish rapidly in air; hardness increases with Z (Samarium steel jitna hard hota hai).
- Coloured ions (trivalent ions mein f–f transitions; par La³⁺ (f⁰), Lu³⁺ (f¹⁴), Ce⁴⁺ (f⁰) colourless hain).
- Paramagnetic (except f⁰ aur f¹⁴).
- **Mischmetall:** ~95% lanthanoid metal + ~5% Fe + traces of S, C, Ca, Al. Mg ke saath alloy banakar bullets, shells aur lighter flints mein use hota hai.
- Mixed oxides of lanthanoids petroleum cracking mein catalysts aur TV phosphors/optical glass mein use hote hain.

## 2. The Actinoids (5f Series: Th, Z = 90 to Lr, Z = 103)

- **General outer electronic configuration:** **[Rn] 5f¹⁻¹⁴ 6d⁰⁻¹ 7s²**
- **Sabhi actinoids radioactive hain!** (Uranium ke baad wale elements — Z > 92 — **transuranic elements** kehlate hain, sab man-made hain).
- **Wide Range of Oxidation States (+3 to +7):**
  - Kyun? Kyunki **5f, 6d, aur 7s orbitals ki energies lagbhag barabar** hoti hain! Isliye saare electrons bonding mein participate kar sakte hain.
  - Common state +3 hai, par Np aur Pu **+7** tak dikhate hain (jaise U: +3, +4, +5, +6; Np: +3 to +7).
- **Actinoid Contraction:** 5f electrons ka shielding effect 4f se bhi zyada poor hota hai, isliye **actinoid contraction lanthanoid contraction se bhi zyada steep (badi)** hoti hai.
- Chemical reactivity: Highly reactive metals (especially finely divided), boiling water se oxide + hydride banate hain, dilute HCl se react karte hain, par HNO₃ se passive (protective oxide layer) ho jate hain; alkalies se react nahi karte.

---

## 3. Comparison: Lanthanoids vs Actinoids

| Property | Lanthanoids (4f) | Actinoids (5f) |
|---|---|---|
| Radioactivity | Non-radioactive (except **Promethium, Pm**) | **All** are radioactive |
| Oxidation states | Mainly +3 (occasionally +2, +4) | Wide range: +3, +4, +5, +6, +7 |
| Complex formation | Less tendency to form complexes | Much greater tendency to form complexes |
| Oxo-cations | Do NOT form oxo-cations | Form oxo-cations like **UO₂²⁺, PuO₂²⁺, UO⁺** |
| Basicity of oxides/hydroxides | Less basic than actinoids | More basic |
| Contraction magnitude | Smaller (4f shielding poor) | Greater (5f shielding even poorer) |
| Magnetic properties | Easily explained | More complex (strong orbital coupling) |

## Solved Examples

### Example 1: Explaining Oxidation States of Eu²⁺ and Ce⁴⁺
Why does Europium (Z = 63) form stable Eu²⁺ while Cerium (Z = 58) forms Ce⁴⁺? Which one is a strong reducing agent and which is a strong oxidising agent?

**Solution:**
- **Eu (Z = 63):** [Xe] 4f⁷ 6s² → losing 2 electrons gives **Eu²⁺ = [Xe] 4f⁷** (extra-stable half-filled f-subshell). Since +3 is the most stable state for lanthanoids overall, Eu²⁺ tends to lose 1 more electron (Eu²⁺ → Eu³⁺ + e⁻), acting as a **strong reducing agent**.
- **Ce (Z = 58):** [Xe] 4f¹ 5d¹ 6s² → losing 4 electrons gives **Ce⁴⁺ = [Xe] 4f⁰** (noble gas configuration). Since +3 is preferred in aqueous solution, Ce⁴⁺ readily gains an electron (Ce⁴⁺ + e⁻ → Ce³⁺), acting as a **strong oxidising agent**.

Jaanch: Eu²⁺ = 63 − 2 = 61 e⁻ = 54([Xe]) + 7(4f⁷); Ce⁴⁺ = 58 − 4 = 54 e⁻ = [Xe] ✅

---

### Example 2: Basicity Order of Lanthanoid Hydroxides
Arrange La(OH)₃, Gd(OH)₃, and Lu(OH)₃ in increasing order of basic strength. Give reason.

**Solution:**
Due to **lanthanoid contraction**, ionic radius decreases steadily from La³⁺ (106 pm) to Gd³⁺ (94 pm) to Lu³⁺ (85 pm).
According to Fajans' rule, smaller cation size increases the covalent character of the M–OH bond, making it harder to release OH⁻ ions.
Increasing order of basic strength: **Lu(OH)₃ < Gd(OH)₃ < La(OH)₃**.

Jaanch: Largest cation La³⁺ → most ionic M–OH bond → strongest base La(OH)₃ ✅

---

### Example 3: Why Actinoids Show More Oxidation States than Lanthanoids
Why do actinoids show a much wider range of oxidation states (+3 to +7) than lanthanoids (+3, +2, +4)?

**Solution:**
In actinoids, the energy gap between **5f, 6d, and 7s** subshells is very small, so electrons from all three subshells can participate in chemical bonding. In lanthanoids, the **4f** orbitals are deeply buried inside the atom and well shielded, so mostly only 6s and 5d electrons participate, restricting them largely to +3.

Jaanch: Closer energies of 5f, 6d, 7s → variable states up to +7 in Np and Pu ✅

## Aam Galtiyan (Common Mistakes)

1. **Saare lanthanoids ko non-radioactive bol dena:**
   - *Galat:* Koi bhi lanthanoid radioactive nahi hota.
   - *Sahi:* **Promethium (Pm, Z = 61)** ekmatra **radioactive lanthanoid** hai!

2. **La(OH)₃ aur Lu(OH)₃ ke basicity order ko ulta kar dena:**
   - *Galat:* Lu(OH)₃ zyada basic hai.
   - *Sahi:* Size ghatne se covalent character badhta hai, isliye **La(OH)₃ sabse zyada basic** aur **Lu(OH)₃ sabse kam basic** hai!

3. **Zr aur Hf ko ek hi period ka samajhna:**
   - *Galat:* Zr aur Hf aas-paas ke elements hain.
   - *Sahi:* Zr (Z = 40, 4d) aur Hf (Z = 72, 5d) ek hi Group 4 ke alag periods mein hain; unke size **lanthanoid contraction** ki wajah se barabar hote hain!

## Quick Recap

- **Lanthanoids (4f, Ce–Lu):** [Xe] 4f¹⁻¹⁴ 5d⁰⁻¹ 6s²; main state +3; Ce⁴⁺ (oxidising, f⁰), Eu²⁺ & Yb²⁺ (reducing, f⁷/f¹⁴); Pm is radioactive.
- **Lanthanoid Contraction:** Poor shielding by 4f electrons → Zr ≈ Hf radii, basicity La(OH)₃ > Lu(OH)₃, hard to separate.
- **Mischmetall:** ~95% lanthanoids + ~5% Fe + traces of S, C, Ca, Al.
- **Actinoids (5f, Th–Lr):** All radioactive; wide oxidation states (+3 to +7) due to small 5f–6d–7s energy gap; stronger contraction; form oxo-cations (UO₂²⁺).

## Practice Questions

1. Write the general electronic configuration of lanthanoids and actinoids.
2. What is lanthanoid contraction? Explain its underlying cause.
3. List three important consequences of lanthanoid contraction.
4. Which lanthanoid element is radioactive?
5. Why is La(OH)₃ more basic than Lu(OH)₃?
6. Explain why Eu²⁺ acts as a strong reducing agent whereas Ce⁴⁺ acts as a strong oxidising agent.
7. What is mischmetall? State its composition and one important use.
8. Give three points of difference between lanthanoids and actinoids.
9. Why is actinoid contraction greater from element to element than lanthanoid contraction?
10. Write the electronic configurations of Gd (Z = 64) and Lu³⁺ (Z = 71).

---

### Answers

1. Lanthanoids: **[Xe] 4f¹⁻¹⁴ 5d⁰⁻¹ 6s²**. Actinoids: **[Rn] 5f¹⁻¹⁴ 6d⁰⁻¹ 7s²**.
2. **Lanthanoid contraction** is the steady decrease in atomic and ionic radii from La (Z = 57) to Lu (Z = 71). **Cause:** As nuclear charge increases across the series, new electrons enter the diffuse 4f orbitals which offer very poor shielding against the nuclear pull.
3. (i) Almost identical radii of 4d and 5d transition pairs (e.g., Zr ≈ Hf), making their separation difficult. (ii) Decrease in basic strength of hydroxides from La(OH)₃ to Lu(OH)₃. (iii) Very similar chemical properties of lanthanoids, requiring ion-exchange methods for separation.
4. **Promethium (Pm, Z = 61)** is the only radioactive lanthanoid.
5. Because Lu³⁺ is smaller than La³⁺ (lanthanoid contraction), the Lu–OH bond has greater covalent character (Fajans' rule) and releases OH⁻ less easily than the more ionic La–OH bond.
6. Eu²⁺ ([Xe] 4f⁷) tends to oxidise to the most stable lanthanoid state Eu³⁺ (strong reducing agent). Ce⁴⁺ ([Xe] 4f⁰) tends to reduce to the more stable aqueous state Ce³⁺ (strong oxidising agent).
7. **Mischmetall** is an alloy of ~95% lanthanoid metals, ~5% iron, and traces of S, C, Ca, and Al. It is used in magnesium-based alloys for bullets, shells, and lighter flints.
8. (i) Lanthanoids are non-radioactive (except Pm); all actinoids are radioactive. (ii) Lanthanoids show mainly +3 state; actinoids show wide states (+3 to +7). (iii) Lanthanoids do not form oxo-cations; actinoids form oxo-cations like UO₂²⁺.
9. Because **5f electrons** are even more diffuse and offer **poorer shielding** of the nuclear charge than 4f electrons.
10. - **Gd (Z = 64):** **[Xe] 4f⁷ 5d¹ 6s²** (half-filled 4f⁷ stability).
    - **Lu³⁺ (Z = 71, 68 e⁻):** **[Xe] 4f¹⁴** (5d¹ and 6s² removed).
    Jaanch: Gd = 54 + 7 + 1 + 2 = 64; Lu³⁺ = 54 + 14 = 68 = 71 − 3 ✅
