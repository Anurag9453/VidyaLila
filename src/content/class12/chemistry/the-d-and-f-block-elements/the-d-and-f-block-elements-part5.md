## Naye Shabd (Revision Snapshot)

Is wrap-up topic mein hum sabhi key formulas aur rules ko combine karenge:
- **Ion configuration:** Remove ns first, then (n−1)d.
- **Spin-only magnetic moment:** μ = √[n(n + 2)] BM.
- **Redox n-factors:** K₂Cr₂O₇ (acidic) = 6; KMnO₄ (acidic = 5, neutral/faintly alkaline = 3, strongly alkaline = 1).
- **Contraction rules:** 4f shielding poor → Zr ≈ Hf, La(OH)₃ > Lu(OH)₃; 5f shielding even poorer → wide actinoid states (+3 to +7).

## Mixed Solved Examples

### Example 1: Combining Configuration, Colour, Magnetism & Oxidation State
An aqueous solution contains an ion M²⁺ of the 3d series (Z = 21 to 30). It is coloured, has a spin-only magnetic moment of 3.87 BM, and its M³⁺ ion is a well-known stable ion in octahedral complexes. Identify two possible candidates for M²⁺ and compare their configurations.

**Solution:**
μ = 3.87 BM → n(n + 2) = 3.87² ≈ 15 → **n = 3** unpaired electrons.
In the 3d series, configurations with 3 unpaired electrons are **3d³** and **3d⁷**:
- **V²⁺ (Z = 23):** [Ar] 3d³ (3 unpaired e⁻, violet; oxidises to V³⁺).
- **Co²⁺ (Z = 27):** [Ar] 3d⁷ (3 unpaired e⁻, pink; forms very stable Co³⁺ complexes!).
Both are coloured (incomplete d-orbitals) and give μ = √15 = 3.87 BM.

Jaanch: V²⁺ = 23−2 = 21 e⁻ ([Ar] 3d³); Co²⁺ = 27−2 = 25 e⁻ ([Ar] 3d⁷ → 2 pairs + 3 unpaired = 3) ✅

---

### Example 2: Joining KMnO₄ & K₂Cr₂O₇ Redox Stoichiometry
20.0 mL of a 0.10 M K₂Cr₂O₇ solution in acidic medium is required to completely oxidise a given sample of FeSO₄. What volume of 0.10 M KMnO₄ solution in acidic medium would be required to oxidise the exact same sample of FeSO₄?

**Solution:**
Equivalents of K₂Cr₂O₇ = M × V × n-factor = 0.10 × 20.0 × 6 = **12.0 milliequivalents**.
To oxidise the exact same FeSO₄ sample, KMnO₄ (acidic n-factor = 5) must provide the same 12.0 milliequivalents:
0.10 × V(KMnO₄) × 5 = 12.0
0.50 × V(KMnO₄) = 12.0 → **V(KMnO₄) = 24.0 mL**.

Jaanch: K₂Cr₂O₇ transfers 6 e⁻ per mole while KMnO₄ transfers 5 e⁻ per mole, so at equal molarity KMnO₄ needs 6/5 times the volume: (6/5) × 20.0 = 24.0 mL ✅

### Example 3: Reasoning Across d- and f-Blocks
Give concise chemical reasons for each:
(a) Cu⁺ salts are white and diamagnetic, whereas Cu²⁺ salts are blue and paramagnetic.
(b) Transition metals form many interstitial compounds, whereas alkali metals do not.
(c) Separation of Zr and Hf is extremely difficult.

**Solution:**
(a) **Cu⁺ ([Ar] 3d¹⁰)** has 0 unpaired electrons → diamagnetic, and no d–d transition → colourless/white. **Cu²⁺ ([Ar] 3d⁹)** has 1 unpaired electron → paramagnetic (μ = 1.73 BM), and d–d transition absorbs orange/red light → blue.
(b) Transition metals have close-packed crystal lattices with small **interstitial voids** and strong metallic bonding that accommodate small non-metal atoms (H, C, N) without collapsing; alkali metals have large atoms and weak bonding.
(c) Due to **lanthanoid contraction**, Zr (4d, 160 pm) and Hf (5d, 159 pm) have nearly identical atomic and ionic radii and almost identical chemical properties.

Jaanch: All three link directly to electronic configuration, lattice structure, and 4f shielding ✅

## Aam Galtiyan (Mixed-Problem Traps)

1. **d³ aur d⁷ dono ke liye n = 3 hona bhool jana:**
   - *Galat:* μ = 3.87 BM dekhte hi sirf Cr³⁺ ya V²⁺ bol dena aur Co²⁺ (3d⁷) ko bhool jana.
   - *Sahi:* d¹ & d⁹ (n=1), d² & d⁸ (n=2), **d³ & d⁷ (n=3)**, d⁴ & d⁶ (n=4) — hamesha dono symmetrical cases check karo!

2. **MnO₄⁻ (d⁰) ko colourless bol dena:**
   - *Galat:* Mn⁷⁺ mein 0 d-electrons hain toh KMnO₄ safed hona chahiye.
   - *Sahi:* MnO₄⁻ aur Cr₂O₇²⁻ mein d–d transition nahi hoti, par **Ligand-to-Metal Charge Transfer (O²⁻ → M)** ki wajah se woh intense purple aur orange hote hain!

## Quick Recap

- **Quick Magnetic Table:** n = 1 (1.73), n = 2 (2.83), n = 3 (3.87), n = 4 (4.90), n = 5 (5.92 BM).
- **Redox Master Ratio:** Acidic K₂Cr₂O₇ (6e⁻) : Acidic KMnO₄ (5e⁻) : Neutral KMnO₄ (3e⁻).
- **Exception Hotlist:** Cr (3d⁵4s¹), Cu (3d¹⁰4s¹), Zn/Cd/Hg (not transition), E°(Cu²⁺/Cu) = +0.34 V, Pm (radioactive lanthanoid), Zr ≈ Hf (lanthanoid contraction).

## Practice Questions

1. Identify a 3d-series metal that: (a) shows only +3 oxidation state, (b) shows the maximum number of oxidation states, (c) has the lowest enthalpy of atomisation.
2. Calculate the spin-only magnetic moment of an aqueous M²⁺ ion if its atomic number is Z = 25.
3. Explain why KMnO₄ is intensely purple even though Mn(+7) has a 3d⁰ configuration.
4. How many moles of acidified KMnO₄ are required to oxidise 2.5 moles of sodium oxalate (Na₂C₂O₄)?
5. What happens when KI solution is treated with KMnO₄ in: (a) acidic medium, (b) neutral/faintly alkaline medium? Write balanced ionic equations.
6. Why is Cr²⁺ a stronger reducing agent than Fe²⁺ in aqueous solution?
7. Arrange the following in increasing order of ionic radius: Lu³⁺, La³⁺, Pm³⁺, Gd³⁺.
8. Why do actinoids show a greater tendency to form complexes than lanthanoids?
9. Complete and balance: Cr₂O₇²⁻ + Fe²⁺ + H⁺ → ?
10. A transition metal ion M³⁺ has 23 electrons. Identify M, write the configuration of M³⁺, and find its spin-only magnetic moment.

---

### Answers

1. (a) **Scandium (Sc, Z = 21)**; (b) **Manganese (Mn, Z = 25)** (+2 to +7); (c) **Zinc (Zn, Z = 30)**.
2. Z = 25 is Mn ([Ar] 3d⁵ 4s²). Mn²⁺ = [Ar] 3d⁵ → n = 5 unpaired electrons.
   μ = √[5(5 + 2)] = √35 = **5.92 BM**.
   Jaanch: 5.92² = 35.05 ≈ 35 ✅
3. Although Mn⁷⁺ has no d-electrons (no d–d transition), an electron momentarily transfers from the filled p-orbital of O²⁻ ligand to the empty d-orbital of Mn⁷⁺ (**ligand-to-metal charge transfer**), absorbing yellow-green light and appearing intensely purple.
4. Balanced equation: 2MnO₄⁻ + 5C₂O₄²⁻ + 16H⁺ → 2Mn²⁺ + 10CO₂ + 8H₂O.
   5 mol oxalate needs 2 mol KMnO₄ → 2.5 mol oxalate needs (2/5) × 2.5 = **1.0 mole of KMnO₄**.
   Jaanch: 2.5 mol C₂O₄²⁻ loses 2.5 × 2 = 5 mol e⁻ = 1.0 mol MnO₄⁻ (5e⁻) ✅
5. (a) Acidic medium (forms I₂): 2MnO₄⁻ + 10I⁻ + 16H⁺ → 2Mn²⁺ + 5I₂ + 8H₂O.
   (b) Neutral/faintly alkaline medium (forms IO₃⁻): 2MnO₄⁻ + H₂O + I⁻ → 2MnO₂ + 2OH⁻ + IO₃⁻.
6. Cr²⁺ (d⁴) oxidises to Cr³⁺ (d³) which attains a very stable half-filled **t₂g³** configuration in aqueous octahedral complexes (E° Cr³⁺/Cr²⁺ = −0.41 V), whereas Fe²⁺ (d⁶) oxidises to Fe³⁺ (d⁵) with a positive potential (E° Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ = +0.77 V). Thus Cr²⁺ is a much stronger reducing agent.
7. Ionic radius decreases across lanthanoids (La = 57 → Pm = 61 → Gd = 64 → Lu = 71).
   Increasing order: **Lu³⁺ < Gd³⁺ < Pm³⁺ < La³⁺**.
8. Actinoid ions have **higher charge, smaller size (greater charge-to-size ratio)**, and more accessible **5f and 6d orbitals** than lanthanoids, allowing stronger ligand binding.
9. **Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 6Fe²⁺ + 14H⁺ → 2Cr³⁺ + 6Fe³⁺ + 7H₂O**.
   Jaanch: Atoms: 2 Cr, 7 O, 6 Fe, 14 H on both sides. Charge: −2 + 12 + 14 = +24 on LHS; 2(+3) + 6(+3) = +24 on RHS ✅
10. M³⁺ has 23 electrons → neutral atom M has 23 + 3 = **26 electrons → Iron (Fe)**.
    Fe³⁺ = **[Ar] 3d⁵** → n = 5 unpaired electrons → μ = √[5(7)] = √35 = **5.92 BM**.
    Jaanch: 18([Ar]) + 5 = 23 electrons ✅
