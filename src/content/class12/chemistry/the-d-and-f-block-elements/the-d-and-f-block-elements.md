## Naye Shabd (Key Terms)

| Term | Hinglish Meaning |
|---|---|
| **d-Block Elements** | Periodic table ke Groups 3 se 12 tak ke elements jinka last electron (n−1)d subshell mein bharta hai |
| **Transition Elements** | Woh d-block elements jinke paas ground state ya kisi common oxidation state mein **incompletely filled d-orbital** (d¹ se d⁹) hota hai |
| **Enthalpy of Atomisation (ΔₐH°)** | 1 mole solid metal ko completely free gaseous atoms mein todne ke liye zaroori energy |
| **Metallic Bonding (d–d bonding)** | ns electrons ke saath-saath unpaired (n−1)d electrons ka strong covalent-like metallic bond banana |
| **3d, 4d, 5d, 6d Series** | Transition elements ki chaar horizontal rows (Sc–Zn, Y–Cd, La–Hg, Ac–Cn) |

## 1. Position and Definition of Transition Elements

d-Block periodic table ke beech mein (s-block aur p-block ke beech, Groups 3 to 12) sthit hai. Isliye inhe "transition" (badlaav ke pul) elements kehte hain — yeh reactive electropositive s-block metals aur electronegative p-block non-metals ke beech properties ka bridge banate hain.

- **General outer electronic configuration:** **(n−1)d¹⁻¹⁰ ns¹⁻²**
- **Four transition series:**
  1. **3d series (4th period):** Scandium (Sc, Z = 21) to Zinc (Zn, Z = 30)
  2. **4d series (5th period):** Yttrium (Y, Z = 39) to Cadmium (Cd, Z = 48)
  3. **5d series (6th period):** Lanthanum (La, Z = 57), then Hafnium (Hf, Z = 72) to Mercury (Hg, Z = 80)
  4. **6d series (7th period):** Actinium (Ac, Z = 89), then Rutherfordium (Rf, Z = 104) to Copernicium (Cn, Z = 112)

### Why are Zn, Cd, and Hg NOT regarded as Transition Elements?
- Inki ground state configuration **(n−1)d¹⁰ ns²** hoti hai (poori tarah bhara hua d-orbital!).
- Inki common oxidation state (+2) mein bhi configuration **(n−1)d¹⁰** (completely filled) rehti hai.
- Isliye **Zn, Cd, aur Hg** d-block elements toh hain, par **transition elements nahi** maane jate!
- **Par Silver (Ag, Z = 47) aur Copper (Cu, Z = 29) transition elements HAIN!** Kyun? Ground state mein dono d¹⁰s¹ hain, par **Cu²⁺ (3d⁹)** aur **Ag²⁺ (4d⁹)** mein incomplete d-orbital hota hai!

## 2. Electronic Configuration of the 3d Series (Sc to Zn)

Har element mein [Ar] = 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ (18 electrons) common hai:

| Element | Z | Ground State Configuration | Common Ion (+2) | Unpaired e⁻ in M²⁺ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sc (Scandium) | 21 | [Ar] 3d¹ 4s² | (Sc³⁺: 3d⁰) | 0 (in Sc³⁺) |
| Ti (Titanium) | 22 | [Ar] 3d² 4s² | [Ar] 3d² | 2 |
| V (Vanadium) | 23 | [Ar] 3d³ 4s² | [Ar] 3d³ | 3 |
| **Cr (Chromium)** | **24** | **[Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹** (Exception!) | [Ar] 3d⁴ | 4 |
| Mn (Manganese) | 25 | [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s² | [Ar] 3d⁵ | 5 |
| Fe (Iron) | 26 | [Ar] 3d⁶ 4s² | [Ar] 3d⁶ | 4 |
| Co (Cobalt) | 27 | [Ar] 3d⁷ 4s² | [Ar] 3d⁷ | 3 |
| Ni (Nickel) | 28 | [Ar] 3d⁸ 4s² | [Ar] 3d⁸ | 2 |
| **Cu (Copper)** | **29** | **[Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹** (Exception!) | [Ar] 3d⁹ | 1 |
| Zn (Zinc) | 30 | [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s² | [Ar] 3d¹⁰ | 0 |

### Why do Cr (3d⁵4s¹) and Cu (3d¹⁰4s¹) show exceptional configurations?
1. **3d aur 4s orbitals ki energy lagbhag barabar** hoti hai.
2. **Half-filled (d⁵) aur fully-filled (d¹⁰) subshells extra stable** hote hain — do wajahon se:
   - **Symmetrical distribution of electrons** (kam electron-electron repulsion).
   - **Maximum Exchange Energy** (parallel-spin electrons aapas mein jagah badal sakte hain; jitne zyada exchanges, utni zyada stability).

**Important rule for ionisation:** Electrons hamesha pehle **outermost 4s** orbital se nikalte hain, phir 3d se! Jaise Fe = [Ar] 3d⁶ 4s² → Fe²⁺ = [Ar] 3d⁶ (4s⁰) → Fe³⁺ = [Ar] 3d⁵.

## 3. General Physical Properties of Transition Elements

### (a) Metallic Character, Melting & Boiling Points
- Sabhi d-block elements typical metals hain (high tensile strength, malleability, ductility, high thermal & electrical conductivity).
- **High melting points:** Unpaired (n−1)d electrons strong interatomic metallic bonds banate hain.
- Har series mein melting point **d⁵ (Cr, Mo, W)** par maximum pahunchta hai (maximum unpaired electrons = 5 + 1 = 6 in Cr/Mo/W), aur phir electron pairing shuru hone par ghat-ta hai.
- **Exceptions (Dips):**
  - **Mn (3d⁵4s²) aur Tc** ka melting point ummeed se kam hota hai — kyunki half-filled 3d⁵ electrons strongly bound (delocalise kam) hote hain aur complex crystal structure banate hain.
  - **Zn, Cd, Hg** ka melting point bahut kam hota hai (Hg toh room temp par liquid hai!) kyunki inmein **zero unpaired d-electrons** hote hain (sirf weak s–s metallic bonding).

### (b) Enthalpy of Atomisation (ΔₐH°)
- Transition metals ki **ΔₐH° bahut high** hoti hai (strong metallic bonding due to large number of unpaired d-electrons).
- 4d aur 5d series ke metals (jaise Mo, W, Os) ki ΔₐH° 3d series se bhi zyada hoti hai (zyada frequent metal–metal bonding).
- **Zn** ki ΔₐH° 3d series mein sabse kam hoti hai (126 kJ mol⁻¹) kyunki 3d¹⁰ mein koi unpaired electron metallic bonding mein hissa nahi leta.

### (c) Atomic and Ionic Radii
- Across a series (Sc → Zn):
  1. **Shuruat mein (Sc → Cr):** Atomic radius gradually **ghat-ta hai** kyunki nuclear charge (Z) badhta hai aur naye electrons andar ke 3d orbital mein jaate hain jinka shielding effect weak hota hai.
  2. **Beech mein (Fe, Co, Ni):** Atomic radius **lagbhag constant** ho jata hai kyunki badhta hua nuclear charge aur badhta hua inter-electronic repulsion aapas mein balance ho jate hain.
  3. **End mein (Cu → Zn):** Radius thoda **badh jata hai** kyunki d¹⁰ mein strong electron-electron repulsion nuclear attraction se jeet jata hai!
- **4d vs 5d radii:** 4d aur 5d series ke corresponding elements (jaise **Zr = 160 pm aur Hf = 159 pm**) ke atomic radii lagbhag **barabar** hote hain — **Lanthanoid Contraction** (intervening 4f electrons ki poor shielding) ki wajah se!

### (d) Ionisation Enthalpies (IE)
- Left to right across 3d series, IE gradually badhti hai (nuclear charge badhne se), par s- aur p-block ke muqable increase slow aur irregular hota hai.
- **IE₂ of Cr and Cu are unusually high:**
  - Cr⁺ = [Ar] 3d⁵ (half-filled stable!) → doosra electron nikalna mushkil.
  - Cu⁺ = [Ar] 3d¹⁰ (fully-filled stable!) → doosra electron nikalna bahut mushkil.
- **IE₃ of Mn²⁺ (3d⁵) and Zn²⁺ (3d¹⁰) are very high** (breaking stable d⁵/d¹⁰), jabki **IE₃ of Fe²⁺ (3d⁶ → 3d⁵)** apekshakrit kam hoti hai!

### (e) Density
- Sc se Cu tak atomic mass badhta hai aur atomic volume ghat-ta hai, isliye **density lagatar badhti hai** (Sc = 3.43 g cm⁻³ se Cu = 8.9 g cm⁻³; Zn mein volume thoda badhne se density 7.1 g cm⁻³ ho jati hai).

## Solved Examples

### Example 1: Writing Electronic Configurations of Transition Ions
Write the ground-state electronic configurations and number of unpaired electrons for: (a) Cr³⁺, (b) Fe³⁺, (c) Cu⁺, (d) Mn²⁺.

**Solution:**
(a) Cr (Z = 24) = [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹. Remove 1 e⁻ from 4s and 2 e⁻ from 3d → **Cr³⁺ = [Ar] 3d³** (3 unpaired electrons).
(b) Fe (Z = 26) = [Ar] 3d⁶ 4s². Remove 2 e⁻ from 4s and 1 e⁻ from 3d → **Fe³⁺ = [Ar] 3d⁵** (5 unpaired electrons).
(c) Cu (Z = 29) = [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹. Remove 1 e⁻ from 4s → **Cu⁺ = [Ar] 3d¹⁰** (0 unpaired electrons).
(d) Mn (Z = 25) = [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s². Remove 2 e⁻ from 4s → **Mn²⁺ = [Ar] 3d⁵** (5 unpaired electrons).

Jaanch: Total electrons check: Cr³⁺ = 18 + 3 = 21 (= 24 − 3); Fe³⁺ = 18 + 5 = 23 (= 26 − 3); Cu⁺ = 18 + 10 = 28 (= 29 − 1); Mn²⁺ = 18 + 5 = 23 (= 25 − 2) ✅

---

### Example 2: Comparing Second Ionisation Enthalpies
Why is the second ionisation enthalpy (IE₂) of copper (1958 kJ mol⁻¹) much higher than that of zinc (1733 kJ mol⁻¹), even though Zn has a higher atomic number?

**Solution:**
- After removing the first electron:
  - Cu⁺ has configuration **[Ar] 3d¹⁰** (completely filled, very stable).
  - Zn⁺ has configuration **[Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹** (one outer 4s electron, easily removed to reach stable 3d¹⁰).
- Removing an electron from the stable closed 3d¹⁰ shell of Cu⁺ requires far more energy than removing the lone 4s¹ electron from Zn⁺.

Jaanch: Cu⁺ (3d¹⁰) → Cu²⁺ (3d⁹) breaks a filled subshell; Zn⁺ (3d¹⁰4s¹) → Zn²⁺ (3d¹⁰) achieves a filled subshell ✅

---

### Example 3: Silver as a Transition Element
Silver atom has completely filled 4d orbitals ([Kr] 4d¹⁰ 5s¹) in its ground state. How can you say that it is a transition element?

**Solution:**
By definition, an element is a transition element if it has an incompletely filled d-orbital in its ground state **or in any of its common oxidation states**. Silver exhibits a **+2 oxidation state** (as in AgF₂ and AgO), in which Ag²⁺ has the configuration **[Kr] 4d⁹** (incompletely filled d-orbital). Therefore, silver is a transition element.

Jaanch: Ag²⁺ has 47 − 2 = 45 electrons → [Kr] (36) + 4d⁹ = 45 electrons (incomplete d-subshell) ✅

## Aam Galtiyan (Common Mistakes)

1. **Ion banate waqt 3d se pehle electron nikal dena:**
   - *Galat:* Fe²⁺ = [Ar] 3d⁴ 4s² likhna.
   - *Sahi:* Ionisation mein **hamesha 4s se pehle electrons nikalte hain**! Fe²⁺ = **[Ar] 3d⁶ 4s⁰** (ya sirf [Ar] 3d⁶).

2. **Saare d-block elements ko transition elements bol dena:**
   - *Galat:* Zinc (Zn) ek transition element hai.
   - *Sahi:* Zn, Cd, Hg **d-block elements hain, par transition elements nahi** (kyunki ground state aur +2 state dono mein d¹⁰ complete hai).

3. **Cr aur Cu ki configuration 3d⁴4s² aur 3d⁹4s² likh dena:**
   - *Galat:* Cr = [Ar] 3d⁴ 4s², Cu = [Ar] 3d⁹ 4s².
   - *Sahi:* Exchange energy aur symmetry ki wajah se Cr = **[Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹** aur Cu = **[Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹** hota hai!

## Quick Recap

- **Transition elements:** Incomplete (n−1)d orbital in ground state or common oxidation state. Zn, Cd, Hg excluded; Cu, Ag, Au included.
- **3d exceptions:** Cr ([Ar] 3d⁵4s¹) and Cu ([Ar] 3d¹⁰4s¹).
- **Ion formation:** Always remove ns electrons first, then (n−1)d electrons.
- **High MP & ΔₐH°:** Due to strong metallic bonding involving unpaired (n−1)d electrons; dips at Mn and Zn.
- **Radii:** Decrease Sc → Cr, nearly constant Fe–Co–Ni, slight rise Cu → Zn; 4d ≈ 5d (Zr ≈ Hf) due to lanthanoid contraction.

## Practice Questions

1. Write the general outer electronic configuration of d-block elements.
2. Explain why Zn, Cd, and Hg are not considered transition elements.
3. Write the electronic configurations of Ti³⁺ (Z = 22), V²⁺ (Z = 23), Co²⁺ (Z = 27), and Ni²⁺ (Z = 28).
4. Why does zinc have the lowest enthalpy of atomisation in the 3d transition series?
5. Why is the third ionisation enthalpy (IE₃) of manganese (Z = 25) exceptionally high compared to iron (Z = 26)?
6. Why do atomic radii of Fe, Co, and Ni remain almost constant across the middle of the 3d series?
7. Why do Zr (4d) and Hf (5d) have almost identical atomic radii (160 pm and 159 pm)?
8. Which element in the 3d series has the highest melting point, and why?
9. Why is mercury (Hg) a liquid at room temperature?
10. Count the number of unpaired electrons in: (a) Sc³⁺, (b) Cr²⁺, (c) Fe²⁺, (d) Cu²⁺.

---

### Answers

1. General outer electronic configuration of d-block elements is **(n−1)d¹⁻¹⁰ ns¹⁻²** (except Pd which is [Kr] 4d¹⁰ 5s⁰).
2. Because Zn, Cd, and Hg have completely filled **(n−1)d¹⁰** orbitals both in their ground state ((n−1)d¹⁰ ns²) and in their only common oxidation state (+2, (n−1)d¹⁰).
3. - Ti³⁺ (22 − 3 = 19 e⁻): **[Ar] 3d¹**
   - V²⁺ (23 − 2 = 21 e⁻): **[Ar] 3d³**
   - Co²⁺ (27 − 2 = 25 e⁻): **[Ar] 3d⁷**
   - Ni²⁺ (28 − 2 = 26 e⁻): **[Ar] 3d⁸**
   Jaanch: 18+1=19, 18+3=21, 18+7=25, 18+8=26 ✅
4. Zinc has configuration [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s² with **zero unpaired d-electrons**. Since d-electrons do not participate in interatomic metallic bonding, metallic bonds in Zn are very weak, giving the lowest ΔₐH°.
5. Mn²⁺ has the highly stable half-filled **[Ar] 3d⁵** configuration, so removing a 3rd electron requires very high energy. In contrast, Fe²⁺ ([Ar] 3d⁶) easily loses a 3rd electron to attain the stable half-filled **[Ar] 3d⁵** configuration (Fe³⁺), so IE₃ of Fe is much lower.
6. In the middle of the series (Fe, Co, Ni), the increase in effective nuclear charge (which pulls electrons inward) is nearly balanced by the increased shielding and inter-electronic repulsion among the added 3d electrons, keeping atomic radii almost constant.
7. Due to **Lanthanoid Contraction** — the 14 intervening 4f electrons before Hf shield the nuclear charge very poorly, causing a steady decrease in size that cancels the expected increase from 4d to 5d.
8. **Chromium (Cr, Z = 24)** has the highest melting point in the 3d series (1857°C) because its [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹ configuration has the maximum number of unpaired electrons (6), creating the strongest interatomic metallic bonding.
9. Mercury ([Xe] 4f¹⁴ 5d¹⁰ 6s²) has no unpaired d-electrons and tightly held 6s² electrons (inert/relativistic contraction), resulting in extremely weak interatomic metallic bonds and a melting point of −39°C.
10. (a) Sc³⁺ ([Ar] 3d⁰): **0** unpaired e⁻
    (b) Cr²⁺ ([Ar] 3d⁴): **4** unpaired e⁻
    (c) Fe²⁺ ([Ar] 3d⁶): **4** unpaired e⁻ (one pair + 4 unpaired)
    (d) Cu²⁺ ([Ar] 3d⁹): **1** unpaired e⁻
    Jaanch: d⁶ has 5 orbitals → 1 filled (2 e⁻) + 4 half-filled = 4 unpaired; d⁹ has 4 filled + 1 half-filled = 1 unpaired ✅
