## Decision Map: Kaunsa Formula Kab Lagayein?

| Sawaal mein kya dikhe? | Kaunsa tool chuno? | Key Formula |
|---|---|---|
| Head-on α-scattering closest approach | Energy Conservation (Convert MeV → J!) | r₀ = (2 k Z e²) / K (1 MeV = 1.6 × 10⁻¹³ J) |
| Bohr orbit radius, speed, energy | Scaling with n and Z | r_n = 0.53 (n²/Z) Å; v_n ∝ Z/n; E_n = −13.6 Z²/n² eV |
| KE, PE & Total Energy in an orbit | Exact sign relations | K = −E_n (+ve); U = 2 E_n (−ve) |
| Photon emitted in n_i → n_f jump | Bohr 3rd postulate / Rydberg | ΔE = E_i − E_f; λ(nm) = 1240/ΔE(eV); 1/λ = R(1/n_f² − 1/n_i²) |
| Number of spectral lines from state n | Combinatorics | N = n(n − 1) / 2 |
| Standing wave in n-th orbit | de Broglie + Bohr | 2π r_n = n λ ⟹ L = n h / (2π) |

## Mixed Solved Examples (Sab Kuch Mila Kar!)

### Example 1: Hydrogen Photon Causing Photoelectric Effect!
An electron in a Hydrogen atom jumps from **n = 3** to **n = 1**. The emitted photon falls on a metal plate of work function **φ₀ = 4.09 eV**. Find (a) the energy and wavelength of the emitted photon, and (b) the stopping potential **V₀** of the photoelectrons!

**Hal (Solution):**
- (a) E₃ = −1.51 eV, E₁ = −13.60 eV ⟹ **ΔE = −1.51 − (−13.60) = 12.09 eV**.
  Wavelength: **λ = 1240 / 12.09 ≈ 102.6 nm** (Lyman series — UV!).
- (b) Einstein's photoelectric equation:
  K_max = e V₀ = ΔE − φ₀ = 12.09 − 4.09 = **8.0 eV** ⟹ **V₀ = 8.0 V**!
- **Jaanch:** 13.6 − 1.51 = 12.09 eV; 12.09 − 4.09 = 8.0 eV ✅.

### Example 2: Absorbing 12.75 eV Energy — Spectral Lines Emitted
Hydrogen atoms in the ground state (**E₁ = −13.6 eV**) absorb **12.75 eV** of energy.
(a) To which excited level **n** do they jump?
(b) How many total spectral lines can be emitted as they return to the ground state?

**Hal (Solution):**
- (a) Final energy: E_n = −13.6 + 12.75 = **−0.85 eV**.
  −13.6 / n² = −0.85 ⟹ n² = 13.6 / 0.85 = **16** ⟹ **n = 4**!
- (b) Total spectral lines: **N = n(n − 1)/2 = 4(3)/2 = 6 lines** (3 Lyman + 2 Balmer + 1 Paschen)!
- **Jaanch:** −13.6 / 16 = −0.85 eV; 4 × 3 / 2 = 6 ✅.

## Aam Galtiyan (Synthesis Mistakes)

1. **Collection of atoms mein total lines n − 1 gin lena:**
   - *Galat:* n = 4 se neeche aane par sirf 3 lines (4→3, 3→2, 2→1) niklengi!
   - *Sahi:* Bahut saare atoms alag-alag raaste chunte hain ⟹ total distinct lines **n(n − 1)/2 = 6**!
2. **Excitation energy aur Ionisation energy ko confuse karna:**
   - *Galat:* 1st excitation energy ko 13.6 eV bol dena!
   - *Sahi:* **1st excitation energy** = n=1 se n=2 (**10.2 eV**); **Ionisation energy** = n=1 se n=∞ (**13.6 eV**)!
3. **He⁺ (Z = 2) ke liye bhi −13.6 / n² laga dena:**
   - *Galat:* Z² multiply karna bhool jaana!
   - *Sahi:* Hydrogen-like ions ke liye **E_n = −13.6 Z² / n² eV** aur **r_n = 0.53 (n² / Z) Å**!

## Practice Questions (Mixed Chapter Challenge)

1. An alpha particle of kinetic energy 4.0 MeV approaches a copper nucleus (Z = 29) head-on. Calculate its distance of closest approach r₀.
2. In the ground state (n = 1) of Hydrogen, E₁ = −13.6 eV. Find the kinetic energy and potential energy of the electron in this state.
3. Calculate the wavelength of the second line (H_β: n = 4 → n = 2) of the Balmer series of Hydrogen (use hc = 1240 eV nm).
4. A photon emitted during the n = 2 → n = 1 transition in Hydrogen falls on a metal of work function 4.2 eV. Find the stopping potential V₀ of the emitted photoelectrons.
5. Find the ratio of the radius of the n = 2 orbit of He⁺ (Z = 2) to the radius of the n = 1 orbit of Hydrogen (Z = 1).
6. Hydrogen atoms in the ground state are bombarded by 12.5 eV electrons (inelastic collisions). What is the highest orbit n to which the Hydrogen atoms can be excited, and how many spectral lines will be emitted?
7. Find the de Broglie wavelength of an electron in the **1st Bohr orbit** (r₁ = 0.53 Å) of a Hydrogen atom using 2π r₁ = 1 × λ (take π = 3.14).
8. What is the ratio of the longest wavelength of the Lyman series (2 → 1) to the longest wavelength of the Balmer series (3 → 2)?
9. State whether True or False: As an electron in a Hydrogen atom jumps from n = 1 to n = 3, its kinetic energy decreases while its potential energy increases.
10. Find the ionisation energy of a Li²⁺ ion (Z = 3) in its ground state (n = 1).

### Answers

1. K = 4.0 × 1.6 × 10⁻¹³ = 6.4 × 10⁻¹³ J.
   2 k Z e² = 2 × (9 × 10⁹) × 29 × (2.56 × 10⁻³⁸) = 522 × 2.56 × 10⁻²⁹ = 1.336 × 10⁻²⁶ J m.
   r₀ = (1.336 × 10⁻²⁶) / (6.4 × 10⁻¹³) ≈ **2.09 × 10⁻¹⁴ m**.
   Jaanch: 522 × 2.56 = 1336.32; 13.36 / 6.4 ≈ 2.088 ✅.

2. **K = −E₁ = +13.6 eV**; **U = 2 E₁ = −27.2 eV**.
   Jaanch: +13.6 − 27.2 = −13.6 eV ✅.

3. 4 → 2: ΔE = E₄ − E₂ = −0.85 − (−3.40) = **2.55 eV**.
   λ = 1240 / 2.55 ≈ **486 nm** (Blue-green visible line!).
   Jaanch: 2.55 × 486 ≈ 1239.3 ≈ 1240 ✅.

4. 2 → 1 photon energy: ΔE = −3.40 − (−13.60) = **10.2 eV**.
   K_max = e V₀ = 10.2 − 4.2 = **6.0 eV** ⟹ **V₀ = 6.0 V**.
   Jaanch: 10.2 − 4.2 = 6.0 V ✅.

5. r_n ∝ n² / Z:
   He⁺ (n = 2, Z = 2): 2² / 2 = **2**. Hydrogen (n = 1, Z = 1): 1² / 1 = **1**.
   Ratio = **2 : 1**.
   Jaanch: 4/2 = 2 ✅.

6. 1→2 needs 10.2 eV; 1→3 needs 12.09 eV; 1→4 needs 12.75 eV (> 12.5 eV, not enough!).
   Isliye highest orbit **n = 3**!
   Total spectral lines: N = 3(2)/2 = **3 lines** (3→2, 3→1, 2→1).
   Jaanch: 12.09 < 12.5 < 12.75 ⟹ n = 3 ✅.

7. 2π r₁ = 1 × λ ⟹ λ = 2 × 3.14 × 0.53 Å ≈ **3.33 Å = 0.333 nm**.
   Jaanch: 6.28 × 0.53 = 3.3284 Å ✅.

8. ΔE_Lyman(2→1) = 10.2 eV (= 3/4 × 13.6); ΔE_Balmer(3→2) = 1.89 eV (= 5/36 × 13.6).
   λ ∝ 1 / ΔE ⟹ **λ_Lyman / λ_Balmer = (5/36) / (3/4) = 5 / 27 ≈ 0.185**!
   Jaanch: 121.6 nm / 656 nm ≈ 0.1854; 5/27 = 0.1852 ✅.

9. **True ✅.** n = 1 se n = 3 jaane par K (+13.6 → +1.51 eV) **ghatti** hai, aur U (−27.2 → −3.02 eV, less negative!) **badhti** hai!

10. Z = 3, n = 1: E₁ = −13.6 × (3)² / 1² = −13.6 × 9 = −122.4 eV ⟹ Ionisation energy = **+122.4 eV**.
    Jaanch: 13.6 × 9 = 122.4 eV ✅.

## Chapter Ka Saar: Atoms

| Topic | Mukhya Formula / Niyam | Yaad Rakhne Ki Khaas Baat |
|---|---|---|
| Rutherford Scattering | r₀ = (2 k Z e²) / K; b = 0 ⟹ θ = 180° | Proved tiny nucleus (~10⁻¹⁵ m); failed on stability & line spectra |
| Bohr's Hydrogen Model | L = nh/(2π); r_n = 0.53 n²/Z Å; v_n ∝ Z/n; E_n = −13.6 Z²/n² eV | K = −E_n (+ve); U = 2E_n (−ve); no radiation in stationary orbits |
| Hydrogen Spectral Series | 1/λ = R(1/n_f² − 1/n_i²); λ(nm) = 1240/ΔE(eV) | Lyman (UV), Balmer (Visible), Paschen/Brackett/Pfund (IR); N = n(n−1)/2 |
| de Broglie Standing Wave | 2π r_n = n λ ⟹ L = n h / (2π) | Explains Bohr's 2nd postulate; Bohr model works only for 1-e⁻ atoms |
