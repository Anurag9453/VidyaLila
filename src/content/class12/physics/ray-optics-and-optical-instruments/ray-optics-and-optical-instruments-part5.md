## Naye Shabd (New Words)

- **Least Distance of Distinct Vision (D):** Normal healthy human eye bina strain ke jitni kam se kam doori par saaf dekh sakti hai: **D = 25 cm**.
- **Angular Magnification / Magnifying Power (M):** Instrument se dekhne par image dwara aankh par bana angle (β) aur bina instrument (naked eye) se object dwara bana angle (α) ka ratio: **M = β / α**.
- **Objective Lens (f_o):** Woh lens jo **object** ki taraf hota hai.
- **Eyepiece / Ocular (f_e):** Woh lens jiske paas hum apni **aankh** lagakar dekhte hain (hamesha ek simple magnifier ki tarah kaam karta hai!).

---

## 1. Simple Microscope (Magnifying Glass)

Yeh sirf ek chhoti focal length **f** wala **single convex lens** hota hai! Object ko lens aur uske focus ke beech (**u < f**) rakhte hain taaki virtual, erect aur magnified image bane:
1. **Final image at Near Point (v = −D = −25 cm — maximum magnification):**
   **M = 1 + D / f**
2. **Final image at Infinity (Relaxed / Normal Eye — bina aankh thake):**
   **M = D / f**

---

## 2. Compound Microscope (Do Convex Lenses!)

Nanhi cheezon (bacteria, cells) ko 50× se 1000× tak bada dekhne ke liye **do convex lenses** use hote hain:
- **Objective (f_o):** **Bahut chhoti focal length** aur chhota aperture — object ka real, inverted, magnified image **A'B'** banata hai!
- **Eyepiece (f_e):** Thodi badi focal length — us image A'B' ko object maankar ek final virtual, bahut badi image banata hai!

### Magnifying Power (M = m_o × m_e):
- **General exact form (image at D):** **M = (v_o / u_o) × (1 + D / f_e)** (magnitude)
- **Standard tube-length approximation (L = tube length = distance between second focal point of objective and first focal point of eyepiece):**
  - **Image at Near Point (D):** **M = (L / f_o) × (1 + D / f_e)**
  - **Image at Infinity (Normal Adjustment):** **M = (L / f_o) × (D / f_e)**

*(High magnification ke liye **f_o aur f_e dono chhote** hone chahiye!)*

---

## 3. Astronomical Refracting Telescope

Door ke taaron aur grahon ko dekhne ke liye:
- **Objective (f_o):** **Bahut BADI focal length** aur **bahut BADA aperture (diameter)** (taaki door ke dhundhle taare se zyada se zyada light ikattha ho sake aur resolution achha ho!).
- **Eyepiece (f_e):** **Chhoti focal length**!

### Normal Adjustment (Final Image at Infinity — Sabse Aam Setting!):
- **Magnifying Power:** **M = f_o / f_e**  *(magnitude; with sign −f_o/f_e kyunki final image inverted hoti hai!)*
- **Length of Telescope Tube:** **L = f_o + f_e**
- *(Agar final image Near Point D par bane: **M = (f_o / f_e) × (1 + f_e / D)**!)*

---

## 4. Reflecting Telescope (Cassegrain Telescope)

Bade-bade modern observatories (jaise Hubble aur James Webb Space Telescope!) lenses ki jagah **bade concave parabolic mirror** ko objective banate hain!

### Reflecting Telescope ke 3 Bade Faayde (Refracting ke Muqable):
1. **Zero Chromatic Aberration:** Mirrors mein refraction nahi hota, isliye rang bikharne (chromatic aberration) ki koi samasya nahi hoti!
2. **No Spherical Aberration:** Parabolic mirror use karne se spherical aberration bhi khatam ho jaata hai!
3. **Lighter, Cheaper & Stronger Support:** Bade glass lens ko sirf kinaron se pakadna padta hai (bhaari hokar jhuk jaata hai), jabki bade mirror ko poore peeche se support kiya ja sakta hai — isliye bahut bade aperture banana aasaan aur sasta hota hai!

## Solved Examples

### Example 1: Simple Microscope
A simple microscope uses a convex lens of focal length **f = 5 cm**. Find its magnifying power when the final image is formed at (a) the least distance of distinct vision (**D = 25 cm**), and (b) **infinity**.

**Hal (Solution):**
- (a) Near point: **M = 1 + D / f = 1 + 25 / 5 = 1 + 5 = 6**.
- (b) Infinity (relaxed eye): **M = D / f = 25 / 5 = 5**.
- **Jaanch:** 25 / 5 = 5; 1 + 5 = 6 ✅.

### Example 2: Compound Microscope (Normal Adjustment)
A compound microscope has an objective of focal length **f_o = 2.0 cm**, an eyepiece of focal length **f_e = 5.0 cm**, and tube length **L = 20 cm**. Find its magnifying power when the final image is formed at infinity (D = 25 cm).

**Hal (Solution):**
- **M = (L / f_o) × (D / f_e) = (20 / 2.0) × (25 / 5.0) = 10 × 5 = 50**.
- **Jaanch:** m_o = 10, m_e = 5 ⟹ 10 × 5 = 50 ✅.

### Example 3: Astronomical Refracting Telescope
An astronomical telescope in normal adjustment has an objective of focal length **f_o = 120 cm** and an eyepiece of focal length **f_e = 6.0 cm**. Calculate (a) its magnifying power **M**, and (b) the length **L** of the telescope tube.

**Hal (Solution):**
- (a) **M = f_o / f_e = 120 / 6.0 = 20**.
- (b) **L = f_o + f_e = 120 + 6.0 = 126 cm = 1.26 m**.
- **Jaanch:** 120 / 6 = 20; 120 + 6 = 126 cm ✅.

## Aam Galtiyan (Common Mistakes)

1. **Microscope aur Telescope ke Objective ki focal length ulta bol dena:**
   - *Galat:* Microscope mein bada f_o aur telescope mein chhota f_o chahiye!
   - *Sahi:* **Microscope:** **chhota f_o** (M ∝ 1/f_o); **Telescope:** **bada f_o** (M = f_o/f_e) aur bada aperture!
2. **Near point (D) aur Infinity (relaxed eye) formulas ko mix kar dena:**
   - *Galat:* Relaxed eye (infinity) mein 1 + D/f laga dena!
   - *Sahi:* **Near point (D = 25 cm): 1 + D/f**; **Infinity (relaxed eye): D/f**!
3. **Sochna ki Reflecting Telescope mein bhi Chromatic Aberration hota hai:**
   - *Galat:* Har telescope mein rangon ka blur (chromatic aberration) hota hai.
   - *Sahi:* Reflecting telescope mein **mirror** use hota hai — reflection mein sabhi wavelengths same angle par reflect hoti hain ⟹ **ZERO chromatic aberration**!

## Quick Recap

| Instrument | Normal Adjustment (Image at ∞) | Image at Near Point (D = 25 cm) | Key Design Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple Microscope | M = D / f | M = 1 + D / f | Single convex lens, small f |
| Compound Microscope | M = (L/f_o)(D/f_e) | M = (L/f_o)(1 + D/f_e) | Both f_o and f_e **small** |
| Refracting Telescope | M = f_o / f_e; **L = f_o + f_e** | M = (f_o/f_e)(1 + f_e/D) | **Large f_o & large aperture**, small f_e |
| Reflecting Telescope | M = f_o / f_e (mirror f_o = R/2) | — | **No chromatic aberration**, cheaper & lighter |

## Practice Questions

1. What is the least distance of distinct vision (D) for a normal human eye?
2. A magnifying glass has a focal length of 10 cm. Calculate its maximum magnifying power (at D = 25 cm) and its magnifying power for a relaxed eye (at infinity).
3. Why do we prefer **small focal lengths** for both the objective and eyepiece of a compound microscope?
4. A compound microscope has f_o = 1.0 cm, f_e = 2.5 cm, and tube length L = 15 cm. Calculate its magnifying power when the final image is formed at (a) infinity, and (b) the near point D = 25 cm.
5. An astronomical telescope has an objective of focal length 150 cm and an eyepiece of focal length 5 cm. Find its magnifying power and tube length in normal adjustment.
6. In Question 5, find the magnifying power if the final image is formed at the least distance of distinct vision (D = 25 cm).
7. Why should the objective of an astronomical telescope have a **large aperture (diameter)**?
8. State three advantages of a reflecting telescope (Cassegrain type) over a refracting telescope.
9. State whether True or False: In normal adjustment of an astronomical refracting telescope, the distance between the objective and eyepiece equals f_o − f_e.
10. A small telescope has an objective of focal length 144 cm and an eyepiece of focal length 6.0 cm. What is its magnifying power and the separation between the two lenses in normal adjustment?

### Answers

1. Normal healthy human eye ke liye **D = 25 cm**.

2. (a) Max M (at D = 25 cm): M = 1 + 25 / 10 = 1 + 2.5 = **3.5**.
   (b) Relaxed eye (at ∞): M = 25 / 10 = **2.5**.
   Jaanch: 25/10 = 2.5; 1 + 2.5 = 3.5 ✅.

3. Compound microscope ki magnifying power **M ≈ (L / f_o) × (D / f_e)** dono f_o aur f_e ke **inversely proportional** hoti hai — isliye jitne chhote f_o aur f_e honge, utni zyada magnification milegi!

4. (a) At infinity: M = (15 / 1.0) × (25 / 2.5) = 15 × 10 = **150**.
   (b) At near point: M = (15 / 1.0) × (1 + 25 / 2.5) = 15 × 11 = **165**.
   Jaanch: 15 × 10 = 150; 15 × 11 = 165 ✅.

5. Normal adjustment:
   **M = f_o / f_e = 150 / 5 = 30**.
   **L = f_o + f_e = 150 + 5 = 155 cm**.
   Jaanch: 150/5 = 30; 150 + 5 = 155 cm ✅.

6. At near point D = 25 cm:
   M = (f_o / f_e) × (1 + f_e / D) = 30 × (1 + 5 / 25) = 30 × 1.2 = **36**.
   Jaanch: 1 + 0.2 = 1.2; 30 × 1.2 = 36 ✅.

7. Bada aperture door ke dhundhle taaron se **zyada light gather** karta hai (bright image!) aur telescope ki **resolving power** (do paas-paas taaron ko alag-alag dekhne ki shamta) badhata hai!

8. (i) **No chromatic aberration** (mirrors do not disperse light); (ii) **No spherical aberration** (parabolic mirror); (iii) **Lighter, cheaper & easy to support** from behind for giant apertures.

9. **False ❌.** Normal adjustment mein dono lenses ke beech ki doori (tube length) **L = f_o + f_e** hoti hai, f_o − f_e nahi!

10. **M = 144 / 6.0 = 24**; Separation **L = 144 + 6.0 = 150 cm = 1.50 m**.
    Jaanch: 24 × 6 = 144; 144 + 6 = 150 cm ✅.
