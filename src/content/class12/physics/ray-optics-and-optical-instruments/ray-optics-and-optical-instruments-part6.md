## Decision Map: Kaunsa Formula Kab Lagayein?

| Sawaal mein kya dikhe? | Kaunsa tool chuno? | Key Formula |
|---|---|---|
| Concave / Convex mirror | Mirror formula (PLUS!) | 1/v + 1/u = 1/f; m = −v/u; f = R/2 |
| Pool depth / coin raised / optical fibre / diamond | Apparent depth & TIR | d_app = d_real/n; sin i_c = 1/n (Denser → Rarer!) |
| Grinding lens / dipping lens in liquid | Lens Maker's Formula | 1/f = (n_lens/n_med − 1)(1/R₁ − 1/R₂) |
| Thin lens & contact lenses | Lens formula (MINUS!) & Power | 1/v − 1/u = 1/f; m = +v/u; P = 100/f(cm); P = P₁ + P₂ |
| Triangular prism minimum deviation | Exact Prism Formula | n = sin[(A + D_m)/2] / sin(A/2); Thin: δ = (n−1)A |
| Microscope / Telescope | Optical Instruments | Micro: (L/f_o)(D/f_e); Tele: M = f_o/f_e, L = f_o + f_e |

## Mixed Solved Examples (Sab Kuch Mila Kar!)

### Example 1: Convex Lens Dipped in Higher-Index Liquid (Nature Flips!)
A double convex glass lens (**n_g = 1.5**) has focal length **+20 cm** in air. What is its focal length and nature when immersed in carbon disulphide liquid of refractive index **n_l = 1.6**?

**Hal (Solution):**
- Air mein: 1/f_air = (1.5 − 1)(1/R₁ − 1/R₂) = 0.5 × K = 1/20 ⟹ **K = (1/R₁ − 1/R₂) = 1/10 cm⁻¹**.
- Liquid (n_l = 1.6) mein:
  n_rel = n_g / n_l = 1.5 / 1.6 = 15 / 16.
  1/f_liq = (15/16 − 1) × (1/10) = (−1/16) × (1/10) = **−1/160 cm⁻¹** ⟹ **f_liq = −160 cm**!
- Minus sign batata hai ki ab yeh convex lens **Diverging (Concave) lens** ki tarah behave karega!
- **Jaanch:** 15/16 − 1 = −1/16; (−1/16)(1/10) = −1/160 ⟹ −160 cm ✅.

### Example 2: Convex Lens + Plane Mirror (Auto-Collimation!)
An object needle is placed **15 cm** in front of a convex lens. A plane mirror is placed just behind the lens, and the final inverted image coincides exactly with the needle itself! What is the focal length and power of the convex lens?

**Hal (Solution):**
- Image tabhi object par wapas banti hai jab lens se guzarne ke baad rays **parallel** ho jaayein aur plane mirror se **90°** par takrakar apna hi raasta retrace karein!
- Rays parallel tabhi hoti hain jab object lens ke **Focus** par rakha ho ⟹ **f = +15 cm**!
- Power: **P = 100 / 15 ≈ +6.67 D**.
- **Jaanch:** u = −15 cm, f = +15 cm ⟹ v = ∞ (parallel rays hit plane mirror normally and retrace!) ✅.

### Example 3: Choosing Lenses for a Telescope vs Microscope
You are given three convex lenses of powers **+1 D**, **+10 D**, and **+20 D**. Which two lenses should you choose as objective and eyepiece to make:
(a) the best **astronomical telescope** (maximum magnification), and
(b) the best **compound microscope**?

**Hal (Solution):**
- Focal lengths: f₁ = 100/1 = **100 cm**; f₂ = 100/10 = **10 cm**; f₃ = 100/20 = **5 cm**.
- (a) **Telescope** needs **largest f_o** and **smallest f_e** ⟹ **Objective = +1 D (100 cm)**, **Eyepiece = +20 D (5 cm)**!
  Magnification **M = 100 / 5 = 20**; Tube length **L = 100 + 5 = 105 cm**.
- (b) **Compound Microscope** needs **both f_o and f_e small** (smallest for objective!) ⟹ **Objective = +20 D (5 cm)**, **Eyepiece = +10 D (10 cm)**!
- **Jaanch:** Telescope M = 100/5 = 20 (highest possible pair!); Microscope uses two smallest focal lengths ✅.

## Aam Galtiyan (Synthesis Mistakes)

1. **Convex lens ko har medium mein hamesha converging maan lena:**
   - *Galat:* Convex shape hai to hamesha +f hi rahega!
   - *Sahi:* Agar surrounding liquid ka refractive index lens se zyada ho (**n_liquid > n_lens**), to convex lens **diverging (−f)** ban jaata hai!
2. **Telescope mein M = f_e / f_o ulta kar dena:**
   - *Galat:* Telescope ki magnification 5/100 = 0.05 nikaal dena!
   - *Sahi:* Telescope ke liye **M = f_o / f_e > 1** (bada f_o upar, chhota f_e neeche)!
3. **Chromatic aberration aur Spherical aberration ko ek hi samajhna:**
   - *Galat:* Dono ek hi defect ke do naam hain.
   - *Sahi:* **Chromatic aberration** = alag-alag rangon (wavelengths) ka alag-alag focus par milna (sirf lenses mein!); **Spherical aberration** = kinare (marginal) aur beech (paraxial) rays ka alag focus par milna (spherical mirrors aur lenses dono mein!).

## Practice Questions (Mixed Chapter Challenge)

1. A concave mirror of focal length 12 cm forms a real image 3 times the size of the object. Find the object distance u and image distance v.
2. A coin lies at the bottom of a beaker containing water (n = 4/3) of depth 16 cm. What is its apparent depth when viewed vertically from above?
3. Calculate the critical angle (sin i_c) for light going from diamond (n = 2.4) into air.
4. A double convex lens (n = 1.5) has focal length +15 cm in air. Find its new focal length when immersed in water (n = 4/3).
5. Two thin lenses of powers +6 D and −2 D are placed in contact. Find the focal length of the combination and the image distance for an object placed 50 cm in front of it.
6. An equilateral prism (A = 60°) has refractive index n = √2. Find its angle of minimum deviation D_m and the angle of incidence i at minimum deviation.
7. A compound microscope has f_o = 1.5 cm, f_e = 5.0 cm, and tube length L = 18 cm. Find its magnifying power in normal adjustment (D = 25 cm).
8. An astronomical telescope in normal adjustment has magnifying power 15 and tube length 80 cm. Find the focal lengths f_o and f_e of its objective and eyepiece.
9. State whether True or False: If a convex glass lens (n = 1.5) is dipped in a liquid of refractive index 1.5, its focal length becomes zero.
10. A 45°–90°–45° prism is made of glass of refractive index 1.5. Why does a light ray falling normally on one of its shorter faces undergo Total Internal Reflection at the hypotenuse face?

### Answers

1. Real image ⟹ m = −3 = −v/u ⟹ v = 3u. f = −12 cm:
   1/(3u) + 1/u = 1/(−12) ⟹ 4/(3u) = −1/12 ⟹ **u = −16 cm**, **v = 3(−16) = −48 cm**.
   Jaanch: −1/48 − 1/16 = −4/48 = −1/12 ✅.

2. d_app = 16 / (4/3) = 16 × 3 / 4 = **12 cm**.
   Jaanch: 12 × 4/3 = 16 cm ✅.

3. sin i_c = 1 / 2.4 = 10 / 24 = **5 / 12 ≈ 0.417** (i_c ≈ 24.6°).
   Jaanch: 5/12 ≈ 0.4167 ✅.

4. Glass (1.5) in water (4/3) ⟹ f_water = 4 × f_air = 4 × 15 = **+60 cm**.
   Jaanch: (1.5 − 1)/(9/8 − 1) = 0.5/0.125 = 4; 4 × 15 = 60 cm ✅.

5. P_eq = +6 − 2 = +4 D ⟹ **f_eq = 100 / 4 = +25 cm**.
   u = −50 cm (= −2f!) ⟹ 1/v = 1/25 + 1/(−50) = +1/50 ⟹ **v = +50 cm** (Real, inverted, same size!).
   Jaanch: 1/50 − 1/(−50) = 2/50 = 1/25 ✅.

6. √2 = sin[(60° + D_m)/2] / sin 30° ⟹ sin[(60° + D_m)/2] = √2 / 2 = 1/√2 = sin 45°
   ⟹ (60° + D_m)/2 = 45° ⟹ **D_m = 30°**; **i = (60° + 30°)/2 = 45°**.
   Jaanch: sin 45° / sin 30° = √2 ✅.

7. M = (L / f_o) × (D / f_e) = (18 / 1.5) × (25 / 5.0) = 12 × 5 = **60**.
   Jaanch: 18 / 1.5 = 12; 12 × 5 = 60 ✅.

8. M = f_o / f_e = 15 ⟹ f_o = 15 f_e.
   L = f_o + f_e = 80 ⟹ 16 f_e = 80 ⟹ **f_e = 5.0 cm**, **f_o = 15 × 5 = 75 cm**.
   Jaanch: 75/5 = 15; 75 + 5 = 80 cm ✅.

9. **False ❌.** n_rel = 1.5/1.5 = 1 ⟹ 1/f = (1 − 1)(...) = 0 ⟹ focal length **INFINITE (∞)** ho jaati hai (zero nahi)!

10. Normal incidence se ray seedhi hypotenuse par **i = 45°** par girti hai. Glass ka critical angle i_c = sin⁻¹(1/1.5) ≈ **41.8°** hai. Kyunki light denser (glass) se rarer (air) ja rahi hai aur **i (45°) > i_c (41.8°)**, isliye 100% TIR hota hai!

| Spherical Mirrors | 1/v + 1/u = 1/f; m = −v/u; f = R/2 | Concave: f < 0; Convex: f > 0 (always virtual & diminished) |
