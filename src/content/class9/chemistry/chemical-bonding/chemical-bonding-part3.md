# Topic 3: Covalent Bond: Electron Baantne Ka Rishta

Pichhle topic mein ek **metal** aur ek **non-metal** ne electron ka **de-le** kiya: Na ne diya, Cl ne liya, aur bane Na⁺ aur Cl⁻, jinhe unki opposite charge ne jod diya. Lekin sawaal ye hai: agar **dono hi non-metal** hon, jaise do chlorine ya hydrogen aur oxygen, to? Dono ko electron **chahiye**, koi dene ko taiyaar nahi. Phir bond kaise banega? Aaj uska jawab milega: **baant lo**.

---

## Earphones ki kahani

Ms. Wagle ki class ke do dost, Tanay aur Kushal, bus mein school trip par ja rahe the. Tanay ke paas ek hi earphone ka jodi tha (do earbuds, ek left aur ek right) aur wo gaana sunna chahta tha. Kushal ke paas koi earphone nahi tha, aur uska bhi gaana sunne ka mann tha.

Dono ke paas ek hi problem thi: **ek ko poora jodi mile to doosre ka kya?** Tanay ne kaha, "Chalo, **ek-ek earbud** le lete hain." Ab dono ne ek-ek earbud kaan mein laga liya. Gaana dono tak pahunch gaya.

Ms. Wagle ne ye dekh kar kaha, "Dekho, Tanay ne apna earphone Kushal ko **de nahi diya**, aur Kushal ne **le nahi liya**. Dono ne **sirf share** kiya. Jodi dono ke beech mein hai aur dono ki zarurat poori ho rahi hai. Atoms bhi yahi karte hain: jab dono ko electron chahiye aur koi dene wala nahi, to wo **electron ki jodi (pair) share** kar lete hain."

Aur ek aur baat dekho. Gaana tabhi chalta hai jab **dono** earbuds ek jodi se jude rahein. Jodi ko doosre ke paas se kheench lo to dono ka gaana band. Atoms mein bhi share kiya hua electron pair dono atoms ko **ek saath bandh ke rakhta hai**. Yahi hai **covalent bond**.

```
      Tanay                         Kushal
     (kaan)                         (kaan)
       (o)                            (o)
        \                             /
         \____ ek hi earphone ______/
              ek earbud   ek earbud
              Tanay ka    Kushal ka

       Dono ne jodi share ki: na koi dene wala, na lene wala
```

> **Figure 3.1** – Earphone sharing ka analogy: ek jodi, do kaan. Jodi ke dono earbuds dono doston ko "jodte" hain, jaise ek share kiya hua electron pair do atoms ko jodta hai. Ye bas ek sketch hai, asli electron earbud nahi hai.

## Part A: Do non-metals ek doosre ko electron kyun nahi dete?

Topic 1 mein hum dekh chuke the ki metals (1 se 3 baahari electrons) electron **dene** mein aasan paate hain. Non-metals (4 se 7 baahari electrons) ko electron **lena** ya **share karna** theek lagta hai.

Ab ek hi tarah ke do non-metal milein, jaise do Cl atoms. Dono ke 7 baahari electrons hain. Dono ko 1 electron chahiye. Koi dene ko taiyaar nahi, kyunki kisi ke paas "extra" nahi hai. Agar ek electron deta, to uski apni shell 6 ki reh jaati. Ye bilkul wahi baat hai: **do bachche, dono ko ek hi chocolate chahiye, aur chocolate sirf ek**. Samjhauta: dono ek saath khaa lo.

Isi tarah, jab H aur Cl milein, ya C aur H, ya N aur H, har baar dono ko electron chahiye (ya dena bahut mushkil hai). Isliye share karte hain.

> **Thumb rule:** do **non-metals** mil rahe hon to **covalent bond**. Metal aur non-metal ho to Topic 2 wala **ionic bond**.

(Non-metal atom se electron nikaal kar positive ion banana bahut mushkil hai, isliye ye raasta nahi chuna jaata. Iska poora "kyun" aage ki class mein.)

## Part B: Share karne ka niyam: ginti se check karo

Ek covalent bond mein **ek shared pair = 2 electrons**. Ye 2 electrons **dono atoms ki ginti mein** aate hain. Jaise earphone ki jodi: Tanay ke kaan mein jo earbud hai, wo Tanay ko bhi gaana deta hai, aur wahi jodi Kushal ko bhi.

Isliye kisi atom ki **octet (ya duplet)** ki ginti = **uske lone pairs ke electrons + har shared pair ke dono electrons** (chahe pair ka ek electron doosre atom se aaya ho).

Diagrams mein ek atom ke electrons **. (dot)** se aur doosre atom ke **x (cross)** se dikhaye hain, taaki pata rahe kaun sa electron kahan se aaya. Dots kis taraf rakhe hain, ye sirf picture hai (Topic 1); **ginti** hi maayne rakhti hai.

**Ginti ka tareeka (har atom ke liye):**

```
   atom ke paas kul = (lone pair ke electrons) + (shared pairs ke electrons)
                    = (apne bache electrons)   + 2 x (bonds ki ginti)

   H ke liye 2 hona chahiye (duplet).   Baaki sab ke liye 8 (octet).
```

Chaliye ek-ek karke dekhte hain, pehle sabse chhote se.

## Part C: Single bond: H₂, Cl₂, HCl

### H₂: sabse chhota molecule

H ke paas sirf 1 electron hai (K shell mein). Duplet banane ke liye 1 aur chahiye. Do H milein to **dono apne 1-1 electron ki jodi** share karte hain.

```
     H .   +   x H      ---->      H .x H      ---->      H - H

   (pehle H ka      (doosre H ka        shared pair          dash
    electron .)      electron x)        (2 electron)       (ek bond)
```

Ginti: pehle H ke paas ab 2 (shared pair), doosre H ke paas bhi 2. Dono ka **duplet poora**, helium jaisa.

### Cl₂: do chlorine

Cl: 2, 8, 7. Baahari 7. Octet mein **1** kam. Do Cl milein to **1 jodi** share karte hain. Baaki 6 electrons (3 jodi) har Cl ke paas alag rehte hain: ye **lone pairs** hain.

```
        ..         xx                 ..    xx
      : Cl .  +  x Cl xx     --->   : Cl .x Cl xx     --->   Cl - Cl
        ..         xx                 ..    xx
   (7 e)          (7 e)            shared pair beech mein          dash
```

Ginti: har Cl ke paas 3 lone pairs (6 e) + 1 shared pair (2 e) = **8**. Dono ka octet poora.


### HCl: do alag atoms, ek bond

H: 1 electron (duplet ke liye 1 aur chahiye). Cl: 7 baahari (octet ke liye 1 aur). Dono ko **1 chahiye**, aur ek jodi share karne se dono ka kaam ho jaata hai.

```
                  ..                 ..                    ..
      H x   +   . Cl :   --->   H x. Cl :     --->     H - Cl :
                  ..                 ..                    ..
   (1 e)         (7 e)      shared pair beech mein      single bond
```

Ginti: H ke paas 2 (shared pair) = duplet. Cl ke paas 3 lone pairs (6) + 1 shared pair (2) = 8 = octet. Dono khush.

```
   H-H            Cl-Cl           H-Cl
   H₂             Cl₂             HCl
   (1 bond)       (1 bond,        (1 bond,
                   6 e baaki)      6 e Cl par baaki)
```

> **Figure 3.2** – H₂, Cl₂ aur HCl: har molecule mein sirf **ek** shared pair hai (single bond, dash se dikhaya). H par koi lone pair nahi, jabki har Cl par 3 lone pairs (6 electrons) rehte hain.

## Part D: Solved examples: single bond

### Example 1. H₂ ka bond banao aur ginti se check karo

- H: 1 electron. Duplet ke liye 1 aur chahiye.
- Do H apne 1-1 electron ki jodi share karte hain: **H .x H**, dash mein **H−H**.
- Molecule ka formula **H₂**.

Jaanch: pehle H ke paas 2 electron (shared pair) = duplet ✅. Doosre H ke paas bhi 2 ✅. Kul electrons = 1 + 1 = 2 ✅ (jitne the utne hi, sirf share hue).

### Example 2. Cl₂ ka bond

- Cl: 2, 8, 7. Baahari 7, 1 kam.
- Ek shared pair banaya. Har Cl ke paas 6 electron (3 lone pairs) bache.
- Dash diagram: **Cl−Cl** (aur har Cl par 3 lone pairs).

Jaanch: har Cl: 6 + 2 = **8** ✅. Kul baahari electrons = 7 + 7 = 14. Bond mein 2 + lone pairs mein 6 + 6 = **14** ✅.

### Example 3. HCl ka bond

- H: 1 baahari, 1 chahiye. Cl: 7 baahari, 1 chahiye.
- Dono ne 1 jodi share ki: **H−Cl**.

Jaanch: H: 2 = duplet ✅. Cl: 6 + 2 = 8 ✅. Kul baahari electrons = 1 + 7 = 8; bond 2 + Cl ke lone pairs 6 = **8** ✅.

Ye bhi dhyan do: HCl mein ek atom H hai, ek Cl, aur donon alag atoms hain, to ye **compound** hai (Chapter 2). H₂ aur Cl₂ **tatva ke molecule** hain.

## Part E: Ek se zyada bond wale atoms: H₂O, NH₃, CH₄

Pehle valency yaad karo (Chapter 5): H 1, O 2, N 3, C 4. **Valency = us atom ke bond ki ginti.** Ab dekhte hain kaise.

### H₂O: paani

O: 2, 6. Baahari 6, octet ke liye **2** chahiye. H ko 1 chahiye. To **ek O do H ke saath** judta hai: O har H ke saath 1 jodi share karta hai.

```
   Dot (O ke electron) aur cross (H ke electron):

             ..
        H x. O .x H       <-- O ke upar aur neeche 2 lone pairs (4 e)
             ..

   Dash diagram:   H - O - H      (O par 2 lone pairs)
```

Ginti: O ke paas 2 shared pairs (4 e) + 2 lone pairs (4 e) = **8** ✅. Har H ke paas 2 = duplet ✅.

### NH₃: ammonia

N: 2, 5. Baahari 5, octet ke liye **3** chahiye. Isliye **3 H** (har ek ke 1 electron) share hote hain: 3 bond. N par 2 baahari electron (1 jodi) bach jaate hain.

```
   Dot (N ke electron) aur cross (H ke electron) ke saath NH₃:

            H
            x
            .
       H x. N .x H         <-- N ke neeche 1 lone pair (2 e)
            ..

   Dash diagram:     H - N - H       (N par 1 lone pair)
                         |
                         H
```

Ginti: N: 3 shared pairs (6 e) + 1 lone pair (2 e) = **8** ✅. Har H: 2 ✅.

```
   Paani, H₂O                    Ammonia, NH₃

          ..                          ..
        H-O-H    (2 bond,           H-N-H     (3 bond,
          ..      2 lone pairs)       |        1 lone pair)
                                      H
```

> **Figure 3.3** – H₂O mein O ke 2 bond aur 2 lone pairs hain, NH₃ mein N ke 3 bond aur 1 lone pair. Dono mein har H duplet aur O ya N octet mein hai. (Asli molecule ka aakaar, jaise H₂O ka "bent" hona, aage ki class mein; yahan hum sirf jodi ginte hain.)

### CH₄: methane

C: 2, 4. Baahari 4, octet ke liye **4** chahiye. To **4 H** (har ek 1 electron) share karte hain: 4 single bonds. C ke koi lone pair nahi bachte, kyunki saare 4 electron share ho gaye.

```
             H
             x                        H
             .                        |
        H x. C .x H     ---->     H - C - H
             .                        |
             x                        H
             H

        C ke 4 electron (dot) aur 4 H ke electron (x): 4 shared pairs, koi lone pair nahi
```

Ginti: C: 4 shared pairs (8 e) = **8** ✅. Har H: 2 ✅.

> **Figure 3.4** – CH₄ (methane): beech mein C aur 4 H. 4 single bonds, C par koi lone pair nahi. Dash diagram sirf bonds ki ginti dikhata hai; asli molecule 3D hota hai, par uski baat aage ki class mein.

## Part F: Solved examples: H₂O, NH₃, CH₄

### Example 4. H₂O ke bonds banao aur check karo

- O (2, 6): octet ke liye 2 aur chahiye, to 2 bond. H (1): duplet ke liye 1, to 1 bond.
- O do H ke saath 2 jodi share karta hai. Bache: O ke 6 − 2 = 4 electron = **2 lone pairs**.
- Dash: **H−O−H**.

Jaanch: O: 2 shared pairs (4 e) + 2 lone pairs (4 e) = **8** ✅. Har H: 2 ✅. Kul baahari electrons = 6 + 1 + 1 = 8; bond mein 2 × 2 = 4 + lone pairs 4 = **8** ✅.

### Example 5. NH₃ ke bonds

- N (2, 5): 3 aur chahiye, to 3 bond, 3 H ke saath. Bache: 5 − 3 = 2 e = **1 lone pair**.

Jaanch: N: 3 shared pairs (6 e) + 1 lone pair (2 e) = **8** ✅. Har H: 2 ✅. Kul baahari = 5 + 3 = 8 ✅; bond 6 + lone pair 2 = **8** ✅.

### Example 6. CH₄ ke bonds

- C (2, 4): 4 aur chahiye, to 4 bond, 4 H ke saath. Bache: 4 − 4 = 0 lone pair.

Jaanch: C: 4 shared pairs (8 e) = **8** ✅. Har H: 2 ✅. Kul baahari = 4 + 4 = 8; bond mein 4 × 2 = **8** ✅.

Ye sab ek pattern dikha rahe hain: **bond ki ginti = valency.** H₂O mein O ke 2 bond (valency 2), NH₃ mein N ke 3 (valency 3), CH₄ mein C ke 4 (valency 4). Chapter 5 mein valency baahari electrons se nikli thi, aur ab dikha ki valency **kitni jodi share hogi** wo bhi batati hai.

## Part G: Double bond aur triple bond

Kabhi-kabhi ek jodi se kaam nahi chalta. Ek hi do atoms ke beech **2 ya 3 jodi** share karni padti hain. Phir se earphone: agar Tanay aur Kushal ke paas ek jodi ki jagah **do jodi earphones** hoti aur dono ek-doosre ke saath 2-2 share karte, to bandhan **zyada pakka**. Yahi baat double aur triple bond mein hai.

### O₂: double bond

O: 2, 6. Baahari 6, 2 aur chahiye. Do O atoms, har ek ko 2 chahiye. Agar sirf 1 jodi share karte to har O ke paas 7 hote (6 + 1), octet nahi. To **2 jodi** share karte hain: **O=O**.

```
       ..       xx                 ..      ..
       O  .x.x  O       ---->      O   =   O        (har O par 2 lone pairs)
       ..       xx                 ..      ..
   (ek O ke electron ., doosre ke x; beech mein 2 jodi, har O par 4 bache)
```

Ginti: har O: 2 shared pairs (4 e) + 2 lone pairs (4 e) = **8** ✅.

### N₂: triple bond

N: 2, 5. Baahari 5, 3 aur chahiye. Do N atoms **3 jodi** share karte hain: **N≡N**. Har N par 1 lone pair bachta hai.

```
       : N .x.x.x N xx     ---->     : N ≡ N :      (har N par 1 lone pair)
   (ek N ke electron ., doosre ke x; beech mein 3 jodi)
```

Ginti: har N: 3 shared pairs (6 e) + 1 lone pair (2 e) = **8** ✅. Hawa ki 78% gas yahi N₂ hai.

### CO₂: do double bonds

C: 2, 4 (4 chahiye). O: 2, 6 (2 chahiye). Ek C do O ke saath judta hai: C har O ke saath **2 jodi** share karta hai (kul 4 jodi, C ke 4 electron ki ginti). Dash: **O=C=O**.

```
       ..              ..              ..          ..
       O  ..xx C xx..  O      ---->    O  =  C  =  O
       ..              ..              ..          ..
   (C ke 4 electron x: 2 ek O ki taraf, 2 doosre O ki taraf; har O aur C ke beech 2 jodi)
```

Ginti: C: 2 double bonds = 4 shared pairs (8 e) = **8** ✅. Har O: 2 shared pairs (4 e) + 2 lone pairs (4 e) = **8** ✅.

```
   O=O  (O₂)            N≡N  (N₂)           O=C=O  (CO₂)

   2 jodi share         3 jodi share        C aur har O ke beech
   har O par 2          har N par 1         2 jodi, kul 4 jodi
   lone pairs           lone pair           har O par 2 lone pairs
```

> **Figure 3.5** – O₂ (double bond), N₂ (triple bond) aur CO₂ (do double bonds). Jitni jodi share hoti hain, utne dash. Har atom ka octet poora hota hai. Dhyan do: CO₂ mein C ke 4 bond hain (do double bonds), jabki har O ke 2.

## Part H: Solved examples: double aur triple bond

### Example 7. O₂ mein double bond kyun?

- O: 2, 6, 2 chahiye. Ek jodi se 7, do jodi se 8.
- Isliye **2 jodi share**: O=O.

Jaanch: har O: 2 shared pairs (4) + 2 lone pairs (4) = **8** ✅. Kul baahari = 6 + 6 = 12. Bond ki 2 jodi = 4 electron, aur lone pairs 2 + 2 = 4 jodi = 8 electron; 4 + 8 = **12** ✅.

### Example 8. N₂ mein triple bond

- N: 2, 5, 3 chahiye. 3 jodi share karna hai.
- N≡N, har N par 1 lone pair.

Jaanch: har N: 3 shared pairs (6) + 1 lone pair (2) = **8** ✅. Kul baahari = 5 + 5 = 10; bond 3 × 2 = 6 + lone pairs 2 + 2 = 4, kul **10** ✅.

### Example 9. CO₂ ka bond

- C (2, 4): 4 chahiye. Har O (2, 6): 2 chahiye.
- C ke 4 electron do O mein baant do: 2 + 2. Har O ke saath 2 jodi. O=C=O.

Jaanch: C: 4 shared pairs (8) = **8** ✅. Har O: 4 + 4 = **8** ✅. Kul baahari = 4 + 6 + 6 = 16; bond 4 jodi × 2 = 8 + O ke lone pairs 4 + 4 = 8, kul **16** ✅.

### Example 10. Kaun sa bond? Sirf formula dekh ke batao

Formula **HF** ke liye: H (1) aur F (2, 7), dono non-metal. Dono ko 1 chahiye. To **1 jodi share**, **H−F**, single bond. F par 3 lone pairs (6 e) bache.

Jaanch: H: 2 ✅. F: 6 + 2 = **8** ✅. Is formula ka pattern HCl jaisa hi hai kyunki F aur Cl dono ke 7 baahari electrons hain.

## Part I: Covalent compound molecules mein hote hain

Chapter 4 mein humne **formula unit mass** padha tha aur kaha tha ki NaCl jaise ionic compound mein ek akela "molecule" nahi hota. Topic 2 mein humne uska reason dekha: lattice. Ab covalent ke liye ulta hai.

H₂O ka ek molecule: **2 H aur 1 O**, bond se jude. Ye jhund apne aap mein **poora aur alag** hai. Paani ke ek glass mein aise bahut saare alag molecule hain, jaise ek bag mein bahut saari gend. Isliye covalent compounds ke liye **molecule** sahi shabd hai, aur **molecular mass** (Chapter 4) ka matlab bhi yahin se banta hai:

| Molecule | Atoms | Molecular mass in u (H = 1, C = 12, N = 14, O = 16, Cl = 35.5) |
|---|---|---|
| H₂ | 2 H | 2 × 1 = **2** |
| Cl₂ | 2 Cl | 2 × 35.5 = **71** |
| HCl | 1 H, 1 Cl | 1 + 35.5 = **36.5** |
| H₂O | 2 H, 1 O | 2 + 16 = **18** |
| NH₃ | 1 N, 3 H | 14 + 3 = **17** |
| CH₄ | 1 C, 4 H | 12 + 4 = **16** |
| O₂ | 2 O | 2 × 16 = **32** |
| N₂ | 2 N | 2 × 14 = **28** |
| CO₂ | 1 C, 2 O | 12 + 32 = **44** |

Ek aur baat: covalent bond ek molecule ke **andar** hota hai. Alag-alag molecules ke beech sirf kamzor khinchaav (aakarshan) hota hai, jo Chapter 1 mein dekha tha. Isi bhed ka asar kaisa hota hai, wo agle topic mein.

## Aam galtiyan (bachke rehna)

**Galti 1: Saare baahari electrons share kar dena**

Cl₂ mein har Cl ke 7 mein se sirf **1** electron share hota hai. Baaki 6 (3 lone pairs) apne paas rehte hain. Octet poora karne ke liye 1 hi chahiye tha.

**Galti 2: Atom ke apne electrons bhool jaana**

Ginti karte waqt sirf shared pairs dekhna kaafi nahi. O₂ mein har O ke 4 lone electrons bhi ginne hain: 4 + 4 = 8, sirf 4 nahi.

**Galti 3: Duplet aur octet mix karna**

H ke liye **2** kaafi hai, 8 nahi. H₂O mein O ko 8 chahiye, par har H ko sirf 2.

**Galti 4: Bond ki ginti galat likhna**

Dash ki ginti valency ke barabar honi chahiye: O ke 2, N ke 3, C ke 4, H ke 1. CH₄ mein C par 3 ya 5 dash likhna galat hai.

**Galti 5: Covalent molecule mein ions likhna**

H₂O mein H⁺ aur O²⁻ nahi hote. Yahan electron **share** hue hain, de-le nahi hue, aur koi atom charged ion nahi bana.

**Galti 6: Metal aur non-metal par covalent bond lagana**

Do non-metals = covalent. Metal + non-metal (jaise NaCl) = ionic, jo Topic 2 mein tha.

## Quick Recap

- Do **non-metals** jab milte hain, to koi electron deta nahi; wo **electron ki jodi share** karte hain. Isse bana bond **covalent bond** hai.
- Ek shared pair = 2 electrons, aur ye dono atoms ki octet (ya duplet) ginti mein aata hai.
- **Single bond:** H₂ (H−H), Cl₂ (Cl−Cl), HCl (H−Cl), H₂O (H−O−H), NH₃ (N par 3 bond), CH₄ (C par 4 bond).
- **Double bond:** O₂ (O=O), CO₂ (O=C=O). **Triple bond:** N₂ (N≡N).
- Ginti ka niyam: **lone pairs + shared pairs ke electrons = 8** (H ke liye 2).
- Bond ki ginti = atom ki valency: H 1, O 2, N 3, C 4.
- Covalent compound alag **molecules** bana ke rehta hai, isliye **molecular mass** ka matlab hai (H₂O = 18, CO₂ = 44).

### Answers

1. Dono Cl ke 7 baahari electrons hain, to dono ko 1 electron chahiye. Koi bhi apna electron dene ko taiyaar nahi (wo 6 par aa jaata). To dono **ek jodi share** karte hain. Isse har Cl ke paas 6 + 2 = **8** electrons ho jaate hain: octet.

2. H .x H, dash mein **H−H**. Har H ke paas shared pair ke **2** electrons hain, jo **duplet** hai (helium jaisa). Isse **covalent bond (single bond)** kehte hain.

3. H (1 e) aur Cl (7 e) ek jodi share karte hain: **H−Cl**. Cl par 3 lone pairs. H: **2** = duplet ✅. Cl: 6 + 2 = **8** = octet ✅.

4. O ke do H ke saath **2 shared pairs** hain, aur **2 lone pairs**. O: 4 + 4 = **8** ✅. Har H: 2 ✅. Dash diagram H−O−H.

5. NH₃: N ke 3 bond aur **1 lone pair**. CH₄: C ke 4 bond. Kyunki C ke paas 4 baahari electrons hain aur saare 4 share ho jaate hain, to koi lone pair nahi bachta. C: 4 jodi = 8 ✅.

6. O ko 2 aur chahiye, to do O **2 jodi** share karte hain (O=O). N ko 3 chahiye, to do N **3 jodi** (N≡N). Har baar bond ki jodi ki ginti = jitne electron chahiye. Tab har atom ke paas 8 ho jaate hain ✅.

7. **O=C=O**. C ke paas 2 double bonds, yaani 4 shared pairs = **8** electrons ✅. Har O: 2 shared pairs (4) + 2 lone pairs (4) = **8** ✅.

8. C ke 4 baahari electrons hain aur octet ke liye 4 aur chahiye. 3 bond se C ke paas 6 hi hote (3 jodi). Octet adhura. C par **4 bond** (4 H ke saath) hone chahiye. Tab 4 jodi = 8 ✅.

9. Cl₂ = 2 × 35.5 = **71**. NH₃ = 14 + 3 × 1 = **17**. CH₄ = 12 + 4 × 1 = **16**. CO₂ = 12 + 2 × 16 = **44**.

10. (a) **False** ❌. Har Cl sirf **1** electron share karta hai (1 jodi), 6 apne paas rakhta hai. (b) **False** ❌. H₂ mein **single bond** (H−H) hai. (c) **True** ✅. Har H ke paas 2 electron hain. (d) **True** ✅. O=C=O.

11. Dono atom ek hi non-metal (F) ke hain, koi electron dene ko taiyaar nahi, to **covalent bond**. F (2, 7) ko 1 chahiye, to dono **ek jodi share** karte hain: **F−F**, aur har F par 3 lone pairs. Har F: 6 + 2 = **8** ✅. Kul baahari electrons 7 + 7 = 14 = bond 2 + lone pairs 12 ✅.
